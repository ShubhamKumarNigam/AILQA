Summary:
The Official Secrets Act, 1923 (Act No. 19 OF 1923) is a law in India that aims to consolidate and amend the law related to official secrets. The Act is applicable to the whole of India and extends to servants of the Government and citizens of India outside India. The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "document," "munitions of war," "office under Government," "photograph," and "prohibited place." The Act outlines penalties for spying, including approaching, inspecting, passing over, or being in the vicinity of a prohibited place, making a sketch, plan, model, or note that is useful to an enemy, or obtaining, collecting, recording, or publishing or communicating any secret official code or pass word, or any sketch, plan, model, article or note or other document or information which is useful to an enemy. The Act also includes provisions for communications with foreign agents to be evidence of commission of certain offences and wrongful communication, retention, or use of information. The Act also includes provisions for penalties for harbouring spies. The Act includes provisions for search warrants and the exclusion of the public from proceedings. The Act also includes provisions for offences by companies and the liability of directors, managers, secretaries, or other officers of the company.
