
Summary:
The Ancient Monuments Preservation Act, 1904 is a law in India that aims to preserve ancient monuments and objects of archaeological, historical, or artistic interest. The Act applies to the whole of India except the state of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act defines an ancient monument as a structure, erection, monument, tumulus, place of interment, cave, rock-sculpture, inscription, or monolith, which is of historical, archaeological, or artistic interest, or any remains thereof. Antiquities include any movable objects which the Central Government thinks it necessary to protect against injury, removal, or dispersion. The Act allows the Central Government to declare an ancient monument to be a protected monument within the meaning of this Act. The Act also provides for the acquisition of rights in or guardianship of an ancient monument, preservation of ancient monument by agreement, ownership under disability or not in possession, enforcement of agreement, application of endowment to repair of an ancient monument, compulsory purchase of ancient monument, maintenance of certain protected monuments, voluntary contributions, protection of place of worship from misuse, pollution or desecration, relinquishment of Government rights in a monument, right of access to certain protected monuments, and penalties. The Act also includes provisions for the control of traffic in antiquities and the protection of sculptures, carvings, images, bas-reliefs, inscriptions, or like objects. The Act also empowers the Central Government to notify areas as protected and transfer ownership of antiquities buried in protected areas to the Government.
