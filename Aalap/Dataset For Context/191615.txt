Summary:

The Hindu Disposition of Property Act, 1916, is an Act that aims to remove certain disabilities in the power of disposition of property by Hindus for the benefit of individuals who are not yet born at the time of such disposition. This Act is also known as Act No. 15 of 1916, and it was enacted on September 28, 1916.

The Act consists of five sections. Section 1 states that this Act may be called the Hindu Disposition of Property Act, 1916, and it extends to the whole of India. Section 2 states that no disposition of property by a Hindu, whether by transfer inter vivos (between living persons) or by will, shall be invalid by reason only that any person for whose benefit it may have been made was not in existence at the date of such disposition. However, this is subject to the limitations and provisions specified in Section 3.

Section 3 outlines the limitations and conditions for dispositions of property by Hindus. In the case of transfers inter vivos, the limitations and provisions are those contained in Chapter II of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882. In the case of dispositions by will, the limitations and provisions are those contained in sections 113, 114, 115, and 116 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925.

Section 4, which has been repealed, dealt with the failure of prior disposition. Section 5 allows for the extension of the provisions of this Act to the Khoja community, subject to certain conditions.

Overall, the Hindu Disposition of Property Act, 1916, aims to remove disabilities in the power of disposition of property by Hindus for the benefit of individuals who are not yet born at the time of such disposition, subject to certain limitations and conditions. This Act applies to the whole of India, and its provisions can also be extended to the Khoja community under certain circumstances.
