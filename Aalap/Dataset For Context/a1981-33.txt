Summary:
The Cine-workers Welfare Fund Act, 1981, also known as Act No. 33 of 1981, was enacted on 17th September 1981, to finance activities that promote the welfare of certain cine-workers. The Act defines various terms used throughout, including "cinematograph film," "cine-worker," "feature film," "Fund," "prescribed," and "producer."

The Act establishes a Fund, known as the Cine-workers Welfare Fund, to which various sums are credited, including amounts provided by the Central Government, grants made by the Central Government, donations, and income from investments of the amounts in the Fund.

The Fund is used to meet the expenditure incurred in connection with measures and facilities necessary or expedient to promote the welfare of cine-workers, including defraying the cost of welfare measures or facilities, providing assistance in the form of grants or loans to indigent cine-workers, sanctioning money in aid of approved schemes for the welfare of cine-workers, and meeting the allowances and salaries of members of Advisory Committees and persons appointed under the Act.

The Act also provides for the establishment of Advisory Committees and a Central Advisory Committee to advise the Central Government on matters arising out of the administration of the Act. The Advisory Committees and the Central Advisory Committee are composed of an equal number of members representing the Government, cine-workers, and producers.

The Act empowers the Central Government to appoint Welfare Commissioners, Welfare Administrators, Inspectors, and other officers and staff as necessary for the purposes of the Act. These officers and staff are deemed to be public servants within the meaning of section 21 of the Indian Penal Code.

The Act requires the Central Government to publish an annual report giving an account of the activities financed under the Act during the financial year, together with a statement of accounts. The Central Government may also require a producer to furnish statistical and other information in such form and within such period as may be prescribed.

The Act empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of the Act, including the manner in which the Fund may be applied, the conditions governing the grant of any loan, the composition of the Advisory Committees and the Central Advisory Committee, the recruitment, conditions of service, and duties of all persons appointed under the Act, the powers that may be exercised by a Welfare Commissioner, a Welfare Administrator, and an Inspector, and the furnishing of information by a producer.

The Act provides that any rule made under the Act may direct that a breach thereof shall be punishable with fine, and that every rule made under the Act shall be laid before both Houses of Parliament for a total period of thirty days. If both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be.
