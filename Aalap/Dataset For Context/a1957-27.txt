The Wealth-Tax Act, 1957 is a law in India that provides for the levy of wealth-tax. The Act came into force on April 1, 1957. The Act defines wealth-tax as a tax on the wealth of individuals, Hindu undivided families, and companies. The Act applies to the whole of India.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, including "assessee," "assets," "Assessing Officer," and "assessment." An assessee is a person who is liable to pay wealth-tax or any other sum of money under the Act. Assets are defined as property of every description, movable or immovable, but certain types of assets are excluded from this definition. The Act also defines the Assessing Officer as the Deputy Commissioner of Income-tax or the Assistant Commissioner or the Income-tax Officer who is responsible for assessing the wealth-tax liability of an assessee. An assessment is the process of determining the wealth-tax liability of an assessee.

The Act specifies the types of assets that are subject to wealth-tax. These include buildings, land, motor cars, jewellery, bullion, furniture, utensils, yachts, boats, aircrafts, and urban land. Cash in hand in excess of fifty thousand rupees is also subject to wealth-tax for individuals and Hindu undivided families, and any amount not recorded in the books of account for other persons. Certain assets are excluded from the definition of assets, such as agricultural land, animals, and a right to an annuity where the terms and conditions preclude commutation.

The Act also specifies the rates of wealth-tax. For the assessment year 2020-2021, the rate of wealth-tax is 1% of the net wealth exceeding fifty lakh rupees. Net wealth is calculated as the total value of assets less the total value of liabilities.

The Act provides for the assessment of wealth-tax, which is the process of determining the wealth-tax liability of an assessee. The Assessing Officer is responsible for conducting the assessment. The Act also provides for appeals against the assessment, which can be made to the Commissioner (Appeals) and then to the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, specifies the types of assets that are subject to wealth-tax, and provides for the assessment and appeals process. The rate of wealth-tax is 1% of the net wealth exceeding fifty lakh rupees for the assessment year 2020-2021.
