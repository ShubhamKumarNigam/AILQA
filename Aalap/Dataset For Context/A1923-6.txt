Summary:

THE CANTONMENTS (HOUSE-ACCOMMODATION) ACT, 1923, is an act that aims to further amend and consolidate the law relating to the provision of house-accommodation for military officers in cantonments. The act is divided into six chapters, each with its own set of sections.

Chapter I, titled 'Preliminary,' includes sections 1 and 2. Section 1 defines the short title, extent, and commencement of the act, while section 2 provides definitions for various terms used throughout the act.

Chapter II, titled 'Application of Act,' includes section 3, which outlines the cantonments or parts of cantonments in which the act will be operative, and section 4, which saves written instruments from the provisions of the act.

Chapter III, titled 'Appropriation of Houses,' includes sections 5 to 11. Section 5 states that every house in a cantonment or part of a cantonment in respect of which a notification under section 3 is in force is liable to appropriation by the Central Government on a lease. Section 6 outlines the conditions on which houses may be appropriated, while section 7 outlines the procedure for taking a house on lease. Section 8 has been repealed, and section 9 states that no house in any cantonment or part of a cantonment in which this Act is operative shall be occupied for certain purposes without the previous sanction of the Officer Commanding the District. Section 10 lists the cases in which houses shall not be appropriated, and section 11 outlines the time to be allowed for giving possession of a house.

Chapter IV, titled 'Procedure in References,' includes sections 19 to 21. These sections outline the jurisdiction, procedure, and powers of the court in references made under the act.

Chapter V, titled 'Appeals,' includes section 29, which states that an appeal shall lie to the High Court against the decision of the Court of the District Judge upon a reference tried by it.

Chapter VI, titled 'Supplemental Provisions,' includes sections 30 to 38. These sections outline various provisions related to appeals, service of notices, computation of periods of limitation, power for the Central Government to make rules, inapplicability of section 556 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, to trials of offences, protection to persons acting under the act, and repeals.

The act also includes a schedule, which has been repealed. The appendix includes forms for use under the act, and the footnotes provide additional information regarding the act's provisions and history.
