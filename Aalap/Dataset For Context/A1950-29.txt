The Transfer of Prisoners Act, 1950 (Act No. 29 of 1950) came into effect on the 12th of April, 1950. This Act applies to the entire country of India. The Act's primary objective is to enable the transfer of prisoners from one state to another.

The Act defines several key terms, including "court," which refers to any officer exercising civil, criminal, or revenue jurisdiction. "Government" or "State Government" refers to the administrator of a Part C State. "Prison" includes any place declared as a subsidiary jail by the State Government.

Under Section 3 of the Act, the Government of a State may, with the consent of the Government of another State, order the removal of a prisoner from one prison to another. This applies to prisoners who are confined under a sentence of death, imprisonment or transportation, in default of payment of a fine, or in default of giving security for keeping the peace or maintaining good behavior.

The officer in charge of the prison to which the prisoner is removed is responsible for receiving and detaining the prisoner according to the exigency of any writ, warrant, or order of the court that committed the prisoner.

The Act also includes a repealed section, which was related to the amendment of section 29, Act III of 1900.

The Act has been extended to Kohima and Mokakchung Districts by Regulation 7 of 1961, Dadra and Nagar Haveli by Regulation 6 of 1963, and Goa, Daman and Diu by notification No. GSR 430, dated 9-3-1965. The words "extends to the State of Jammu and Kashmir" were omitted by Act 25 of 1968, section 2 and the Schedule (w.e.f. 15-8-1968). Sub-clause (b) was also substituted by the Adaptation of Laws (No. 3) Order, 1956, for clause (b) (w.e.f. 1-11-1956).
