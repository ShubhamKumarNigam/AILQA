Summary:
The Union Territories (Laws) Act, 1950, is an Act that provides for the extension of laws to certain Union territories, specifically Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, and Tripura. The Act empowers the Central Government to extend any enactment that is in force in a State to these Union territories, with any restrictions and modifications as it thinks fit.

Section 3 of the Act extends certain Acts and Ordinances to the States of Tripura, Vindhya Pradesh, and Manipur. The Indian Penal Code in its application to the State of Manipur shall have effect as if certain alterations have been made, such as substituting the word "fifteen" for "eighteen" in section 361 and "fourteen" for "sixteen" and "thirteen" for "fifteen" in section 375, Fifthly.

Section 4 of the Act deals with repeals and savings. It repeals certain laws, such as the Delhi Laws Act, 1912, the Ajmer Merwara (Extension of Laws) Act, 1947, and any law that corresponds to an Act or Ordinance extended to any of the States of Manipur, Tripura, and Vindhya Pradesh. However, the repeal does not affect any previous operations of the law, any penalty, forfeiture, or punishment incurred in respect of any offence committed against the law, or any investigation, legal proceeding, or remedy in respect of any such penalty, forfeiture, or punishment.

The Schedule to the Act lists the Acts and Ordinances that are extended to the States of Tripura and Vindhya Pradesh and those that are extended to the State of Manipur as from the date of commencement of the Union Territories (Laws) Amendment Act, 1956.

The Act also includes provisions for the adaptation of laws to the matter before a Court or other authority, and for the continuation of certain actions taken under the repealed laws.
