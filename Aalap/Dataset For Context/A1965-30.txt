Summary:

The Goa, Daman and Diu (Extension of the Code of Civil Procedure and the Arbitration Act) Act, 1965, extends the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and the Arbitration Act, 1940, to the Union territory of Goa, Daman and Diu. This Act received the assent of the President on September 25, 1965.

Section 1 of the Act outlines the short title and commencement of the Act, which states that the Act may be called the Goa, Daman and Diu (Extension of the Code of Civil Procedure and the Arbitration Act) Act, 1965, and that it shall come into force on the date appointed by the Central Government through a notification in the Official Gazette.

Section 2 defines certain terms used in the Act, including "Goa, Daman and Diu" and "Lieutenant-Governor."

Section 3 extends the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and the Arbitration Act, 1940, to Goa, Daman and Diu.

Section 4 deals with the repeal and saving of certain laws in force in Goa, Daman and Diu. It states that any law in force in Goa, Daman and Diu that corresponds to the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, or the Arbitration Act, 1940, or any part of the said Code or Act, shall stand repealed as from the coming into force of this Act in Goa, Daman and Diu. However, the repeal shall not affect the previous operation of any law so repealed or anything duly done or suffered thereunder, or any right, privilege, obligation, or liability acquired, accrued, or incurred under any law so repealed, or any investigation, legal proceeding, or remedy in respect of any such right, privilege, obligation, or liability as aforesaid.

Section 5 lays down the rules of construction of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and the Arbitration Act, 1940, in relation to Goa, Daman and Diu. It states that any reference to any provision of law not in force or to any functionary not in existence in Goa, Daman and Diu shall be construed as a reference to the corresponding law in force or to the corresponding functionary in existence in that Union territory. It also states that any reference to the State Government shall be construed as a reference to the Central Government and also as including a reference to the Lieutenant-Governor.

Section 6 empowers the Central Government to remove any difficulty that arises in giving effect to the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, or the Arbitration Act, 1940, extended by this Act to Goa, Daman and Diu.

Section 7 contains consequential provisions that insert certain words in the Goa, Daman and Diu (Judicial Commissioner’s Court) Regulation, 1963.

The Act also contains a Schedule, an Annexure, an Appendix, and Footnotes.
