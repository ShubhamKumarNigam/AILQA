Summary:

The National Judicial Appointments Commission Act, 2014 outlines the process for appointing judges to the Supreme Court and High Courts in India. The Act establishes the National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC), which is responsible for making recommendations to the Central Government for appointing judges. The NJAC is headed by a Chairperson and includes six other members, including the Law Minister, two eminent persons nominated by a committee, and the Chief Justice of India or a senior judge of the Supreme Court.

Under the Act, the Central Government is required to intimate the NJAC of any vacancies in the posts of judges in the Supreme Court and High Courts. The NJAC is then responsible for recommending candidates for these positions based on their ability, merit, and other criteria of suitability. The Act specifies that the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court should be recommended for appointment as the Chief Justice of India, provided they are considered fit to hold the office. For appointments to the High Court, the NJAC considers inter se seniority of High Court judges and their ability, merit, and other criteria of suitability.

The Act also provides for the transfer of Chief Justices and other judges of High Courts from one High Court to another. The NJAC is responsible for specifying the procedure for such transfers.

The Act specifies that the President shall appoint the Chief Justice of India or a judge of the Supreme Court or, as the case may be, the Chief Justice of a High Court or a judge of a High Court based on the recommendations made by the NJAC. The President may require the NJAC to reconsider its recommendations if necessary.

The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules and the NJAC to make regulations to carry out the provisions of this Act. Every rule and regulation made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days. If both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or regulation or both Houses agree that the rule or regulation should not be made, the rule or regulation shall have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be.

The Act also provides for the removal of difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of this Act. The Central Government may, after consultation with the NJAC, make such provisions, not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act as appear to it to be necessary or expedient for removing the difficulty. Every order made under this section shall be laid before each House of Parliament.
