Summary:
The President's Emoluments and Pension Act, 1951, provides for the emoluments of the President and pensions to retiring Presidents. According to Section 1A of the Act, the President is entitled to a monthly emolument of five lakh rupees. Upon retirement, a President is entitled to a pension of fifty per cent of the emoluments per month for the remainder of their life (Section 2). The pension is not payable if the person has previously held the office of the Vice-President and is already receiving a pension under the Vice-President’s Pension Act, 1997.
Retiring Presidents are also entitled to various benefits, including the use of a furnished residence, secretarial staff, medical attendance and treatment, and travel expenses (Section 2(2)). The spouse of a retiring President is also entitled to medical attendance and treatment for the remainder of their life (Section 2A). If a President dies while in office, their spouse is entitled to medical attendance and treatment, free of charge, for the remainder of their life (Section 3). The spouse of a President who dies after ceasing to hold office is entitled to free accommodation, including a furnished residence, secretarial staff, a telephone and a motor-car, and travel expenses (Section 3A).
Any sum payable under this Act shall be charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (Section 4). The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act (Section 5). If any difficulty arises in giving effect to the provisions of this Act, the Central Government may, by order, do anything not inconsistent with such provisions for the purpose of removing the difficulty (Section 6).
