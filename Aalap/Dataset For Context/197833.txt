Summary:
THE METRO RAILWAYS (CONSTRUCTION OF WORKS) ACT, 1978

This Act is designed to provide for the construction of works relating to metro railways in metropolitan cities. It was enacted on August 21, 1978, and applies to the metropolitan city of Calcutta, the National Capital Region, and other metropolitan cities and areas as may be specified by the Central Government.

The Act defines various terms used throughout, including "Advisory Board," "appellate authority," "building," "commissioner," "competent authority," "development," "land," "metro alignment," "metro city," "metro railway," "metro railway administration," "metropolitan area," and "right of user."

Chapter II of the Act establishes the Metro Railway Administration and outlines its functions, including the appointment of a General Manager for every metro railway.

Chapter III deals with the acquisition of land, buildings, streets, roads, or passages, or the right of user or any right in the nature of easement, for the construction of metro railways or other connected works. The process for acquiring land involves the publication of a notification in the Official Gazette, the survey and measurement of the land, and the hearing of objections from interested parties. If no objections are received or if they are disallowed, the land is declared to be acquired and vests in the Central Government free from all encumbrances.

Chapter IV outlines the powers and functions of the metro railway administration in constructing works, including the ability to open, divert, or temporarily close streets, roads, or other infrastructure, and to lay down signalling and other communication facilities, electric sub-stations, supply lines, and other works.

Chapter V establishes the role of commissioners in inspecting metro railways and ensuring their safety. Commissioners have the power to enter upon and inspect any metro railway or rolling stock, make enquiries or take measurements, and require answers or returns from officers or employees of the metro railway administration.

Chapter VI contains miscellaneous provisions, including the sale or disposal of surplus land, the prohibition of obstruction, the requirement for local authorities to assist, and the penalty for failure to comply with directions issued under section 21.

The Act also includes a Schedule that specifies the metro alignment for each metropolitan city to which the Act applies.
