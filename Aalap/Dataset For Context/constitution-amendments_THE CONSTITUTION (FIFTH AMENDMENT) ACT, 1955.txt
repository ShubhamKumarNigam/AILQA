Summary:

The Constitution (Fifth Amendment) Act, 1955, is a modification of Article 3 of the Indian Constitution. This amendment introduces a proviso to Article 3, which specifies that no Bill for the purpose of altering the area, boundaries, or name of any of the States specified in Part A or Part B of the First Schedule can be introduced in either House of Parliament without the President's recommendation. Furthermore, if such a proposal affects the area, boundaries, or name of any of these States, the Bill must be referred to the Legislature of that State for its views within a specified period. If the State does not express its views within the specified time, the Bill may be passed by Parliament. This amendment aims to prevent delays in the passage of State Reorganization laws by setting a time limit for expressing views by the States.
