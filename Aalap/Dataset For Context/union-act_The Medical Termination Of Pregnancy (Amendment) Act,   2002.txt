The Medical Termination of Pregnancy (Amendment) Act, 2002 is an amendment to the Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971. This amendment act changes certain definitions and provisions in the original act.

One significant change is in the definition of "lunatic" in section 2, which has been replaced with "mentally ill person." Additionally, the amendment act defines "mentally ill person" as someone who requires treatment due to mental disorder, excluding mental retardation.

Another important change is in section 4, which specifies where pregnancy can be terminated. According to this amendment, termination of pregnancy can only occur in a government-established or maintained hospital or a place approved by the government or a District Level Committee. The District Level Committee must have not less than three and not more than five members, including the Chief Medical Officer or District Health Officer as the Chairperson.

The amendment also modifies section 5 of the original act, which deals with offenses related to termination of pregnancy. The amendment act states that the termination of pregnancy by a person who is not a registered medical practitioner is punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term of two to seven years under the Indian Penal Code. It also states that terminating pregnancy in a place other than the approved places is punishable with rigorous imprisonment for the same term. Finally, the amendment act states that the owner of a place that is not approved for the purpose of terminating pregnancy under this act is punishable with rigorous imprisonment for the same term.

In summary, the Medical Termination of Pregnancy (Amendment) Act, 2002, amends the Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971, to change certain definitions and provisions related to the termination of pregnancy in India, specifying where and by whom pregnancy can be terminated and the penalties for violating these provisions.
