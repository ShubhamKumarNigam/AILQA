Summary:
The Ka’zi’s Act, 1880, is a law that empowers the State Government to appoint Ka’zi’s (Muslim judges or legal officers) for any local area in India. This Act was enacted on 9th July, 1880, and initially applied only to the territories administered by the Governor of Fort Saint George in Council. However, the Government of any other State can extend it to the whole or any part of the territories under its administration.

Under Section 2 of this Act, the State Government may appoint one or more fit persons as Ka’zi’s for any local area if a considerable number of Muhammadans resident in that area desire it. The decision of the State Government on the rightful appointment of a Ka’zi’ is conclusive. The State Government may also suspend or remove any Ka’zi’ who is guilty of misconduct, absent for six months or more, declared insolvent, desires to be discharged from the office, or becomes unfit or incapable to discharge the duties of the office.

Section 3 of this Act allows any Ka’zi’ appointed under this Act to appoint one or more persons as his Naib or Naibs (assistants) to act in his place in all or any of the matters appertaining to his office throughout the whole or in any portion of the local area for which he is appointed. The Ka’zi’ may also suspend or remove any naib so appointed.

Section 4 clarifies that nothing in this Act confers any judicial or administrative powers on any Ka’zi’ or Naib Ka’zi’ appointed under this Act. It also states that the presence of a Ka’zi’ or Naib Ka’zi’ is not necessary at the celebration of any marriage or the performance of any rite or ceremony, and it does not prevent any person from discharging any of the functions of a Ka’zi’.

The Ka’zi’s Act, 1880, has been extended to the Union territories of Lakshadweep and Pondicherry, and certain places in the Bombay Presidency, West Bengal, the U.P., the Punjab, the C.P., and Assam.
