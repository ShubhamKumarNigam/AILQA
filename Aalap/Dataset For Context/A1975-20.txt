Summary:
The Tokyo Convention Act, 1975, is an Act to give effect to the Convention on offences and certain other acts committed on board aircraft. The Act defines various terms, including aircraft, appropriate authority, commander, Convention country, Indian registered aircraft, military aircraft, operator, pilot in command, Tokyo Convention, and flight. The Act applies to any act or omission taking place on board an Indian registered aircraft while in flight elsewhere than in or over India, which would constitute an offence under any law in force in India. The Act also provides for the powers of the commander of an aircraft in flight to take reasonable measures, including restraint of a person, if he has reasonable grounds to believe that the person has done or is about to do any act on the aircraft that jeopardises or may jeopardise the safety of the aircraft or of persons or property on board the aircraft, maintains good order and discipline on board the aircraft, or has done any act which in the opinion of the commander is an offence under any law in force in the country in which the aircraft is registered. The Act also provides for jurisdiction, evidence in connection with aircraft, provisions as to documentary evidence, power to apply the provisions of the Act with modifications to certain aircraft, contracting parties to the Convention, and power to treat certain aircraft to be registered in a Convention country.
