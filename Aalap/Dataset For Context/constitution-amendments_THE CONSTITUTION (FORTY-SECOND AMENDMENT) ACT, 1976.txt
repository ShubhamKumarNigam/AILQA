Summary:

The Constitution (Forty-Second Amendment) Act, 1976 was enacted to make several changes to the Constitution of India. The Act aimed to emphasize the high ideals of socialism, secularism, and the integrity of the nation, and to make the Directive Principles of State Policy more comprehensive. It also sought to give these principles precedence over fundamental rights that have been used to obstruct socio-economic reforms. The Act also specified the fundamental duties of citizens and made special provisions for dealing with anti-national activities.

Furthermore, the Act proposed to strengthen the presumption in favor of the constitutionality of legislation enacted by Parliament and State Legislatures. It provided for a requirement of a minimum number of Judges to determine questions as to the constitutionality of laws and for a special majority of not less than two-thirds to declare any law to be constitutionally invalid. The Act also took away the jurisdiction of High Courts with regard to determination of Constitutional validity of Central laws and conferred exclusive jurisdiction in this behalf on the Supreme Court.

In addition, the Act proposed to reduce the mounting arrears in High Courts and to secure the speedy disposal of service matters, revenue matters, and certain other matters of special importance. It also made certain modifications in the writ jurisdiction of the High Courts under article 226.

Lastly, the Act made certain other amendments which had become necessary in the light of the working of the Constitution. The various amendments proposed in the Bill were explained in the notes on clauses. The Bill sought to achieve the above objects.
