Summary:

THE LEGAL PRACTITIONERS ACT, 1879, is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to Legal Practitioners in India. The Act defines various legal terms, including 'Judge', 'subordinate Court', 'revenue-office', and 'legal practitioner'. It specifies the qualifications and powers of advocates, vakils, attorneys, pleaders, mukhtars, and revenue-agents. The Act also lays down penalties for illegal practice, failure to deliver certificates, and unprofessional conduct. The Second Schedule specifies the value of stamps for certificates. The Act has been extended to various parts of India, except for the State of Manipur, and has been amended several times. The Act applies to the Union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
