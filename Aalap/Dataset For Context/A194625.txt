Summary:
The Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946, establishes a special police force called the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) for the investigation of certain offences in the Union territories. The Act vests the superintendence of the DSPE in the Central Government for all matters except for the investigation of offences alleged to have been committed under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, where the superintendence vests in the Central Vigilance Commission. The administration of the DSPE is vested in an officer appointed by the Central Government, known as the Director, who exercises powers similar to those of an Inspector-General of Police in a State. The Act also empowers the Central Government to extend the powers and jurisdiction of members of the DSPE to any area (including railway areas) in a State, not being a Union territory, for the investigation of any offences or classes of offences specified in a notification under section 3. However, such extension of powers and jurisdiction requires the consent of the State Government. The DSPE shall not conduct any inquiry or investigation into any offence alleged to have been committed under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, except with the previous approval of the Central Government where such allegation relates to certain employees of the Central Government or officers appointed by the Central Government in corporations established by or under any Central Act, Government companies, societies and local authorities owned or controlled by that Government. The Act has been extended to Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Pondicherry, Lakshadweep, and Sikkim.
