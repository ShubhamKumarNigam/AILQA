Summary:

The National Service Act, 1972, is an Act to provide for the registration of qualified persons and for the rendering of national service by such persons and for matters connected therewith. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government.

The Act defines several terms, including "employer," "establishment," "national service," "notification," "prescribed," and "qualified person." A qualified person is a citizen of India who is ordinarily resident in India and has obtained a recognized medical qualification or has obtained or passed an examination that entitles him to obtain a degree of a university or its equivalent qualification in any branch of engineering or technology or both.

The Act imposes a liability on qualified persons to render national service for a period of not more than four years. The period of national service begins from the date on which the qualified person is required by an enlistment notice served under the Act to present himself to the authority specified in the notice.

The Act also provides for voluntary service in lieu of national service and exempts certain qualified persons from being called up for national service. The Act also specifies the liability of persons to complete interrupted service and empowers the Central Government to direct a person to render service with the Armed Forces of the Union or other national service.

The Act also provides for discipline, power to require an employer to release qualified persons, transfer, and the liability of qualified persons to complete interrupted service.

The Act establishes a National Service Register and requires qualified persons to register under the Act. The Act also provides for the service of a notice of likelihood of calling up for national service and an examination of the physical and mental fitness of persons subject to registration under the Act.

The Act also provides for the enlistment of qualified persons for national service and the payment of salary, wages, and allowances to persons enlisted for national service. The Act also provides for training during the term of national service.

The Act establishes a National Service (Hardship) Committee to consider applications for postponement of liability to be called up for national service. The Act also provides for appeals from the decision of the National Service (Hardship) Committee and the revocation of postponement certificates.

The Act provides for the reinstatement of qualified persons called up for national service and the preservation of certain rights of qualified persons required to render national service.

The Act specifies offences and penalties for contravention of the provisions of the Act. The Act also provides for the summary trial of offences and jurisdiction to try offences. The Act also provides for protection of action taken in good faith and the removal of difficulties.

The Act empowers the Central Government to delegate its powers to any State Government or other authority owned or controlled by the Central Government. The Act also provides for the making of rules and regulations to carry out the purposes of the Act. The rules and regulations made under the Act are required to be laid before both Houses of Parliament.
