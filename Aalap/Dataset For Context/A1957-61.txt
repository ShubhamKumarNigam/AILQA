Summary:

The Delhi Development Act, 1957, is an Act of the Parliament of India to provide for the development of Delhi according to plan and for matters ancillary thereto. The Act establishes the Delhi Development Authority (DDA) to carry out the development of Delhi. The DDA is responsible for the preparation of a master plan and zonal development plans for the city, and for the acquisition and disposal of land for development purposes. The Act also provides for the regulation of building operations and land use in Delhi, and for the imposition of betterment charges on property owners whose property has increased in value as a result of development activities. The Act also includes provisions for the protection of amenities and open spaces, and for the provision of public facilities and services. The Act extends to the whole of the National Capital Territory of Delhi and came into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.
