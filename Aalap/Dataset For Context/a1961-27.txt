Summary:
The Motor Transport Workers Act, 1961 is an Act that aims to provide for the welfare of motor transport workers and to regulate the conditions of their work. The Act applies to every motor transport undertaking employing five or more motor transport workers, and may also apply to motor transport undertakings employing less than five motor transport workers if the State Government so decides.

The Act defines various terms, including "adolescent," "adult," "child," "day," "employer," "hours of work," "motor transport undertaking," "motor transport worker," "prescribed," "qualified medical practitioner," "spread-over," "wages," and "week."

The Act includes provisions for the registration of motor transport undertakings, the appointment of inspecting staff, and the powers and duties of inspectors. It also includes provisions for the welfare and health of motor transport workers, including requirements for canteens, rest rooms, uniforms, and medical facilities.

The Act regulates the hours and limitations of employment for motor transport workers, including provisions for daily intervals for rest, spread-over, and split duty. It also includes provisions for the notice of hours of work and weekly rest.

The Act prohibits the employment of children in motor transport undertakings and includes provisions for the employment of adolescents as motor transport workers. It requires adolescents to have a certificate of fitness and to carry a token giving a reference to such certificate while at work.

The Act includes provisions for the payment of wages and leave to motor transport workers. It applies the Payment of Wages Act, 1936 to motor transport workers and provides for extra wages for overtime and annual leave with wages.

The Act includes penalties and procedures for the enforcement of its provisions. It provides for penalties for obstructions, the use of false certificates of fitness, contravention of provisions regarding employment of motor transport workers, and other offenses. It also includes provisions for the prosecution of offenses and the limitation of prosecutions.

The Act includes miscellaneous provisions, including provisions for the effect of laws and agreements inconsistent with this Act, exemptions, powers to give directions, and the power to make rules. The Schedule to the Act includes a list of the powers that may be exercised by inspectors.
