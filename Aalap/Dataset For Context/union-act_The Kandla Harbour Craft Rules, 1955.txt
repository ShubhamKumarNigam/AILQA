Summary:

The Kandla Harbour Craft Rules, 1955 were made by the Central Government under the Indian Port Act, 1908. These rules apply to the Port of Kandla and regulate the use of harbour craft in the port.

Under these rules, every harbour craft must be licensed before it can be used to carry goods or passengers to or from any vessel at the port or from place to place, or operate within the port for whatever purpose. A harbour craft licensed to ply between a vessel and the shore may also ply from place to place within the port without a separate license.

To obtain a license, an application must be made to the licensing officer in writing, providing the owner's name and address, the name and address of the agent, if any, the name of the tindal, and the nature of the licence required. The licensing officer will then survey and measure the harbour craft and grant a license if the craft is seaworthy and fit for service at the port.

Harbour craft possessing a fishing license issued by the Chief Commissioner for Kutch must also apply for a license under these rules before commencing fishing operations. These crafts must observe special precautions, including bringing and landing fish only at appointed places, paying wharfage charges, abiding by the rules and regulations of the port, observing formalities required by the Customs Authority and the Government of Kutch, and being liable for any damage caused to government property. No fishing is permitted in the whole of Kandla Creek and in such parts of the approaches to the Creek as may be laid down from time to time by the licensing officer.

These rules also provide that nothing in these rules shall apply to any craft coming from any system of inland navigation into the Port of Kandla and define certain terms used in the rules.
