

Summary:
The Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005, was enacted on 20th January, 2006, to provide for the constitution of a National Commission and State Commissions for Protection of Child Rights and Children's Courts for providing speedy trial of offences against children or of violation of child rights. The Act aims to ensure the protection of children's rights in India, in line with the United Nations (UN) General Assembly Summit in 1990 and the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) adopted in 1992.

The Act defines child rights as the children's rights adopted in the United Nations convention on the Rights of the Child on the 20th November, 1989 and ratified by the Government of India on the 11th December, 1992. The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights is constituted under Chapter II of the Act, with a Chairperson and six Members appointed by the Central Government. The Commission is responsible for examining and reviewing the safeguards provided for the protection of child rights, presenting annual reports to the Central Government, inquiring into violations of child rights, and recommending appropriate remedial measures.

The State Commissions for Protection of Child Rights are constituted under Chapter IV of the Act, with a Chairperson and six Members appointed by the State Government. The State Commissions have similar functions to the National Commission, but are responsible for performing these functions within the State.

Chapter V of the Act deals with the financing, accounts, and audit of the Commissions. The Central and State Governments are required to pay grants to the Commissions for their functioning, and the Commissions are required to maintain proper accounts and prepare annual statements of accounts. The accounts of the Commissions are to be audited by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.

Chapter VII of the Act contains miscellaneous provisions, including protection of action taken in good faith, Chairperson, Members and other officers to be public servants, directions by the Central Government, returns or information, power of the Central Government to make rules, power of the State Government to make rules, and power to remove difficulties.

In summary, the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005, provides for the constitution of National and State Commissions for Protection of Child Rights and Children's Courts to ensure the protection of children's rights in India. The Act defines child rights, sets out the functions and powers of the Commissions, and provides for the financing, accounts, and audit of the Commissions. The Act also contains miscellaneous provisions to facilitate its implementation.
