Summary:

THE SUITS VALUATION ACT, 1887, is an Act that prescribes the mode of valuing certain suits for the purpose of determining the jurisdiction of courts with respect to them. The Act has three parts.

Part I, titled "SUITS RELATING TO LAND," applies to specific local areas, and its provisions come into force in those areas on dates determined by the State Government. The State Government has the power to make rules for determining the value of land for jurisdictional purposes in suits mentioned in the Court-fees Act, 1870. The rules can determine the value of any class of land or interest in land in the whole or any part of a local area and may prescribe different values for different places within the same local area. The amount at which relief sought in a suit relating to land or interest in land is valued for jurisdictional purposes cannot exceed the value of the land or interest as determined by those rules. The State Government must consult the High Court before making rules under section 3, and a rule shall not take effect till the expiration of one month after the rule has been published in the Official Gazette.

Part II, titled "OTHER SUITS," applies to suits other than those referred to in the Court-fees Act, 1870, section 7, paragraphs v, vi, and ix, and paragraph x, clause (d). Where court-fees are payable ad valorem under the Court-fees Act, 1870, the value as determinable for the computation of court-fees and the value for purposes of jurisdiction shall be the same. When the subject-matter of suits of any class does not admit of being satisfactorily valued, the High Court may direct that such suits shall, for the purposes of the Court-fees Act, 1870, and this Act, be treated as if their subject-matter were of such value as the High Court thinks fit to specify in this behalf.

Part III, titled "SUPPLEMENTAL PROVISIONS," lays down the procedure for dealing with objections regarding the over-valuation or under-valuation of a suit or appeal. The objection must be taken in the Court of first instance at or before the hearing at which issues are first framed and recorded, or in the lower Appellate Court in the memorandum of appeal to that Court. The Appellate Court may entertain the objection if it is satisfied that the suit or appeal was over-valued or under-valued and that the over-valuation or under-valuation thereof has prejudicially affected the disposal of the suit or appeal on its merits. The provisions of this section shall apply to a Court exercising revisional jurisdiction under section 622 of the Code of Civil Procedure or other enactment for the time being in force.

The Act applies to the whole of India except the territories which, immediately before the 1st November, 1956, were comprised in Part B States. The Act has been amended in several states, and its provisions have been extended to various Union territories. The Act has been repealed in its application to certain areas.
