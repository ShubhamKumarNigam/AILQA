Summary:
The Companies (Amendment) Act, 2006, is an Act that amends the Companies Act, 1956. The Act includes provisions for the allotment of a Director Identification Number (DIN) to every individual intending to be appointed as a director of a company or already appointed as a director of a company before the commencement of this Act. The DIN is to be obtained from the Central Government by making an application in a prescribed form and manner along with a prescribed fee. The Central Government is required to allot the DIN within one month from the receipt of the application. No individual is allowed to obtain more than one DIN. Every director is required to intimate their DIN to the company or all companies where they are directors. Every company is required to furnish the DIN of all its directors to the Registrar or any other officer or authority as may be specified by the Central Government. Penalties have been prescribed for contravention of provisions related to DIN. The Act also includes provisions for filing applications, documents, inspection, etc., through electronic form. The Central Government may make rules for this purpose. The Act also provides for the power to modify the Act in relation to electronic records (including the manner and form in which electronic records shall be filed). The provisions of the Information Technology Act, 2000, relating to electronic records shall apply in relation to the records in electronic form under section 610B of this Act. The Act also provides for providing value-added services through the electronic form and levying fees as may be prescribed.
