Summary:
The Rajghat Samadhi Act, 1951, is an Indian law that aims to administer and control the Rajghat Samadhi in Delhi. The Act establishes a Committee, which is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, responsible for overseeing the administration and control of the Samadhi. The Committee is composed of the Mayor of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi, three officials nominated by the Central Government, four non-officials nominated by the Central Government, and three members of Parliament. The Central Government may appoint any person to be the Chairman of the Committee, and all members nominated by the Central Government shall hold office during the pleasure of the Central Government.

The Committee has the power to administer the affairs of the Samadhi, keep it in proper order and in a state of good repair, organize and regulate periodical functions at the Samadhi, and do such other things as may be incidental or conducive to the efficient administration of the affairs of the Samadhi. The Central Government may make rules to carry out the objects of this Act and regulate access to the Samadhi or any portion thereof. The Committee may make bye-laws consistent with this Act and the rules made thereunder for the purpose of regulating its meetings, employees, duties, and powers.

Every rule and every bye-law made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days, and if both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or bye-law or both Houses agree that the rule or bye-law should not be made, the rule or bye-law shall have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be. No act or proceeding of the Committee shall be deemed to be invalid merely by reason of any vacancy in, or any defect in the constitution of, the Committee. The Samadhi premises are bounded on the north by a vacant piece of land belonging to the Delhi Improvement Trust, on the south by Power House, and on the east and west by Bela Road.
