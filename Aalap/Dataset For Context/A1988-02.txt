Summary:
The Chandigarh (Delegation of Powers) Act, 1987, also known as Act No. 2 of 1988, came into effect on January 3, 1988. This Act applies to the entire Union territory of Chandigarh. The Act's primary objective is to delegate powers that the Administrator of Chandigarh holds under any law currently in force within the Union territory.

Section 1 of the Act provides the short title and extent of the Act. It can be called the Chandigarh (Delegation of Powers) Act, 1987, and it applies to the whole of the Union territory of Chandigarh.

Section 2 of the Act defines the term "Administrator" as the administrator of the Union territory of Chandigarh appointed by the President under article 239 of the Constitution.

Section 3 of the Act authorizes the Central Government or the Administrator to delegate any power, authority, or jurisdiction or any duty vested in the Administrator under any law in force in the Union territory of Chandigarh to any officer or other authority. The Administrator may also transfer any appeal, application for revision, or any other matter pending before him to an officer or other authority. Furthermore, the Administrator may withdraw for disposal by himself any appeal, application for revision, or any other matter pending before an officer or other authority.

Section 4 of the Act validates the exercise of power, authority, or jurisdiction or any duty by any officer or other authority before the commencement of this Act. It states that such power, authority, jurisdiction, or duty shall be deemed to have been validly and effectively exercised or discharged by such officer or other authority as if the provisions of sub-section (1) of section 3 were in force at all material times when such power, authority, or jurisdiction was exercised or such duty was discharged. This provision ensures that no suit or other proceeding shall be instituted, maintained, or continued in any court, tribunal, or before other authority on the ground that such officer or other authority was not competent to exercise such power, authority, or jurisdiction or to discharge such duty.

In summary, the Chandigarh (Delegation of Powers) Act, 1987, delegates the Administrator's powers to other officers or authorities as specified by the Central Government or the Administrator. The Act also validates the exercise of power, authority, or jurisdiction or any duty by any officer or other authority before the commencement of this Act, ensuring that such actions are deemed valid and effective.
