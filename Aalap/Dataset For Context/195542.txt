Summary:

THE PRIZE COMPETITIONS ACT, 1955 (ACT NO. 42 OF 1955) is a legal document that aims to control and regulate prize competitions in India. The Act extends to the territories which, immediately before the 1st November, 1956, were comprised in the States of Andhra, Bombay, Madras, Orissa, Uttar Pradesh, Hyderabad, Madhya Bharat, Patiala and East Punjab States Union and Saurashtra and all Part C States. The Act defines key terms such as "licensing authority," "money," "prescribed," and "prize competition." It also provides interpretations for printing and distribution.

The Act prohibits any person from promoting or conducting any prize competition where the prize offered exceeds one thousand rupees a month. However, subject to the provisions of section 4, no person shall promote or conduct any prize competition where the total value of the prize or prizes (whether in cash or otherwise) to be offered in any month does not exceed one thousand rupees unless he has obtained a licence granted in accordance with the provisions of this Act and the rules made thereunder.

The Act outlines the process for obtaining a license, the conditions for granting a license, and the powers of the licensing authority to cancel or suspend licenses. It also specifies penalties for promoting or conducting any prize competition in contravention of the provisions of sections 4 and 5, failure to keep and submit accounts, and other offenses in connection with prize competitions. The Act also includes provisions for offenses by corporations and power of entry and search.

The Act also includes provisions for forfeiture of newspapers and publications containing prize competitions, appeals, licensing authority and other officers to be public servants, jurisdiction to try offenses, protection of action taken under this act, and power to make rules.
