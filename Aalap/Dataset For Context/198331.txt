Summary:
The Emigration Act, 1983 is an Act of the Indian Parliament that consolidates and amends the law relating to emigration of citizens of India. The Act applies to citizens of India outside India and extends to the whole of India. The Act defines various terms such as certificate, conveyance, dependent, emigrant, emigrant conveyance, employer, employment, notification, prescribed, Protector of Emigrants, recruiting agent, recruitment, registering authority, work, and other related terms.

Chapter I of the Act deals with preliminary provisions, including the short title, extent, application, and commencement of the Act.

Chapter II of the Act deals with emigration authorities, including the appointment, duties, and powers of Protectors of Emigrants, the power to authorize persons to exercise functions of a Protector, the setting up of emigration check-posts, the appointment of officers in charge of emigration check-posts, and the appointment of other emigration officers and staff.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the registration of recruiting agents, including the appointment of the registering authority, the requirement of a valid certificate for functioning as a recruiting agent, the application for registration, the terms and conditions of registration, and the renewal of registration.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with permits for recruitment by employers, including the competent authority for issuing permits, the procedure for obtaining permits, the period of validity of permits, the registration of certain permits, and the cancellation or suspension of a permit.

Chapter V of the Act deals with emigration clearance, including the requirement of emigration clearance, the procedure for obtaining emigration clearance, the grounds for rejection of an application for emigration clearance, and the registration of certain permits.

Chapter VI of the Act deals with appeals, including the grounds for preferring an appeal, the period for preferring an appeal, the procedure for disposing of an appeal, and the finality of the order made on an appeal.

Chapter VII of the Act deals with offences and penalties, including the offences and penalties under the Act, the offences to be cognizable, the previous sanction of the Central Government necessary for prosecution, punishment to be without prejudice to any other action, and the determination of question as to whether a person is an emigrant.

Chapter VIII of the Act deals with miscellaneous provisions, including the power to prohibit emigration to any country in the interests of the general public, the power to prohibit emigration due to outbreak of epidemics, civil disturbances, etc., in a country, the power to prohibit emigration of any class or category of persons, provisions as to security, refund of security, power to search, seize and detain persons, conveyance, etc., returns and registers, authorities and officers to have certain powers of civil court, power to give directions, effect of other laws, delegation, power to exempt, and power to make rules.

The Act also contains provisions for the repeal of the Emigration Act, 1922, and the laying of notifications and rules before Parliament.
