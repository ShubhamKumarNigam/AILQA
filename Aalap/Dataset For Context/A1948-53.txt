Summary:

THE OILFIELDS (REGULATION AND DEVELOPMENT) ACT, 1948 is an Act to provide for the regulation of oilfields and for the development of mineral oil resources. The Act extends to the whole of India. The Act defines terms such as "mine", "mining lease", "oilfield", and "mineral oils" to include natural gas, petroleum, and other products of petroleum in a liquid or solid state. The Act states that no mining lease shall be granted after the commencement of this Act otherwise than in accordance with the rules made under this Act, and any mining lease granted contrary to the provisions of sub-section (1) shall be void and of no effect. The Central Government may make rules for regulating the grant of mining leases or for prohibiting the grant of such leases in respect of any mineral oil or in any area, and for the conservation and development of mineral oils. The rules may provide for matters such as the manner in which applications for mining leases may be made, the authority by which leases may be granted, the maximum or minimum area and the period for which any mining lease may be granted, and the fixing of the maximum and minimum rent payable by a lessee. The Act also provides for the modification or alteration of the terms and conditions of any mining lease granted prior to the commencement of this Act so as to bring such lease into conformity with the rules made under sections 5 and 6. The Act also includes provisions for penalties, laying of rules and notifications, power of inspection, relaxation of rules in special cases, and protection of action taken in good faith. The Schedule to the Act specifies the rates of royalty for crude oil, casing-head condensate, and natural gas.
