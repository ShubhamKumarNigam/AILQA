The High Denomination Bank Notes (Demonetisation) Act, 1978, is a law passed in India that demonetized high denomination bank notes of the denominations of one thousand rupees, five thousand rupees, and ten thousand rupees. The Act came into force on January 16, 1978, and it ceased to be legal tender all high denomination bank notes on the same day. The Act prohibits the transfer and receipt of high denomination bank notes after the specified date. The Act also requires banks and government treasuries to declare high denomination bank notes held by them at the close of business on the 16th day of January, 1978, and to keep them in a separate receptacle and seal the same with their seal and of the officers having custody of such receptacle. Banks and government treasuries can obtain an equivalent amount in exchange for the high denomination bank notes declared by them in the return referred to in section 5 by credit to an account maintained with the Reserve Bank or a public sector bank or in bank notes. Other persons can exchange high denomination bank notes held by them after the 16th day of January, 1978, only on tender of the note in the manner provided in the Act. The Act also specifies penalties for non-compliance with its provisions.
