THE NATIONAL TAX TRIBUNAL ACT, 2005

The National Tax Tribunal Act, 2005 establishes the National Tax Tribunal to adjudicate disputes with respect to levy, assessment, collection, and enforcement of direct taxes, as well as disputes with respect to the determination of the rates of duties of customs and central excise on goods and the valuation of goods for the purposes of assessment of such duties. The Tribunal also has jurisdiction over matters relating to the levy of tax on service. The Tribunal is established in pursuance of article 323B of the Constitution and is intended to provide a specialized forum for the resolution of tax disputes.

The Act defines various terms used throughout, including "Bench," "Board of Directaxes," "Board of Excise and Customs," "Central Excise Act," "Central Excise Tariff Act," "Chairperson," "Companies (Profits) Surtax Act," "Customs Act," "Customs Tariff Act," "Expenditure-tax Act," "Gift-tax Act," "Income-tax Act," "Income-tax Appellate Tribunal," "Interest-tax Act," "law officer," "Member," "National Tax Tribunal," "notification," "prescribed," "Supreme Court," "Wealth-tax Act," and "Wealth-tax Appellate Tribunal."

The Act is divided into six chapters. Chapter I, titled "Preliminary," contains provisions related to the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act, as well as definitions of various terms used throughout. Chapter II, titled "Establishment of the National Tax Tribunal," establishes the Tribunal and sets forth its composition, jurisdiction, and powers. Chapter III, titled "Jurisdiction, Powers and Functions of National Tax Tribunal," sets forth the Tribunal's jurisdiction, powers, and functions in relation to the adjudication of tax disputes. Chapter IV, titled "Appeals to Supreme Court," sets forth the grounds and procedures for appealing decisions of the Tribunal to the Supreme Court. Chapter V, titled "Miscellaneous," contains provisions related to the transfer of pending cases from High Courts, the power to remove difficulties, the power to make rules, and the laying of rules before Parliament. Chapter VI, titled "Consequential Amendments," sets forth amendments to various other Acts as a consequence of the enactment of this Act.

The Act includes a schedule listing the enactments that are amended by this Act, including the Income-tax Act, 1961, the Wealth-tax Act, 1957, the Expenditure-tax Act, 1987, the Interest-tax Act, 1974, the Finance (No. 2) Act, 1998, the Customs Act, 1962, and the Central Excise Act, 1944. These amendments are intended to ensure consistency and compatibility between the provisions of this Act and those of the other Acts.

Overall, the National Tax Tribunal Act, 2005 provides a specialized forum for the resolution of tax disputes and aims to streamline the process for adjudicating such disputes. The Act includes provisions for the establishment and composition of the Tribunal, its jurisdiction and powers, and the procedures for appealing its decisions. The Act also includes amendments to various other Acts to ensure consistency and compatibility with its provisions.
