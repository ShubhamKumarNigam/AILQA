The Indian Red Cross Society Act, 1920, is an Act that established the Indian Red Cross Society. This organization is a voluntary humanitarian organization that provides relief in times of emergencies, disasters, and armed conflicts. It is part of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement.

The Act has been extended to include the Union territories of Goa, Daman and Diu, Lakshadweep, and Pondicherry. This means that the Indian Red Cross Society is responsible for providing relief and assistance in these regions in addition to the rest of India.

The Act outlines the powers and duties of the Indian Red Cross Society, including the ability to raise and receive funds, acquire and dispose of property, and cooperate with other organizations and authorities. The Society is also responsible for promoting the principles and ideals of the Red Cross Movement, including the protection of human dignity and the promotion of peace and understanding.

The Act also establishes the Indian Red Cross Society's governing body, which is responsible for managing and overseeing the organization's affairs. The governing body is comprised of both elected and appointed members, including representatives from the government and the medical community.
