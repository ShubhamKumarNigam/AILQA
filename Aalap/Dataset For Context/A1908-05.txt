The Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, is a law that consolidates and amends the laws relating to the procedure of the Courts of Civil Judicature in India. This document is divided into several parts, with Part 0 being the preliminary section that includes the short title, commencement, and extent of the Act. The Act extends to the whole of India, except for the State of Nagaland and the tribal areas, unless extended by the State Government with supplemental, incidental, or consequential modifications.

Part 0 also includes definitions of various legal terms used throughout the Act. For example, "decree" is defined as the formal expression of an adjudication that conclusively determines the rights of the parties with regard to all or any of the matters in controversy in the suit. It may be preliminary or final, and does not include any adjudication from which an appeal lies or any order of dismissal for default. A "decree-holder" is any person in whose favor a decree has been passed or an order capable of execution has been made.

Part 1 of the Act deals with the jurisdiction and powers of civil courts. Section 9 states that every suit shall be instituted in the court of the lowest grade competent to try it. Section 10 states that the jurisdiction of the courts with respect to suits shall be determined by the pecuniary limits of their ordinary jurisdiction. Section 11 provides that the court shall have power to try all suits of a civil nature unless it is barred by any law for the time being in force.

Part 2 of the Act deals with written statements, setting out the defendant's version of the case. Section 14 states that the defendant shall, within 30 days from the service of the summons, present a written statement of his defense. Section 15 provides that the plaintiff shall be at liberty to file a reply to the written statement.

Part 3 of the Act deals with the production and inspection of documents. Section 16 states that the parties to a suit shall be entitled to present any documents which they rely on in support of their cases. Section 17 provides that the court may at any time require any party to produce any document or thing.

Part 4 of the Act deals with the trial of suits. Section 18 states that the trial of a suit shall be continued from day to day until all the witnesses in attendance have been examined unless the court finds that adjourning the trial will be in the interest of justice. Section 19 provides that the court shall pronounce judgment within 30 days from the conclusion of the trial.

Part 5 of the Act deals with the execution of decrees and orders. Section 36 states that the court shall execute a decree or order for the payment of money forthwith. Section 37 provides that the court may, in its discretion, order the payment of the amount in installments.

Part 6 of the Act deals with appeals and revisions. Section 96 states that any person aggrieved by a decree or order may appeal to the court authorized to hear appeals from the decisions of the court which passed the decree or order. Section 97 provides that the High Court may call for the record of any case which has been decided by any court subordinate to it for the purpose of satisfying itself as to the correctness, legality, or propriety of any decision recorded or order made, and as to the regularity of any proceedings of such court.

Part 7 of the Act deals with supplemental provisions. Section 148 states that no decree shall be executed unless the decree-holder has obtained a certificate from the court stating that the decree has become final. Section 149 provides that the court may, in its discretion, stay the execution of a decree or order.
