Summary:
The Indian Council of World Affairs Act, 2001 (Act No. 29 of 2001) declares the Indian Council of World Affairs to be an institution of national importance and provides for its incorporation and matters connected therewith. The Act defines various terms used in the Act, including "appointed day," "Chairperson," "Council," "Director-General," "existing Council," "Fund," "Governing Body," "member," "President," "regulations," "rules," and "Vice-Presidents."

The Act incorporates the Indian Council of World Affairs as a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, with the power to acquire, hold, and dispose of property, both movable and immovable, and to contract and sue. The head office of the Council shall be at Delhi, and the Council may establish branches at other places in India with the previous approval of the Central Government.

The Act transfers all properties and other assets vested in the existing Council to the Council, along with all debts, obligations, and liabilities incurred, all contracts entered into, and all matters and things engaged to be done by, with, or for the existing Council. The Act also provides for the absorption of employees of the existing Council into the Council's regular service, without any compensation or claim for compensation.

The Act provides for the composition of the Council, which initially consists of the Vice-President of India, the Prime Minister of India, the speaker of the Lok Sabha, the Leader of the House, Rajya Sabha, the Leader of the Opposition, Lok Sabha, and the Leader of the Opposition, Rajya Sabha. From a date appointed by the Central Government, the Council shall consist of the Vice-President of India, three Vice-Presidents, the Director-General, five members of the Lok Sabha and three members of the Rajya Sabha, seven members from the fields of diplomacy, international affairs, international law, Multilateral or United Nations affairs, security and disarmament, seven members from the fields of history, economics and other social sciences, seven members from the fields of media or organizations such as India International Centre, Centre for Policy Research, Indian Council of Social Science Research, Institute of Defence Studies and Analyses, Indian Council of Cultural Relations, and interested in the work and objectives of the Council, five members from Business or Chambers of Commerce, and five members nominated by the Central Government to represent the Ministries of Education, Culture, Urban Development, Science and Technology, and Defence.

The Act provides for the term of office and vacancies among members, the powers and functions of the President, the powers and functions of the Vice-Presidents, allowances of members, meetings of the Council, objects of the Council, the Governing Body and other committees of the Council, staff of the Council, functions of the Council, payment to the Council, the Fund of the Council, budget of the Council, accounts and audit, annual report, authentication of orders and instruments of the Council, vacancy, etc., not to invalidate proceedings of the Council, transitory provision, reports, returns, and information, power to make rules, power to make regulations, rules and regulations to be laid before Parliament, power to remove difficulties, power to remove difficulties, and repeal and saving.

The Schedule to the Act lists the names and designations of the members of the Governing Body of the Council constituted under sub-section (1) of section 7. The Annexure to the Act lists the names and designations of the members of the Council constituted under sub-section (1) of section 7. The Appendix to the Act contains forms for use under the Act. The Footnotes to the Act contain amendments made to the Act by the Indian Council of World Affairs (Amendment) Act, 2003 (5 of 2004).
