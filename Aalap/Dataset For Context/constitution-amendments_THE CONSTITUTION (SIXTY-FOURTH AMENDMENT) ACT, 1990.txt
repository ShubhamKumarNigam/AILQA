
Amendment of article 356.-

In article 356 of the Constitution, in clause (3), for the words "one year", the words "two years" shall be substituted.



Explanation.-

In this article, "State" does not include the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union territory of Pondicherry.



Amendment of article 356.-

In article 356 of the Constitution, in clause (4), after the words "at any time", the words "after the expiration of one year from the date of the Proclamation" shall be inserted.



Explanation.-

In this article, "State" does not include the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union territory of Pondicherry.

Amendment of the Ninth Schedule.-

In the Ninth Schedule to the Constitution, after entry 219, the following entry shall be inserted, namely:-

"220. The Constitution (Sixty-second Amendment) Act, 1989."



The Constitution (Sixty-fourth Amendment) Act, 1990, amends the Constitution of India in several ways. Firstly, it changes the duration of President's rule in a state under Article 356 from one year to two years. However, the President can revoke the Proclamation at any time after the expiration of one year from the date of the Proclamation. This amendment applies to all states except the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union territory of Pondicherry.

Further, the Act amends Article 356 by inserting a new clause (4A), which provides that if the Proclamation of President's rule is in operation for more than one year, the President shall refer the matter to the Election Commission for the purpose of holding elections to the Legislative Assembly of the State concerned. The Election Commission shall, within six months from the date of receipt of the reference, hold elections to the Legislative Assembly of the State and the results of the elections shall be communicated to the President.

Lastly, the Act amends the Ninth Schedule to the Constitution by adding a new entry, namely, the Constitution (Sixty-second Amendment) Act, 1989. This amendment was earlier held to be unconstitutional by the Supreme Court in the Kesavananda Bharati case. However, the present amendment seeks to validate the earlier amendment.

In summary, the Constitution (Sixty-fourth Amendment) Act, 1990, extends the duration of President's rule in a state from one year to two years, provides for the Election Commission to hold elections to the Legislative Assembly of the State concerned after the expiration of one year from the date of the Proclamation, and validates the Constitution (Sixty-second Amendment) Act, 1989.
