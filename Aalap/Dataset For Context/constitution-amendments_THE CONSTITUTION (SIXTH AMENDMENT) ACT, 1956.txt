The Constitution (Sixth Amendment) Act, 1956, amends the Seventh Schedule, Union List, and State List, as well as Articles 269 and 286. The amendment adds a new entry, 92-A, to the Union List, which grants Parliament the power to tax interstate sales and purchases of goods, excluding newspapers. The amendment also empowers Parliament to formulate principles for determining when a sale or purchase of goods takes place in the context of interstate trade or commerce. The amendment also removes the Explanation from Article 286(1) and replaces clauses (2) and (3) with new provisions. The new clause (2) grants Parliament the power to formulate principles for determining when a sale or purchase of goods takes place in any of the ways mentioned in clause (1). The new clause (3) restricts State laws that impose or authorize the imposition of taxes on the sale or purchase of goods declared by Parliament to be of special importance in interstate trade or commerce, subjecting them to restrictions and conditions in regard to the system of levy, rates, and other incidents of the tax as Parliament may specify.
