Summary:
THE FAMILY COURTS ACT, 1984 is an Act to provide for the establishment of Family Courts with a view to promote conciliation in, and secure speedy settlement of, disputes relating to marriage and family affairs. The Act establishes Family Courts for specific areas and specifies the qualifications, appointment, and powers of the Judges of these Courts. The Act also provides for the association of social welfare agencies, counsellors, officers, and other employees to assist the Family Courts in their functions. The jurisdiction of the Family Courts includes all suits and proceedings of a certain nature related to marriage, property, maintenance, and custody, as well as the jurisdiction exercisable by a Magistrate of the first class under Chapter IX of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. The Act also specifies the procedure to be followed by the Family Courts, which includes making efforts for settlement, following the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 and the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, and holding proceedings in camera. The Act also allows for the assistance of medical and welfare experts and provides for the right to legal representation. The Act further specifies the record of oral evidence, evidence of formal character on affidavit, and judgment. The execution of decrees and orders passed by the Family Courts is to be done in the same manner as prescribed by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. The Act also provides for appeals and revisions, and has overriding effect over any other law for the time being in force. The High Court and the Central Government have the power to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
