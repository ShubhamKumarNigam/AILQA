Summary:

The Constitution (Twenty-fifth Amendment) Act, 1971 is an amendment to the Constitution of India that introduces a new article 31C. This article provides that any law passed to give effect to the Directive Principles contained in clauses (b) and (c) of article 39 and contains a declaration to that effect, will not be deemed void or questioned on the grounds that it takes away or abridges any of the rights contained in article 14, 19 or 31.

The Act also amends article 31 of the Constitution by substituting a new clause (2) and inserting a new clause (2B). The new clause (2) provides that no property shall be compulsorily acquired or requisitioned save for a public purpose and save by authority of a law which provides for acquisition or requisitioning of the property for an amount fixed by such law. The new clause (2B) states that nothing in sub-clause (f) of clause (1) of article 19 shall affect any such law as is referred to in clause (2).

For this provision to apply in the case of laws made by State Legislatures, it is necessary that the relevant Bill should be reserved for the consideration of the President and receive his assent.

In summary, the Constitution (Twenty-fifth Amendment) Act, 1971 is a significant amendment to the Constitution of India that aims to give effect to the Directive Principles contained in clauses (b) and (c) of article 39 and provides protection to laws passed for a public purpose from being questioned on the grounds that they take away or abridge any of the rights contained in article 14, 19 or 31.
