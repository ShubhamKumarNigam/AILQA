Summary:
The Petroleum Act, 1934, consolidates and amends the law relating to the import, transport, storage, production, refining, and blending of petroleum in India. The Act defines key terms such as petroleum, petroleum Class A, petroleum Class B, petroleum Class C, flash-point, transport, import, store, motor conveyance, prescribed, and licence. The Act is divided into three chapters, with Chapter I focusing on control over petroleum, Chapter II on the testing of petroleum, and Chapter III on penalties and procedure. The Act outlines the rules and regulations for importing, transporting, and storing petroleum, as well as producing, refining, and blending it. It also specifies the penalties for violating these rules, including fines and imprisonment. The Act also includes provisions for inspecting and sampling petroleum, as well as testing it to determine its flash-point. Additionally, the Act outlines the procedure for granting licences for importing, transporting, and storing petroleum, as well as producing, refining, and blending it. The Act also includes provisions for exempting certain petroleum from the provisions of the Act, as well as for inspections of places where petroleum is being imported, stored, produced, refined, or blended or is under transport. Finally, the Act includes provisions for notice of accidents with petroleum and inquiries into serious accidents with petroleum. The Act was extended to Berar by the Petroleum (Berar Extension) Act, 1937, and to Goa, Daman and Diu, Pondicherry, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Lakshadweep with modifications. The Act was also amended several times, including by the Inflammable Substances Act, 1952, the Repealing Act, 1938, and the Petroleum Rules, 1976.
