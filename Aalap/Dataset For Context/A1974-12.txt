Summary:
The Economic Offences (Inapplicability of Limitation) Act, 1974, is a law that applies to the territories where the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (2 of 1974) is in effect. This Act came into force on April 1, 1974. The Act states that Chapter XXXVI of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, which deals with the limitation period for filing cases, will not apply to certain economic offenses. These offenses are listed in the Schedule of this Act and include offenses under various enactments such as the Indian Income Tax Act, 1922, the Income Tax Act, 1961, the Wealth-Tax Act, 1957, the Gift-Tax Act, 1958, the Central Sales Tax Act, 1956, the Central Excises and Salt Act, 1944, the Medicinal and Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act, 1955, the Customs Act, 1962, the Gold (Control) Act, 1968, the Imports and Exports (Control) Act, 1947, the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947, the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973, the Capital Issues (Control) Act, 1947, the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, the Emergency Risks (Goods) Insurance Act, 1962, the Emergency Risks (Factories) Insurance Act, 1962, the Emergency Risks (Goods) Insurance Act, 1971, the Emergency Risks (Undertakings) Insurance Act, 1971, the General Insurance Business (Nationalisation) Act, 1972, the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951, and the Industries (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act, 1981. The Act also includes provisions for the inclusion of new offenses in the Schedule by way of amendments. The Act further provides that any offenses listed in the Schedule may be taken cognizance of by the court having jurisdiction as if the provisions of Chapter XXXVI of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, were not enacted. This means that there is no limitation period for filing cases related to these economic offenses.
