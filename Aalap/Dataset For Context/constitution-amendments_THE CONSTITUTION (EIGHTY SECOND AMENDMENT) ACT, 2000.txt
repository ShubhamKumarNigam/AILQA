The Constitution (Eighty-second Amendment) Act, 2000 is an amendment to the Constitution of India that aims to restore relaxations for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes that were withdrawn in 1997. The Act was enacted in the Fifty-first year of the Republic of India and can be called the Constitution (Eighty-second Amendment) Act, 2000. The need for this amendment arose due to the adverse effects of an order dated 22.07.1997 on the interests of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. The Government received representations from several quarters, including Members of Parliament, expressing concerns about the impact of the order on these communities. After reviewing the situation, the Government decided to move for a constitutional amendment to restore the relaxations that had been withdrawn.
