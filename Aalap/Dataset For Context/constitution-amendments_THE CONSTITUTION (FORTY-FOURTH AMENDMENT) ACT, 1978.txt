Summary:
The Constitution (Forty-Fourth Amendment) Act, 1978, made significant changes to the Constitution of India to ensure the protection of secular and democratic values, fundamental rights, free and fair elections, and the independence of the judiciary. The Act removed property as a fundamental right, making it only a legal right. It also strengthened the provisions related to the proclamation of an emergency, requiring the approval of Parliament and a majority of the electorate in a referendum for certain changes to the Constitution. Additionally, the Act provided safeguards against the misuse of emergency provisions, ensured the right to life and liberty, and guaranteed the right of the media to report freely and without censorship the proceedings in Parliament and the State Legislatures. The Act also aimed to remove or correct the distortions that came into the Constitution during the period of the Internal Emergency.
