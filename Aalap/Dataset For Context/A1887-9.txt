Summary:
The Provincial Small Cause Courts Act, 1887, is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to Courts of Small Causes established beyond the Presidency-towns. This Act establishes Courts of Small Causes at various places within the territories under the administration of state governments beyond the local limits of the ordinary original civil jurisdiction of High Courts of Judicature. These courts have jurisdiction over all suits of a civil nature of which the value does not exceed five hundred rupees, and also over suits of a civil nature of which the value does not exceed one thousand rupees if directed by the state government. The Act also specifies the jurisdiction of these courts, which includes the power to try suits, pass decrees, and execute decrees. Additionally, the Act specifies the procedure for appeals and revisions of decisions made by these courts. The Act also provides for the appointment of a Registrar and other ministerial officers, and specifies their duties and powers. The Act further provides for the subordination of these courts to the administrative control of the District Court and the superintendence of the High Court. The Act also specifies the seal to be used by these courts and the powers of the state government to abolish these courts. The Act also applies to Courts invested with the jurisdiction of a Court of Small Causes so far as regards the exercise of that jurisdiction by those Courts.
