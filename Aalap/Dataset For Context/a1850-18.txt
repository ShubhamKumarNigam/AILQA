Summary:
THE JUDICIAL OFFICERS PROTECTION ACT, 1850, also known as Act No. 18 of 1850, was enacted on the 4th of April, 1850. The Act aims to protect Judicial Officers, including Judges, Magistrates, Justices of the Peace, Collectors, and other persons acting judicially, from being sued in any Civil Court for their official acts done in good faith, even if they exceed their jurisdiction. This protection extends to officers executing lawful warrants or orders of such Judicial Officers.

The Act has been declared in force in all provinces of India, except the Scheduled Districts, and has been extended to certain Scheduled Districts and new provinces. The Act has been amended by the Indian Short Titles Act, 1897, and the Scheduled Districts Act, 1874.

The Act includes a provision for criminal prosecution against judges and public servants under the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (Act 2 of 1974), section 197. The Act also includes state amendments, specifically from Karnataka, which amends the Central Act XVIII of 1850 by inserting a new section 1, which provides for the short title and extent of the Act.
