Summary:
The Legal Tender (Inscribed Notes) Act, 1964, restricts the negotiability of currency and other notes inscribed with messages of a political character. This Act, which extends to the whole of India, revokes the Legal Tender (Inscribed Notes) Ordinance, 1942. According to Section 2 of this Act, a currency note of the Government of India, a bank note issued by the Reserve Bank of India, or a Government of India one-rupee note issued under the Currency Ordinance, 1940, bearing written or visible representations intended to convey a message of a political character, is not considered legal tender. The Reserve Bank of India is not obligated to receive or exchange such notes or refund their value. However, the Reserve Bank of India may, at its discretion, refund the whole or part of the value of any such note as a matter of grace. Section 3 of this Act repeals the Legal Tender (Inscribed Notes) Ordinance, 1942, while ensuring that any actions taken under the repealed Ordinance are deemed to have been taken under this Act.
