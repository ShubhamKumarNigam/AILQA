Summary:

The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 (Act No. 6 of 2009) is an Act of the Indian Parliament that provides for the formation and regulation of limited liability partnerships (LLPs) in India. The Act defines an LLP as a partnership formed and registered under this Act, which is a separate legal entity from its partners, with perpetual succession. The Act states that the provisions of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, shall not apply to an LLP, and provides for the appointment, rights, and obligations of partners and designated partners. The Act also lays down provisions for the registration, maintenance, and audit of accounts, as well as the investigation of the affairs of an LLP. The Act further provides for the conversion of a firm, private company, or unlisted public company into an LLP, subject to certain conditions and procedures. The Act also includes provisions for the winding up and dissolution of an LLP. The Act came into force on 7th January, 2009.

The Act is divided into 14 chapters and 81 sections, and includes 4 schedules. The First Schedule provides for the mutual rights and duties of partners and the LLP, in the absence of any agreement on such matters. The Second Schedule provides for the conversion of a firm into an LLP, while the Third Schedule provides for the conversion of a private company into an LLP. The Fourth Schedule provides for the conversion of an unlisted public company into an LLP.

The Act includes provisions for the registration of an LLP, which requires the filing of an incorporation document and statement with the Registrar of Companies. The Registrar may refuse to register an LLP if he is not satisfied with the particulars or other information furnished under the Act. The Act also provides for the effect of registration, which includes the transfer of property, assets, interests, rights, privileges, liabilities, obligations, and the undertaking of the firm or company to the LLP. The Act also includes provisions for the registration in relation to property, pending proceedings, continuance of conviction, ruling, order or judgment, existing agreements, existing contracts, continuance of employment, existing appointment, authority or power, and application of paragraphs 6 to 14.

The Act also includes provisions for the winding up and dissolution of an LLP, which may be either voluntary or by the Tribunal. The Act provides for the circumstances in which an LLP may be wound up by the Tribunal, including the inability to pay its debts, the commencement of proceedings against the LLP for winding up, or if the Tribunal is of the opinion that it is just and equitable that the LLP be wound up. The Act also includes provisions for the rules for winding up and dissolution, and the power to alter schedules.

The Act also includes provisions for the power to make rules, the power to remove difficulties, and transitional provisions. The Act also includes provisions for the payment of additional fee, enhanced punishment, application of other laws not barred, jurisdiction of Tribunal and Appellate Tribunal, penalty on non-compliance of any order passed by Tribunal, general penalties, power to strike defunct LLP off register, offences to LLPs, and jurisdiction of Court.

In conclusion, the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 provides a legal framework for the formation and regulation of LLPs in India, with provisions for the appointment, rights, and obligations of partners and designated partners, the registration, maintenance, and audit of accounts, the investigation of the affairs of an LLP, the conversion of a firm, private company, or unlisted public company into an LLP, and the winding up and dissolution of an LLP. The Act aims to promote entrepreneurship and innovation, while providing flexibility and limited liability to partners.
