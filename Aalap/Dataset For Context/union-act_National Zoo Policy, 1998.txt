The National Zoo Policy, 1998, outlines the objectives and strategies for the management of zoos in India. The primary objective of zoos is to support the conservation of endangered species by breeding them in captivity and potentially reintroducing them into the wild. Additionally, zoos aim to educate visitors about the importance of conservation and provide opportunities for scientific research. The policy emphasizes the need for zoos to prioritize endangered species, regulate the number of animals in their collection, and avoid keeping single animals of non-viable sex ratios. It also stresses the importance of providing adequate housing, upkeep, and health care for all animals in the zoo. Zoos are encouraged to cooperate with one another and share resources to ensure the success of breeding programs. The policy also highlights the need for zoos to provide education and outreach activities to promote conservation and engage with the community. Finally, the policy emphasizes the importance of providing basic amenities to visitors while avoiding any infrastructure that is inconsistent with the stated objective of zoos.
