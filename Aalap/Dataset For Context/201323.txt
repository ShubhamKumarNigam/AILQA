Summary:

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 2013, establishes the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (Authority) to promote old age income security by establishing, developing and regulating pension funds, and to protect the interests of subscribers to schemes of pension funds. The Act defines various terms related to the National Pension System, including individual pension account, National Pension System, National Pension System Trust, point of presence, pension fund, pension fund adviser, retirement adviser, intermediary, and subscriber. The Act also provides for the composition, appointment, term of office, and conditions of service of members of the Authority, as well as the administrative powers of the Chairperson and the duties, powers, and functions of the Authority. The Act applies to the National Pension System and any other pension scheme not regulated by any other enactment. The Act also provides for the transfer of assets, liabilities, and rights of the Interim Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority to the Authority. The Authority has the power to regulate and approve pension schemes, register and regulate intermediaries, protect the interests of subscribers, establish mechanisms for redressal of grievances, promote professional organizations, adjudicate disputes, collect data, undertake studies, research, and projects, educate subscribers and the general public, standardize dissemination of information, regulate regulated assets, levy fees or other charges, specify the form and manner of maintaining books of account, call for information from, inspect, conduct inquiries and investigations, and exercise other powers and functions as may be prescribed. The Act also provides for the investigation of the affairs of intermediaries and other entities or organizations connected with pension funds, and the search and seizure of documents and records. The Act also provides for the management of the affairs of the central recordkeeping agency or pension fund by an administrator appointed by the Authority in certain circumstances. The Act establishes the National Pension System, which has certain basic features, including individual pension accounts, the option to invest up to 100% of funds in Government Securities, the option to invest in schemes providing minimum assured returns, portability of individual pension accounts, collection and transmission of contributions and instructions through points of presence, the absence of any implicit or explicit assurance of benefits, and the requirement to purchase an annuity from a life insurance company at exit. The Act also provides for the appointment of a central recordkeeping agency, which is responsible for receiving instructions from subscribers, transmitting such instructions to pension funds, effecting switching instructions, and discharging other duties as may be assigned to it under the certificate of registration or as may be determined by regulations. The Act also provides for the registration of pension funds, points of presence, and other intermediaries, and the suspension or cancellation of their certificates of registration. The Act imposes penalties for the failure to comply with the provisions of the Act, the rules, regulations, and directions issued by the Authority, and the terms and conditions of a certificate of registration. The Act also provides for the attachment of assets and supersession of management of intermediaries in certain circumstances. The Act defines offences and provides for the punishment of offenders, and grants immunity from prosecution and the imposition of penalties to persons who make a full and true disclosure in respect of alleged violations and satisfy certain conditions. The Act also provides for the exemption of the Authority from tax on wealth, income, profits, and gains, and the cognizance of offences by courts. The Act provides for the establishment of a Securities Appellate Tribunal to hear appeals against orders made by the Authority or by an adjudicating officer under the Act, and the appeal to the Supreme Court against decisions or orders of the Securities Appellate Tribunal. The Act also provides for the maintenance of accounts and audit by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India, and the laying of accounts and reports before Parliament. The Act also provides for the delegation of powers and functions of the Authority to committees, members, officers, or other employees of the Authority, and the power to make rules and regulations.
