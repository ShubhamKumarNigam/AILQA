Summary:

The Rehabilitation Council of India Act, 1992 (Act No. 34 of 1992) is an Act to provide for the constitution of the Rehabilitation Council of India for regulating and monitoring the training of rehabilitation professionals and personnel, promoting research in rehabilitation and special education, maintaining a Central Rehabilitation Register, and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act consists of 30 sections divided into three chapters: I. Preliminary, II. The Rehabilitation Council of India, and III. Functions of the Council.

Chapter I, Preliminary, contains two sections. Section 1 defines the short title and commencement of the Act, while Section 2 provides definitions for various terms used throughout the Act.

Chapter II, The Rehabilitation Council of India, contains 10 sections. Section 3 establishes the Rehabilitation Council of India, which is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, and has the power to acquire, hold, and dispose of property, both movable and immovable, and to contract and sue and be sued by the said name. The Council consists of a Chairperson and up to 21 members appointed by the Central Government, including representatives from various ministries, universities, medical professionals, and social workers. The Council is responsible for regulating and monitoring the training of rehabilitation professionals and personnel, promoting research in rehabilitation and special education, and maintaining a Central Rehabilitation Register.

Section 4 outlines the term of office of the Chairperson and members of the Council, while Section 5 lists the disqualifications for membership. Section 6 outlines the grounds for vacation of office by members, and Section 7 establishes the Executive Committee and other committees for general or special purposes. Section 8 outlines the appointment and powers of the Member-Secretary and employees of the Council. Section 9 states that vacancies in the Council do not invalidate acts or proceedings, and Section 10 outlines the dissolution of the Rehabilitation Council and transfer of rights, liabilities, and employees to the Council.

Chapter III, Functions of the Council, contains 17 sections. Section 11 outlines the recognition of qualifications granted by universities or institutions in India for rehabilitation professionals, while Section 12 allows for the recognition of qualifications granted by institutions outside India. Section 13 grants rights to persons possessing qualifications included in the Schedule to be enrolled on the Register, and Section 14 requires information to be furnished by universities or institutions in India as to courses of study and examinations. Section 15 outlines the powers and duties of Inspectors, while Section 16 establishes the powers and duties of Visitors. Section 17 outlines the withdrawal of recognition, and Section 18 allows for the prescription of minimum standards of education. Section 19 establishes the Register, while Section 20 grants privileges to persons who are registered on the Register. Section 21 outlines the professional conduct and removal of names from the Register, while Section 22 allows for appeals against orders of removal from the Register. Section 23 establishes the Register, and Section 24 requires the Council to furnish information to the Central Government and allows for the publication of such information. Section 25 outlines the cognizance of offenses, while Section 26 provides protection of action taken in good faith. Section 27 states that employees of the Council are public servants, while Section 28 allows for the making of rules by the Central Government. Section 29 allows for the making of regulations by the Council, and Section 30 requires the laying of rules and regulations before Parliament.

The Schedule to the Act lists the recognized rehabilitation qualifications granted by universities or institutions in India. The Schedule is divided into six parts, each part listing qualifications for different categories of rehabilitation professionals, including rehabilitation engineers and technicians, audiologists and speech therapists, teachers of special schools and integrated schools for the disabled, multipurpose rehabilitation therapists technicians/assistant/worker, vocational counsellors, and diploma in communication disorders. Each part lists the name of the university or institution, the name of the course, the qualification, and any remarks.

In summary, the Rehabilitation Council of India Act, 1992 establishes the Rehabilitation Council of India to regulate and monitor the training of rehabilitation professionals and personnel, promote research in rehabilitation and special education, maintain a Central Rehabilitation Register, and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act outlines the composition, powers, and duties of the Council, as well as the recognition of qualifications for rehabilitation professionals, the registration of rehabilitation professionals, and the professional conduct and removal of names from the Register. The Act also provides for the making of rules and regulations, and the laying of such rules and regulations before Parliament.
