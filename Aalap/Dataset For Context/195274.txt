Summary:
The Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952, is an Act to provide for the regulation of certain matters relating to forward contracts, the prohibition of options in goods, and for matters connected therewith. The Act is divided into six chapters. Chapter I contains the preliminary provisions and definitions. Chapter II establishes the Forward Markets Commission, which is responsible for exercising functions and discharging duties related to forward markets. Chapter III deals with recognized associations, which are associations concerned with the regulation and control of forward contracts and have been granted recognition by the Central Government. Chapter IV regulates forward contracts and options in goods, declaring certain forward contracts to be illegal or void in certain circumstances. Chapter V outlines the penalties and procedures for offenses under the Act, including the power to search and seize books of account or other documents, and the presumptions to be drawn in certain cases. Chapter VI contains miscellaneous provisions, including the power to delegate, the power to exempt, and the protection of action taken in good faith. The Act also includes provisions for the repeal and savings of previous laws, and the transfer and vesting of undertakings of the Commission.
