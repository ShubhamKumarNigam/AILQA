The Constitution (Forty-Eighth Amendment) Act, 1984, is an amendment to the Constitution of India which was enacted in the year 1984. The Act specifically amends article 356 of the Constitution, which deals with the imposition of President's rule in a state.

The amendment adds a new proviso to clause (5) of article 356, which deals with the continuance of President's rule in a state beyond one year. According to this proviso, in the case of the Proclamation issued under clause (1) on 6th October 1983 with respect to the State of Punjab, the reference to "any period beyond the expiration of one year" in this clause shall be construed as a reference to "any period beyond the expiration of two years."

In simpler words, the amendment allows for the continuance of President's rule in Punjab for a period of two years, instead of the usual one year, if a resolution is passed by Parliament with respect to the continuance in force of the Proclamation. This was done to address the specific situation in Punjab at the time, and to provide the central government with additional time to restore normalcy in the state.
