Summary:

THE NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN ACT, 1990 is an Act of the Parliament of India to constitute a National Commission for Women and to provide for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act defines the functions and duties of the Commission, which include investigating and examining all matters relating to the safeguards provided for women under the Constitution and other laws, presenting reports to the Central Government, making recommendations for the effective implementation of such safeguards, reviewing existing provisions of the Constitution and other laws affecting women, taking up cases of violation of provisions of the Constitution and other laws relating to women with appropriate authorities, and funding litigation involving issues affecting a large body of women. The Commission also has the power to call for special studies or investigations into specific problems or situations arising out of discrimination and atrocities against women, undertake promotional and educational research, participate and advise on the planning process of socio-economic development of women, evaluate the progress of the development of women under the Union and any State, inspect or cause to be inspected jails, remand homes, women’s institutions or other places of custody where women are kept as prisoners or otherwise, and make periodical reports to the Government on any matter pertaining to women. The Act also provides for the appointment of officers and employees of the Commission, their salaries and allowances, and the procedure for the Commission’s meetings and decision-making. The Central Government is required to consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting women. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act.
