Summary:
The Andhra Pradesh and Madras (Alteration of Boundaries) Act, 1959 is an Act of the Indian Parliament that aims to alter the boundaries of the States of Andhra Pradesh and Madras. The Act defines the territories transferred from one state to another and the areas included in each state after the transfer. The Act also provides for the transfer of pending legal proceedings, the continuance of officers in the same posts, and the extension of jurisdiction of certain existing road transport permits. The Act also contains provisions for the continuance of certain existing financial corporations, the amendment of certain laws, and the power to construe laws. The Act also provides for the transfer of assets and liabilities between the two states, the legal proceedings related to the transfer, and the right of pleaders to practice in certain courts. The Act also provides for the effect of provisions inconsistent with other laws, the power to remove difficulties, and the power to make rules. The Act includes several schedules that provide details of the territories transferred, the modifications of the First Schedule to the Delimitation of Parliamentary and Assembly Constituencies Order, 1956, the modifications of the Second Schedule to the Delimitation of Parliamentary and Assembly Constituencies Order, 1956, and the modifications of the Delimitation Commission’s Final Order No. 19 dated the 4th October, 1954.
