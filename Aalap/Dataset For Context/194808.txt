Summary:
THE PHARMACY ACT, 1948 is an Act to regulate the profession and practice of pharmacy in India. The Act establishes the Pharmacy Council of India, which is a body corporate, and is responsible for the regulation of the profession and practice of pharmacy. The Act also establishes State Pharmacy Councils, which are responsible for maintaining a register of pharmacists in their respective states.

Chapter I of the Act deals with preliminary matters, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act. It also defines certain terms used throughout the Act.

Chapter II of the Act deals with the Pharmacy Council of India. It sets out the composition of the Council, which includes representatives from the pharmaceutical industry, the medical profession, and the government. The Council is responsible for the regulation of the profession and practice of pharmacy, and has the power to make regulations for this purpose.

Chapter III of the Act deals with State Pharmacy Councils. It sets out the composition of the Councils, which includes representatives from the pharmaceutical industry, the medical profession, and the government. The Councils are responsible for maintaining a register of pharmacists in their respective states, and have the power to make regulations for this purpose.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the registration of pharmacists. It sets out the qualifications required for registration, and establishes a process for the registration of pharmacists. The Act also provides for the renewal of registration, and sets out the grounds for removal from the register.

Chapter V of the Act deals with miscellaneous matters, including penalties for falsely claiming to be registered, dispensing by unregistered persons, and failure to surrender a certificate of registration. The Act also provides for the appointment of a Commission of Enquiry to investigate complaints against the Pharmacy Council of India or a State Pharmacy Council.

Overall, THE PHARMACY ACT, 1948 is an important piece of legislation that regulates the profession and practice of pharmacy in India. It establishes the Pharmacy Council of India and State Pharmacy Councils, which are responsible for the regulation of the profession and practice of pharmacy. The Act sets out the qualifications required for registration, and establishes a process for the registration of pharmacists. It also provides for the renewal of registration, and sets out the grounds for removal from the register.
