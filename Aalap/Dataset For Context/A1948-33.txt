Summary:

The Calcutta Port (Pilotage) Act, 1948, is an Act to transfer control over pilotage on the River Hooghly to the Commissioners for the Port of Calcutta. The Act defines certain terms such as "Appointed day," "Commissioners," and "Hooghly area." As of the appointed day, it is the duty of the Commissioners to maintain pilots for the safe navigation of vessels in the Hooghly area. No person can be appointed as a pilot without authorization from the Central Government under the Indian Ports Act, 1908. The Commissioners can make rules to fix and regulate the salaries, wages, and allowances of pilots and to regulate their behavior and conduct. The Commissioners can enforce these rules by imposing penalties, suspension, or deprivation of appointment. Any such order made by the Commissioners is subject to the previous sanction of the Central Government if the officer's salary amounts to or exceeds one thousand rupees. The Commissioners can levy fees for pilotage of vessels in the Hooghly area at rates fixed under the Indian Ports Act, 1908. All fees for pilotage and all fines and penalties levied under this Act from pilots or other persons employed in the pilot service, except fines and penalties imposed by a Court, shall be accounted for and expended by the Commissioners in accordance with the provisions of section 36 of the Indian Ports Act, 1908. The Commissioners have the power to apply any sum out of the moneys credited to the general account towards meeting deficits, if any, in the pilotage account, and to transfer the whole or part of the surplus funds, if any, in such pilotage account to the general account. Certain provisions of Bengal Act 3 of 1890 are incorporated in this Act with modifications.
