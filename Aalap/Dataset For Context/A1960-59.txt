Summary:

THE PREVENTION OF CRUELTY TO ANIMALS ACT, 1960 is an Act to prevent the infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering on animals and for that purpose to amend the law relating to the prevention of cruelty to animals. The Act establishes an Animal Welfare Board of India and sets out its constitution. The Board is responsible for advising the Central Government on matters relating to animal welfare and for making regulations for the prevention of cruelty to animals. The Act also sets out duties of persons having charge of animals, defines cruelty to animals, and provides penalties for committing cruelty to animals. The Act also includes provisions for the regulation of experiments on animals and for the control and supervision of performing animals. The Act applies to the whole of India, and it came into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

There are several state amendments to the Act, including amendments to the definition of cruelty to animals, the addition of provisions for the conduct of traditional sports events such as "Kambala" and "Bulls race or Bullock cart race" in Karnataka and Maharashtra, and the exemption of certain activities from the provisions of the Act in Karnataka and Maharashtra.
