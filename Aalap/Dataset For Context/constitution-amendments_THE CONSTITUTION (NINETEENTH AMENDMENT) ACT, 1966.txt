Summary:
The Constitution (Nineteenth Amendment) Act, 1966, made significant changes to the Indian Constitution regarding the appointment of election tribunals. This amendment removed the power of the Election Commission to appoint election tribunals for resolving doubts and disputes arising out of or in connection with elections to Parliament and State Legislatures. Consequently, the trial of election petitions is now handled by High Courts, instead of election tribunals. This amendment was implemented by modifying article 324 of the Constitution, which deals with the power of superintendence, direction, and control of elections. Additionally, the Representation of the People Act, 1951, was amended to provide for the trial of election petitions by High Courts.
