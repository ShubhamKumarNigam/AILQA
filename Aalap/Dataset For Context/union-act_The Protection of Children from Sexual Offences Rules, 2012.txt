Summary:

The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Rules, 2012, outlines the procedures for implementing the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012. The rules define key terms, including District Child Protection Unit (DCPU), expert, special educator, support person, and person familiar with the manner of communication of the child. The rules also specify the qualifications and experience required for interpreters, translators, special educators, and experts.

The rules require that the DCPU maintain a register of interpreters, translators, and special educators for the purposes of the Act. The rules also outline the procedures for engaging interpreters, translators, special educators, and experts. The rules specify that interpreters, translators, and special educators should be unbiased and impartial and should disclose any real or perceived conflict of interest.

The rules also outline the procedures for providing care and protection to children who are victims of sexual offences. The rules require that the SJPU or local police disclose certain details to the person making the report, including their name and designation, address and telephone number, and the name and contact details of the officer who supervises the officer receiving the information. The rules also specify the procedures for recording and registering a First Information Report, providing emergency medical care, taking the child to the hospital for medical examination, and ensuring that samples collected for forensic tests are sent to the forensic laboratory at the earliest.

The rules also require that the SJPU or local police inform the child and his parent or guardian or other person in whom the child has trust and confidence of the availability of support services, including counselling, and assist them in contacting the persons who are responsible for providing these services and relief.

The rules also outline the procedures for producing the child before the Child Welfare Committee (CWC) and determining whether the child is in need of care and protection. The rules specify the factors that the CWC must take into account in making this determination, including the capacity of the parents or guardian to provide for the immediate care and protection needs of the child, the need for the child to remain in the care of his parent, family and extended family, and the child's age, level of maturity, gender, and social and economic background.

The rules also provide for the assignment of a support person to render assistance to the child through the process of investigation and trial. The support person may be a person or organization working in the field of child rights or child protection, or an official of a children's home or shelter home having custody of the child, or a person employed by the DCPU.

The rules also outline the procedures for monitoring the implementation of the Act by the National Commission for the Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) or the State Commission for the Protection of Child Rights (SCPCR). The NCPCR or SCPCR may call for a report on any specific case of child sexual abuse falling within the jurisdiction of a CWC and may collect information and data on reported cases of sexual abuse and their disposal under the processes established under the Act.

The rules also provide for the payment of compensation to the victim in appropriate cases. The compensation may be awarded at any stage after registration of the First Information Report and may be paid by the State Government from the Victims Compensation Fund or other scheme or fund established by it for the purposes of compensating and rehabilitating victims.
