Summary:
The Arms Act, 1959 is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to arms and ammunition. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government. The Act defines key terms such as 'acquisition', 'ammunition', 'arms', 'district magistrate', 'firearms', 'licence', 'licensing authority', 'magistrate', 'prescribed', 'prohibited ammunition', 'prohibited arms', 'public servant', 'transfer', and 'transit'.

Chapter II of the Act deals with the acquisition, possession, manufacture, sale, import, export, and transport of arms and ammunition. Under this chapter, no person shall acquire, have in his possession, or carry any firearm or ammunition unless he holds a licence issued in accordance with the provisions of this Act and the rules made thereunder. However, a person may carry any firearm or ammunition in the presence or under the written authority of the holder of the licence. The Act also specifies the conditions under which a person can acquire, possess, or carry more than two firearms.

Chapter III of the Act deals with provisions relating to licenses. The licensing authority may grant or refuse to grant a license under this Act. The Act also specifies the conditions under which a license may be varied, suspended, or revoked. The Act also provides for appeals against orders of the licensing authority.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with powers and procedure. The Act empowers police officers and other officers to demand the production of a license from any person carrying arms or ammunition. The Act also specifies the procedure for searching and seizing arms and ammunition.

Chapter V of the Act deals with offences and penalties. The Act specifies the punishment for various offences, including the manufacture, sale, transfer, conversion, repair, test, or proof of any arms or ammunition in contravention of the provisions of this Act. The Act also specifies the punishment for carrying or possessing any arms or ammunition in contravention of the provisions of this Act.

Chapter VI of the Act deals with miscellaneous provisions. The Act provides for the sanction of the Central Government for warehousing of arms, the criminal responsibility of persons in occupation of premises in certain cases, information to be given regarding certain offences, arrest and searches, offences to be cognizable, previous sanction of the district magistrate necessary in certain cases, protection of action taken in good faith, power to exempt, power to take census of firearms, power to delegate, and power to make rules.

The Act has been amended several times to strengthen the provisions related to the control of arms and ammunition. The latest amendment to the Act was made by the Arms (Amendment) Act, 2019.
