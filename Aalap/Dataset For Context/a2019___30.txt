Summary:
The National Medical Commission Act, 2019 (Act No. 30 of 2019) aims to provide a medical education system that improves access to quality and affordable medical education, ensures availability of adequate and high-quality medical professionals in all parts of the country, and promotes equitable and universal healthcare. The Act establishes the National Medical Commission (NMC), an autonomous body responsible for regulating medical education and medical professionals in India. The NMC comprises a Chairperson, ten ex officio Members, and twenty-two part-time Members. The Act also establishes four Autonomous Boards - the Under-Graduate Medical Education Board, the Post-Graduate Medical Education Board, the Medical Assessment and Rating Board, and the Ethics and Medical Registration Board - to perform specific functions under the overall supervision of the NMC. The Act provides for the establishment of a Medical Advisory Council to advise the NMC on matters related to medical education and training. The Act also lays down policies for maintaining high standards in medical education, regulating medical institutions, and ensuring ethical conduct among medical professionals. The Act also provides for the establishment of a National Register of medical practitioners and a National Exit Test for granting licenses to practice medicine. The Act repeals the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956, and provides for transitional provisions for the Medical Council of India and its assets and liabilities. The Act received the assent of the President on 8th August, 2019, and came into force on the date notified by the Central Government.
