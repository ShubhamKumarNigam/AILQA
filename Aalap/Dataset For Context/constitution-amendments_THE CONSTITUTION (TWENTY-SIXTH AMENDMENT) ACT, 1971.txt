Summary:

The Constitution (Twenty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1971, was enacted to eliminate the practice of privy purses and make the political system more democratic. This amendment removed Articles 291 and 362 from the Constitution, which had restricted equality and social justice by granting tax-free privy purses to royal families, which were approximately one-fourth of their earlier earnings. The amendment also inserted a new article, 363A, which declared that the recognition of rulers of Indian states would cease and that all rights, liabilities, and obligations in respect of privy purses would be extinguished. Furthermore, the amendment substituted a new clause for clause (22) in article 366, which defined "Ruler" as the Prince, Chief, or other person who was recognized by the President as the Ruler of an Indian State before the commencement of the Constitution (Twenty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1971, or any person who was recognized by the President as the successor of such Ruler. The aim of this amendment was to abolish the special privileges and financial benefits enjoyed by former rulers of India and promote equality and social justice in the democratic system.
