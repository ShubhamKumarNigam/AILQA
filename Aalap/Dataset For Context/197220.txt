Summary:

THE ARCHITECTS ACT, 1972 (ACT NO. 20 OF 1972) is an Act to provide for the registration of architects and for matters connected therewith. The Act establishes the Council of Architecture, a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, with the power to acquire, hold and dispose of property, both movable and immovable, and to contract, and may by that name sue or be sued. The Head Office of the Council is at Delhi. The Council consists of 21 members, including architects possessing recognized qualifications, persons nominated by the All India Council for Technical Education, heads of architectural institutions in India imparting full-time instruction for recognized qualifications, the Chief Architects in the Ministries of the Central Government, a person nominated by the Central Government, an architect from each State nominated by the Government of that State, and two persons nominated by the Institution of Engineers (India) and the Institution of Surveyors of India. The President and Vice-President of the Council are elected by the members of the Council. The Council may constitute an Executive Committee and other committees for general or special purposes. The President, Vice-President, and other members of the Council are entitled to such fees and allowances as the Council may, with the previous sanction of the Central Government, fix. The Council may appoint a Registrar, who shall act as its Secretary, and such other officers and employees as it deems necessary. The Council shall establish a Fund into which shall be paid all moneys received by the Council and out of which shall be met all expenses and liabilities properly incurred by the Council. The Council shall keep proper accounts of the Fund and cause the annual accounts to be audited by an auditor appointed annually by the Council. The Council may recognize qualifications granted by authorities in India or foreign countries, subject to certain conditions and procedures. The Council may prescribe the minimum standards of architectural education required for granting recognized qualifications by colleges or institutions in India. The Council may prescribe standards of professional conduct and etiquette and a code of ethics for architects. The Council shall maintain a register of architects, which shall include the name, date of birth, nationality, residential address, qualification for registration, date of first admission to the register, professional address, and such further particulars as may be prescribed by rules. The register shall be published in such manner as the Central Government may direct, and any person aggrieved by a decision of the Registration Tribunal expressed or implied in the register so published may appeal to an authority appointed by the Central Government in this behalf. A person shall be entitled to have his name entered in the register if he resides or carries on the profession of architect in India, holds a recognized qualification, or does not hold such a qualification but, being a citizen of India, has been engaged in practice as an architect for a period of not less than five years prior to the date appointed under sub-section (2) of section 24, or possesses such other qualifications as may be prescribed by rules. The Act also provides for the removal of names from the register, the restoration of names to the register, the issue of duplicate certificates, the printing of the register, the effect of registration, penalties for falsely claiming to be registered, prohibition against the use of the title "architect," failure to surrender certificate of registration, cognizance of offenses, information to be furnished by the Council and publication thereof, protection of action taken in good faith, members of the Council and officers and employees to be public servants, power to remove difficulties, power of the Central Government to make rules, and power of the Council to make regulations. The Schedule to the Act lists the recognized qualifications granted by authorities in India.
