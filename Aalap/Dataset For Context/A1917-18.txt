Summary:
The Post Office Cash Certificates Act, 1917 (Act No. 18 of 1917) came into effect on 19th September, 1917, and its purpose is to restrict the transfer of Post Office 5-year Cash Certificates and to establish procedures for the payment of certificates that belong to deceased individuals.

The Act contains three sections. Section 1 is the short title, which is the Post Office Cash Certificates Act, 1917. Section 2 prohibits the transfer of Post Office 5-year Cash Certificates without the written consent of an authorized officer. The term "transfer" in this context refers to transfers made during the holder's lifetime and does not include transfers made by operation of law. Section 3 outlines the payment procedures for Post Office 5-year Cash Certificates held by individuals who have passed away. The provisions of the Government Savings Banks Act, 1873 apply to the payment of these certificates, except that the powers conferred by the said provisions on the Secretary of a Government Savings Bank are exercisable by the Post Master General for the area within which the post office of issue of such certificate is situate.

The Act has been extended to various regions, including the Union territory of Goa, Daman, and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Pondicherry. It has been amended by several acts, including Act 32 of 1920, Act 2 of 1943, and the A.O. 1948.
