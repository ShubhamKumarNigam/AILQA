Summary of THE WHITE PHOSPHORUS MATCHES PROHIBITION ACT, 1913:
The White Phosphorus Matches Prohibition Act, 1913, is an Act that prohibits the importation, manufacture, and sale of matches made with white phosphorus. The Act may be called the White Phosphorus Matches Prohibition Act, 1913, and it extends to the whole of India except the territories that were comprised in Part B States before the 1st of November, 1956. The Act shall come into force on the first day of July, 1913, with the exception of section 6, which shall come into force on the first day of July, 1914. The Act defines white phosphorus as the substance commonly known as white or yellow phosphorus. The Act prohibits the use of white phosphorus in the manufacture of matches. Any person who uses white phosphorus in the manufacture of matches shall be punishable with fine, which may extend to two hundred rupees. The Act also empowers an Inspector of Factories appointed under the Indian Factories Act, 1911, to take samples of any material in use or mixed for use in such manufacture. Any person who refuses to permit any such Inspector of Factories to take a sample shall be punishable with fine, which may extend to two hundred rupees. The Act also prohibits the sale, or offer or exposure for sale, or possession for the purposes of sale, of any matches made with white phosphorus. Any person who contravenes the provisions of sub-section (1) may be ordered to forfeit any such matches in his possession, and any matches so forfeited shall be destroyed or otherwise dealt with as the Magistrate may direct. Section 3 of the Act, which prohibited the importation of white phosphorus matches, has been repealed by the Repealing Act, 1938 (1 of 1938), s. 2 and the Schedule.
