Summary:

The Indian Trusts Act, 1882, is an Act to define and amend the law relating to private trusts and trustees. The Act extends to the whole of India and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, but the Central Government may extend it to other territories. The Act does not apply to certain legal matters such as rules of Muhammadan law, mutual relations of members of an undivided family, public or private religious or charitable endowments, or trusts to distribute prizes taken in war among the captors. The Act also does not apply to trusts created before a certain date.

The Act repeals certain statutes and acts mentioned in the Schedule annexed to it. The Act includes an interpretation clause that defines various terms used in the Act, such as "trust," "author of the trust," "trustee," "beneficiary," "trust-property," "beneficial interest," "instrument of trust," "breach of trust," "registered," and "notice."

Chapter II of the Act deals with the creation of trusts and specifies the rules for creating a valid trust. The Act states that a trust may be created for any lawful purpose, and the purpose of a trust is lawful unless it is forbidden by law, defeats the provisions of any law, is fraudulent, involves injury to the person or property of another, or is immoral or opposed to public policy. The Act also specifies that a trust of immoveable property is not valid unless declared by a non-testamentary instrument in writing signed by the author of the trust or the trustee and registered, or by the will of the author of the trust or of the trustee. A trust of moveable property is not valid unless declared as aforesaid or unless the ownership of the property is transferred to the trustee. These rules do not apply where they would operate so as to effectuate a fraud.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the duties and liabilities of trustees and specifies the trustee's duty to execute the trust, inform himself of the state of the trust-property, protect the title to the trust-property, and not set up title adverse to the beneficiary. The Act also specifies the trustee's duty to be impartial, prevent waste, account for profits, and convert perishable property. The Act also specifies the trustee's liability for breach of trust and sets out the rules for calculating interest and compensation in such cases.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the rights and powers of trustees and specifies the trustee's right to title-deed, reimbursement of expenses, indemnity from gainer by breach of trust, and settlement of accounts. The Act also specifies the trustee's general authority to deal with the trust-property and the power to sell, convey, or vary investments. The Act also specifies the trustee's power to apply property of minors, etc., for their maintenance, etc., and power to give receipts.

Chapter V of the Act deals with the disabilities of trustees and specifies that a trustee cannot renounce after acceptance, delegate, or act singly unless authorized by the instrument of trust or the court. The Act also specifies that a trustee cannot exercise a discretionary power unreasonably or in bad faith and that a trustee may not use trust-property for his own profit.

Chapter VI of the Act deals with the rights and liabilities of the beneficiary and specifies the beneficiary's rights to rents and profits, specific execution, inspection and copies of the instrument of trust, accounts, etc., transfer of beneficial interest, and right to sue for execution of the trust. The Act also specifies the beneficiary's liability for joining in breach of trust and the rights and liabilities of the beneficiary's transferee.

Chapter VII of the Act deals with vacating the office of trustee and specifies the rules for discharge of trustees, petition to be discharged from trust, and appointment of new trustees on death, etc. The Act also specifies the rules for vesting of trust-property in new trustees.

Chapter VIII of the Act deals with the extinction of trusts and specifies the rules for trust how extinguished, revocation of trust, and revocation not to defeat what trustees have duly done.

Chapter IX of the Act deals with certain obligations in the nature of trusts and specifies the rules for where obligation in the nature of trust is created, transfer for illegal purpose, bequest for illegal purpose, transfer pursuant to rescindable contract, debtor becoming creditor’s representative, advantage gained by fiduciary, advantage gained by exercise of undue influence, advantage gained by qualified owner, property acquired with notice of existing contract, purchase by person contracting to buy property to be held on trust, and advantage secretly gained by one of several compounding creditors.

The Act includes a schedule that lists the statutes and acts that have been repealed by the Act.
