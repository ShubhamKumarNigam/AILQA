Summary:

THE AGRICULTURAL PRODUCE (GRADING AND MARKING) ACT, 1937 was enacted on 24th February, 1937, to provide for the grading and marking of agricultural and other produce. The Act defines agricultural produce as all produce of agriculture or horticulture and all articles of food or drink wholly or partly manufactured from any such produce, and fleeces and the skins of animals.

The Act empowers the Central Government to make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act, including fixing grade designations to indicate the quality of any scheduled article, defining the quality indicated by every grade designation, specifying grade designation marks to represent particular grade designations, and providing for the payment of any expenses incurred in connection with the manufacture or use of any implement necessary for the reproduction of a grade designation mark or with the manufacture or use of any covering or label marked with a grade designation mark or with measures for the control of the quality of articles marked with grade designation marks including testing of samples and inspection of such articles or with any publicity work carried out to promote the sale of any class of such articles.

The Act also provides for penalties for unauthorized marking with grade designation mark, counterfeiting grade designation mark, and selling migraded articles, with imprisonment and fine. Additionally, the Central Government has the power to prescribe compulsory grade designations in respect of certain articles, and to institute prosecution.

The Act applies to the whole of India and has been extended to Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Pondicherry, and Goa, Daman and Diu. The Schedule to the Act includes various articles of agricultural produce such as fruit, vegetables, eggs, dairy produce, tobacco, coffee, hides and skins, fruit products, atta, oilseeds, vegetable oils, cotton, rice, lac, wheat, san hemp, sugarcane gur (Jaggery), myrobalans, bura, wool and goat hair, bristles, rosin and turpentine, arecanuts, essential oil, cashewnut, cardamom, pepper, ginger, honey, curry powder, kapok, raw jute, paddy, millets, mesta, chilies, turmeric, tapioca chips and tapioca flour, sisal and aloe fibres, oil cakes, condiments and spices, and pulses. The Act also empowers the Central Government to declare that the provisions of this Act shall apply to an article of agricultural produce not included in the Schedule or to an article other than an article of agricultural produce, by notification in the Official Gazette.
