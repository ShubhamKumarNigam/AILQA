Summary:

The Indian Telegraph Act, 1885, is a law that governs the establishment, maintenance, and working of telegraphs in India. The Act grants the Central Government the exclusive privilege of establishing, maintaining, and working telegraphs within India, subject to certain conditions. The Act also allows the Central Government to grant licenses to individuals or entities to establish, maintain, or work a telegraph within any part of India.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, including "telegraph," "telegraph officer," "message," "telegraph line," "post," "telegraph authority," and "local authority."

The Act empowers the Central Government or a State Government, or any officer specially authorized in this behalf by the Central or a State Government, to take temporary possession of any telegraph established, maintained, or worked by any person licensed under this Act in the event of a public emergency or in the interest of public safety. The Act also permits the interception of messages in the interest of sovereignty, integrity, security, friendly relations with foreign states, public order, or for preventing incitement to the commission of an offense.

The Act requires any Railway Company to permit the Government to establish and maintain a telegraph upon any part of the land of the Company and to give every reasonable facility for working the same.

The Act empowers the Central Government to notify the rates at which, and the other conditions and restrictions subject to which, messages shall be transmitted to any country outside India. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for the conduct of all or any telegraphs established, maintained, or worked by the Government or by persons licensed under this Act. These rules may provide for the rates at which, and the other conditions and restrictions subject to which, messages shall be transmitted within India, the precautions to be taken for preventing the improper interception or disclosure of messages, the fees to be charged for searching for telegrams or other documents in the custody of any telegraph officer, and the charges in respect of the establishment, maintenance, working, repair, transfer, shifting, withdrawal, or disconnection of any telegraph line, appliance, or apparatus for telegraphic communication.

The Act provides for the arbitration of disputes between the telegraph authority and the person for whose benefit the line, appliance, or apparatus is, or has been, provided. The Act also provides for the revocation of licenses granted under the Act and exempts the Government from responsibility for any loss or damage that may occur in consequence of any telegraph officer failing in his duty with respect to the receipt, transmission, or delivery of any message.

The Act includes provisions for the establishment of the Universal Service Obligation Fund, which is used to meet the Universal Service Obligation to provide access to telegraph services to people in rural and remote areas at affordable and reasonable prices. The Act also includes provisions for the administration and utilization of the Fund and for the payment of compensation to all persons interested for any damage sustained by them by reason of the exercise of the powers conferred by the Act.

The Act permits the telegraph authority to place and maintain a telegraph line under, over, along, or across, and posts in or upon, any immovable property, subject to certain conditions. The Act also empowers the telegraph authority to enter on property in order to repair or remove telegraph lines or posts and includes provisions for disputes between the telegraph authority and local authorities or other property owners.
