Summary:

The Constitution (Ninetieth Ninth Amendment) Act, 2014 received the assent of the President on December 31, 2014 and was published in the Gazette of India. The Act aimed to amend relevant provisions of the Constitution and establish a National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) for making recommendations for the appointment of Judges to the Supreme Court and High Courts. The NJAC would provide a meaningful role to the judiciary, the executive, and eminent persons in the selection process, ensuring transparency and accountability.

The proposed Constitution (One Hundred and Twenty-first Amendment) Bill, 2014 sought to insert new Articles 124-A, 124-B, and 124-C after Article 124 of the Constitution. The Bill provided for the composition and functions of the NJAC and empowered Parliament to regulate the procedure for appointment of Judges and enable the NJAC to lay down procedures by regulation for the discharge of its functions, manner of selection of persons for appointment, and other necessary matters.

The NJAC aimed to broaden the method of appointment of Judges in the Supreme Court and High Courts, enable participation of the judiciary, executive, and eminent persons, and ensure greater transparency, accountability, and objectivity in the appointment of Judges. However, the Supreme Court declared the Act unconstitutional in Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Assn. v. Union of India, 2015 SCC OnLine SC 964.
