Summary of THE NATIONAL FOOD SECURITY ACT, 2013:

The National Food Security Act, 2013 is an Act to provide for food and nutritional security in a human life cycle approach, by ensuring access to adequate quantity of quality food at affordable prices to people to live a life with dignity. The Act extends to the whole of India and is deemed to have come into force on the 5th day of July, 2013.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as 'anganwadi', 'central pool', 'eligible households', 'fair price shop', 'foodgrains', 'food security', 'food security allowance', 'local authority', 'meal', 'minimum support price', 'notification', 'other welfare schemes', 'person with disability', 'priority households', 'prescribed', 'ration card', 'rural area', 'Schedule', 'senior citizen', 'social audit', 'State Commission', 'State Government', 'Targeted Public Distribution System', 'Vigilance Committee', and other terms defined in the Essential Commodities Act, 1955 or any other relevant Act.

The Act is divided into fourteen chapters, each dealing with different aspects of food security.

Chapter I, Preliminary, deals with the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act, and the definitions of various terms used in the Act.

Chapter II, Provisions for Food Security, deals with the right to receive foodgrains at subsidized prices by persons belonging to eligible households under the Targeted Public Distribution System, nutritional support to pregnant women and lactating mothers, and nutritional support to children. The Chapter also deals with the prevention and management of child malnutrition, and the implementation of schemes for the realization of entitlements.

Chapter III, Food Security Allowance, deals with the right to receive food security allowance in certain cases.

Chapter IV, Identification of Eligible Households, deals with the coverage of population under the Targeted Public Distribution System, and the identification of priority households.

Chapter V, Reforms in the Targeted Public Distribution System, deals with the reforms that the Central and State Governments shall endeavor to progressively undertake in the Targeted Public Distribution System.

Chapter VI, Women Empowerment, deals with the eldest woman of the household being the head of the household for the purpose of issue of ration cards.

Chapter VII, Grievance Redressal Mechanism, deals with the internal grievance redressal mechanism, the District Grievance Redressal Officer, and the State Food Commission.

Chapter VIII, Obligations of the Central Government for Food Security, deals with the allocation of required quantity of foodgrains from the central pool to the State Governments, and the powers and duties of the Central Government.

Chapter IX, Obligations of the State Government for Food Security, deals with the implementation and monitoring of schemes for ensuring food security, and the powers and duties of the State Government.

Chapter X, Obligations of Local Authorities, deals with the implementation of the Targeted Public Distribution System by local authorities in their areas.

Chapter XI, Transparency and Accountability, deals with the disclosure of records of the Targeted Public Distribution System, and the conduct of social audits.

Chapter XII, Provisions for Advancing Food Security, deals with food security for people living in remote, hilly, and tribal areas, and steps to further advance food and nutritional security.

Chapter XIII, Miscellaneous, deals with other welfare schemes, penalties, power to adjudicate, power to delegate by the Central and State Governments, the Act to have overriding effect, power of the Central Government to give directions, power of the Central Government to make rules, power of the State Government to make rules, transitory provisions for schemes, guidelines, etc., power to remove difficulties, utilization of institutional mechanism for other purposes, and force majeure.

The Act also includes four Schedules, namely, Schedule I, Schedule II, Schedule III, and Schedule IV, dealing with subsidized prices under the Targeted Public Distribution System, nutritional standards, provisions for advancing food security, and state-wise allocation of foodgrains, respectively.

The Act is a significant step towards ensuring food and nutritional security in India, and aims to provide adequate quantity of quality food at affordable prices to people to live a life with dignity.
