Summary:
THE NATIONAL INVESTIGATION AGENCY ACT, 2008

The National Investigation Agency Act, 2008 (Act No. 34 of 2008) was enacted on 31st December, 2008, to constitute an investigation agency at the national level to investigate and prosecute offences affecting the sovereignty, security and integrity of India, security of State, friendly relations with foreign States and offences under Acts enacted to implement international treaties, agreements, conventions and resolutions of the United Nations, its agencies and other international organizations and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

The Act consists of 26 sections and one schedule. It is divided into five chapters. Chapter I is the preliminary chapter, which defines the short title, extent, and application of the Act. Chapter II deals with the National Investigation Agency, its constitution, superintendence, and conditions of service of members. Chapter III deals with the investigation by the National Investigation Agency, including the investigation of scheduled offences, power to transfer investigation to the State Government, power to investigate connected offences, state government to extend assistance to the National Investigation Agency, and power of State Government to investigate scheduled offences. Chapter IV deals with the Special Courts, their designation, place of sitting, jurisdiction, powers with respect to other offences, public prosecutors, procedure and powers, protection of witnesses, and sanction for prosecution. Chapter V deals with miscellaneous provisions, including the power of High Courts to make rules, power to remove difficulties, power to make rules, and laying of rules.

The Schedule to the Act lists the enactments, including the Explosive Substances Act, 1908; the Atomic Energy Act, 1962; the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967; the Anti-Hijacking Act, 2016; the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against Safety of Civil Aviation Act, 1982; the SAARC Convention (Suppression of Terrorism) Act, 1993; the Suppression of Unlawful Acts Against Safety of Maritime Navigation and Fixed Platforms on Continental Shelf Act, 2002; the Weapons of Mass Destruction and their Delivery Systems (Prohibition of Unlawful Activities) Act, 2005; and offences under the Indian Penal Code, Arms Act, and Information Technology Act.

The Act provides for the investigation and prosecution of offences affecting the sovereignty, security, and integrity of India, security of State, friendly relations with foreign States, and offences under Acts enacted to implement international treaties, agreements, conventions, and resolutions of the United Nations, its agencies, and other international organizations. The Act also provides for the designation of Special Courts for the trial of scheduled offences, the power of the Central Government to transfer cases to regular courts, and the power of the High Courts to make rules. The Act also provides for the protection of witnesses and sanction for prosecution. The Act empowers the National Investigation Agency to investigate and prosecute offences committed outside India if the offence is committed against an Indian citizen or affects the interests of India. The Act also empowers the State Government to designate one or more courts of session as Special Courts for the trial of offences under any or all the enactments specified in the Schedule. The Act also provides for the removal of difficulties and the making of rules.
