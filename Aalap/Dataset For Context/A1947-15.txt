Summary:
The Armed Forces (Emergency Duties) Act, 1947, is a law in India that enables the government to impose duties in connection with vital services on the armed forces during an emergency. The Act is also known as Act No. 15 of 1947 and was enacted on March 20, 1947.

The Act has three main sections. Section 1 is the short title and extend section, which states that the Act may be called the Armed Forces (Emergency Duties) Act, 1947. Section 2 is the emergency duties of Armed Forces section, which states that the Central Government may declare any specified service in a State to be a service of vital importance to the community. Once such a declaration is made, it becomes the duty of every person subject to the Army Act, 1950, the Air Force Act, 1950, or the Navy Act, 1957, to obey any command given by any superior officer in relation to employment upon or in connection with the service specified in the declaration.

Section 3 is the validation of certain past commands section, which states that every command given before the commencement of this Act to any person referred to in sub-section (2) of section 2 by any superior officer in relation to employment upon or in connection with any such service as the Central Government may specify in its behalf shall be deemed to have been a lawful command within the meaning and for the purposes of the Acts referred to in that sub-section.

The Act has been extended to the new Provinces and merged States by the Merged States (Laws) Act, 1949, to the States of Manipur, Tripura and Vindhya Pradesh by the Part C States (Laws) Act, 1950, in Pondicherry on October 1, 1963, to Lakshdeep (with modifications) on October 1, 1987, in Goa, Daman and Diu (with modifications) on July 1, 1965, and in Dadra and Nagar Haveli on July 1, 1965. The words "of the Crown" were omitted by the A.O. 1950, and the brackets and figure "(1)" were omitted by Act 4 of 1948. The words "the Indian Army Act, 1911, or the Indian Air Force Act, 1932" were substituted by Act 3 of 1951 for "the Indian Army Act, 1911, or the Indian Air Force Act, 1932." The words "the Naval Discipline Act, in the form in which it is set forth in the First Schedule to" were omitted by the A.O. 1950, and the words "the Indian Navy (Discipline) Act, 1934" were substituted by Act 58 of 1960 for "the Indian Navy (Discipline) Act, 1934."
