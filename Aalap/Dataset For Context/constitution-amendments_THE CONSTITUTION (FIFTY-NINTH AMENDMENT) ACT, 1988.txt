The Constitution (Fifty-Ninth Amendment) Act, 1988, made significant changes to the Indian Constitution concerning the state of Punjab. This Act made it possible for the President's rule to be implemented in Punjab for a longer period than before. The Act amended Article 356 of the Constitution to extend the maximum period of President's rule in Punjab from two years to three years.

Additionally, the Act inserted a new article, Article 359A, into the Constitution. This article made certain modifications to Part XVIII of the Constitution, which deals with Emergency Provisions. Specifically, it made changes to Article 352, 358, and 359, which deal with the declaration of a national emergency, suspension of fundamental rights, and the power of the President to modify the provisions of the Constitution during an emergency, respectively.

The modifications made by Article 359A were specific to the state of Punjab. For instance, it added a new condition for the declaration of an emergency in Punjab, which was the threat to the integrity of India by internal disturbance in the whole or any part of the territory of Punjab. It also expanded the scope of the President's rule to include disturbances and not just armed rebellion or external aggression.

However, it is important to note that the amendments made by this Act were not permanent. The Act provided that the amendment made to the Constitution by sub-section (1) shall cease to operate on the expiry of a period of two years from the commencement of this Act, except as respects things done or omitted to be done before such cesser.
