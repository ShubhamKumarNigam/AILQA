Summary:

The Religious Institutions (Prevention of Misuse) Act, 1988, is an Indian law designed to prevent the misuse of religious institutions for political and other purposes. The Act, which came into force on May 26, 1988, applies to the entire country.

The Act defines several key terms, including:

* "Ammunition," which has the same meaning as in the Arms Act, 1959.
* "Arms," which also has the same meaning as in the Arms Act, 1959.
* "Manager," which refers to any person who administers, manages, or controls the affairs, functions, or properties of a religious institution.
* "Political activity," which includes any activity promoting or propagating the aims or objects of a political party or cause, issue, or question of a political nature.
* "Political party," which includes any association or body of persons organized to carry on any political activity or to acquire or exercise political power through election or otherwise.
* "Religious institution," which means an institution for the promotion of any religion or persuasion, and includes any place or premises used as a place of public religious worship.

The Act prohibits the use of religious institutions for certain purposes, including:

* The promotion or propagation of any political activity.
* The harboring of any person accused or convicted of an offense under any law.
* The storing of any arms or ammunition.
* The keeping of any goods or articles in contravention of any law.
* The erection or putting up of any construction or fortification without a valid license or permission under any law.
* The carrying on of any unlawful or subversive act prohibited under any law.
* The doing of any act which promotes or attempts to promote disharmony or feelings of enmity, hatred, or ill-will between different religious, racial, language, or regional groups or castes or communities.
* The carrying on of any activity prejudicial to the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
* The doing of any act in contravention of the provisions of the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971.

The Act also prohibits the entry of any arms or ammunition or of any person carrying any arms or ammunition into a religious institution, except for the wearing and carrying of a Kirpan by any person professing the Sikh religion or for any arms used in religious ceremonies or rituals.

Additionally, the Act prohibits the use of funds or other properties belonging to a religious institution for the benefit of any political party or for the purpose of any political activity or for the commission of any act which is punishable as an offense under any law. The Act also prohibits religious institutions from allowing any ceremony, festival, congregation, procession, or assembly organized or held under its auspices to be used for any political activity.

Violations of the Act are punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and with fine which may extend to ten thousand rupees. Managers or other employees of a religious institution who are convicted of an offense under this Act shall stand removed from their office or post and shall be disqualified for appointment in any religious institution as manager or in any other capacity for a period of six years from the date of their conviction.

The Act also requires every manager or other employee of a religious institution to give information to the officer in charge of the police station within whose local jurisdiction the religious institution is situate of any contravention or any impending contravention of the provisions of this Act. Failure to do so is punishable under section 176 of the Indian Penal Code.

The Religious Institutions (Prevention of Misuse) Ordinance, 1988, is repealed by this Act, but anything done or any action taken under the said Ordinance shall be deemed to have been done or taken under the corresponding provisions of this Act.
