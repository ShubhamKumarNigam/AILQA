THE TRANSPLANTATION OF HUMAN ORGANS AND TISSUES ACT, 1994

Act ID: ACT NO. 42 OF 1994

Enactment Date: [8th July, 1994.]

This Act, known as the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994, provides for the regulation of removal, storage, and transplantation of human organs and tissues for therapeutic purposes and for the prevention of commercial dealings in human organs and tissues.

The Act applies to the whole of the States of Goa, Himachal Pradesh, and Maharashtra and to all the Union territories. It also applies to any other State which adopts this Act by resolution passed in that behalf under clause (1) of article 252 of the Constitution.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "appropriate authority," "authorisation committee," "brain-stem death," "deceased person," "donor," "hospital," "human organ," "human organ retrieval centre," "minor," "near relative," "notification," "payment," "prescribed," "recipient," "registered medical practitioner," "therapeutic purposes," and "transplantation."

The Act regulates the authority for the removal of human organs or tissues or both. It sets out the conditions under which a donor may authorize the removal of any human organ or tissue or both before his death, and the conditions under which a person lawfully in possession of the dead body of a deceased person may grant facilities for the removal of any human organ or tissue or both for therapeutic purposes. The Act also sets out the conditions under which a near relative may give authority for the removal of any human organ or tissue or both from the body of a deceased minor or mentally challenged person.

The Act prohibits the removal or transplantation of human organs or tissues or both for any purpose other than therapeutic purposes. It also requires registered medical practitioners to explain all possible effects, complications, and hazards connected with the removal and transplantation of human organs or tissues or both to the donor and the recipient.

The Act establishes an appropriate authority to perform various functions, such as granting registration, suspending or canceling registration, enforcing standards, investigating complaints, inspecting hospitals and tissue banks, and undertaking other measures. The Act also establishes an advisory committee to aid and advise the appropriate authority in discharging its functions.

The Act regulates the registration of hospitals engaged in the removal, storage, or transplantation of human organs or tissues or both. It sets out the conditions under which a hospital may be registered, and requires hospitals to appoint a transplant coordinator having certain qualifications and experience.

The Act provides for penalties for various offenses, such as removing a human organ or tissue or both without authority, engaging in commercial dealings in human organs or tissues or both, and contravening any other provision of the Act.

The Act also includes provisions for the protection of action taken in good faith, the power to make rules, and the repeal and savings of certain earlier laws.
