Summary:

The Union Territories (Separation of Judicial and Executive Functions) Act, 1969, is an Act to provide for the separation of judicial and executive functions in Union Territories. The Act extends to all Union territories except the Union territory of Chandigarh. It came into force in a Union territory on a date appointed by the Central Government.

The Act amends the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, to separate judicial and executive functions. The amendments specify the classes of Magistrates, invest Judicial Magistrates with the powers of a Chief Judicial Magistrate, and confer on any person who is a civil Judge or a member of the Judicial Service of a Union territory or a group of such territories, the powers of any class of Judicial Magistrates in any district. The Act also provides for the subordination of Judicial Magistrates and Benches to Chief Judicial Magistrates and of Chief Judicial Magistrates and Assistant Sessions Judges to Sessions Judges.

The Act also empowers the State Government, in consultation with the High Court, to invest any Chief Judicial Magistrate or any other Judicial Magistrate of the first class with power to try as a Judicial Magistrate all offences not punishable with death or with imprisonment for life or with imprisonment for a term exceeding seven years. The Act also provides for the subordination of Executive Magistrates and confers additional powers on Magistrates.

The Act also amends sections 37, 38, 39, 40, 57, 63, 78, 88, 95, 96, 98, 106, 107, 108, 109, 124, 125, 126, 127, 133, 143, 144, 145, 146, 147, 148, 155, 164, 167, 170, 174, 190, 192, 193, 196B, 206, 249, 250, 260, 261, 263, 265, 269, 337, 338, 346, 349, 373, 380, 406A, 406B, 407, 408, 409, 412, 413, 435, 436, 437, 438, 439, 479, 488, 512, 515, 524, 528, 559, 561, 562, 565, and Schedules II, III, and IV of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898.
