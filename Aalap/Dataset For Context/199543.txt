Summary:

THE WAQF ACT, 1995 (ACT NO. 43 OF 1995) is a law that provides for the better administration of Auqaf (waqf properties) in India. The Act applies to all auqaf, whether created before or after the commencement of this Act, except for the Durgah Khawaja Saheb, Ajmer which is governed by the Durgah Khawaja Saheb Act, 1955.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, including beneficiary, benefit, Board, Chief Executive Officer, Council, encroacher, Executive Officer, list of auqaf, member, mutawalli, net annual income, person interested in a waqf, prescribed, regulations, Shia waqf, Sunni waqf, Survey Commissioner, Tribunal, waqf, waqf deed, and Waqf Fund.

Chapter II of the Act deals with the Survey of Auqaf. The State Government may appoint a Survey Commissioner of Auqaf and Additional or Assistant Survey Commissioners of Auqaf for the purpose of making a survey of auqaf in the State. The Survey Commissioner shall submit a report to the State Government containing particulars such as the number of auqaf in the State, the nature and objects of each waqf, the gross income of the property comprised in each waqf, and such other particulars as may be prescribed. The Survey Commissioner shall have the same powers as are vested in a civil court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 in respect of summoning and examining any witness, requiring the discovery and production of any document, issuing commissions for the examination of any witness or accounts, making any local inspection or local investigation, and such other matters as may be prescribed.

Overall, THE WAQF ACT, 1995 aims to provide a legal framework for the administration and management of auqaf in India, ensuring that these properties are used for the purposes for which they were created, and that the benefits derived from them are distributed fairly and transparently.
