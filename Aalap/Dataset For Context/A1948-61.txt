Summary:
The Central Silk Board Act, 1948, establishes a Central Silk Board to oversee the development of the silk industry in India. The Act outlines the composition of the Board, the powers and duties of the Board and its members, and the procedures for nominating and appointing members. The Act also grants the Central Government the power to declare certain kinds or varieties of silk-worm seed to be notified kinds or varieties, and sets out the conditions and requirements for their production, supply, distribution, trade, and commerce. The Act imposes a cess on filature raw silk and spun silk, and establishes a fund for the Board to finance its activities. The Act also sets out penalties for non-compliance with its provisions. The Act has been amended several times, most recently by the Central Silk Board (Amendment) Act, 2006.
