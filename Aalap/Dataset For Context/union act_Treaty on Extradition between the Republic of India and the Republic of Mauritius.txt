The Treaty on Extradition between the Republic of India and the Republic of Mauritius is a legal agreement between the two countries regarding the extradition of individuals accused or convicted of committing an extradition offence. The treaty outlines the circumstances and conditions under which extradition shall be granted, including the definition of an extradition offence and the procedure for making a request for extradition. The treaty also includes provisions for the postponement of surrender, grounds for refusal of extradition, and the rule of speciality. In addition, the treaty addresses evidence, competing requests, capital punishment, surrender of property, mutual legal assistance in extradition, and final provisions. The treaty applies to requests made after its entry into force and is subject to ratification. Either contracting state may terminate the treaty at any time by giving notice to the other through the diplomatic channel. The Central Government of India has also directed that the provisions of the Extradition Act, 1962, other than Chapter III, shall apply to the Republic of Mauritius.
