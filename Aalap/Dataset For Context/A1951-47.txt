Summary:
The Assam (Alteration of Boundaries) Act, 1951, also known as Act No. 47 of 1951, was enacted on the 1st of September, 1951. The Act aims to alter the boundaries of the State of Assam due to the cession of a strip of territory to the Government of Bhutan.

Section 1 of the Act states that the Act may be called the Assam (Alteration of Boundaries) Act, 1951.

Section 2 of the Act specifies that the territory of the State of Assam will cease to include the strip of territory specified in the Schedule, which will be ceded to the Government of Bhutan. The boundaries of the State of Assam will be deemed to have been altered accordingly.

Section 3 of the Act amends the First Schedule to the Constitution. The amendment adds the words "but shall not include the territories specified in the Schedule to the Assam (Alteration of Boundaries) Act, 1951" after the words "Assam Tribal Areas" in the first paragraph of Part A of the First Schedule to the Constitution.

The Schedule to the Act specifies the strip of territory that will be ceded to the Government of Bhutan. The territory measures 32.81 square miles and is located to the south of Bhutan, between 26o 45' and 27o North Latitude and 91o 15' and 91o 45' East Longitude. The territory is demarcated on the west, north, and east by boundary pillar Nos. 90, 91, 92, 93, and 94 erected in 1930-31, and on the south by boundary pillar Nos. 90, 91, 92, 93, and 94 erected in 1950.
