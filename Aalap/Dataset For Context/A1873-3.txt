Summary:
THE MADRAS CIVIL COURTS‟ ACT, 1873 is an Act that consolidates and amends the law relating to the Civil Courts of the Madras Presidency subordinate to the High Court. The Act is divided into six parts: Preliminary, Establishment and Constitution of Civil Courts, Jurisdiction, Misconduct of Judges, Ministerial Officers, and Miscellaneous.

Part I, Preliminary, includes the short title, local extent, and commencement of the Act. The short title of the Act is "The Madras Civil Courts‟ Act, 1873," and it extends to all the territories for the time being under the government of the Governor of Fort. St. George in Council, except the Tracts respectively under the jurisdiction of the Agents for Ganjam and Vizagapatam. The Act came into force on the first day of March, 1873.

Part II, Establishment and Constitution of Civil Courts, includes provisions related to the number of District Courts, Subordinate Judges, and District Munsifs. The number of District Courts, Subordinate Judges, and District Munsifs to be established or continued under this Act shall be fixed and may from time to time be altered by the Local Government. No increase to the number of such Courts shall be made by such Government without the previous sanction of the Governor General in Council.

Part III, Jurisdiction, includes provisions related to the local limits of jurisdiction of District Courts, Subordinate Judges, and District Munsifs. The Local Government shall fix, and may from time to time vary, the local limits of the jurisdiction of any District Judge or Subordinate Judge under this Act, and the High Court shall fix, and may from time to time modify, the local jurisdiction of District Munsifs. The jurisdiction of a District Judge or a Subordinate Judge extends to all original suits and proceedings of a civil nature, and the jurisdiction of a District Munsif extends to all like suits and proceedings, not otherwise exempted from his cognizance, of which the amount or value of the subject-matter does not exceed two thousand five hundred rupees.

Part IV, Misconduct of Judges, includes provisions related to the suspension or removal of Judges for misconduct. Any District Judge, Subordinate Judge, or District Munsif may, for any misconduct, be suspended or removed by the Local Government. The High Court may suspend a Subordinate Judge pending the orders of the Local Government, and the High Court may suspend any District Munsif who is alleged to have misconducted himself.

Part V, Ministerial Officers, includes provisions related to the appointment, suspension, removal, and duties of Ministerial Officers of District Courts, Subordinate Courts, and District Munsifs. The Ministerial Officers of the District Courts shall be appointed, and may be suspended or removed, by the Judges of such Courts, whose orders in such matters shall be final.

Part VI, Miscellaneous, includes provisions related to the temporary discharge of duties of District Judge, District Judge to control Civil Courts of District, Investiture of District or Subordinate Judge with Small Cause jurisdiction, and Vacation. The High Court may permit the Civil Courts under its control to adjourn from time to time for periods not exceeding in the aggregate two months in each year.

Overall, THE MADRAS CIVIL COURTS‟ ACT, 1873 is an important Act that governs the functioning of the Civil Courts in the Madras Presidency. The Act lays down provisions related to the establishment, jurisdiction, misconduct of Judges, Ministerial Officers, and miscellaneous matters. The Act aims to ensure the smooth functioning of the Civil Courts and to provide a fair and just system of administration of justice.
