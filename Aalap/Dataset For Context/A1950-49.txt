THE CONTINGENCY FUND OF INDIA ACT, 1950

Act No. 49 of 1950

This Act, enacted on August 14, 1950, establishes the Contingency Fund of India. The Fund, which is held on behalf of the President by a Secretary to the Government of India in the Ministry of Finance, is intended to provide for unforeseen expenditure pending authorization of such expenditure by Parliament under appropriations made by law.

The Fund is initially established with a sum of fifty crores of rupees, which may be increased during specific periods as stated in the Act. For instance, between July 28, 1999, and March 31, 2000, the Fund was increased to five hundred and fifty crores of rupees. Similarly, between January 24, 1998, and March 31, 1998, the Fund was increased to thirty-two thousand four hundred and ninety crores of rupees.

The Central Government has the power to make rules regulating all matters connected with or ancillary to the custody of, the payment of moneys into and the withdrawal of moneys from, the Contingency Fund of India. These rules must be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days, during which time they may be modified or annulled by both Houses.

The Act applies to Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Pondicherry.
