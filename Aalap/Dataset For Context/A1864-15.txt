The Indian Tolls Act, 1864, also known as Act No. 15 of 1864, was enacted on March 24, 1864. This Act amends the earlier Act 8 of 1851, which authorized the levy of certain rates of toll on public roads and bridges. The Act enables the government to levy tolls on public roads and bridges.

The Act contains four sections and one schedule.

Section 1 of the Act repeals the Schedule of Act 8 of 1851 and substitutes it with a new Schedule.

Section 2 of the Act permits collectors of tolls to compound for tolls leviable under Act 8 of 1851 or this Act. This means that collectors of tolls may enter into an agreement with any person for a certain sum to be paid in lieu of the rates of toll authorized to be levied under the said Act 8 of 1851.

Section 3 of the Act empowers the State Government to extend this Act to any place in which the said Act 8 of 1851 is in force. The State Government of any place in which the said Act 8 of 1851 is not in force may extend the said Act 8 of 1851 and this Act to such place.

Section 4 of the Act, which contained an interpretation clause, has been repealed.

The Schedule to the Act, which has been omitted, contained a list of the rates of toll that could be levied under Act 8 of 1851.

This Act has been extended to various parts of the country, including the Santhal Parganas, the C. P. and the Sambalpur District, the Districts of Hazaribagh Lohardaga, Manbhum, and Pargana Dhalbhum and the Kolhan in the District of Singhbhum, the District of Lahaul, the Scheduled District of Coorg, the Scheduled Districts in Vizagapatam and Ganjam, and the District of Darjeeling. The Act has also been extended to the Union territory of Pondicherry. However, the Act has been repealed in Mysore and has been amended in Andhra Pradesh. The Act has been deemed to be in force in the Punjab from March 24, 1864.
