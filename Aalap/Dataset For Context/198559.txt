Summary:

The Judges (Protection) Act, 1985, is a law that aims to provide additional protection for Judges and others acting judicially in India. The Act is officially known as Act No. 59 of 1985, and it was enacted on the 6th of September, 1985.

The Act defines a Judge as any person who is officially designated as a Judge or any person who is empowered by law to give a definitive judgment or a judgment that, if not appealed against, would be definitive. This includes members of a body of persons who are empowered by law to give such judgments.

The Act provides additional protection to Judges by prohibiting any court from entertaining or continuing any civil or criminal proceeding against any person who is or was a Judge for any act, thing, or word committed, done, or spoken by him when, or in the course of, acting or purporting to act in the discharge of his official or judicial duty or function. However, this protection does not affect the power of the Central Government, the State Government, the Supreme Court of India, any High Court, or any other authority under any law for the time being in force to take action against any person who is or was a Judge.

The provisions of this Act are in addition to, and not in derogation of, the provisions of any other law for the time being in force providing for protection of Judges. The Act initially excluded the State of Jammu and Kashmir, but this exclusion was omitted by Act 34 of 2019, s. 95 and the Fifth Schedule (w.e.f. 31-10-2019).
