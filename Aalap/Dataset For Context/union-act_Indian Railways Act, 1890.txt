
The Railway Act, 1890, is an Act of the Indian Parliament that governs the operation of railways in India. The Act extends to the whole of Bangladesh and applies to all citizens of Bangladesh, regardless of their location. It came into force on the first day of May, 1890.

The Act includes various definitions, including the definition of "railway," which refers to the railway or any portion of the railway, including land, lines of rails, stations, offices, warehouses, wharves, workshops, manufactories, fixed plant and machinery, ferries, ships, boats, and rafts used for the purposes of the railway. The definition of "railway servant" includes any person employed by the railway administration in connection with the service of the railway.

The Act also includes provisions for the appointment and duties of Inspectors, who are appointed by the Government to inspect the railway and report on its fitness for public carriage of passengers. Inspectors have the power to enter upon and inspect any railway or rolling-stock, require the attendance of railway servants, and require the production of books or documents belonging to the railway administration. The railway administration is required to afford all reasonable facilities for performing the duties and exercising the powers imposed on Inspectors by the Act.

The Act grants the railway administration the authority to execute all necessary works for the construction, maintenance, alteration, or repair of the railway. This includes the power to make or construct in, upon, across, under, or over any lands, streets, hills, valleys, roads, rivers, canals, brooks, streams, or other waters, or any drains, water-pipes, gas-pipes, or telegraph lines. The exercise of these powers is subject to the control of the Government and the provisions of any enactment for the time being in force for the acquisition of land for public purposes.

The Act also includes provisions for the alteration of pipes, wires, and drains, protection for Government property, temporary entry upon land for repairing or preventing accidents, payment of compensation for damage caused by the lawful exercise of powers under the foregoing provisions of this Chapter, accommodation works, power for owner-occupier or local authority to cause additional accommodation works to be made, fences, screens, gates, and bars, over and under bridges, removal of trees dangerous to or obstructing the working of the railway, right to use locomotives, and notice of intended opening of the railway.

Overall, the Railway Act, 1890, provides a comprehensive framework for the operation and regulation of railways in India, with a focus on ensuring the safety and efficiency of railway operations.
