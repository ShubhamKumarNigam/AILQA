Summary:
The Land Ports Authority of India Act, 2010, establishes the Land Ports Authority of India to develop, sanitize and manage the facilities for cross border movement of passengers and goods at designated points along the international borders of India. The Authority has the power to put in place systems that address security imperatives at the integrated check posts on the border, plan and construct infrastructure, provide necessary services, and establish and maintain warehouses, container depots, and cargo complexes for the storage or processing of goods. The Authority is also responsible for coordinating and facilitating the working of agencies who have been engaged to undertake various activities at the integrated check posts. The Act also specifies the responsibilities and powers of other agencies, such as border guarding forces, customs, immigration, and quarantine officials. The properties and assets of the Authority are vested in it, and it has the power to charge fees or rent for various services. The Central Government may provide capital or pay grants to the Authority, and the Authority's fund is to be maintained and invested as prescribed. The Authority is required to submit a programme of activities and financial estimates for the approval of the Central Government before the commencement of each financial year. The Authority may borrow money as prescribed, and the accounts and audit of the Authority are to be conducted as prescribed. The Authority is required to submit an annual report to the Central Government, which is to be laid before both Houses of Parliament. The Authority may delegate its powers and functions to the Chairperson or any other member or officer of the Authority. The Authority and its officers and employees are deemed to be public servants, and the Act provides protection for action taken in good faith. The Central Government may supersede the Authority for a specified period if it is of the opinion that the Authority is unable to discharge its functions or has persistently defaulted in complying with any direction issued by the Central Government. The Central Government may also issue directions to the Authority regarding the discharge of its functions. The Act provides for the making of rules and regulations for carrying out its provisions. Every rule, regulation, and notification made under the Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. The Act also provides for the removal of difficulties in giving effect to its provisions.
