Summary:
The Indian Statistical Institute Act, 1959 (Act No. 57 of 1959) declares the Indian Statistical Institute as an institution of national importance. The Act provides for the grant of degrees and diplomas in statistics, mathematics, quantitative economics, computer science and related subjects, and empowers the Institute to hold examinations for the same. The Central Government may provide grants, loans, or other financial assistance to the Institute for its various functions, including research, education, training, project activities, and statistical work related to planning for national development. The accounts of the Institute are to be audited by auditors qualified under the Companies Act, 1956, and appointed by the Central Government. The Institute requires the prior approval of the Central Government for certain actions, such as altering its purposes, amalgamating with other institutions, altering or amending the memorandum or rules and regulations, selling or disposing of property acquired with money specifically provided by the Central Government, or being dissolved. The Central Government may constitute Committees for preparing programmes of work by the Institute and assigning duties related to the preparation and submission of statements showing programmes of work, general financial estimates, and the settlement of programmes. The Central Government may also constitute a Committee for reviewing the work done by the Institute, inspecting its buildings, equipment, and other assets, evaluating the work done by the Institute, and advising the Government on any matter of importance in connection with the work of the Institute. The Institute is required to afford all necessary facilities to any Committee constituted under the Act for the purpose of enabling it to carry out its duties. The Central Government may issue directions to the Institute, including directions requiring the Institute to amend the memorandum or to make or amend any rule or regulation within a specified period, or to give priorities to the work undertaken or to be undertaken by the Institute. If the Institute fails to comply with any direction issued by the Central Government, the Central Government may assume control of the Institute or any part thereof for a specified period.
