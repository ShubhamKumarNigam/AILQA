The Customs Tariff (Determination of Origin of Goods Under the Preferential Trade Agreement Between the Governments of the Republic of India and Malaysia) Rules, 2011 

 Summary:

These rules, known as the Customs Tariff (Determination of Origin of Goods Under the Preferential Trade Agreement Between the Governments of the Republic of India and Malaysia) Rules, 2011, came into force on July 1, 2011. They define terms related to the determination of the origin of goods for the purpose of the preferential trade agreement between India and Malaysia.

Under these rules, goods are considered originating if they are wholly obtained or produced in the territory of the exporting party, or if they are not wholly obtained or produced but satisfy certain product-specific rules. These rules specify that materials used in the production of goods must undergo a change in tariff classification or meet a specific manufacturing or processing operation, or satisfy a qualifying value content criterion.

The rules also include provisions for the cumulative rule of origin, which allows goods that comply with the rules of origin and are used in the territory of a party as materials for finished goods to be considered originating in the territory of the latter party. There are also provisions for de minimis, which allows goods that do not undergo a change in tariff classification to be considered originating if the value of non-originating materials used in their production does not exceed a certain percentage of the FOB value.

The rules also specify that certain minimal operations and processes, such as preservation, simple operations, changes in packing, and affixing marks or labels, do not confer originating status on goods. There are also specific provisions for textiles and textile goods.

Goods are considered directly consigned from the territory of the exporting party to the territory of the importing party if they are transported without passing through the territory of any non-party, or if they are transported through the territory of a non-party under certain conditions.

The rules also include provisions for the treatment of packing materials and containers, accessories, spare parts, tools, and instructional or other information material, and indirect materials.

A certificate of origin is required to support a claim that imported goods are eligible for preferential tariff treatment. This certificate must be issued by an authority designated by the government of the exporting party and must comply with the format specified in Annexure-IV to these rules.
