Summary:

The Khaddar (Protection of Name) Act, 1950, is a law in India that aims to regulate the use of the words “Khaddar” and “Khadi” when applied as a trade description for woven materials. The Act, which extends to all of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir (now applicable to the Union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh), states that these words, when used to describe any woven material, must indicate that such material is cloth woven on handlooms in India from cotton, silk, or woolen yarn that has been hand-spun in India, or from a mixture of any two or all of these yarns. This use of the words “Khaddar” and “Khadi” is considered a trade description within the meaning of the Indian Merchandise Marks Act, 1889.

Section 3 of the Act has been repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act, 1957.

The Act was originally not applicable to the State of Jammu and Kashmir, but with the Notification No. S.O. 3912 (E), dated 30th October, 2019, the Act is now made applicable to the Union territory of Jammuand Kashmir and the Union territory of Ladakh.
