Summary:
The Export-Import Bank of India Act, 1981 establishes a corporation known as the Export-Import Bank of India (Exim Bank) to provide financial assistance to exporters and importers, and to function as the principal financial institution for coordinating the working of institutions engaged in financing export and import of goods and services. The Act defines various terms used throughout the document, such as "export" and "import," "goods," "services," and "Development Bank."

Chapter I of the Act covers preliminary provisions, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act.

Chapter II establishes the Exim Bank and sets out its authorized capital.

Chapter III covers the management of the Exim Bank, including the composition and powers of the Board of Directors, the appointment and removal of the chairman and managing director, and the conduct of meetings.

Chapter IV sets out the business that the Exim Bank may carry on, including granting loans and advances, underwriting the issue of stocks and shares, issuing bid bonds or guarantees, and buying or selling foreign exchange.

Chapter V covers the resources of the Exim Bank, including loans from the Central Government, borrowings and acceptance of deposits, and loans in foreign currency.

Chapter VI establishes the Export Development Fund, which is used to finance exports and to provide loans and advances for the purpose of export or import.

Chapter VII covers general provisions relating to the accounts and audit of the Exim Bank and the Export Development Fund.

Chapter VIII provides for the transfer of part of the business of the Development Bank to the Exim Bank.

Chapter IX contains miscellaneous provisions, including the appointment of staff, delegation of powers, returns, obligation as to fidelity and secrecy, defects in appointments, arrangement with the Exim Bank on appointment of directors, indemnity of directors, protection of action taken in good faith, the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891 to apply in relation to the Exim Bank, and the winding up of the Exim Bank.

The First Schedule to the Act contains a declaration of fidelity and secrecy that must be made by every director, member of a committee, auditor, officer, or other employee of the Exim Bank.

The Second Schedule, which contained provisions relating to the transfer of certain assets and liabilities of the Development Bank to the Exim Bank, has been repealed.
