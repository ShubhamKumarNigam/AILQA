Summary:

The Intelligence Organisations (Restriction of Rights) Act, 1985, is a law established in India to restrict certain rights conferred by Part III of the Constitution to members of specific intelligence organizations established by the Central Government for purposes of intelligence or counter-intelligence. The Act aims to ensure the proper discharge of their duties and maintain discipline among them. The Act has five sections and a schedule.

Section 1 of the Act deals with the short title, extent, and commencement. The Act extends to the whole of India and will come into force on a date appointed by the Central Government through a notification in the Official Gazette.

Section 2 defines the terms used in the Act. An Intelligence Organization refers to any organization established by the Central Government for purposes of intelligence or counter-intelligence and specified in the Schedule. A member of an Intelligence Organization means any person appointed to or employed in any Intelligence Organization.

Section 3 outlines the restrictions on the rights of members of an Intelligence Organization. No member of an Intelligence Organization shall be a member of or associated with any trade union, labour union, political association, or any other society, institution, association, or organization that is not recognized by the Central Government as part of the Intelligence Organization of which he is a member or is not of a purely social, recreational, or religious nature. Members are also restricted from communicating with the press or publishing any document without the prior permission of the head of the Intelligence Organization. They are also restricted from contacting or communicating with any person on any matter relating to the functioning, structure, personnel, or organizational affairs of the Intelligence Organization of which he is a member.

Section 4 states that any person who contravenes any of the provisions of section 3 shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years, or with fine which may extend to two thousand rupees, or with both.

Section 5 states that all offences punishable under section 4 shall be cognizable, meaning that the police have the authority to arrest a person without a warrant.

Section 6 provides the Central Government with the power to amend the Schedule by including any other organization established by the Central Government for purposes of intelligence or counter-intelligence or by omitting any organization already specified therein.

Section 7 provides the Central Government with the power to make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act.

The Schedule to the Act specifies the Intelligence Bureau, the Research and Analysis Wing, the Directorate General of Security, and the National Technical Research Organisation as Intelligence Organizations.
