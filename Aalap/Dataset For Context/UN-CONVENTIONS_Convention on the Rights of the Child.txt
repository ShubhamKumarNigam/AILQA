Summary:
The document summarized is the Convention on the Rights of the Child, which is a treaty adopted by the United Nations General Assembly on November 20, 1989. The Convention outlines the civil, political, economic, social, and cultural rights of children, defined as individuals under the age of 18, without discrimination of any kind. It sets forth the best interests of the child as a primary consideration in all actions concerning children and emphasizes the importance of the child's family, community, and culture in their upbringing.
The Convention covers a wide range of topics, including the right to life, survival, and development; the right to identity, name, and nationality; the right to freedom of expression, thought, conscience, and religion; the right to association and peaceful assembly; the right to privacy; and the right to access information and material from a diversity of sources. It also addresses the child's right to education, health, and social security, as well as their right to be protected from economic exploitation, sexual exploitation, and all other forms of violence and abuse.
The Convention establishes a Committee on the Rights of the Child, which is responsible for monitoring the implementation of the Convention by States Parties. States Parties are required to submit regular reports to the Committee on the measures they have adopted to implement the rights recognized in the Convention and on the progress made in the enjoyment of those rights. The Committee may also request further information from States Parties and make suggestions and general recommendations based on the information received.
The Convention has been ratified or acceded to by 196 States Parties, making it one of the most widely ratified human rights treaties in history. It entered into force on September 2, 1990.
