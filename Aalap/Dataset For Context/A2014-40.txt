Summary:

THE NATIONAL JUDICIAL APPOINTMENTS COMMISSION ACT, 2014 (ACT NO. 40 OF 2014) is an Act that outlines the procedure for appointing the Chief Justice of India and other Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts. The Act defines the National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) as the body responsible for making these appointments, and sets forth the procedures for making recommendations for appointments, transfers, and other related matters.

The Act includes several key provisions:

* The NJAC will consist of the Chief Justice of India, two senior-most judges of the Supreme Court, the Union Minister for Law and Justice, and two eminent persons nominated by a committee consisting of the Prime Minister, the Chief Justice of India, and the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha.
* The NJAC will recommend names for appointment as the Chief Justice of India and other Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, and for their transfers.
* The NJAC will take into account the seniority, ability, merit, and any other criteria of suitability as may be specified by regulations.
* The NJAC will not recommend a person for appointment if any two members of the Commission do not agree for such recommendation.
* The President may require the NJAC to reconsider its recommendations.
* The NJAC may specify the procedure for the discharge of its functions.
* The Central Government may make rules and the NJAC may make regulations to carry out the provisions of this Act.

The NJAC Act is a significant piece of legislation that aims to ensure a transparent and merit-based process for appointing the Chief Justice of India and other Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts. It represents a shift towards a more collaborative approach to judicial appointments, involving both the judiciary and the executive.
