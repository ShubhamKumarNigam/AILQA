Summary:
THE LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITIES ACT, 1987 is an Act to constitute legal services authorities to provide free and competent legal services to the weaker sections of the society. It aims to ensure that opportunities for securing justice are not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other disabilities, and to organize Lok Adalats to secure that the operation of the legal system promotes justice on a basis of equal opportunity.

The Act consists of three chapters. Chapter I, titled 'Preliminary', defines key terms used throughout the Act. Chapter II, titled 'The National Legal Services Authority', outlines the composition, functions, and powers of the National Legal Services Authority, including the appointment of a Member-Secretary and other officers and employees. Chapter III, titled 'State Legal Services Authority', establishes a State Legal Services Authority for each state and defines its composition, functions, and powers.

The Act also includes provisions for the establishment of District Legal Services Authorities and Taluk Legal Services Committees, as well as provisions for the organization of Lok Adalats. Lok Adalats are informal, accessible, and inexpensive forums for the resolution of disputes, and are intended to provide an alternative to formal court proceedings.

The Act also sets out the criteria for eligibility for legal services, including income limits, and establishes a National Legal Aid Fund and State Legal Aid Funds to finance the provision of legal services. The Act also includes provisions for the accounts and audit of these funds.

In addition, the Act includes provisions for the protection of action taken in good faith, the power to remove difficulties, and the power of the Central Government to make rules. The Act also includes a schedule outlining the powers and functions of the National Legal Services Authority and the State Legal Services Authorities.
