In summary, the Constitution (Third Amendment) Act, 1954, made significant changes to the Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution. Specifically, it amended Entry 33 of List III to give the central government more power over trade and commerce in certain products, as well as the production, supply, and distribution of those products. This amendment allowed the central government to control the production, supply, and distribution of foodstuffs, including edible oil seeds and oils, cattle fodder, including oilcakes and other concentrates, raw cotton, raw jute, and imported goods of the same kind as the products of any industry where the control of such industry by the Union is declared by Parliament by law to be expedient in the public interest. The amendment was introduced by Article 368 and was ratified by several state legislatures.
