Summary:

The Additional Duties of Excise (Goods of Special Importance) Act, 1957 is a law that levies and collects additional duties of excise on certain goods such as sugar, tobacco, cotton fabrics, man-made fabrics, and woollen fabrics, produced or manufactured in India. These duties are in addition to any other duties of excise chargeable on such goods under any other law. The Act applies to the whole of India, including the Union Territories of Goa, Daman and Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Pondicherry. The net proceeds of the additional duties are distributed among the States in accordance with the principles of distribution formulated and the recommendations made by the Finance Commission in its report dated the 30th day of September, 1957. The rules for the time at which, and the manner in which, any payments under the provisions of this Act are to be made, for the making of adjustments between one financial year and another, and for any other incidental or ancillary matters are made by the Central Government. Every rule made under this section shall be laid before both Houses of Parliament while it is in session for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule, or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so however that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.
