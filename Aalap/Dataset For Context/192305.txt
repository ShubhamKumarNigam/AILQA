Summary:

THE BOILERS ACT, 1923, is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to steam-boilers. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government. In this Act, several definitions are provided, including "boiler," "boiler component," "Chief Inspector," "Deputy Chief Inspector," "Inspector," "owner," "prescribed," "steam pipe," and "structural alteration, addition or renewal."

The Act prohibits the use of unregistered or uncertified boilers, except in certain cases. The owner of a boiler may apply for its registration, and the Inspector may inspect the boiler and grant a certificate of registration. The Chief Inspector may renew the certificate for a specified period at a pressure not exceeding the maximum pressure specified in the certificate.

The Act lays down the duties and powers of the Chief Inspector, Deputy Chief Inspectors, and Inspectors. It also provides for the appointment of a Technical Adviser and Competent Authorities and Persons.

The Act prohibits the use of boilers without a valid certificate or at a pressure higher than the maximum pressure specified in the certificate. It also provides for the punishment of offences under the Act.

The Act empowers the Central Government to make rules to carry out the provisions of the Act. The State Government may also make rules consistent with this Act and the regulations made thereunder.

The Act applies to boilers and boiler components belonging to the Government. The State Government may exempt certain boilers or classes or types of boilers from the operation of the Act.

The Act repealed the enactments mentioned in the Schedule, which was repealed by the Repealing Act, 1927.
