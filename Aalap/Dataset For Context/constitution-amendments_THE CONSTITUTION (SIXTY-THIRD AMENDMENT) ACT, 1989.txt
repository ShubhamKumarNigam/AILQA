THE CONSTITUTION (SIXTY-THIRD AMENDMENT) ACT, 1989 

 Amendment of article 356.-In article 356 of the Constitution, for sub-clause (b) of clause 2, the following sub-clause shall be substituted, namely:-

"(b) that a situation has arisen in which the Government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution."

THE CONSTITUTION (SIXTY-THIRD AMENDMENT) ACT, 1989 

 Repeal of Constitution (Sixty-second Amendment) Act, 1989.-The Constitution (Sixty-second Amendment) Act, 1989 is hereby repealed.


Summary:

The Constitution (Sixty-Third Amendment) Act, 1989 was an amendment to the Constitution of India. It was enacted in the Fortieth Year of the Republic of India. The Act came into force with immediate effect.

The main provision of this Act was the amendment of article 356 of the Constitution. The sub-clause (b) of clause 2 of article 356 was substituted with a new sub-clause, which stated that a situation has arisen in which the Government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution.

Additionally, the Constitution (Sixty-Second Amendment) Act, 1989 was repealed by this Act. The Sixty-Second Amendment Act had granted special powers to the President in regard to the Proclamation of Emergency in Punjab, but these powers were deemed unnecessary and therefore repealed.

In conclusion, the Constitution (Sixty-Third Amendment) Act, 1989 made significant changes to the Constitution of India by amending article 356 and repealing the Constitution (Sixty-Second Amendment) Act, 1989. These changes aimed to ensure that the Government of the State could be carried on in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution.
