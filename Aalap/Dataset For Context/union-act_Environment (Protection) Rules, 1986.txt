Summary:
The Environment (Protection) Rules, 1986, is a set of rules that aim to protect and improve the quality of the environment and prevent and abate environmental pollution. The rules define various terms and specify standards for emission or discharge of environmental pollutants from industries, operations, or processes. The standards are specified in Schedules I to IV and can be made more stringent by the Central Board or a State Board depending on the quality of the recipient system. The rules also specify the procedure for giving directions, prohibition and restriction on the location of industries and the carrying on processes and operations in different areas, procedure for taking samples, service of notice, procedure for submission of samples for analysis, and the form of laboratory report thereon. The rules also outline the functions of environmental laboratories and the qualifications of Government Analyst. The rules also specify the manner of giving notice and the procedure for furnishing information to authorities and agencies in certain cases. The rules also prohibit and restrict the handling of hazardous substances in different areas and require the submission of an environmental statement every year.
