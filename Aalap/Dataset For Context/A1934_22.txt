Summary:
The Aircraft Act, 1934, is a law in India that aims to control the manufacture, possession, use, operation, sale, import, and export of aircraft. The Act extends to the whole of India and applies to citizens of India, aircraft registered in India, and aircraft registered outside India but operating in or over India. The Act also applies to non-Indian citizens who have their principal place of business or permanent residence in India.

The Act includes definitions for terms such as "aircraft," "aerodrome," and "import" and "export." The Central Government has the power to exempt certain aircraft or classes of aircraft and persons or classes of persons from the provisions of the Act. The Central Government may also make rules for implementing the Convention of 1944, which relates to International Civil Aviation.

The Act establishes the Directorate General of Civil Aviation, the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security, and the Aircraft Accidents Investigation Bureau. These organizations are responsible for carrying out safety oversight and regulatory functions, regulatory and oversight functions in respect of matters relating to civil aviation security, and functions in respect of matters relating to investigation of aircraft accidents or incidents. The superintendence of these organizations vests in the Central Government.

The Act also grants the Central Government the power to make rules for regulating the manufacture, possession, use, operation, sale, import, or export of any aircraft or class of aircraft and for securing the safety of aircraft operations. These rules may provide for the licensing, inspection, and regulation of aerodromes, the registration and marking of aircraft, the conditions under which aircraft may be flown, and the fees to be charged in connection with any inspection, examination, test, certificate, or licence. The Act also includes provisions for the investigation of aircraft accidents or incidents and the payment of compensation to persons who sustain loss or damage as a result of any direction contained in a notification issued under the Act.

The Act imposes penalties for contravening the provisions of the Act or the rules made thereunder, including imprisonment and fines. The Act also includes provisions for the composition of offences and the laying of rules before Parliament. The Act includes a saving for acts done in good faith under the Act and bars certain suits. The Act also includes provisions for the use of patented inventions on aircraft not required in India and the bar of certain suits. The Act applies to the naval, military, or air forces of the Union and other armed forces of the Union, as well as to lighthouses to which the Indian Lighthouse Act, 1927 applies. The Act repeals certain previous laws and includes a schedule of provisions that have been repealed.
