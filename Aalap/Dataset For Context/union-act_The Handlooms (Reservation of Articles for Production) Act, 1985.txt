
Summary:

The Handlooms (Reservation of Articles for Production) Act, 1985 is a law in India that aims to protect and develop the handloom industry. The Act empowers the Central Government to reserve certain articles or classes of articles for exclusive production by handlooms. The Act defines various terms such as "handloom," "manufacturer," "powerloom," "processor," and "producer."

The Act establishes an Advisory Committee to determine the nature of any article or classes of articles that may be reserved for exclusive production by handlooms. The Advisory Committee considers various factors such as the article or class of articles being produced by handlooms for mass consumption, the article or class of articles being produced traditionally by handlooms, the level of employment generated by the production of the article or class of articles exclusively by handlooms, and the protection of interests of persons engaged in the handloom industry.

The Act prohibits the production of any article or class of articles reserved for exclusive production by handlooms on any loom other than a handloom. However, any person who was engaged in the production of such article or class of articles in any loom other than a handloom immediately before the date of reservation may continue to do so for a period of three months from the date of such reservation.

The Act empowers the Central Government to require any manufacturer to furnish information or samples for the purposes of this Act. The Act also empowers authorized officers to enter, inspect, and search any place or premises of any manufacturer and require the production of any books of account, registers, records, or other documents.

The Act provides for penalties for contravention of orders made under section 3, including imprisonment, fine, and forfeiture of the article or class of articles or any powerloom used for the production of such article or class of articles. The Act also provides for penalties for making false statements or furnishing false information.

The Act applies to the whole of India and came into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint. The Act also provides for the delegation of powers to officers or authorities subordinate to the Central Government or State Government. The Act also provides for the protection of action taken in good faith and the power to exempt any article or class of articles from the operation of the Act. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
