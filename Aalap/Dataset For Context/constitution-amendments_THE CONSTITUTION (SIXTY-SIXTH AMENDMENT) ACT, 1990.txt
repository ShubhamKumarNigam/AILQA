Summary:

The Constitution (Sixty-Sixth Amendment) Act, 1990, was enacted to amend the Constitution of India and to further ensure that progressive legislation, particularly in the area of land reforms, is not imperiled by litigation. The amendment aimed to include all land reform laws in the Ninth Schedule, thereby immunizing them from legal challenges. This decision was made based on suggestions from various State Governments and the Union territory of Pondicherry.

Additionally, the amendment proposed to include certain amending Acts in the Ninth Schedule to ensure that their implementation is not adversely affected by litigation. The Bill was introduced to achieve the above objectives, and the Act may be called the Constitution (Sixty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1990.
