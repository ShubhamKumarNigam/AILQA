Summary:
The Export (Quality Control and Inspection) Act, 1963, is an Act to provide for the sound development of the export trade of India through quality control and inspection. The Act establishes the Export Inspection Council, which is responsible for advising the Central Government on measures for the enforcement of quality control and inspection in relation to commodities intended for export and drawing up programmes for the same. The Council may also co-opt persons with special knowledge and practical experience in matters relating to any commodity or trade therein and constitute specialist committees for conducting investigations on special problems connected with its functions. The Central Government may notify commodities that are subject to quality control or inspection or both prior to export, specify the type of quality control or inspection to be applied, establish, adopt or recognise one or more standard specifications for a notified commodity, and prohibit the export of a notified commodity unless it is accompanied by a certificate issued under section 7. The Act also empowers the Central Government to establish or recognise agencies for quality control or inspection or both, and sets out the powers and duties of the Director of Inspection and Quality Control. The Act provides for the punishment of offences such as contravention of orders, fraudulent obtaining of certificates, and affixing or applying any such mark or seal as is referred to in sub-section (1) of section 8. The Act also includes provisions for the delegation of powers, procedure for prosecution, protection of action taken in good faith, and the power to make rules.
