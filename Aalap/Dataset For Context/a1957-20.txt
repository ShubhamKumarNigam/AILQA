The Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition and Development) Act, 1957, is an Act of the Parliament of India that aims to establish greater public control over the coal mining industry and its development. The Act does this by providing for the acquisition by the State of unworked land containing or likely to contain coal deposits or of rights in or over such land, and for the extinguishment or modification of such rights accruing by virtue of any agreement, lease, license, or otherwise. The Act extends to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir.

The Act establishes a competent authority, who is appointed by the Central Government, to carry out the provisions of the Act. The competent authority has the power to enter and inspect any property for the purpose of determining whether and, if so, in what manner an order under this Act should be made in relation to any property or with a view to securing compliance with any order made under this Act.

The Act also sets out the procedure for acquiring land or rights in or over land notified under section 4. If the Central Government is satisfied that coal is obtainable in the whole or any part of the land notified under section 4, it may acquire the whole or any part of the land or any rights in or over such land. The acquisition process involves issuing a notification, giving an opportunity for objections, and making a declaration of acquisition.

The Act provides for the payment of compensation for any necessary damage done under section 4 and for the acquisition of land or rights in or over land notified under section 4. The amount of compensation is determined in accordance with the provisions of the Act, and any dispute regarding compensation is settled by a Tribunal.

The Act also includes provisions for appeals, power to obtain information, protection of action taken in good faith, jurisdiction of civil courts, and power to make rules.
