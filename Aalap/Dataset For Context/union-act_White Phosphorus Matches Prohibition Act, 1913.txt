Summary:

The White Phosphorus Matches Prohibition Act was enacted in 1913 and applies to the entire country of India, with the exception of territories that were previously part of Part B States. The Act prohibits the use of white phosphorus in the manufacture of matches and imposes a fine of up to 200 rupees for any violation of this provision. The Act also empowers Inspectors of Factories to take samples of materials used in the manufacture of matches for analysis. The Act further prohibits the sale, offering, or exposing for sale, or possession of matches made with white phosphorus. Any matches found in violation of this provision may be forfeited and destroyed or otherwise dealt with as directed by a Magistrate.
