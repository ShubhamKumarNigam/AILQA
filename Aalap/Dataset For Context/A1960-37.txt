Summary:

The Banking Companies (Second Amendment) Act, 1960, is an Act that further amends the Banking Companies Act, 1949. This Act, which received the President's assent on September 19, 1960, includes several sections that replace or amend certain sections of the 1949 Act.

The Act begins by stating its short title, which is the Banking Companies (Second Amendment) Act, 1960. Section 2 repeals and replaces section 39 of the 1949 Act. However, this amendment was later repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act (52 of 1964) with effect from December 29, 1964.

Similarly, sections 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 of the 1960 Act replace and amend sections 41, 43A, 44A, and 45L of the 1949 Act. These amendments were also later repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act (52 of 1964) with effect from December 29, 1964.

Section 8 of the 1960 Act amends section 50 of the 1949 Act, and section 9 amends section 51 of the 1949 Act. These amendments were also later repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act (52 of 1964) with effect from December 29, 1964.

Section 10 of the 1960 Act states that the amendments made in sections 3 and 4 of the 1960 Act shall not apply to, and in relation to, the winding-up of a banking company where any preliminary dividend has been paid in the course of such winding-up before the commencement of this Act. Instead, the provisions of the principal Act as they stood immediately before such commencement shall apply to, and in relation to, such winding-up.

In summary, the Banking Companies (Second Amendment) Act, 1960, made several amendments to the Banking Companies Act, 1949. However, these amendments were later repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act (52 of 1964) with effect from December 29, 1964. Section 10 of the 1960 Act provides an exception to this repeal for certain winding-up proceedings.
