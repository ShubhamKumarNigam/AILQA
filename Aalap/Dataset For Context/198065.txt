Summary:

THE NATIONAL SECURITY ACT, 1980 is an Act to provide for preventive detention in certain cases and for matters connected therewith. The Act applies to the whole of India and extends to foreigners. The Act defines key terms such as "appropriate Government," "detention order," "foreigner," "person," and "State Government."

Under Section 3, the Central Government or the State Government may make a detention order for preventive detention if they are satisfied that it is necessary to prevent a person from acting in any manner prejudicial to the defense of India, the relations of India with foreign powers, or the security of India, or to regulate a foreigner's continued presence in India or make arrangements for their expulsion from India. The Act also empowers the State Government to delegate its powers under Section 3 to a District Magistrate or Commissioner of Police in certain circumstances.

Section 4 provides for the execution of detention orders, while Section 5 regulates the place and conditions of detention. The Act also includes provisions for the grounds of detention to be severable (Section 5A) and for detention orders not to be invalid or inoperative on certain grounds (Section 6).

Section 7 deals with powers in relation to absconding persons, while Section 8 requires the authority making the detention order to communicate the grounds on which the order has been made to the person detained and afford him the earliest opportunity of making a representation against the order to the appropriate Government.

Section 9 provides for the constitution of Advisory Boards, while Section 10 requires the appropriate Government to place before the Advisory Board the grounds on which the order has been made and the representation, if any, made by the person affected by the order. Section 11 outlines the procedure of Advisory Boards, while Section 12 deals with action upon the report of the Advisory Board.

Section 13 provides for the maximum period of detention, while Section 14 deals with the revocation of detention orders. The Act also includes provisions for the circumstances in which persons may be detained for periods longer than three months without obtaining the opinion of Advisory Boards (Section 14A).

Section 15 allows for the temporary release of persons detained, while Section 16 provides protection of action taken in good faith. Section 17 states that the Act does not apply or have any effect with respect to detentions under State laws, and Section 18 deals with the repeal and saving. The Act includes a schedule, annexure, and appendix, as well as forms and footnotes.
