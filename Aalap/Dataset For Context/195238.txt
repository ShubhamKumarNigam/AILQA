Summary:
The Delhi and Ajmer Rent Control Act, 1952 is an Act to provide for the control of rents and evictions, and for the lease of vacant premises to Government, in certain areas in the States of Delhi and Ajmer. The Act has 5 chapters and 46 sections, along with a First Schedule and a Second Schedule.

Chapter I: Preliminary

* Short title, extent and commencement: The Act may be called the Delhi and Ajmer Rent Control Act, 1952. It extends to the areas specified in the First Schedule and may be extended by the Central Government to other areas in the State of Delhi or Ajmer. It came into force on such date as the Central Government may appoint.
* Definitions: The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as fair rate, hotel or lodging house, landlord, lawful increase, manager of a hotel, owner of a lodging house, premises, prescribed, standard rent, and tenant.
* Act not to apply to certain premises: The Act does not apply to any premises belonging to the Government or to any tenancy or other like relationship created by a grant from the Government in respect of the premises taken on lease, or requisitioned, by the Government.

Chapter II: Standard Rent and Provisions Relating to Other Charges by the Landlord

* Rent in excess of standard rent not recoverable: A tenant is not liable to pay to his landlord for the occupation of any premises any amount in excess of the standard rent of the premises, unless such amount is a lawful increase of the standard rent in accordance with the provisions of this Act.
* Unlawful charges not to be claimed or received: No person shall claim or receive any rent in excess of the standard rent, notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary. No person shall claim or receive the payment of any premium, pugree, fine, advance or any other like sum in addition to the rent.
* Lawful increases of standard rent: A landlord may lawfully increase the standard rent per year by an amount not exceeding seven and a half per cent. of such cost, if he has incurred expenditure for any improvement, addition or structural alteration in the premises.
* Notice of increase of, or addition to, rent: A landlord shall give the tenant notice of his intention to make the increase, and the increase shall be due and recoverable only in respect of the period of the tenancy after the end of the month in which the notice is given.
* Cases in which standard rent may be fixed by court: The court may fix the standard rent at such an amount as, having regard to the provisions of this Act and the circumstances of the case, it deems just, if a dispute arises between a landlord and the tenant regarding the amount of standard rent payable in respect of any premises.
* Fixation of interim rent by the court: The court shall, as expeditiously as possible, make an order specifying the amount of the rent or the lawful increase to be paid by the tenant to the landlord pending the final decision of the application.
* Limitation of liability of middleman: A collector of rents or middleman shall not be liable to pay to his principal any sum by way of rental charges which exceeds the amount which he is entitled under this Act to realize from the tenant or tenants of the premises.
* Limitation for applications for fixation of standard rent: A landlord or tenant may file an application to the court for fixing the standard rent of the premises or for determining the lawful increase of such rent within six months from the date of the cause of action.

Chapter III: Control of Eviction of Tenants

* Protection of a tenant against eviction: The court shall not pass any decree or order for the recovery of possession of any premises in favor of the landlord against any tenant, except in certain circumstances.
* Recovery of possession for occupation and re-entry: A landlord may recover possession of any premises from the tenant by virtue of any decree or order made on the grounds specified in clause (e) of the proviso to sub-section (1) of section 13.
* Recovery of possession for repairs and re-building and re-entry: The court shall ascertain from the tenant whether he elects to be placed in occupation of the premises or part thereof from which he is to be evicted and if the tenant so elects, the court shall record the fact of the election in the decree or order and specify therein the date on or before which he shall deliver possession.
* Recovery of possession in case of tenancies for limited period: The court may place a landlord in vacant possession of the premises or part thereof by evicting the tenant and every other person who may be in occupation of such premises, if the tenant does not vacate the premises on the expiry of the said period.
* Special provision for recovery of possession in certain cases: The court may recover possession of any premises in certain circumstances, such as where the landlord in respect of any premises is a company or other body corporate or any local authority or any public institution and the premises are required for the use of employees of such landlord.
* Permission to construct additional structures: The court may permit the landlord to make any improvement or construct any additional structure on the building if the tenant refuses to allow the landlord to make such improvement or construct such additional structure.
* Special provision regarding vacant building sites: The provisions of this section shall apply notwithstanding anything contained in section 13, but only in relation to premises in such areas as the Central Government may specify.

Chapter IV: Hotels and Lodging Houses

* Application of this Chapter: The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to all hotels and lodging houses within the Municipalities of New Delhi and Delhi and the Notified Area of the Civil Station, Delhi and may be applied by the Central Government to such other areas in the State of Delhi or Ajmer as may be specified in the notification.
* Appointment of Controller: The Central Government may appoint any person to be a Controller for the purpose of performing the functions assigned to him by this Chapter.
* Fixing of fair rate: The Controller may fix a fair rate to be charged for board, lodging or other services provided in the hotel or lodging house and in fixing such fair rate, specify separately the rate for lodging, board or other services.
* Revision of fair rate: The Controller may revise the fair rate to be charged for board, lodging or other service, and fix such rate as he may deem fit having regard to any general rise or fall in the cost of living which may have occurred after the fixing of the fair rate.
* Charges in excess of fair rate not recoverable: The manager of the hotel or the owner of the lodging house shall not charge any amount in excess of the fair rate and shall not, except with the previous written consent of the Controller, withdraw from the lodgers any concession or service allowed at the time when the Controller determined the fair rate.
* Provisions relating to inquiries by Controller: The Controller may require the manager of a hotel or the owner of a lodging house to produce before him any books of account, documents or other information relating to the hotel or lodging house concerned which he may consider necessary and may himself enter upon any premises to which the inquire relates.
* Recovery of possession by manager of a hotel or the owner of a lodging house: The manager of a hotel or owner of a lodging house shall be entitled to recover possession of the accommodation provided by him on obtaining a certificate from the Controller certifying that the lodger has been guilty of conduct which is a nuisance or which causes annoyance to any adjoining or neighbouring lodger or that the accommodation is reasonably and bona fide required by the owner of the hotel or lodging house for his own occupation or for the occupation of any person for whose benefit the accommodation is held or any other cause which may be deemed satisfactory by the Controller.
* Appeals: Any person aggrieved by the order of the Controller under this Chapter may, within fifteen days on which the order is communicated to him, prefer an appeal in writing to the Chief Commissioner.
* Penalty: Any manager of a hotel or owner of a lodging house who fails or refuses to produce before the Controller any books of account or document or other information which the Controller may require him to produce under sub-section (3) of section 27 or charges any amount in excess of the fair rate in contravention of section 26 shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years or with fine or with both.
* Controller to be deemed to be public servant: A controller appointed under this Act shall be deemed to be a public servant within the meaning of section 21 of the Indian Penal Code (Act XLV of 1860).
* Protection of action taken under this Chapter: No suit, prosecution or other legal proceeding shall lie against a Controller in respect of anything which is in good faith done or intended to be done under this Chapter.

Chapter V: Jurisdiction of Courts, Appeals, Review and Revision

* Jurisdiction of courts: Any civil court in the State of Delhi or Ajmer which has jurisdiction to hear and decide a suit for recovery of possession of any premises shall have jurisdiction to hear and decide any case under this Act relating to such premises if it has pecuniary jurisdiction and is otherwise competent to hear and decide such a case under any law for the time being in force.
* Appeals: Any person aggrieved by any decree or order of a court passed under this Act may prefer an appeal in such manner as may be prescribed to the court of the senior subordinate judge, if the value of the case does not exceed two thousand rupees, or to the court of the district judge, if the value of the case exceeds two thousand rupees but does not exceed ten thousand rupees, or to the High Court, if the value of the case exceeds ten thousand rupees.
* Revision and review: The High Court may satisfy itself that a decision made in any case under this Act is according to law and may pass such order in relation thereto as it thinks fit. Any court may review its own order.
* Limitation: An appeal shall lie to any court other than the High Court within thirty days from the date of the decree or order and to the High Court within sixty days from the date of the decree or order.
* Procedure before courts: The court may hold a summary inquiry into any case under this Act and the practice and procedure of a court of small causes shall apply to such cases as if they were suits and other proceedings cognizable by a court of small causes.

Chapter VI: Miscellaneous

* Act to over-ride other laws: The provisions of this Act and of the rules made thereunder shall have effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith contained in any other law for the time being in force or in any instrument having effect by virtue of any such law.
* Exemption of certain premises from the operation of the Act: All premises, the construction of which is completed after the 1st day of June, 1951, but before the expiry of three years from the commencement of this Act, shall be exempt from the operation of all the provisions of this Act for a period of seven years from the date of such completion.
* Landlord’s duty to keep the premises in good repair: The landlord shall be bound to keep the premises in good and tenantable repair, notwithstanding anything contained in any law for the time being in force and in the absence of agreement to the contrary by the tenant.
* Cutting off or withholding essential supply or service: No landlord shall, without just or sufficient cause, cut off or withhold any essential supply or service enjoyed by the tenant in respect of the premises let to him.
* Landlord’s duty to give notice of new constructions to Government: The landlord shall, within thirty days of the completion of any premises, give intimation thereof in writing to the Estate Officer to the Government of India or to such other officer as may be specified in this behalf by the Government.
* Leases of vacant premises to Government: The provisions of this section shall apply only in relation to premises within the Municipality of New Delhi which are, or are intended to be let for use as a residence.
* Penalties: If any person receives any payment in contravention of the provisions of section 5, he shall be punishable with simple imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months, or with fine which may extend to an amount exceeding one thousand rupees by the amount of unlawful charges so received by him, or with both.
* Power to make rules: The Central Government may make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act.
* Repeals and savings: The Delhi and Ajmer-Merwara Rent Control Act, 1947 is hereby repealed. Notwithstanding such repeal, all suits and other proceedings pending at the commencement of this Act, whether before any court or the Rent Controller appointed under the Fourth Schedule to the said Act, shall be disposed of in accordance with the provisions of the said Act as if the said Act had continued in force and this Act had not been passed. Part IV of the Bombay Rents, Hotel Rates and Lodging House Rates (Control) Act, 1944 as extended to the Municipality of New Delhi, the Notified Area of the Civil Station, Delhi and the Municipality of Delhi by a notification of the Government of India in the late Department of Works, Mines and Power No.1884-W.II/47, dated the 18th March, 1947 shall cease to have effect in the said areas.

The First Schedule:

* The First Schedule lists the areas to which the Act extends. It includes the Municipality of Delhi, the Municipality of New Delhi, the Cantonment of Delhi, the Notified Area of the Civil Station, Delhi, the Municipality of Shahdara, the Notified Area, Red Fort, and the West Notified Area, Delhi in the State of Delhi, and the Municipality of Ajmer and all land within one mile of the limits of that Municipality, the Municipality of Beawar and all land within one mile of the limits of that Municipality, and the Cantonment of Nasirabad and all land within one mile of the limits of that Cantonment in the State of Ajmer.

The Second Schedule:

* The Second Schedule provides for the determination of the standard rent of premises. It has two parts, Part A for the State of Delhi and Part B for the State of Ajmer.
* Part A of the Second Schedule provides for the determination of the standard rent of premises in the State of Delhi. It defines basic rent and provides for the calculation of the standard rent based on the basic rent.
* Part B of the Second Schedule provides for the determination of the standard rent of premises in the State of Ajmer. It defines basic rent and provides for the calculation of the standard rent based on the basic rent.
