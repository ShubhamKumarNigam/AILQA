Summary:

THE PUBLIC GAMBLING ACT, 1867 is an Act to provide for the punishment of public gambling and the keeping of common gaming-houses in the United Provinces, East Punjab, Delhi, and the Central Provinces. The Act defines a common gaming-house as any house, walled enclosure, room, or place in which cards, dice, tables, or other instruments of gaming are kept or used for the profit or gain of the person owning, occupying, using, or keeping such house, enclosure, room, or place. The Act empowers the State Government to extend the application of certain sections of the Act to any city, town, suburb, railway-station, house, or place within a specified distance from a railway station-house within the States. The Act imposes penalties for owning or keeping, or having charge of, a gaming-house, and for being found in a gaming-house. The Act also authorizes the entry and search of suspected gaming-houses by Magistrates or officers of police. The possession of instruments of gaming found in a suspected gaming-house is considered evidence of the use of the premises as a common gaming-house. The Act exempts certain games of skill from its provisions. The Act also prohibits gaming and setting birds and animals to fight in public streets and authorizes the seizure and destruction of instruments of gaming found in public places. The Act provides for the trial of offences by any Magistrate having jurisdiction in the place where the offence is committed. The Act imposes enhanced penalties for subsequent offences. The Act allows for the recovery and application of fines imposed under the Act, and provides for the payment of a portion of the fine to an informer. The Act also repealed the provision that made offences under this Act to be "offences" within the meaning of the Penal Code. The Act has been amended and extended to various parts of India over the years.
