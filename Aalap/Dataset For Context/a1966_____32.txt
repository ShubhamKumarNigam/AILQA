Summary:
The Beedi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966, is a law that aims to provide for the welfare of workers in beedi and cigar establishments and regulate their working conditions. The Act applies to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and came into force on a date specified by each State Government.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "adult," "child," "competent authority," "contractor," "contract labour," "employee," "employer," "establishment," "godown," "industrial premises," "Inspector," "manufacturing process," "prescribed," "principal employer," "private dwelling house," "State Government," "week," and "young person."

The Act requires that industrial premises be licensed, and a licence is granted by the competent authority based on certain criteria. The licence is valid for one financial year and can be renewed annually. The competent authority may cancel or suspend a licence if it is obtained by misrepresentation or fraud or if the licensee has contravened or failed to comply with any of the provisions of the Act or the rules made thereunder.

The Act sets out the powers and duties of Inspectors appointed under the Act. They have the power to enter industrial premises, make examinations, hold inquiries, and require the production of any prescribed register or other document. The Act also provides that an Inspector shall not disclose the source of any complaint made to him regarding the contravention of any of the provisions of this Act.

The Act sets out various provisions regarding cleanliness, ventilation, overcrowding, drinking water, latrines and urinals, washing facilities, and creches. The Act also requires employers to provide first aid facilities and canteens in certain establishments.

The Act regulates the working hours of employees, providing for a maximum of nine hours of work per day and forty-eight hours per week. Employees are entitled to overtime wages if they work in excess of these limits. The Act also provides for weekly holidays and paid annual leave with wages.

The Act prohibits the employment of children and women or young persons during certain hours. It also requires employers to maintain records of periods of work and display them in a conspicuous place in the industrial premises.

The Act provides for penalties for obstructing Inspectors, contravening any of the provisions of the Act or the rules made thereunder, and failing to pay wages or compensation in accordance with any order of the appellate authority. The Act also provides for offences by companies and indemnity for good faith actions taken under the Act.

The Act applies to industrial premises and not to shops or commercial establishments. The Act also provides for the application of the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946, and the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, to every establishment. The Act applies notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith contained in any other law for the time being in force or in the terms of any award, agreement, or contract of service. The Act also empowers the State Government to exempt any class of industrial premises or class of employers or employees from all or any of the provisions of this Act or of any rules made thereunder. The Act also provides for the power of the Central Government to give directions to a State Government as to the carrying into execution of the provisions of this Act.

The Act does not apply to the owner or occupier of a private dwelling house who carries on any manufacturing process in such private dwelling house with the assistance of the members of his family living with him in such dwelling house and dependent on him. The Act also empowers the State Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
