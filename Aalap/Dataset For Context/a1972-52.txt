The Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972, is a law in India that aims to regulate the export trade in antiquities and art treasures, prevent smuggling and fraudulent dealings in antiquities, and provide for the compulsory acquisition of antiquities and art treasures for preservation in public places. The Act defines antiquities as any coin, sculpture, painting, epigraph, or other work of art or craftsmanship, any article, object, or thing detached from a building or cave, any article, object, or thing illustrative of science, art, crafts, literature, religion, customs, morals, or politics in bygone ages, any article, object, or thing of historical interest, or any manuscript, record, or other document which is of scientific, historical, literary, or aesthetic value and has been in existence for not less than seventy-five years. The Act also defines art treasures as any human work of art, not being an antiquity, declared by the Central Government to be an art treasure for the purposes of this Act, having regard to its artistic or aesthetic value.

The Act prohibits the export of any antiquity or art treasure by any person other than the Central Government or any authority or agency authorized by the Central Government. Any export of antiquities or art treasures must be made only under and in accordance with the terms and conditions of a permit issued for the purpose by an authority as may be prescribed. The Customs Act, 1962, shall have effect in relation to all antiquities and art treasures, the export of which by any person (other than the Central Government or any authority or agency authorized by the Central Government) is prohibited under section 3, save insofar as that Act is inconsistent with the provisions of this Act.

The Act also regulates the sale of antiquities by requiring any person desiring to carry on the business of selling or offering to sell any antiquity to obtain a license under section 8. The Act specifies the factors to be considered while granting a license, including the experience of the applicant with respect to trade in antiquities, the village, town, or city where the applicant intends to carry on business, the number of persons already engaged in the business of selling, or offering for sale of, antiquities in the said village, town, or city, and such other factors as may be prescribed. The Act also specifies the conditions and form in which the license may be granted.

The Act provides for the registration of antiquities and specifies the antiquities that shall be registered under this Act. The Act also specifies the factors to be considered while registering an antiquity, including the necessity for conserving the objects of art, the need to preserve such objects within India for the better appreciation of the cultural heritage of India, and such other factors as will, or are likely to, contribute to the safeguarding of the cultural heritage of India.

The Act also empowers the Central Government to compulsorily acquire any antiquity or art treasure in a public place if it is of the opinion that it is desirable to preserve such antiquity or art treasure in a public place. The Act specifies the procedure for the compulsory acquisition of antiquities and art treasures, including the payment of compensation to the owner.

The Act provides for penalties for the contravention of its provisions, including imprisonment and fines. The Act also provides for the protection of action taken in good faith and the application of other laws not barred.
