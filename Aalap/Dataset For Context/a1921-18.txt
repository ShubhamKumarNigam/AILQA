Summary:
The Maintenance Orders Enforcement Act, 1921, is an act that facilitates the enforcement of maintenance orders made in reciprocating territories in India and vice versa. The act applies to the whole of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The act defines various terms such as "Court of summary jurisdiction," "dependants," "India," "maintenance order," "prescribed," "proper authority," and "reciprocating territory."

Section 3 of the act allows the Central Government to declare that the act applies in respect of any country or territory outside India if legal provision exists in that country or territory for the enforcement of maintenance orders made by courts in India.

Section 4 of the act deals with the registration of maintenance orders made in the reciprocating territories in India. The section states that where a maintenance order has been made against any person by any court in any reciprocating territory, and a certified copy of the order has been transmitted by the proper authority of that territory to the Central Government, the Central Government shall send a copy of the order to the prescribed officer of a court in India for registration.

Section 5 deals with the transmission of maintenance orders made in India to the reciprocating territories. The section states that where a court in India has made a maintenance order against any person, and it is proved that the person against whom the order was made is resident in a reciprocating territory, the court shall send to the Central Government, for transmission to the proper authority of that territory, a certified copy of the order.

Section 6 of the act empowers summary courts in India to make provisional maintenance orders against persons resident in reciprocating territories. The section states that where application is made to a court of summary jurisdiction in India for a maintenance order against any person, and it is proved that that person is resident in a reciprocating territory, the court may, in the absence of that person, if after hearing the evidence it is satisfied of the justice of the application, make any such order as it might have made if that person had wilfully neglected to attend the court. However, the order shall be provisional only and shall have no effect unless and until confirmed by a competent court in such territory.

Section 7 deals with the powers of a court of summary jurisdiction to confirm maintenance orders made out of India. The section states that where a maintenance order has been made by a court in a reciprocating territory and the order is provisional only, and has no effect unless and until confirmed by a court of summary jurisdiction in India, and a certified copy of the order, together with the depositions of the witnesses and a statement of the grounds on which the order might have been opposed, has been transmitted to the Central Government, the Central Government may send the said documents to the prescribed officer of a court of summary jurisdiction, with a requisition that a summons be issued calling upon the person to show cause why that order should not be confirmed.

Section 8 deals with the enforcement of maintenance orders. The section states that where an order has been registered under this Act in a High Court, the order shall, from the date of such registration, be of the same force and effect, and all proceedings may be taken thereon as if it had been an order originally obtained in the High Court in the exercise of its civil jurisdiction.

Section 9 deals with the payment of charges for the transmission of sums awarded as maintenance and other costs and charges. The section states that a court in registering or confirming an order for maintenance in accordance with the provisions of this Act shall direct that the charges for the transmission of the sum awarded as maintenance shall be borne by the person against whom the order has been so made or confirmed and shall be recovered from him in addition to the sum awarded as maintenance and in addition to, and in the same manner as, such other costs and charges as may be awarded or levied by the court.

Section 10 deals with the proof of documents signed by officers of a court. The section states that for the purposes of this Act, any document purporting to be signed by a judge or officer of a court outside India shall, until the contrary is proved, be deemed to have been so signed without proof of the signature of judicial or official character of the person appearing to have signed it.

Section 11 deals with depositions to be evidence. The section states that depositions taken in a court in any reciprocating territory may, for the purposes of this Act, be received in evidence in proceedings before courts of summary jurisdiction under this Act.

Section 12 deals with rule-making power. The section states that the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules for the purpose of carrying into effect the purposes of this Act.

The act also includes a schedule that lists the countries or territories outside India in respect of which the act applies by virtue of a declaration under section 3. The document also includes various amendments and notifications related to the act.
