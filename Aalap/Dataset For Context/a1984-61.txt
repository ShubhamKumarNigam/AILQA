Summary:
The Terrorist Affected Areas (Special Courts) Act, 1984 was enacted to provide for the speedy trial of certain offences in terrorist affected areas. The Act applies to the whole of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and it came into force on 14th July, 1984. The Act defines various terms such as "Code", "High Court", "judicial zone", "notification", "Public Prosecutor", "scheduled offence", "Special Court", "terrorist", and "terrorist affected area". The Act empowers the Central Government to declare any area as a terrorist affected area and constitute it into a single judicial zone or into as many judicial zones as it may deem fit. The Act also enables the Central Government to establish a Special Court or an Additional Special Court in relation to a judicial zone. The Special Court shall be presided over by a judge appointed by the Central Government with the concurrence of the Chief Justice of the High Court. The Act empowers the Special Court to try scheduled offences committed in a judicial zone in a State at any time during the period during which such judicial zone is, or is part of, a terrorist affected area. The Act also provides for the protection of witnesses and the power to transfer cases to regular courts. An appeal shall lie as a matter of right from any judgment, sentence, or order, not being interlocutory order, of a Special Court to the Supreme Court both on facts and on law. The Act also modifies the application of certain provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, and imposes limitations on granting of bail to persons accused of scheduled offences. The Act also abolishes certain Special Courts where the area comprising a judicial zone has ceased to be a terrorist affected area and no cases are pending before a Special Court or an Additional Special Court established in relation to such judicial zone. The Act shall have effect notwithstanding anything contained in the Code or any other law, but save as expressly provided in this Act, the provisions of the Code shall, in so far as they are not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, apply to the proceedings before a Special Court. The Central Government may, by notification, delegate, subject to such conditions as may be specified, all or any of the powers exercisable by it under this Act to the State Government. The Supreme Court may, by notification, make such rules, if any, as it may deem necessary for carrying out the purposes of this Act. The Act also amends the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, by inserting a new section 111A which provides for the presumption as to certain offences. The Terrorist Affected Areas (Special Courts) Ordinance, 1984 is repealed, and anything done or any action taken under the said Ordinance shall be deemed to have been done or taken under the corresponding provisions of this Act.
