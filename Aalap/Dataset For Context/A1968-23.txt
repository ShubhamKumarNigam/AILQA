Summary:
The Public Provident Fund Act, 1968, establishes a provident fund for the general public. The Act defines key terms such as "Fund," "minor," "Scheme," "subscriber," and "year." The Act allows any individual to subscribe to the Fund on their own behalf or on behalf of a minor, with specified maximum and minimum limits. The interest rate on subscriptions is notified by the Central Government and calculated in the manner specified in the Scheme. Subscribers can make withdrawals from the Fund after five years, and the entire balance can be withdrawn after fifteen years. Loans can be granted to subscribers under specified terms and conditions. On the death of a subscriber, the amount standing to their credit in the Fund is payable to the nominee or legal heirs. The Fund is protected from attachment under any decree or order of any court. No legal proceedings can be initiated against any person for anything done in good faith under this Act or the Scheme. The Central Government can remove difficulties in implementing the provisions of this Act or the Scheme. The Scheme must be laid before both Houses of Parliament and may be modified or annulled by them. The Schedule to the Act outlines the matters that may be provided for in the Scheme.
