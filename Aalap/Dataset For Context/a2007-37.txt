Summary:
The Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007 is an Act to make provisions for the development and regulation of warehouses, negotiability of warehouse receipts, establishment of a Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

The Act consists of eleven chapters. Chapter I, titled 'Preliminary', defines various terms used in the Act. Chapter II, titled 'Regulation of Warehousing Business', deals with the requirement of registration for warehouses issuing negotiable warehouse receipts and the registration of warehousemen. Chapter III, titled 'Warehousemen', outlines the liabilities and duties of warehousemen. Chapter IV, titled 'Warehouse Receipts', defines warehouse receipts and their negotiability. Chapter V, titled 'The Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority', establishes the Authority and sets out its composition, tenure of office of Chairperson and other members, removal from office, salaries, allowances and other terms and conditions of Chairperson and other members, bar on future employment of members, and powers and functions of the Authority. Chapter VI, titled 'Finance, Accounts and Audit', deals with the grants by the Central Government, constitution of fund, accounts and audit, furnishing of returns, annual report, etc., to the Central Government. Chapter VII, titled 'Powers of Central Government', grants the Central Government the power to issue directions and supersede the Authority. Chapter VIII, titled 'Appeals', allows for appeals to the Appellate Authority. Chapter IX, titled 'Offences and Penalties', outlines the offences and penalties for violating the provisions of the Act. Chapter X, titled 'Offences by Companies', holds the company and its officers liable for offences committed by the company. Chapter XI, titled 'Miscellaneous', includes provisions for the Chairperson, members, officers and other employees of the Authority and the Appellate Authority to be public servants, protection of action taken in good faith, delegation of powers, exemption from tax on wealth and income, power of Central Government to make rules, power of Authority to make regulations, rules and regulations to be laid before Parliament, act to have overriding effect, power to remove difficulties, amendment of Act 2 of 1899, and repeal and savings.

The Act aims to provide a legal framework for the development and regulation of warehouses, negotiability of warehouse receipts, and the establishment of a Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority. It sets out the liabilities and duties of warehousemen, defines warehouse receipts and their negotiability, and establishes the Authority to regulate the warehousing business. The Act also provides for offences and penalties for violating its provisions, and allows for appeals to the Appellate Authority.
