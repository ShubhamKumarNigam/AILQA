Summary:
The National Jute Board Act, 2008 is an Act of the Indian Parliament that aims to promote the development of jute and jute products by establishing a National Jute Board. The Board is responsible for promoting the development of jute cultivation, manufacture, and marketing through various measures such as evolving an integrated approach to jute cultivation, promoting production of better quality raw jute, enhancing productivity of raw jute, promoting standardisation of raw jute and jute products, and assisting and encouraging studies and research for improvement of processing, quality, techniques of grading and packaging of raw jute.

The Act provides for the constitution and incorporation of the Board, which shall consist of members from various backgrounds including government officials, jute farmers, jute workers, jute industry representatives, jute exporters, and experts from the field of jute technology and related fields. The term of office of the members, other than the ex-officio members, and the manner of filling vacancies among, and the procedure to be followed in the discharge of their functions by, such members shall be as may be prescribed.

The Act also provides for the transfer and vesting of the Jute Manufactures Development Council and the National Centre for Jute Diversification in the Board. The transfer includes all assets, rights, powers, authorities, and privileges, and all property movable and immovable, real or personal, corporeal or incorporeal, present or contingent, of whatever nature and wheresoever situate, including lands, buildings, machinery, equipments, cash balances, capital, reserves, reserve funds, investments, tenancies, leases, and book debts and all other rights and interests arising out of such property as were immediately before the appointed day in the ownership or possession or power of the Council or the Society.

The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules and regulations to carry out the purposes of this Act. The rules and regulations made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. The Act also provides for the removal of difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of this Act.

In conclusion, the National Jute Board Act, 2008 is an important legislation for the development of the jute sector in India. The Act establishes a National Jute Board to promote the development of jute cultivation, manufacture, and marketing, and provides for the transfer and vesting of the Jute Manufactures Development Council and the National Centre for Jute Diversification in the Board. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules and regulations to carry out the purposes of this Act and provides for the removal of difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of this Act.
