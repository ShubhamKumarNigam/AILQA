Summary of the Prisons Act, 1894:

The Prisons Act, 1894, is an Act that amends the law relating to prisons in India, except for the territories that were formerly part of Part B States. The Act aims to provide rules for the regulation of prisons and ensure the safe custody and rehabilitation of prisoners.

The Act begins by defining key terms, such as "prison," "criminal prisoner," "convicted criminal prisoner," "civil prisoner," "remission system," "history-ticket," "Inspector General," "Medical Subordinate," and "prohibited article."

Chapter II of the Act deals with the maintenance and officers of prisons. It establishes the position of the Inspector General, who is responsible for the general control and superintendence of all prisons in the territories subject to each State Government. The Act also specifies the officers required for every prison, including a Superintendent, Medical Officer, Medical Subordinate, Jailer, and other officers as necessary.

Chapter III outlines the duties of officers of prisons. It specifies the responsibilities of the Superintendent, Medical Officer, Jailer, and other subordinate officers. For example, the Superintendent is responsible for managing the prison in all matters relating to discipline, labor, expenditure, punishment, and control. The Medical Officer is responsible for the sanitary administration of the prison and performing duties prescribed by rules made by the State Government.

Chapter IV deals with the admission, removal, and discharge of prisoners. It specifies that prisoners shall be examined on admission, and all weapons and prohibited articles shall be taken from them. It also specifies that no prisoner shall be removed or discharged from prison without a medical certificate stating that the prisoner is free from any illness rendering him unfit for removal or discharge.

Chapter V outlines the separation of prisoners and specifies that female prisoners shall be imprisoned in separate buildings or separate parts of the same building, and male prisoners under the age of 21 shall be separated from other prisoners. Unconvicted criminal prisoners shall also be kept apart from convicted criminal prisoners, and civil prisoners shall be kept apart from criminal prisoners.

Chapter VI deals with the food, clothing, and bedding of civil and unconvicted criminal prisoners. It specifies that civil prisoners and unconvicted criminal prisoners shall be supplied with sufficient clothing and bedding if they are unable to provide it themselves.

Chapter VII deals with the employment of prisoners. It specifies that civil prisoners may work and follow any trade or profession, while criminal prisoners shall be employed on labor or other work as determined by the Superintendent.

Chapter VIII deals with the health of prisoners and specifies that sick prisoners shall be attended to by the Medical Officer or Medical Subordinate. It also specifies that all directions given by the Medical Officer or Medical Subordinate in relation to any prisoner shall be entered day by day in the prisoner's history-ticket or in such other record as the State Government may direct.

Chapter IX deals with visits to prisoners and specifies that due provision shall be made for the admission, at proper times and under proper restrictions, into every prison of persons with whom civil or unconvicted criminal prisoners may desire to communicate.

Chapter X deals with offences in relation to prisoners and specifies penalties for introducing or removing prohibited articles into or from prison and communicating with prisoners.

Chapter XI deals with prison-offences and specifies the acts that constitute prison-offences, such as disobedience to prison regulations, assault, use of criminal force, and damage to prison property.

Chapter XII deals with miscellaneous provisions and specifies that prisoners shall be deemed to be in prison when being taken to or from any prison in which they may be lawfully confined, or whenever they are working outside or are otherwise beyond the limits of any such prison in or under the lawful custody or control of a prison-officer belonging to such person.

The Act also empowers the State Government to make rules consistent with the Act for the regulation of prisons, such as defining the acts which shall constitute prison-offences, determining the classification of prison-offences into serious and minor offences, fixing the punishments admissible under this Act which shall be awardable for commission of prison-offences or classes thereof, and regulating the use of arms against any prisoner or body of prisoners in the case of an outbreak or attempt to escape.

The Schedule to the Act, which has been repealed, contained a list of enactments repealed by the Act.
