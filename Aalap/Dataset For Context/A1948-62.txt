Summary:

The Reserve Bank (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948, is an Act to bring the share capital of the Reserve Bank of India into public ownership and to make consequential amendments in the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. This Act is comprised of seven sections.

Section 1, titled "Short title," states that the Act may be called the Reserve Bank (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948.

Section 2, titled "Definitions," defines three key terms used in the Act, including "appointed day," "Bank," and "principal Act."

Section 3, titled "Transfer of Bank shares," outlines the transfer of all shares in the capital of the Bank to the Central Government on the appointed day. It also specifies the compensation to be paid to shareholders in promissory notes of the Central Government.

Section 4, titled "Vacation of office by existing office bearers," states that any person who held office as a Director of the Central Board of the Bank, other than as Governor or Deputy Governor or as a member of a Local Board, shall vacate his office on the appointed day.

Section 5, titled "Interim arrangements," allows the Governor of the Bank or a Deputy Governor of the Bank to exercise all powers, and do all acts and things which may be exercised or done by the Bank, pending the constitution of the Central Board under section 8 of the principal Act.

Section 6, titled "Rule-making power," grants the Central Government the power to make rules for the purpose of giving effect to the objects of this Act.

Section 7, titled "[Repealed.]," has been repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act, 1950.

The Schedule, titled "THE SCHEDULE," has also been repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act, 1950.

In summary, the Reserve Bank (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948, is a law that transfers the share capital of the Reserve Bank of India into public ownership and makes consequential amendments to the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The Act defines key terms, outlines the transfer of Bank shares, vacates the office of existing office bearers, allows for interim arrangements, and grants the Central Government the power to make rules for the purpose of giving effect to the objects of this Act. The Schedule and Section 7 of the Act have been repealed.
