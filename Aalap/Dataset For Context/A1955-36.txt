Summary:

The Durgah Khawaja Saheb Act, 1955, is an act that provides for the proper administration of the Durgah and the Endowment of the Durgah of Khawaja Moin-ud-din Chishti, generally known as Durgah Khawaja Saheb, Ajmer. The act establishes a committee, consisting of not less than five and not more than nine members, to administer, control, and manage the Durgah Endowment. The committee is responsible for keeping the buildings within the boundaries of the Durgah Sharif and all buildings, houses, and shops comprised in the Durgah Endowment in proper order and in a state of good repair, receiving all moneys and other income of the Durgah Endowment, paying salaries, allowances, and perquisites and making all other payments due out of, or charged on, the revenues or income of the Durgah Endowment, determining the privileges of the Khadims and regulating their presence in the Durgah by the grant to them of licenses in that behalf, if the Committee thinks it necessary so to do, defining the powers and duties of the Advisory Committee, determining the functions and powers, if any, which the Sajjadanashin may exercise in relation to the Durgah, appointing, suspending or dismissing servants of the Durgah Endowment, making such provision for the education and maintenance of the indigent descendants of Khawaja Moin-ud-din Chishti and their families and the indigent Khadims and their families residing in India as the Committee considers expedient consistently with the financial position of the Durgah, delegating to the Nazim such powers and functions as the Committee may think fit, and doing all other such things as may be incidental or conducive to the efficient administration of the Durgah. The act also establishes a Board of Arbitration to resolve any disputes that may arise between the Committee and the Sajjadanashin, any Khadim, or any person claiming to be the servant of the Durgah under some hereditary right. The act also provides for the enforcement of final orders of the Committee, the audit of accounts and annual report, the making of bye-laws, and transitional and repealing provisions. The act also provides a penalty for soliciting or receiving offerings in contravention of the provisions made by or under this Act.
