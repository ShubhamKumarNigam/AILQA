Summary:
The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 is an Act to provide for the better protection of the interests of consumers and for that purpose to make provision for the establishment of consumer councils and other authorities for the settlement of consumers’ disputes and for matters connected therewith. The Act establishes three tiers of consumer dispute redressal agencies, namely, the District Forum, the State Commission, and the National Commission. The District Forum has jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services and the compensation, if any, claimed does not exceed rupees twenty lakhs. The State Commission has jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services and the compensation, if any, claimed exceeds rupees twenty lakhs but does not exceed rupees one crore, and appeals against the orders of any District Forum within the State. The National Commission has jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services and the compensation, if any, claimed exceeds rupees one crore, and appeals against the orders of any State Commission. The Act also provides for the appointment of members to these forums, their qualifications, terms of office, salaries and allowances, resignation, removal, and other terms and conditions of service. The Act also lays down the procedure for filing and disposing of complaints and appeals, and the powers and functions of the consumer dispute redressal agencies. The Act also provides for penalties for non-compliance with the orders of the consumer dispute redressal agencies, and protection of action taken in good faith. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of the Act, and the State Governments to make rules for carrying out the provisions of the Act in relation to the State Commissions and District Fora. The Act also empowers the National Commission to make regulations not inconsistent with the provisions of the Act for carrying out the provisions of the Act.
