(1) This Act may be called the Constitution (Eleventh Amendment) Act, 1961.

(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.


2. Amendment of article 66.-In article 66 of the Constitution, for sub-clause (1), the following sub-clause shall be substituted, namely:-
"(1) The Vice-President shall be elected by members of an electoral college consisting of the members of both Houses of Parliament."


3. Amendment of article 71.-In article 71 of the Constitution, after clause (3), the following clause shall be inserted, namely:-
"(4) No election of the President or Vice-President shall be questioned on the ground of the existence of any vacancy for any reason among the members of the electoral college electing him."


The Constitution (Eleventh Amendment) Act, 1961, amends the Constitution of India to change the way the Vice-President is elected. Before this amendment, the Vice-President was elected by members of both Houses of Parliament assembled at a joint meeting. However, this requirement seemed unnecessary and could cause practical difficulties. Therefore, the amendment changes the Vice-President's election to be conducted by members of an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament.

Additionally, the amendment adds a new clause (4) to article 71 of the Constitution, which states that no election of the President or Vice-President can be questioned on the ground of any vacancy for any reason among the members of the electoral college electing him. This change ensures that the election of the President or Vice-President cannot be challenged due to any vacancy in the electoral college.

Overall, the Constitution (Eleventh Amendment) Act, 1961, aims to streamline the process of electing the Vice-President and provide clarity on the election of the President and Vice-President.
