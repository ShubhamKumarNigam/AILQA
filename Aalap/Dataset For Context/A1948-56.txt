Summary:
The Territorial Army Act, 1948, establishes a Territorial Army in India, which is a part-time volunteer force that can be called upon to support the regular armed forces during times of emergency. The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "enrolled", "officer", "non-commissioned officer", and "public utility service". The Act outlines the constitution of the Territorial Army, the classes of personnel (officers and enrolled persons), and the eligibility for enrolment. The Act also specifies the liability of certain persons for compulsory service in the Territorial Army, as well as the liability for military service. The Act provides for the reinstatement of persons required to perform military service in their civil employment and the preservation of certain rights of such persons. The Act also specifies the discharge of enrolled persons, the application of the Army Act, 1950, and the power to make rules.
