Summary:

The Scheduled Areas (Assimilation of Laws) Act, 1951, is a law that relates to the assimilation of laws in the Scheduled Areas of India. The Act specifies the "appointed day" as the date on which it comes into force, as determined by the Central Government.

The Act defines "law" as any Act, Ordinance, Regulation, rule, order, or bye-law that relates to any of the matters enumerated in List I and III in the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution. "Scheduled Areas" refers to the areas specified in the Schedule of this Act.

Section 3 of the Act states that all laws that were in force in the Scheduled Areas immediately before the appointed day cease to be in force in those areas, except for things done or omitted to be done before that day. However, the Central Government or the State Government of Assam may direct that certain laws shall continue to be in force in the Scheduled Areas for a specified period not exceeding twelve months from the appointed day.

Section 4 of the Act provides that notwithstanding anything contained in Section 3, all suits, cases, and other legal proceedings between parties who belong to certain Scheduled Tribes specified in the Act shall continue to be tried under the Assam Frontier (Administration of Justice) Regulation, 1945.

Overall, the Scheduled Areas (Assimilation of Laws) Act, 1951, aims to assimilate laws in the Scheduled Areas of India, while providing transitional provisions and savings for certain legal proceedings.
