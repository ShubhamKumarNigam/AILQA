Summary:
The Delhi and Ajmer-Merwara Land Development Act, 1948, is an Act aimed at providing for the preparation and execution of land development schemes, the reclamation of waste-land, and the control of private forests and grass-land in the Provinces of Delhi and Ajmer-Merwara. The Act establishes Land Development Boards for each Province and empowers them to prepare and execute land development schemes. The schemes may include provisions for preservation and improvement of soil, prevention of soil erosion, improvement of water supply, improvement in methods of cultivation, supply of seed and fertilizers, development of horticulture, reclamation of waste-land, and regulation of grazing and browsing, among other things.

The Act also lays down provisions for the reclamation of waste-land, where the Board may take temporary possession of such land for the purpose of executing any scheme of reclamation. The Land Development Commissioner may arrange for the reclamation of the land by retaining it under his management, settling it with the person in lawful possession of the land, or by settling it with any other person. The Act also provides for the payment of compensation to the landlord and tenant for the period of possession by the Government.

The Act further empowers the Board to control private forests and grass-land in the Provinces of Delhi and Ajmer-Merwara. The Act amends the Indian Forest Act, 1927, in its application to the Province of Delhi and applies the provisions of Chapter V of that Act to the Province of Ajmer.

The Act also provides for the protection of action taken under the Act and prohibits any suit, prosecution, or other legal proceedings against any person for anything done in good faith in pursuance of the Act. The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
