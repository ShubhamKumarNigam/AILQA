Summary:

The Minimum Wages Act, 1948, is an Act to provide for fixing minimum rates of wages in certain employments. The Act defines various terms such as 'appropriate Government', 'competent authority', 'cost of living index number', 'employer', 'employee', 'prescribed', 'scheduled employment', 'wages', and 'wage-periods'. The Act specifies the procedure for fixing and revising minimum wages, and the powers and duties of committees, sub-committees, and the Advisory Board. The Act also provides for the appointment of Inspectors and their powers and duties. The Act lays down provisions for the payment of minimum wages, the fixation of hours for a normal working day, overtime, wages of a worker who works for less than a normal working day, and wages for two or more classes of work. The Act also provides for the maintenance of registers and records, and the punishment for non-compliance with the provisions of the Act. The Act also empowers the appropriate Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of the Act. The Schedule to the Act lists the scheduled employments in respect of which minimum rates of wages can be fixed.
