Summary:

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 is a law in India that aims to protect homebuyers and promote the growth and development of the real estate sector. It establishes a regulatory authority for the real estate sector, which will be responsible for registering and regulating real estate projects and agents. The Act also provides for the establishment of an appellate tribunal to hear appeals against decisions of the regulatory authority.

The Act defines key terms such as "promoter," "allottee," and "real estate agent," and sets out the responsibilities and obligations of these parties. It also specifies the information and documents that must be provided to the regulatory authority and the allottees, and sets out the procedures for registration, extension, and revocation of registration of real estate projects and agents.

The Act also provides for the adjudication of disputes between promoters and allottees, and sets out the penalties for non-compliance with the Act's provisions. It also includes provisions for the protection of the interests of allottees and the promotion of the growth and development of the real estate sector.

In addition, the Act includes provisions for the establishment of a central advisory council to advise the central government on matters related to the implementation of the Act, and for the appointment of officers and employees of the regulatory authority and appellate tribunal.

Overall, the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 is a comprehensive law that aims to protect the interests of homebuyers and promote the growth and development of the real estate sector in India.
