Summary:
The Calcutta High Court (Jurisdictional Limits) Act, 1919, defines the limits of the ordinary original civil jurisdiction of the High Court of Judicature at Fort William in Bengal. The Act states that the High Court shall have and exercise ordinary original civil jurisdiction within such local limits as may from time to time be declared and prescribed by any law made by competent legislative authority for India. The Act further specifies the limits of the High Court's jurisdiction in the Schedule attached to the Act. The Schedule describes the boundaries of the High Court's jurisdiction in detail, starting from the western side of the river Hooghly and moving along the northern, eastern, and southern boundaries to complete the circle. The Schedule also includes provisions for dealing with water-lines, pucca ghats, and other objects permanently attached to the bank and in contact with the water, as well as the definition of terms such as "solid side" or "solid corner." The Act provides that nothing in this Act shall affect any suit or other legal proceeding pending in any Court at the date of the commencement of this Act.
