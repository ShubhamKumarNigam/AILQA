Summary:
The Limitations Act, 1963 is a law in India that consolidates and amends the law for the limitation of suits and other proceedings and for purposes connected therewith. The Act defines various legal terms such as "applicant," "application," "bill of exchange," "bond," "defendant," "easement," "foreign country," "good faith," "plaintiff," "period of limitation," "prescribed period," "promissory note," "suit," and "tort." The Act is divided into five parts. Part I is the preliminary part, Part II deals with the limitation of suits, appeals, and applications, Part III deals with the computation of the period of limitation, Part IV deals with the acquisition of ownership by possession, and Part V deals with miscellaneous provisions. The Act also includes a schedule that lists the periods of limitation for various types of suits, appeals, and applications.
