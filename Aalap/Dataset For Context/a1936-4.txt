Summary:

The Payment of Wages Act, 1936, is an Act to regulate the payment of wages to certain classes of employed persons. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government. The Act applies to the payment of wages to persons employed in any factory, railway, and industrial or other establishment specified in the Act. The Act also allows the appropriate government to extend its provisions to any class of persons employed in any establishment or class of establishments. The Act applies to wages payable to an employed person in respect of a wage period if such wages for that wage period do not exceed twenty four thousand rupees per month.

The Act defines "appropriate government" as the Central Government in relation to railways, air transport services, mines, and oilfields, and the State Government in relation to all other cases. The Act also defines "employed person" as including the legal representative of a deceased employed person, "employer" as including the legal representative of a deceased employer, "factory" as including any place to which the provisions of the Factories Act, 1948 have been applied, "industrial or other establishment" as any establishment or class of establishments specified in the Act, and "wages" as all remuneration expressed in terms of money or capable of being so expressed which would, if the terms of employment, express or implied, were fulfilled, be payable to a person employed in respect of his employment or of work done in such employment.

The Act imposes responsibility on the employer for the payment of wages to persons employed by him, and specifies the wage-periods and time of payment of wages. The Act also specifies the deductions that may be made from wages, including fines, deductions for absence from duty, deductions for damage to or loss of goods entrusted to the employed person, deductions for house-accommodation supplied by the employer, deductions for amenities and services supplied by the employer, deductions for recovery of advances, deductions for recovery of loans, deductions for income-tax payable by the employed person, deductions required to be made by order of a court or other authority, deductions for subscriptions to provident funds, deductions for contribution to the National Defence Fund or to any Defence Savings Scheme, deductions for payment to co-operative societies, deductions for payment of insurance premia on Fidelity Guarantee Bonds, deductions for recovery of losses sustained by a railway administration, deductions for contributions to any insurance scheme framed by the Central Government for its employees, and deductions for repayment to the State Government of the amount of financial assistance granted under the Kerala Financial and other laws.

The Act also specifies the conditions under which deductions may be made, and requires the employer to maintain registers and records giving particulars of persons employed, the work performed by them, the wages paid to them, the deductions made from their wages, the receipts given by them, and such other particulars and in such form as may be prescribed. The Act also provides for the appointment of inspectors to enforce the provisions of the Act, and requires employers to afford all reasonable facilities for making any entry, inspection, supervision, examination, or inquiry under the Act.

The Act also provides for claims arising out of deductions from wages or delay in payment of wages, and specifies the authority to hear and decide such claims. The Act also provides for appeals against orders dismissing applications made under the Act or against directions made under the Act, and specifies the court before which such appeals may be preferred. The Act also provides for the conditional attachment of property of the employer or other person responsible for the payment of wages, and specifies the penalties for offences under the Act. The Act also provides for the bar of suits in certain cases, and for the protection of action taken in good faith. The Act also provides that any contract or agreement whereby an employed person relinquishes any right conferred by the Act shall be null and void. The Act also empowers the appropriate government to make rules for the purpose of carrying into effect the provisions of the Act.
