Summary:
The Police Act, 1861, is an Act for the Regulation of Police. It defines various terms used in the Act, such as "Magistrate," "police," "general police-district," and "District Superintendent." The Act establishes the police force as a single entity under the supervision of the State Government. The Inspector-General of Police is responsible for administering the police force, and the District Superintendent is responsible for administering the police force in each district. The Act also grants various powers and duties to police officers, including the power to lay information before a Magistrate, take charge of unclaimed property, and keep order on public roads and in public places. The Act also regulates public assemblies and processions and provides for the licensing of the same. Additionally, the Act imposes penalties for various offenses, including disobeying orders issued under the Act, neglecting to protect dangerous places, and committing certain offenses on roads or in public places. The Act also provides for the jurisdiction of Magistrates in cases involving police officers and the recovery of penalties and fines imposed by Magistrates. The Act applies to the whole or any part of a State, as determined by the State Government. The Act also allows the State Government to make rules consistent with the Act for the regulation of procedure, claims for compensation, and other matters.
