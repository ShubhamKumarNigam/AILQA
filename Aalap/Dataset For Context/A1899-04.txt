Summary:
The Government Buildings Act, 1899, is a law that applies to the whole of India, except for certain territories. The Act provides for the exemption of certain government buildings and lands from the operation of municipal building laws within the limits of a municipality. The term "municipal authority" in this Act refers to a municipal corporation or a body of municipal commissioners established under any law.

Section 3 of the Act states that any law or enactment regulating the erection, re-erection, construction, alteration, or maintenance of buildings within a municipality does not apply to any building used or required for public service or public purpose, which is the property or in the occupation of the government, or which is to be erected on land that is the property or in the occupation of the government. However, reasonable notice of the proposed work must be given to the municipal authority before the erection, re-erection, construction, or material structural alteration of any such building is commenced, except in the case of buildings connected with defense or those whose plans or construction should be treated as confidential or secret.

Section 4 of the Act provides that the municipal authority or any person authorized by it may inspect the land and building and all plans connected with its erection, re-erection, construction, or material structural alteration with the permission of the State Government. The municipal authority may submit to the State Government a statement in writing of any objections or suggestions regarding the erection, re-erection, construction, or material structural alteration. The State Government shall consider every objection or suggestion submitted and pass orders thereon, and the building shall be erected, re-erected, constructed, or altered in accordance with such orders. If the State Government overrules or disregards any such objection or suggestion, it shall give its reasons for doing so in writing.

The Act was amended by the Adaptation of Laws (No. 2) Order, 1956, to substitute the term "Part B States" with "the territories which, immediately before the 1st November, 1956, were comprised in Part B States." The Act was further amended by the A. O. 1937 and the A. O. 1948 to replace the term "Imperial" with "Central" and the term "the Govt." with "the Government concerned," respectively. Sub-section (3) of Section 1 and Section 4(3) of the Act were also repealed by the A. O. 1937.
