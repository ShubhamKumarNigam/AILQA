
Summary:

The Transplantation of Human Organs Rules, 1995, made under the Transplantation of Human Organs Act, 1994, outlines the procedures and regulations for organ donation in India. The rules define various terms used in the Act, including "National Accreditation Board for Laboratories" (NABL), "Registered Medical Practitioner," and "near relative."

The rules provide for the authorization of organ removal before a person's death by the donor in Form 1(A), 1(B), or 1(C). The medical practitioner removing the organ must ensure that the donor has given authorization, is in proper health, and is a near relative of the recipient. The recipient and donor must jointly submit an application in Form 10, which must be approved by the competent authority or Authorisation Committee.

In case of brain-stem death, a certificate must be signed by all members of the Board of medical experts referred to in sub-section (6) of section 3 of the Act. If the person is less than eighteen years of age, an authority as specified in Form 9 must be signed by either of the parents.

The rules also provide for the preservation of organs according to current and accepted scientific methods. The eye ball removed must be preserved in three steps, namely short-term preservation, medium-term preservation, and long-term preservation, and suitable media must be used for preservation.

The donor and the recipient must jointly make an application to grant approval for removal and transplantation of a human organ to the concerned competent authority or Authorisation Committee as specified in Form 10. The Authorisation Committee shall take a decision on such application in accordance with the guidelines in Rule 6-A.

These rules aim to regulate organ donation in India and prevent commercial dealing in human organs.
