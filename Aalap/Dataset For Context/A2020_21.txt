Summary:
The Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Act, 2020 aims to create an ecosystem where farmers and traders can enjoy freedom of choice in the sale and purchase of farmers' produce. This Act facilitates remunerative prices through competitive alternative trading channels and promotes efficient, transparent, and barrier-free inter-state and intra-state trade and commerce of farmers' produce outside the physical premises of markets or deemed markets notified under various state agricultural produce market legislations. The Act defines various terms such as electronic trading and transaction platform, farmer, farmers' produce, farmer producer organization, inter-state trade, intra-state trade, notification, person, prescribed, scheduled farmers' produce, state, state APMC Act, and trader. The Act consists of five chapters: Chapter I (Preliminary), Chapter II (Promotion and Facilitation of Trade and Commerce of Farmers' Produce), Chapter III (Dispute Resolution), Chapter IV (Penalties), and Chapter V (Miscellaneous). Under Chapter II, farmers, traders, or electronic trading and transaction platforms have the freedom to carry on inter-state or intra-state trade and commerce in farmers' produce in a trade area, subject to the provisions of this Act. The Act also specifies that no market fee or cess or levy, by whatever name called, under any state APMC Act or any other state law, shall be levied on any farmer or trader or electronic trading and transaction platform for trade and commerce in scheduled farmers' produce in a trade area. Additionally, the Act establishes a dispute resolution mechanism for farmers and provides penalties for contravention of the Act and rules. The Act also includes provisions for the protection of action taken in good faith and has overriding effect over any state APMC Act or any other law for the time being in force. The Act also bars the jurisdiction of civil courts and does not apply to certain transactions, such as those made on Stock Exchanges and Clearing Corporations recognized under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956. The Central Government has the power to make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act, and every rule made by the Central Government under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. The Act also includes provisions for removing difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of this Act. The Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Ordinance, 2020 has been repealed, and anything done or any action under the said Ordinance shall be deemed to have been done or taken under the corresponding provisions of this Act.
