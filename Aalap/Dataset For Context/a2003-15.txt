The Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002 (Act No. 15 of 2003) was enacted on 17th January, 2003 to prevent money-laundering and to provide for confiscation of property derived from, or involved in, money-laundering and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act defines money-laundering as the concealment, possession, acquisition or use of property derived from criminal activity or the financing of such activity.

The Act has several chapters, with Chapter I being the preliminary chapter that deals with short title, extent, and commencement of the Act. It also provides definitions for various terms used in the Act.

Chapter II of the Act deals with the offence of money-laundering. It defines the offence and lays down the punishment for committing the offence. The punishment for money-laundering is rigorous imprisonment for a term not less than three years but which may extend to seven years, or with fine which may extend to five lakh rupees, or with both.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the attachment and forfeiture of property involved in money-laundering. It provides for the attachment of property derived from, or involved in, money-laundering. The attachment is made by an order issued by an Adjudicating Authority. The property attached is forfeited to the Central Government if the Adjudicating Authority is satisfied that the property is involved in money-laundering.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the investigation of offences under the Act. It provides for the appointment of Director, Additional Director, Joint Director, Deputy Director, and Assistant Director to investigate offences under the Act. It also provides for the powers of investigation and the production of documents.

Chapter V of the Act deals with the Adjudicating Authority and the Appellate Tribunal. It provides for the appointment of the Adjudicating Authority and the Appellate Tribunal and lays down their powers and functions. It also provides for the appeals against the orders of the Adjudicating Authority and the Appellate Tribunal.

Chapter VI of the Act deals with the miscellaneous provisions. It provides for the protection of action taken in good faith, the power to make rules, and the power to remove difficulties.

The Act applies to the whole of India and extends to all offences committed both within and outside India, provided that the offence involves property in India. The Act also applies to all financial institutions, banks, and intermediaries.

In summary, the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002 is a comprehensive law that aims to prevent money-laundering and to provide for confiscation of property derived from, or involved in, money-laundering. The Act defines the offence of money-laundering, lays down the punishment for committing the offence, and provides for the attachment and forfeiture of property involved in money-laundering. The Act also provides for the investigation of offences under the Act, the appointment of the Adjudicating Authority and the Appellate Tribunal, and the protection of action taken in good faith.
