
Incorporation of certain definitions in article 366.-

In article 366 of the Constitution, after clause (29), the following clause shall be inserted, namely:-

"(29A) 'tax on the sale or purchase of goods' includes-

(i) a tax on the transfer for consideration of controlled commodities;

(ii) a tax on the transfer of property in goods involved in the execution of a works contract;

(iii) a tax on delivery of goods on hire-purchase or any system of payment by instalments;

(iv) a tax on the transfer of the right to use any goods for any purpose for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration;

(v) a tax on the supply of goods by an unincorporated association or body of persons to a member thereof for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration;

(vi) a tax on the supply, by way of or as part of any service, of food or any drink for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration;".



Insertion of new entry in the Union List in the Seventh Schedule.-

In the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution, in the Union List, after entry 92A, the following entry shall be inserted, namely:-

"92B. Taxes on the consignment of goods (whether the consignment is to the person making the consignment or to any other person) where such consignment takes place in the course of inter-State trade or commerce."



Amendment of article 269.-

In article 269 of the Constitution,-

(a) in clause (1), for the words "on the sale or purchase of goods", the words "on the consignment of goods in the course of inter-State trade or commerce" shall be substituted;

(b) in clause (3), after the words "by law", the words "for determining when a consignment of goods takes place in the course of inter-State trade or commerce" shall be inserted;

(c) in clause (4), for the words "on the sale or purchase of goods", the words "on the consignment of goods in the course of inter-State trade or commerce" shall be substituted.



Amendment of article 286.-

In clause (3) of article 286 of the Constitution,-

(a) for the words "goods of the same class or kind", the words "goods" shall be substituted;

(b) for the words "such sale or purchase", the words "such transfer" shall be substituted;

(c) for the words "the sale or purchase of such goods", the words "the transfer of such goods" shall be substituted;

(d) for the words "the sale or purchase of goods", the words "the transfer of goods" shall be substituted;

(e) after the words "by law", the words "concerning the levy of tax on the transfer of goods involved in the execution of a works contract, on the delivery of goods on hire-purchase or any system of payment by instalments and on the right to use any goods" shall be inserted.



Validation of laws and collections.-

(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Constitution,-

(a) any law levying tax on the supply of food or drink for consideration and also the collection or recovery of tax made under any such law;

(b) any tax on the supply of food or drink for consideration levied and collected by any local authority in accordance with the provisions of any law for the time being in force in any State;

(c) any collection or recovery of tax made by any local authority under any such law;

shall be deemed to be and to have always been valid.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Constitution, no sales tax shall be payable on food or drink supplied by a hotelier to a person lodged in the hotel during the period commencing on the 1st day of January, 1976 and ending with the date on which the Constitution (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982 receives the assent of the President if the conditions mentioned in sub-clause (2) of clause (6) of the Constitution (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982 are satisfied.

(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Constitution, no sales tax shall be payable on food or drink supplied by restaurants during the period commencing on the date of the judgment of the Supreme Court in Northern India Caterers (India) Limited case and ending with the date on which the Constitution (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982 receives the assent of the President.



Power of President to remove difficulties.-

(1) If any difficulty arises in giving effect to the provisions of the Constitution (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982, the President may, by order, make such provisions, not inconsistent with the provisions of the Constitution, as appear to him to be necessary or expedient for the removal of the difficulty:

Provided that no such order shall be made after the expiration of two years from the date on which the Constitution (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982 receives the assent of the President.

(2) Every order made under this article shall be laid before each House of Parliament while it is in session for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions and if, before the expiration of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the order or both Houses agree that the order should not be made, the order shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that order.



Short title and commencement.-

(1) This Act may be called THE CONSTITUTION (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982.

(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.



The 46th Amendment to the Constitution of India, also known as The Constitution (Forty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1982, added new definitions to the term "tax on the sale or purchase of goods" in Article 366 of the Constitution. These definitions include taxes on the transfer for consideration of controlled commodities, the transfer of property in goods involved in the execution of a works contract, delivery of goods on hire-purchase or any system of payment by instalments, transfer of the right to use any goods for any purpose for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration, the supply of goods by an unincorporated association or body of persons to a member thereof for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration, and the supply, by way of or as part of any service, of food or any drink for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration.

A new entry, 92B, was also added to the Union List in the Seventh Schedule to enable the levy of tax on the consignment of goods where such consignment takes place in the course of inter-State trade or commerce.

Clauses (1) and (4) of article 269 were amended so that the tax levied on the consignment of goods in the course of inter-State trade or commerce would be assigned to the States, and clause (3) was amended to enable Parliament to formulate by law principles for determining when a consignment of goods takes place in the course of inter-State trade or commerce.

Clauses (3) of article 286 were amended to enable Parliament to specify, by law, restrictions and conditions in regard to the system of levy, rates and other incidents of the tax on the transfer of goods involved in the execution of a works contract, on the delivery of goods on hire-purchase or any system of payment by instalments and on the right to use any goods.

Clause 6 of the Bill seeks to validate laws levying tax on the supply of food or drink for consideration and also the collection or recoveries made by way of tax under any such law. However, no sales tax will be payable on food or drink supplied by a hotelier to a person lodged in the hotel during the period from the date of the judgment in the Associated Hotels of India case and the commencement of the present Amendment Act if the conditions mentioned in sub-clause (2) of clause 6 of the Bill are satisfied. In the case of food or drink supplied by Restaurants this relief will be available only in respect of the period after the date of judgment in the Northern India Caterers (India) Limited case and the commencement of the present Amendment Act.

The proposed amendments would help in the augmentation of the State revenues to a considerable extent.

This Amendment act aims to clarify the definition of "tax on the sale or purchase of goods" in the Constitution of India, and to enable the levy of tax on the consignment of goods in the course of inter-State trade or commerce. It also includes provisions for the assignment of such tax to the States, and for the formulation of principles for determining when a consignment of goods takes place in the course of inter-State trade or commerce. Additionally, the Amendment act includes provisions for the specification of restrictions and conditions in regard to the system of levy, rates and other incidents of the tax on the transfer of goods involved in the execution of a works contract, on the delivery of goods on hire-purchase or any system of payment by instalments and on the right to use any goods. The Amendment act also includes provisions for the validation of laws levying tax on the supply of food or drink for consideration and also the collection or recoveries made by way of tax under any such law. The Amendment act is intended to help in the augmentation of the State revenues to a considerable extent.
