The Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976, establishes and incorporates Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) in India to provide credit and other financial services to small and marginal farmers, agricultural laborers, and small businesses in rural areas. The Act defines key terms such as "Board," "Chairman," "director," "notified area," "prescribed," "Regional Rural Bank," "Sponsor Bank," and "State Government."

The Act outlines the incorporation and capital of RRBs, including their authorized capital of two thousand crore rupees divided into two hundred crore of fully paid-up shares of ten rupees each. The Central Government, concerned State Government, and Sponsor Bank must subscribe to the issued capital of each RRB, with the Central Government and Sponsor Bank holding at least fifty-one percent of the shares. The RRB's Board may increase the issued capital with the approval of the Central Government, the Sponsor Bank, and the concerned State Government.

The Act specifies the management and structure of RRBs, including the appointment of a Chairman by the Sponsor Bank and the nomination of directors by the Central Government, State Government, Sponsor Bank, and Reserve Bank. The Board of directors shall consist of a maximum of fifteen members, including the Chairman, and shall manage the affairs of the RRB. The Chairman shall devote his whole time to the affairs of the RRB and shall have the management of the whole of the affairs of the RRB.

The Act also outlines the disqualifications for being appointed or nominated as a director, including being adjudged insolvent, of unsound mind, or convicted of an offense involving moral turpitude. The Act specifies the vacation of the seat of directors, including becoming subject to disqualification or being absent for more than three consecutive meetings without leave of the Board.

The Act provides for the meetings of the Board, including the rules of procedure and the role of the Chairman in presiding over meetings. The Act also allows for the creation of committees of the Board for specific purposes. The fees and allowances of directors and members of committees shall be determined by the Central Government.

The Act permits RRBs to appoint officers and employees in a manner prescribed by the Central Government. The remuneration of officers and employees shall be determined by the Central Government, taking into account the salary structure of employees of the State Government and local authorities in the notified area.

RRBs are authorized to carry on and transact the business of banking as defined in the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, and may engage in one or more forms of business specified in that Act. RRBs may undertake specific types of business, including granting loans and advances to small and marginal farmers and agricultural laborers.
