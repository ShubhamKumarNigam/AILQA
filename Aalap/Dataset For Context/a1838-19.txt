Summary:

The Coasting-Vessels Act, 1838, is an Act that originally applied to the territories of the former states of Bombay, Saurashtra, and Kutch, but has since been extended to other states with sea-coasts. The Act outlines rules for vessels belonging to any citizen of India and employed on the coasts of these states or in trading coastwise. These rules include marking or branding vessels with the name of the place to which they belong and a number assigned by the officer authorized to make such registry, painting the name and number on each quarter of the vessel, and registering the name, number, burthen, and name or names of the owner or owners in a book kept for that purpose by the person authorized to make such registry. The owner or owners of the vessel must apply for and obtain a certificate of registry, which must be sealed with the seal of the Government of India and signed by the person authorized to make the registry. The Act also specifies fees for certificates and penalties for neglecting to comply with the rules, which may be recovered by sale of the vessel, furniture, ammunition, tackle, and apparel. The Central Government may direct compensation for trouble and diligence in seizing such vessels and goods to the person or persons who seized them.
