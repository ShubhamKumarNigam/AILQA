Summary:

The Mid-Day Meal Rules, 2015, outline the provisions for providing free meals to children aged six to fourteen years who attend school. These rules are enacted under the National Food Security Act, 2013. The meals must meet the nutritional standards specified in Schedule II of the Act and must be served at the school only. Every school must have the facility to cook meals in a hygienic manner. The State Steering-cum-Monitoring Committee (SSMC) oversees the implementation of the scheme, while the School Management Committee (SMC) monitors the quality of meals, cleanliness of the cooking place, and maintenance of hygiene. In case of non-availability of food grains, cooking cost, fuel, or absence of cook-cum-helper, the State Government pays food security allowance to every child. If the mid-day meal is not provided for three days or at least five days in a month, the State Government takes action to fix responsibility on the person or agency. These rules also provide that nothing in these rules affects the guidelines issued by the Central Government regarding the activities relating to Mid Day Meal Scheme.
