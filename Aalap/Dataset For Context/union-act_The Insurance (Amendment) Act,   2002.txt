Summary:

The Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2002 is an amendment to the Insurance Act, 1938. This amendment act includes changes to various sections of the principal act, including changes to the definition of terms, amendments to existing sections, insertion of new sections, and omission of certain schedules. Some of the key changes include:

* Amendments to section 2 of the principal act, which includes changes to the definition of terms such as "insurance co-operative society" and "intermediary or insurance intermediary."
* Amendments to section 2C of the principal act, which allows insurance co-operative societies to carry on any class of insurance business in India under the act on or after the commencement of the Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2002.
* Amendments to section 15 of the principal act, which requires insurers to submit their accounts and statements to the Authority within six months from the end of the period to which they refer.
* Amendments to section 28A, 28B, and 31B of the principal act, which specifies that the forms for submission of returns and statements are to be in the form specified by the regulations made by the Authority.
* Amendments to section 40 of the principal act, which includes changes to the definition of terms such as "insurance agent" and "intermediary or insurance intermediary" and includes intermediary or insurance intermediary in the exception of section 40(1) and (2A)
* Amendments to section 42 of the principal act, which includes changes to the definition of terms such as "requisite qualifications and practical training" and includes a new clause (ea) that requires a company or firm making an application under sub-section (1) or sub-section (3) to have a director or a partner or one or more of its officers or other employees so designated by it, and in the case of any other person, the chief executive, by whatever name called, or one or more of his employees designated by him, to possess the requisite qualifications and practical training and have not passed such an examination as required under clauses (e) and (f).
* Insertion of new section 42E, which specifies the commission, brokerage or fee payable to intermediary or insurance intermediary and the requirements of capital, form of business and other conditions to act as an intermediary or insurance intermediary.
* Amendments to section 49 of the principal act, which includes changes to the definition of terms such as "balance- sheet" and "abstract of the report of the actuary" and includes a new provision that the share of any such surplus allocated to or reserved for the shareholders (including any amount for the payment of dividends guaranteed to them, whether by way of first charge or otherwise) shall not exceed such sums as may be specified by the Authority and such share shall in no case exceed ten per cent. of such surplus in case of participating policies and in other cases the whole thereof.
* Amendments to section 64VB of the principal act, which includes a new provision that the Authority may, from time to time, specify, by the regulations made by it, the manner of receipt of premium by the insurer.
* Insertion of new Part IIIA, which deals with Insurance Co-operative Societies and deems every insurance co-operative society to be an insurer for the purposes of this Act and applies all the provisions applicable to an insurer being an Indian insurance company to an insurance co-operative society.
* Amendments to section 101A of the principal act, which includes changes to the definition of terms such as "Indian re- insurer"
* Amendments to section 114 of the principal act, which omits clause (a) of sub-section (2)
* Amendments to section 114A of the principal act, which includes changes to the definition of terms such as "matters including fee relating to the registration of insurers under section 3" and includes new provisions related to the form of balance- sheet, the form of return, the form of statement, and the manner of receipt of premium.
* Omission of the First Schedule, the Second Schedule, the Third Schedule and the Fourth Schedule to the principal act.

This amendment act aims to bring more clarity and transparency to the insurance industry in India, and also aims to increase the regulatory oversight of the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority (IRDA) over the insurance industry. It also aims to promote the growth of the insurance sector by allowing insurance co-operative societies to carry on any class of insurance business in India under the act on or after the commencement of the Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2002. Additionally, it also aims to protect the interests of policyholders by specifying the commission, brokerage or fee payable to intermediary or insurance intermediary and the requirements of capital, form of business and other conditions to act as an intermediary or insurance intermediary.
