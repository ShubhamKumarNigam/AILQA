Summary:
The Inter-State Corporations Act, 1957, with the Act ID ACT NO. 38 OF 1957, was enacted on the 20th of September, 1957. The Act provides for the reorganization of certain corporations functioning in two or more States by virtue of section 109 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. The Act defines an "inter-State corporation" as any body corporate constituted under any of the Acts specified in the Schedule and functioning in two or more States by virtue of section 109 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 or any other enactment relating to reorganization of States. 

The Act grants the power to the State Governments to frame schemes for the reconstitution and reorganization or dissolution of inter-State corporations. The Central Government may approve the scheme with or without modifications and give effect to the scheme so approved by making such order as it thinks fit. The order made under this section may provide for the dissolution of the inter-State corporation, the reconstitution and reorganization in any manner whatsoever of the inter-State corporation, the area in respect of which the reconstituted corporation or new corporation shall function and operate, the transfer of the assets, rights, and liabilities of the inter-State corporation to any other corporations or State Governments, the substitution of any such transferee for the inter-State corporation, or the addition of any such transferee, as a party to any legal proceeding to which the inter-State corporation is a party, and the transfer or re-employment of any employees of the inter-State corporation to, or by, any such transferee.

The Act also grants the power to the Central Government to add to the Schedule any Act under which a body corporate constituted for a State is functioning in two or more States by virtue of section 109 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956, or of any other enactment relating to reorganization of States. The Schedule lists the Acts under which a body corporate constituted for a State is functioning in two or more States by virtue of section 109 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.

In conclusion, the Inter-State Corporations Act, 1957, provides the legal framework for the reorganization of certain corporations functioning in two or more States by virtue of section 109 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956, and for matters connected therewith. The Act grants the power to the State Governments and Central Government to frame schemes, approve, and give effect to the reconstitution and reorganization or dissolution of inter-State corporations. The Act also grants the power to the Central Government to add to the Schedule any Act under which a body corporate constituted for a State is functioning in two or more States by virtue of section 109 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956, or of any other enactment relating to reorganization of States.
