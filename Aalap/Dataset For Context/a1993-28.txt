Summary:
The Multimodal Transportation of Goods Act, 1993 (Act No. 28 of 1993) came into force on April 2, 1993, and applies to the whole of India except the state of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act aims to provide for the regulation of multimodal transportation of goods, from any place in India to a place outside India, on the basis of a multimodal transport contract.

The Act defines several key terms, including "carrier," "competent authority," "consignor," "consignee," "consignment," "delivery," "endorsee," "endorsement," "goods," "mode of transport," "multimodal transportation," "multimodal transport contract," "multimodal transport document," "multimodal transport operator," "negotiable multimodal transport document," "non-negotiable multimodal transport document," "prescribed," "registration," "special drawing rights," and "taking charge."

Chapter II of the Act deals with the regulation of multimodal transportation. No person can carry on or commence the business of multimodal transportation unless they are registered under this Act. To obtain registration, a person must apply to the competent authority and fulfill certain conditions, such as having a minimum annual turnover of fifty lakh rupees during the immediately preceding financial year or an average annual turnover of fifty lakh rupees during the preceding three financial years. The competent authority may refuse to grant registration if it is satisfied that the applicant does not fulfill the conditions.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the multimodal transport document. The multimodal transport operator must issue a negotiable or non-negotiable multimodal transport document if the consignor and the multimodal transport operator have entered into a contract for the multimodal transportation and the multimodal transport operator has taken charge of the goods. The multimodal transport document must contain certain particulars, such as the general nature of the goods, the leading marks necessary for identification of the goods, the character of the goods, the number of packages or units and the gross weight and quantity of the goods, and the name and principal place of business of the multimodal transport operator.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the responsibilities and liabilities of the multimodal transport operator. The multimodal transport operator is liable for loss resulting from any loss of, or damage to, the consignment or delay in delivery of the consignment. The liability of the multimodal transport operator is limited to two Special Drawing Rights per kilogram of the gross weight of the consignment lost or damaged or 666.67 Special Drawing Rights per package or unit lost or damaged, whichever is higher. However, if the multimodal transportation does not include carriage of goods by sea or by inland waterways, the liability of the multimodal transport operator is limited to an amount not exceeding 8.33 Special Drawing Rights per kilogram of the gross weight of the goods lost or damaged.

Chapter V of the Act deals with miscellaneous provisions, such as the right of the multimodal transport operator to have a lien on the consignment and documents, the power to make rules, and the repeal and savings of certain enactments.

The Schedule to the Act, which has been repealed, dealt with the forms to be used for various purposes under the Act. The Appendix contains the forms, and the Footnotes provide information about amendments to the Act.
