Summary:

The Road Transport Corporations Act, 1950 (Act No. 64 of 1950) was enacted on 4th December, 1950. The Act provides for the incorporation and regulation of Road Transport Corporations. The Act has 5 chapters, and the first chapter deals with preliminary matters, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act. The second chapter establishes the Road Transport Corporations and sets out their powers and duties. The third chapter deals with the finance, accounts, and audit of the Corporations, while the fourth chapter contains miscellaneous provisions. The fifth chapter deals with special provisions relating to Bombay, and the sixth chapter contains special provisions applicable to the Union Territory of Delhi.

Chapter II of the Act deals with the establishment and incorporation of Road Transport Corporations. A Corporation can be established by the State Government for the whole or any part of the Union territory of Delhi. The Corporation shall be a body corporate having perpetual succession and a common seal, with the power to sue and be sued by the name notified under section 3. The management of the Corporation vests in a Board of Directors, which may include representatives of the Central Government, the State Government, and shareholders. The term of office, manner of filling casual vacancies, and disqualifications for being chosen as, or for being, a Director of a Corporation are also set out in the Act.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the powers and duties of the Corporation. The Corporation has the power to operate road transport services in the Union territory of Delhi and in any extended area, provide for any ancillary service, provide suitable conditions of service for its employees, authorize the issue of passes to its employees and other persons, and authorize the grant of refund in respect of unused tickets and concessional passes. The Corporation may also manufacture, purchase, maintain, and repair rolling stock, vehicles, appliances, plant, equipment, or any other thing required for the purpose of any of the activities of the Corporation. The Corporation may acquire and hold property, both movable and immovable, and lease, sell, or otherwise transfer any property held by it. The Corporation may also prepare schemes for the acquisition of, and acquire, either by agreement or compulsorily, the whole or any part of any undertaking of any other person.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the finance, accounts, and audit of the Corporation. Every Corporation shall have its own fund, and all receipts of the Corporation shall be carried thereto and all payments by the Corporation shall be made therefrom. The moneys belonging to the fund shall be deposited in the Reserve Bank of India or with the agents of the Reserve Bank of India, or with the corresponding new banks constituted under section 3 of the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1970, or section 3 of the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1980, a State Finance Corporation, established under section 3 of the State Financial Corporations Act, 1951, the Industrial Finance Corporation of India, established under section 3 of the Industrial Finance Corporation Act, 1948, the Industrial Development Bank of India, established under section 3 of the Industrial Development Bank of India Act, 1964, the Life Insurance Corporation of India, established under section 3 of the Life Insurance Corporation Act, 1956, or any other financial institution providing credit which is subject to the control of the Reserve Bank of India. The Corporation shall prepare an annual statement of accounts, including the profit and loss account and the balance sheet, in such form as may be prescribed by the State Government in consultation with the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India. The accounts of the Corporation shall be audited annually by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India or his nominee, and the audit report shall be laid before both Houses of Parliament.

Chapter V of the Act contains miscellaneous provisions, including directions by the State Government, returns and reports, power to order inquiries, power to control a part of the undertaking of a Corporation, power to supersede a Corporation, and liquidation of a Corporation. The Act also provides for the payment of compensation for the acquisition of road transport undertakings.

Chapter VI of the Act contains special provisions applicable to the Union Territory of Delhi. Section 49 provides that no court shall proceed to the trial of any offence made punishable by or under this Act except on the complaint of, or on an information received from, the General Manager of the new Corporation or a person authorized by him by general or special order in this behalf in writing. All offences against this Act or any rule or regulation made thereunder, or any bye-law continued thereunder, whether committed within or without the limits of the Union territory of Delhi, shall be cognizable by a Magistrate. The State Government may appoint one or more magistrates of the first or second class for the trial of offences against this Act or any rule or regulation made thereunder or any bye-law continued thereunder and may prescribe the time and place at which such magistrate or magistrates shall sit for the despatch of business. The magistrates and the members of their staff shall be paid such salary, pension, leave, and other allowances as may be fixed by the State Government. The new Corporation shall out of its fund, pay to the State Government the amounts of the salary, pension, leave, and other allowances as fixed under sub-section (2) together with all other incidental charges in connection with the establishments of the said magistrates. Such magistrates shall have jurisdiction over the whole of the Union territory of Delhi. For the purposes of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, all magistrates appointed under this section shall be deemed to be magistrates appointed under section 12 of that Code.

In conclusion, the Road Transport Corporations Act, 1950 provides for the incorporation and regulation of Road Transport Corporations, sets out their powers and duties, and provides for their finance, accounts, and audit. The Act also contains miscellaneous provisions and special provisions applicable to the Union Territory of Delhi.
