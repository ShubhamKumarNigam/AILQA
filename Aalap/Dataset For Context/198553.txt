Summary:
The Pondicherry University Act, 1985, established and incorporated a teaching and affiliating university in the Union territory of Pondicherry. The Act outlines the university's objects, powers, and jurisdiction. The university's objects include disseminating and advancing knowledge, making special provisions for studies in French and integrated courses for humanity and science, and promoting inter-disciplinary studies and research. The university has the power to provide instruction, grant degrees, organize extra-mural studies and extension services, institute honorary degrees, provide instruction to non-members, appoint teachers and staff, and recognize institutions and teachers. The university's jurisdiction extends to the Union territory of Pondicherry, and it may extend to the Union territories of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep with the prior approval of the Central Government. The university may not compulsorily affiliate colleges or institutions within its jurisdiction, but it may grant affiliation to those that agree to accept its statutes and ordinances. Any college or institution admitted to the privileges of the university must cease to be associated with any other university. The Act also establishes two schools, the Sri Aurobindo School of Eastern and Western Thought and the Subramania Bharati School of Tamil Language and Literature.

Section 31 outlines the procedure for arbitration in disciplinary cases against employees. According to this section, an employee can request a Tribunal of Arbitration if they are removed from the rolls of the University or debarred from appearing at the examinations for more than one year. The Tribunal will consist of one member appointed by the Executive Council, one member nominated by the employee, and an umpire appointed by the Visitor. The decision of the Tribunal will be final, and no suit shall lie in any civil court in respect of the matters decided by the Tribunal.

Section 32 governs the procedure of appeal and arbitration in disciplinary cases against students. Any student or candidate for an examination whose name has been removed from the rolls of the University by the orders or resolution of the Vice-Chancellor, Discipline Committee, or Examination Committee, and who has been debarred from appearing at the examinations of the University for more than one year, may appeal to the Executive Council. The Executive Council may confirm, modify, or reverse the decision of the Vice-Chancellor or the Committee. Any dispute arising out of any disciplinary action taken by the University against a student shall, at the request of such student, be referred to a Tribunal of Arbitration.

Section 33 provides for the right to appeal for every employee or student of the University or of a College or Institution. They may appeal to the Executive Council against the decision of any officer or authority of the University or of the Principal or the management of any College or Institution.

Section 34 governs the provident and pension funds for the benefit of the University's employees. The University may constitute such pension or provident fund or provide such insurance schemes as it may deem fit in such manner and subject to such conditions as may be prescribed by the Statutes.

Section 35 outlines the procedure for resolving disputes related to the constitution of University authorities and bodies. Section 36 governs the constitution of committees. Where any authority of the University is given power by this Act or the Statutes to appoint committees, such committees shall consist of the members of the authority concerned and of such other person (if any) as the authority in each case may think fit.

Section 38 provides that no act or proceedings of any authority or other body of the University shall be invalid merely by reason of the existence of a vacancy or vacancies among its members.

Section 39 provides protection to the officers and employees of the University for anything which is in good faith done or intended to be done in pursuance of any of the provisions of this Act, the Statutes, or the Ordinances.

Section 40 outlines the mode of proof of University records. A copy of any receipt, application, notice, order, proceeding, resolution of any authority or committee of the University, or other documents in possession of the University, or any entry in any register duly maintained by the University, if certified by the Registrar, shall be received as prima facie evidence of such receipt, application, notice, order, proceeding, resolution, documents, or the existence of entry in the register and shall be admitted as evidence of the matters and transactions therein where the original thereof would, if produced, have been admissible in evidence.

Section 41 provides for the removal of difficulties. If any difficulty arises in giving effect to the provisions of this Act, the Central Government may, by order published in the Official Gazette, make such provisions, not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, as appear to it to be necessary or expedient for removing the difficulty.

Section 42 outlines the transitional provisions. The first Chancellor and the first Vice-Chancellor shall be appointed by the Visitor and each of the said officers shall hold office for a term of five years. The first Registrar and the first Finance Officer shall be appointed by the Visitor and each of the said officers shall hold office for a term of three years. The first Court and the first Executive Council shall consist of not more than thirty members and eleven members, respectively, who shall be nominated by the Visitor and shall hold office for a term of three years. The first Academic Council shall be constituted on the expiry of a period of six months from the commencement of this Act and during the said period of six months, the powers of the Academic Council shall be performed by the Planning Board constituted under section 23.

1. Removal of Employees:
a. Employees, other than teachers or academic staff, can be removed by the authority that appointed them if they are of unsound mind, deaf-mute, suffer from contagious leprosy, are undischarged insolvents, have been convicted of an offence involving moral turpitude and sentenced to imprisonment for not less than six months, or are otherwise guilty of misconduct.
b. A resolution to remove the employee must be passed by the Executive Council by a majority of two-thirds of its members present and voting.
c. The employee must be given a reasonable opportunity to show cause against the proposed action.
d. If the removal is not due to sub-clause (c) or (d) of clause (1), the employee must be given three months' notice or paid three months' salary in lieu of such notice.

2. Resignation:
a. Permanent employees must give three months' notice or pay three months' salary in lieu of notice.
b. Non-permanent employees must give one month's notice or pay one month's salary in lieu of notice.

3. Honorary Degrees:
a. The Executive Council may recommend the conferment of honorary degrees on the recommendation of the Academic Council.
b. The Executive Council may withdraw any honorary degree conferred by the University with the previous sanction of the Visitor.

4. Withdrawal of Degrees, etc.:
a. The Executive Council may withdraw any degree or academic distinction conferred on, or any certificate or diploma granted to, any person by the University for good and sufficient cause.
b. A notice in writing must be given to the person calling upon them to show cause within a specified time.

5. Maintenance of Discipline Among Students:
a. The Vice-Chancellor has all powers relating to discipline and disciplinary action in relation to students of the University.
b. The Vice-Chancellor may delegate his powers to a Proctor and other officers.
c. The Principals of Colleges, Institutions, Heads of Special Centres, Deans of Schools of Studies and Heads of teaching Departments in the University have disciplinary powers over the students in their respective Colleges, Institutions, Special Centres, Schools and teaching Departments.
d. Detailed rules of discipline and proper conduct shall be made by the University.

a. Colleges and Institutions situated within the jurisdiction of the University may be admitted to such privileges of the University as the Executive Council may decide.
b. Every such College or Institution shall have a Governing Body, consisting of not more than fifteen persons approved by the Executive Council.
c.
