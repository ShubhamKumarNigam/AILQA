Summary:
The Civil Defence Act, 1968, is a law in India that aims to provide for civil defense and related matters. The Act is divided into four chapters. Chapter I, titled "Preliminary," includes the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act, as well as definitions of key terms used throughout the Act. Chapter II, titled "Power of Central Government to Make Rules for Civil Defence," outlines the powers of the Central Government to make rules for civil defense, including preventing the prosecution of any work likely to prejudice civil defense, providing and maintaining commodities and things required for civil defense, and regulating traffic and the use of vessels, buoys, lights, and signals in ports and territorial, tidal, and inland waters. Chapter III, titled "Civil Defence Corps," discusses the constitution of the Civil Defence Corps, the appointment of members and officers, the dismissal of members of the Corps, and the functions of members of the Corps. Chapter IV, titled "Miscellaneous," includes provisions on the application of the Personal Injuries (Emergency Provisions) Act to injuries sustained by members of the Corps, penalties for non-compliance with the Act, the effect of the Act and rules inconsistent with other enactments, the limitation of prosecutions, and the power of the Central Government to delegate. The Act also includes provisions on the protection of action taken in good faith, the status of authorized persons and members of the Corps as public servants, and the laying of rules and regulations before Parliament.
