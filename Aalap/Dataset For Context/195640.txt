Summary:

THE BIHAR AND WEST BENGAL (TRANSFER OF TERRITORIES) ACT, 1956, is an Indian law that aims to transfer certain territories from Bihar to West Bengal and address related matters. The Act is divided into eight parts.

Part I, the Preliminary section, defines terms used throughout the Act.

Part II, Transfer of Territories, details the territories to be transferred from Bihar to West Bengal and the resulting changes to the First Schedule of the Constitution.

Part III, Representation in the Legislatures, discusses the impact on the Council of States, the House of the People, Legislative Assemblies, and Legislative Councils, including the allocation of seats, delimitation of constituencies, and the duration of Legislative Assemblies.

Part IV, High Courts, addresses the extension of jurisdiction of the Calcutta High Court, the transfer of proceedings, and the right to appear in any proceedings transferred to the Calcutta High Court.

Part V, Authorisation of Expenditure, covers the appropriation of moneys for expenditure in transferred territories, the distribution of revenues, and the modification of the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Orders.

Part VI, Apportionment of Assets and Liabilities, covers land and goods, treasury and bank balances, arrears of taxes, right to recover loans and advances, credits in certain funds, assets and liabilities of State undertakings, public debt, refund of taxes collected in excess, deposits, provident funds, and pensions.

Part VII, Administrative Provisions, includes provisions related to State Financial Corporations, temporary provisions as to the continuance of certain existing road transport permits, provisions relating to services, provisions as to the continuance of officers in the same posts, and legal and miscellaneous provisions.

Finally, the Schedule outlines the apportionment of liability in respect of pensions.
