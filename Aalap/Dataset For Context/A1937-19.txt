Summary:
The Arya Marriage Validation Act, 1937, is a law in India that recognizes and removes doubts regarding the validity of inter-marriages among Arya Samajists. This Act has the short title of the "Arya Marriage Validation Act, 1937" and extends to the whole of India, except for the territories that were previously part of Part B States, and applies to citizens of India wherever they may be.

The Act states that marriages between Arya Samajists, whether they occurred before or after the commencement of this Act, shall not be invalid due to the fact that the parties belonged to different castes or sub-castes of Hindus or that either or both of the parties belonged to a religion other than Hinduism at any time before the marriage.

This law aims to recognize and legitimize inter-marriages among Arya Samajists, who follow a reformist sect of Hinduism, and to remove any doubts regarding the validity of such marriages due to differences in caste, sub-caste, or religion.
