Summary:
The Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956, aims to prevent immoral traffic and prostitution. The Act defines brothels, prostitution, and related terms. It punishes the keeping and managing of brothels, living on the earnings of prostitution, procuring or inducing a person for the sake of prostitution, and detaining a person in premises where prostitution is carried on. The Act also addresses prostitution in or in the vicinity of public places, seducing or soliciting for the purpose of prostitution, and seduction of a person in custody. It provides for special police officers and an advisory body to deal with offenses under this Act. The Act also includes provisions for the rescue and custody of persons rescued from brothels. The Act applies to the whole of India and came into force on 30th December 1956.
