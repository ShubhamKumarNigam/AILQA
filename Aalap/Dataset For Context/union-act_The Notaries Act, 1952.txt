Summary:
The Notaries Act, 1952 outlines the regulations and qualifications for notaries in India. The Act defines a notary as a person appointed as such under this Act, and includes legal practitioners or other persons who possess prescribed qualifications. The Central Government and State Governments have the power to appoint notaries for the whole or any part of India or the State, respectively. The Act also establishes a Register of Notaries, which includes the notary's full name, date of birth, residential and professional address, qualifications, and any other prescribed particulars.

Notaries are authorized to perform various functions, including verifying, authenticating, certifying, or attesting the execution of any instrument; presenting promissory notes, hundis, or bills of exchange for acceptance or payment; noting or protesting the dishonor of any promissory note, hundi, or bill of exchange; preparing bottomry and respondentia bonds, charter parties, and other mercantile documents; and translating and verifying the translation of any document from one language into another.

To practice as a notary, a person must hold a certificate of practice in force issued to him under section 5 of the Act. Any person who falsely represents that he is a notary without being appointed as such or practices as a notary or does any notarial act in contravention of section 9 shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year, or with fine, or with both. The Act also lays down provisions for removal of names from the Register and construction of references to notaries public in other laws.
