Summary:

The Constitution (Ninety-Fourth Amendment) Act, 2006, received the assent of the President on June 12, 2006, and was published in the Gazette of India, Extra., Part II, Section 1. The Act amends Article 164 of the Constitution to exclude the State of Bihar from the purview of the proviso to clause (1) of the article. This proviso previously required there to be a Minister in charge of tribal welfare in the States of Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, and Orissa, who may also be in charge of the welfare of the Scheduled Castes and Backward Classes or any other work.

The amendment is a response to the reorganization of certain states in India, specifically the creation of the States of Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand from the former States of Madhya Pradesh and Bihar, respectively. The Scheduled Areas of the former State of Madhya Pradesh were redefined and a significant portion of them were transferred to Chhattisgarh, while the entire Scheduled Areas of the former State of Bihar were transferred to the newly formed Jharkhand State.

As a result of these changes, the population of the Scheduled Tribes in Bihar is now very small, and the amendment therefore seeks to exclude Bihar from the purview of the proviso to clause (1) of Article 164. The amendment also extends the provisions of clause (1) of Article 164 to the newly formed States of Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand, requiring there to be a Minister in charge of tribal welfare in those states.

In summary, the Constitution (Ninety-Fourth Amendment) Act, 2006, amends Article 164 of the Constitution to exclude Bihar from the purview of the proviso to clause (1) of the article and extend the provisions of that clause to the newly formed States of Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand. This change is in response to the reorganization of certain states in India and the transfer of Scheduled Areas to the newly formed states.
