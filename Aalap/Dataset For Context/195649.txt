Summary:
The River Boards Act, 1956, establishes a legal framework for the regulation and development of inter-State rivers and river valleys in India. The Act provides for the establishment of River Boards, which are corporate bodies with perpetual succession and a common seal. These Boards are responsible for advising governments on matters concerning the regulation or development of inter-State rivers and river valleys, as well as for performing other functions as specified in the notification establishing the Board. The Act also empowers the Central Government to allocate costs among the governments interested in the execution of a scheme prepared by the Board. The Board may exercise its powers and perform its functions within its area of operation, and must consult with the governments interested at all stages and endeavor to secure agreement among them. The Act also includes provisions for the appointment of advisory committees, the temporary association of persons with the Board, and the appointment of staff. The Board may acquire, hold, and dispose of property, undertake preliminary investigations or surveys, inspect works undertaken by any government, conduct and coordinate research, collect data, and publish statistics or other information. The Central Government may pay sums to the Board for the performance of its functions, and the Board shall have its own fund and may expend such sums as it thinks fit for performing its functions. The Board is required to prepare a budget and an annual report, which shall be forwarded to the Central Government and the governments interested. The accounts of the Board shall be audited at such time and in such manner as may be prescribed. The Act also includes provisions for arbitration, returns and reports, delegation of powers, members and officers of the Board to be public servants, protection of action taken in good faith, dissolution of the Board and transfer of assets and liabilities, power to make rules, and power to make regulations.
