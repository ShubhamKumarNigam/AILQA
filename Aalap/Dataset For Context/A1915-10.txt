Summary:

THE SIR JAMSETJEE JEJEEBHOY BARONETCY ACT, 1915, is an act that repeals the Act No. 20 of 1860 and resettles the Promissory Notes of the Government of India, producing an annual income of one lakh of rupees, and a Mansion House and hereditaments called Mazagon Castle, in the Island of Bombay. The act is designed to support the title and dignity of a Baronet, which was conferred on Sir Jamsetjee Jejeebhoy by Queen Victoria. The act also specifies that the heirs male of his body, who might succeed to the Baronetcy, should take and bear the names of "Jamsetjee Jejeebhoy" in lieu of any other name or names. The Revenue Commissioner for the Northern Division of the Presidency of Bombay, the Accountant-General, and the Sub-Treasurer at Bombay are appointed as Trustees for executing the powers and purposes of the act. The act also provides for the investment of the Promissory Notes and the sale of Mazagon Castle, subject to certain conditions and limitations. The act also includes provisions for the maintenance, education, and benefit of any person who may become entitled to the Baronetcy during his minority. Additionally, the act includes provisions for the discontinuation of the use of the names "Jamsetjee Jejeebhoy" and the consequences of such discontinuation. The act also includes provisions for jointures, the alienation of the estate, and the augmentation of the funds and securities subject to the Settlement. The act also includes provisions for the insurance of Mazagon Castle and other houses or buildings purchased in lieu thereof, the keeping of Mazagon Castle and other houses or buildings purchased in lieu thereof in repair, the sale of land subject to the Settlement, and the investment of the proceeds of sale of lands. The act also includes an indemnity of Trustees and a general saving clause.
