Summary:

The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to the prevention of corruption and for matters connected therewith. The Act defines various terms such as 'public duty', 'public servant', 'undue advantage', and 'corruption'. It also specifies the offences and penalties for corruption, including offences related to bribing a public servant, obtaining undue advantage, criminal misconduct by a public servant, and punishment for habitual offenders. The Act also provides for the appointment of special judges to try offences under the Act and sets out their powers and procedures. It also includes provisions for investigation into cases under the Act, including the power to inspect bankers' books, and the attachment and forfeiture of property. The Act also specifies the requirement for previous sanction for prosecution and includes provisions for appeal and revision. The Act applies to the whole of India and to all citizens of India outside India.
