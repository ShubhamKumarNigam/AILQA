The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (the "Act") is a law passed in India to regulate securitisation and reconstruction of financial assets and enforcement of security interest. The Act was enacted in response to recommendations from committees examining banking sector reforms, which identified the need for changes in the legal system to facilitate securitisation and empower banks and financial institutions to take possession of securities and sell them without court intervention.

The Act enables banks and financial institutions to realize long-term assets, manage liquidity, asset-liability mismatches, and improve recovery by exercising powers to take possession of securities, sell them, and reduce non-performing assets through recovery or reconstruction measures. The Act also provides for the registration and regulation of asset reconstruction companies by the Reserve Bank of India, facilitates securitisation of financial assets with or without underlying securities, and enables asset reconstruction companies to raise funds by issuing security receipts to qualified buyers.

The Act empowers banks and financial institutions to take possession of securities given for financial assistance and sell or lease them or take over management in the event of default. The rights of a secured creditor can be exercised by one or more of its officers authorized in this behalf. An appeal against the action of any bank or financial institution can be made to the Debts Recovery Tribunal and a second appeal to the Appellate Debts Recovery Tribunal.

The Act also sets up or causes to be set up a Central Registry for the purpose of registration of transactions relating to securitisation, asset reconstruction, and creation of security interest. The proposed legislation initially applies to banks and financial institutions and can be extended to non-banking financial companies and other entities by the Central Government.

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (Amendment) Act, 2004 amends the Act to require secured creditors to consider any representation made or objection raised by the borrower in response to a notice issued by the secured creditor under section 13 of the Act. The amendment also enables the borrower to make an application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal without making any deposit and provides for the transfer of pending applications to any one of the Debts Recovery Tribunal in certain cases.

The amendment also makes provision for an appeal to the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal after depositing with the Appellate Tribunal fifty per cent. of the amount of debt due from the borrower, as claimed by the secured creditor or determined by the Debts Recovery Tribunal, whichever is less. The amendment also enables the borrower residing in the State of Jammu and Kashmir to make an application to the Debts Recovery Tribunal.

In summary, the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 and its amendment in 2004 provide a legal framework for securitisation and reconstruction of financial assets and enforcement of security interest in India. The Act empowers banks and financial institutions to take possession of securities given for financial assistance and sell or lease them or take over management in the event of default. 1. Ceases to carry on the business of securitization or asset reconstruction.
2. Ceases to receive or hold any investment from a qualified buyer.
