The Food Safety and Standards (Fortification of Foods) Regulations, 2018, outlines the rules for adding essential micronutrients to food to improve their nutritional quality. These regulations, which will come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette, will apply to all food business operators, who must comply with all provisions by January 1, 2019.

The regulations define key terms, including "fortification," "fortificant," "fortified food," "government-funded program," "micronutrients," "quality assurance," "staple foods," and others. The definitions provided in these regulations will supersede any previous regulations, orders, or guidelines related to food fortification, except those related to nutraceuticals and foods for special dietary uses.

The regulations set out general principles for food fortification, including the prevention or reduction of nutrient deficiencies, improving nutritional status, meeting nutrient requirements, maintaining or improving health, and maintaining or improving the nutritional quality of foods. Mandatory food fortification will be based on the severity and extent of public health needs, as demonstrated by scientific evidence.

The regulations also establish standards for micronutrient content in fortified foods, with minimum levels specified in Schedule I. Manufacturers of fortified food must ensure that the level of micronutrients does not fall below these minimum levels.

Manufacturers and packers of fortified food must provide quality assurance, including certification by a food laboratory notified by the Food Authority, up-to-date record keeping, appropriate monitoring procedures, random testing of fortificants and fortified food, regular audits of technical equipment and processes, and good manufacturing practices.

All fortified food, whether voluntarily fortified or required to undergo mandatory fortification, must comply with the standards specified under the provisions of the Act and regulations made thereunder. Packaging and labeling requirements for fortified food include the words "fortified with [name of the fortificant]" and the logo specified in Schedule II, as well as compliance with the Food Safety and Standards (Packaging and Labeling) Regulations, 2011.

The regulations also encourage the production, manufacture, distribution, sale, and consumption of fortified food, including through conventional breeding or hybridization. The Food Authority will take steps to promote the use of fortified food in government-funded programs and conduct public awareness campaigns on nutrition and fortified food.
