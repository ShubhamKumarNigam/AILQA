Summary:
The Union Territories (Direct Election to the House of the People) Act, 1965, is a law that provides for direct election in certain Union territories, specifically the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli, for filling the seats allotted to them in the House of the People. The Act defines key terms, including "parliamentary constituency," "sitting member," and "Union territory."

Under the Act, the seats allotted to these Union territories in the House of the People are to be filled by persons chosen by direct election. Each Union territory forms one parliamentary constituency for this purpose. This provision applies to the next general election to the House of the People and all subsequent elections.

The Act also includes provisions for the sitting member representing each Union territory. The sitting member shall continue to represent the Union territory until the dissolution of the present House of the People. If the seat allotted to a Union territory in the House of the People becomes vacant before the dissolution of the present House, it shall be filled by a person nominated by the President. This person shall represent the Union territory in the present House of the People until its dissolution.

Two sections of the Act, Section 4 and Section 5, have been repealed. These sections originally amended the Representation of the People Act, 1950 and the Representation of the People Act, 1951, respectively. However, these amendments were later repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act, 1974.

The Act also includes a definition of "Union territory" that was amended by the Laccadive, Minicoy and Aminidivi Islands (Alteration of Name) Adaptation of Laws Order, 1974. This amendment substituted "Lakshadweep" for "Laccadive, Minicoy and Aminidivi Islands."
