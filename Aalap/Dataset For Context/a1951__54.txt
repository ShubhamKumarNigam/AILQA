Summary:
THE COMPANIES (DONATIONS TO NATIONAL FUNDS) ACT, 1951 is an Act that enables companies to make donations to National Funds in India. The Act applies to the whole of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act defines "company" as a company as defined in section 2 of the Companies Act, and includes a company deemed to be incorporated and registered under that Act by virtue of section 2B thereof. "The Companies Act" refers to the Indian Companies Act, 1913.

Under the Act, any company may make donations to the Gandhi National Memorial Fund or the Sardar Vallabhbhai National Memorial Fund, or to any other Fund established for a Charitable purpose which by reason of its national importance has been approved by the Central Government for the purposes of this section. The company can make such donations by passing an extraordinary resolution in accordance with the provisions contained in section 81 of the Companies Act, even if the memorandum or articles of association of the company do not enable it so to do.

The Act repealed the Companies Act, 1948. The Act is now applicable to the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir and the Union territory of Ladakh.
