THE MARINE INSURANCE ACT, 1963

The Marine Insurance Act, 1963 is an Act to codify the law relating to marine insurance. The Act defines various terms related to marine insurance, such as contract of marine insurance, freight, insurable property, marine adventure, maritime perils, movables, policy, ship, and suit.

The Act specifies that a contract of marine insurance must be embodied in a marine policy, which may be executed and issued either at the time when the contract is concluded or afterwards. A marine policy must specify the name of the assured, the subject-matter insured and the risk insured against, the voyage or period of time covered by the insurance, the sum or sums insured, and the name or names of the insurer or insurers.

The Act also lays down the rules for the construction of a policy, including the meaning of terms such as "lost and not lost," "from," "at and from," "from the loading thereof," "safely landed," "touch and stay," "perils of the seas," "pirates," "thieves," "restraint of princes," "barratry," "all other perils," "average unless general," "stranded," "ship," "freight," and "goods."

The Act provides for the avoidance of wagering contracts and defines insurable interest. It also specifies the circumstances under which an assured must disclose material facts to the insurer and the consequences of non-disclosure. The Act lays down the rules for the assignment of policies and the payment of premiums.

The Act defines total and partial losses and provides for the adjustment of claims in the case of partial losses. It also provides for the payment of general average contributions and salvage charges. The Act specifies the rights and liabilities of the insurer and the assured in the event of a loss, including the right of subrogation and the right of contribution.

The Act contains provisions for the return of premiums in certain cases and lays down the rules for the construction of a policy in the absence of any express agreement to the contrary. The Act also contains savings and repealing provisions.

In summary, the Marine Insurance Act, 1963 provides a comprehensive code for the regulation of marine insurance in India, defining various terms, setting out the rules for the construction of policies, specifying the rights and liabilities of the insurer and the assured, and providing for the adjustment of claims and the return of premiums in certain cases.
