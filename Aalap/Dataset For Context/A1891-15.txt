Summary:

The Moorshedabad Act, 1891, confirms and gives effect to an indenture made between the Secretary of State and the Nawab Bahadoor of Moorshedabad, Amir-ul-Omrah. The indenture was made on March 12, 1891, and is between the Secretary of State for India in Council and the Nawab Bahadoor of Moorshedabad, who is the eldest son of the late Nawab Nazim of Bengal, Behar, and Orissa.

The Act consists of six sections, and a schedule is attached to it. The schedule contains the text of the indenture, which outlines the terms of the agreement between the two parties. The indenture specifies the rights and obligations of both parties, including the payment of a personal stipend to the Nawab Nazim, the creation of certain funds, and the settlement of disputes related to the Nizamut.

Section 1 of the Act states that it may be called the Moorshedabad Act, 1891, and it shall come into force immediately. Section 2 confirms the indenture made on March 12, 1891. Section 3 allows the Governor General in Council to add immoveable property to the schedules of immoveable property annexed to the indenture, subject to certain conditions.

Section 4 states that no right to any immoveable property mentioned in the schedules or any addition made to them shall be acquired by any person by adverse possession or assertion of title unless such adverse possession or assertion of title is found to have existed for sixty years. Section 5 states that all property, moveable and immoveable, mentioned in the indenture or in any of the schedules or any addition made to them shall descend and be enjoyed for ever by the Nawab Bahadoor of Moorshedabad for the time being.

Section 6 states that the indenture shall for all the purposes of all enactments for the time being in force be admissible in evidence and have in all other respects the same effect as if it had been duly stamped and registered in such manner as those enactments, or any of them, or any rule or order under any of them, may require.

The schedule to the indenture specifies the jewels and immoveable property that are held by the Government of India for the purpose of upholding the dignity of the Nawab Nazim for the time being. It also specifies the amount due and owing in respect of the debts and liabilities incurred by the Nawab Nazim in India.

In summary, the Moorshedabad Act, 1891, confirms and gives effect to an indenture made between the Secretary of State and the Nawab Bahadoor of Moorshedabad, Amir-ul-Omrah, which outlines the terms of the agreement between the two parties, including the payment of a personal stipend to the Nawab Nazim, the creation of certain funds, and the settlement of disputes related to the Nizamut. The Act also specifies the rights and obligations of both parties, including the addition of immoveable property to the schedules of immoveable property annexed to the indenture, the limitation of claims to scheduled immoveable property, and the perpetual descent of property.
