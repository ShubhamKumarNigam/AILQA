Summary:
THE PRIVATE SECURITY AGENCIES (REGULATION) ACT, 2005, is an act that provides for the regulation of private security agencies and their activities. The act defines private security as security provided by a person, other than a public servant, to protect or guard any person or property or both. The act also defines private security agency as a person or body of persons other than a government agency, department or organization engaged in the business of providing private security services including training to private security guards or their supervisors or providing private security guards to any industrial or business undertaking or a company or any other person or property.

The act establishes a Controlling Authority to regulate and monitor the activities of private security agencies. The Controlling Authority is appointed by the State Government and has the power to grant, renew, suspend or cancel the license of private security agencies.

The act prohibits any person or private security agency from engaging or providing private security guard without a license. The act also prohibits private security agencies from providing private security abroad without obtaining permission from the Controlling Authority.

To obtain a license, an application must be made to the Controlling Authority in the prescribed form, accompanied by the prescribed fee. The Controlling Authority may grant or refuse to grant a license after making necessary inquiries and obtaining a no-objection certificate from the concerned police authority.

The act lays down certain conditions for the commencement of operation and engagement of supervisors by private security agencies. Every private security agency must ensure imparting of training and skills to its private security guards and supervisors as prescribed by the rules. The agency must also employ a specified number of supervisors within a specified time period.

The act specifies the eligibility and qualifications required for a person to be employed as a private security guard. A private security agency shall not employ or engage any person as a private security guard unless he fulfills the conditions specified in section 10.

The act lays down certain conditions for the grant, renewal, suspension or cancellation of a license. The Controlling Authority may cancel a license on various grounds, including misrepresentation or suppression of material facts, violation of the provisions of the act or rules, misuse of information, impersonation, failure to commence activities or engage a supervisor within the specified time period, and violation of court orders or laws.

The act provides for an appeal against the order of the Controlling Authority refusing the license, renewal, suspension or cancellation of a license to the Home Secretary of the State Government.

Every private security agency shall maintain a register containing the names and addresses of the persons managing the private security agency, the names, addresses, photographs, and salaries of the private security guards and supervisors under its control, the names and addresses of the persons whom it had provided private security guards or services, and such other particulars as may be prescribed.

The act empowers the Controlling Authority or any other officer authorized by it to enter the premises of the private security agency and inspect and examine the place of business, the records, accounts, and other documents connected with the license and take copies of any document.

The act requires every private security guard to be issued a photo identity card by the private security agency employing or engaging the guard. The photo identity card shall be in such form as may be prescribed. Every private security guard or supervisor shall carry on his person the photo identity card issued under sub-section (1) and shall produce it on demand for inspection by the Controlling Authority or any other officer authorized by it in this behalf.

The act prohibits any person who may be or has been employed or engaged as a private security guard by the private security agency from divulging to anyone other than the employer any information acquired by him during such employment with respect to the work which he has been assigned by such employer, except such disclosure as may be required under this Act or in connection with any inquiry or investigation by the police or as may be required by an authority or process of law.

The act empowers the State Government to delegate its powers or functions under this Act to any officer or authority subordinate to the Government or officer subordinate to the Controlling Authority.

The act provides for punishment for contravention of certain provisions. Any person who contravenes the provisions of section 4 shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year, or with fine which may extend to twenty-five thousand rupees, or with both. Any person or private security agency who contravenes the provisions of sections 9, 10, and 12 of the Act shall be punishable with a fine which may extend to twenty-five thousand rupees, in addition to suspension or cancellation of the license.

The act prohibits any private security guard or supervisor from wearing the uniform of the Army, Air force, Navy, or any other armed forces of the Union or Police or any dress having the appearance or bearing any of the distinctive marks of that uniform. Any person or private security agency who contravenes this provision shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year or with fine which may extend to five thousand rupees, or with both.

The act provides that where an offence under this Act has been committed by a company, every person who at the time the offence was committed was in charge of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of the business of the company as well as the company, shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.

The act provides that no suit, prosecution, or other legal proceeding shall lie against the Controlling authority or any other officer authorized by it in respect of anything in good faith done or intended to be done under this Act.

The act empowers the Central Government to frame model rules in respect of all or any of the matters with respect to which the State Government may make rules under this Act. The State Government shall, while making any rules in respect of that matter under section 25, so far as is practicable, conform to such model rules.

The act empowers the State Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act. Every rule made by the State Government under this section shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of the State Legislature where it consists of two Houses, or where such Legislature consists of one House, before that House.

The act includes a schedule listing various acts, including The Payment of Wages Act, 1936, The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, The Minimum Wages Act, 1948, The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, The Payment of Bonus Act, 1965, The Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, The Equal Remuneration Act, 1976, and The Inter-State Migrant Workmen (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1979, which shall apply to private security agencies.
