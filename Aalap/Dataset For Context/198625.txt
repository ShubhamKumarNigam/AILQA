Summary:
THE MUSLIM WOMEN (PROTECTION OF RIGHTS ON DIVORCE) ACT, 1986, ACT NO. 25 OF 1986, is an Act to protect the rights of Muslim women who have been divorced by, or have obtained divorce from, their husbands. The Act applies to the whole of India and includes definitions for terms such as "divorced woman," "iddat period," "Magistrate," and "prescribed." The Act outlines the rights of a divorced Muslim woman, including a reasonable and fair provision and maintenance within the iddat period, an amount equal to the sum of mahr or dower agreed to at the time of marriage, and all properties given to her before or at the time of marriage. If these provisions and maintenance or the amount of mahr or dower due have not been made or paid or the properties referred to have not been delivered, a divorced woman or anyone authorized by her may make an application to a Magistrate for an order for payment or delivery. The Magistrate may make an order within one month of the date of filing of the application, directing the former husband to pay reasonable and fair provision and maintenance or for the payment of such mahr or dower or the delivery of such properties. If the person against whom an order has been made fails to comply with the order, the Magistrate may issue a warrant for levying the amount of maintenance or mahr or dower due and may sentence such person to imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year or until payment if sooner made. The Act also provides for an order for payment of maintenance by relatives of the divorced woman who would be entitled to inherit her property on her death according to Muslim law. If the divorced woman has children, the Magistrate shall order only such children to pay maintenance to her. If any of the parents is unable to pay his or her share of the maintenance ordered by the Magistrate, the Magistrate may order that the share of such relatives in the maintenance ordered by him be paid by such of the other relatives as may appear to the Magistrate to have the means of paying the same. The Act also provides for the option to be governed by the provisions of sections 125 to 128 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act, including the form of the affidavit or other declaration in writing, the procedure to be followed by the Magistrate in disposing of applications, and any other matter which is required to be or may be prescribed. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. Every application by a divorced woman under section 125 or under section 127 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 pending before a Magistrate on the commencement of this Act, shall continue to be governed by the provisions of that Code as if this Act had not been passed.
