Summary of THE ORIENTAL GAS COMPANY Act 1857:

The Oriental Gas Company Act 1857, also known as Act No. 5 of 1857, was enacted on 13th February 1857. This Act confers certain powers on the Oriental Gas Company, Limited, which was formed to introduce gas-works into India. The Act allows the Company to open and break up the soil and pavement of streets and bridges, lay down pipes, conduits, and other works, and repair, alter, or remove the same. The Company may also make sewers for carrying off washings and waste liquids that may arise in the making of gas.

The Act specifies that the Company may not lay down or place any pipe or other works into, through, or against any building or land not dedicated to public use without the consent of the owners and occupiers thereof. The Company is required to give notice to the Municipal Commissioners or other persons under whose control or management the streets and bridges may be, before breaking up any street, bridge, sewer, drain, or tunnel. The Act also lays down the procedure for superintendence and plan approval for breaking up streets and opening drains.

The Act requires the Company to reinstate and make good the road or pavement, or the sewer, drain, or tunnel so opened or broken up, and to carry away the rubbish occasioned thereby. The Company is liable to pay a penalty for any delay in completing any such work or in filling in the ground or reinstating and making good the road or pavement, or the sewer, drain, or tunnel so opened or broken up.

The Act allows the Company's Clerk, Engineer, or other officer duly appointed for the purpose to enter any buildings or place lighted with Gas supplied by the Company to inspect the meters, fittings, and works for regulating the supply of Gas, and for the purpose of ascertaining the quantity of Gas consumed or supplied. The Company may stop the Gas from entering the premises of any person who neglects to pay the rent due for the same and recover the rent due together with the expenses of cutting off the Gas by action in any Court of competent jurisdiction.

The Act authorizes the Company, their agents or workmen, after giving twenty-four hours’ previous notice to the occupier, to enter into any house, building, or premises and remove and carry away any pipe, meter, fittings, or other works, the property of the Company, in cases where the supply of Gas is discontinued. The Act also specifies that any meter or fitting let for hire by the Company shall not be subject to distress for rent or revenue or any rate due upon the premises where the same may be used, nor be taken in execution under any process of a Court of law or equity, or any proceeding in insolvency against the person in whose possession the same may be.

The Act imposes a penalty for fraudulently using gas or damaging pipes, and for causing water to be corrupted. The Act also specifies the procedure for examining Gas-pipes to ascertain the cause of water being fouled, and the expenses to abide by the result of the examination.

The Act provides that nothing in this Act shall prevent the Company from being liable to an indictment for nuisance, or to any other legal proceedings to which they may be liable in consequence of making or supplying Gas. The Act requires the Company to keep a copy of the original Deed of Association, and of every other instrument registered under the said “Joint Stock Companies’ Act, 1856”, as constituting the Regulations of the Company, and a copy of every special resolution of a General Meeting whereby any change shall have been, or at any time shall be made in the Regulations of the Company, at the office of the said Company in Calcutta, and shall there be open to the inspection of all persons during the usual hours of business of the said office.

The Act specifies the service of process and the recovery of penalties, damages, and expenses. The Act also provides for the interpretation of certain words and expressions used in this Act. The Act includes a schedule of penalties for offences committed under this Act.
