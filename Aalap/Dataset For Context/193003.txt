Summary:
The Sale of Goods Act, 1930 (Act No. 3 of 1930), is an Indian law that defines and amends the law relating to the sale of goods. The Act has 7 chapters and 66 sections, including provisions related to the formation of the contract, transfer of property, performance of the contract, rights of unpaid seller against the goods, suits for breach of the contract, and miscellaneous provisions. The Act also includes definitions of various legal terms related to the sale of goods, such as buyer, delivery, goods, insolvency, mercantile agent, price, property, quality of goods, seller, and specific goods. The Act applies to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act defines the rights and obligations of buyers and sellers in a sales contract, and provides remedies for breach of contract. The Act also recognizes the concept of implied terms and conditions, which can be negated or varied by express agreement or the course of dealing between the parties. The Act also provides for the calculation of interest and special damages in certain cases.
