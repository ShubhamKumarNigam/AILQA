Summary:
The Chartered Accountants Act, 1949, establishes the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India to regulate the profession of chartered accountants. The Act defines a chartered accountant as a member of the Institute and sets out the qualifications and requirements for membership. The Act also establishes a Council to manage the affairs of the Institute and make regulations for its functioning. The Council is responsible for maintaining a Register of members, granting certificates of practice, and conducting disciplinary proceedings against members for professional misconduct. The Act also sets out penalties for falsely claiming to be a member of the Institute or using the name of the Institute without authorization. The Act provides for the establishment of Regional Councils to advise and assist the Council on matters concerning its functions. The Act also includes provisions for the recognition of foreign qualifications and the establishment of a Quality Review Board to review the quality of services provided by members of the Institute. The Act also includes provisions for the appointment of an Appellate Authority to hear appeals against orders of the Board of Discipline or the Disciplinary Committee.
