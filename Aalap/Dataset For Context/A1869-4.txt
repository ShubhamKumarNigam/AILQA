Summary:
The Divorce Act, 1869, is an Act to amend the law relating to Divorce and Matrimonial Causes. This Act extends to the whole of India and confers jurisdiction in matters matrimonial to certain courts. The Act defines various terms used throughout, such as "High Court", "District Judge", "District Court", and "Court". The Act is divided into several sections, each addressing different aspects of divorce and matrimonial causes. Section 10 outlines the grounds for dissolution of marriage, including adultery, conversion to another religion, unsound mind, venereal disease, disappearance, wilful refusal to consummate the marriage, failure to comply with a decree for restitution of conjugal rights, desertion, and cruelty. Section 10A allows for dissolution of marriage by mutual consent. Section 11 requires that the adulterer or adulteress be made a co-respondent in a petition for divorce based on adultery, unless excused by the court. Section 12 mandates that the court be satisfied of the absence of collusion and inquire into any countercharge made against the petitioner. Section 13 outlines the grounds for dismissal of a petition for dissolution of marriage. Section 14 grants the court the power to decree dissolution of marriage under certain conditions. Section 15 allows for relief in case of opposition on certain grounds. Section 16 states that decrees for dissolution of marriage made by a High Court shall, in the first instance, be decrees nisi, not to be made absolute until after the expiration of a specified time. Section 17 allows for the removal of certain suits to the High Court. Section 18 allows for a petition for decree of nullity, and Section 19 outlines the grounds for such a decree. Section 20, which has been omitted, allowed for the confirmation of a District Judge's decree. Section 21 states that children of an annulled marriage are entitled to succeed to the estate of the parent who was competent to contract at the time of the marriage. Section 22 allows for a petition for judicial separation, and Section 23 outlines the grounds for such a petition. Section 24 states that a wife who has obtained a decree of judicial separation shall, during the continuance of the separation, be considered as unmarried with respect to property, contracts, and suing and being sued. Section 25 allows for a wife to be deemed an unmarried woman for the purposes of contract, wrongs and injuries, and suing and being sued. Section 26 allows for the reversal of a decree of judicial separation obtained during the absence of the husband or wife. Section 27 allows for a deserted wife to apply to the District Court for a protection-order to protect her property from her husband or his creditors. Section 28 allows for the court to grant a protection-order if satisfied of the fact of desertion and that it was without reasonable excuse. Section 29 allows for the discharge or variation of an order. Section 30 holds liable any person who seizes or continues to hold any property of the wife after notice of an order. Section 31 states that during the continuance of an order of protection, the wife shall be in the same position as if she obtained a decree of judicial separation. Section 32 allows for a petition for restitution of conjugal rights, and Section 33 outlines the answer to such a petition. Section 34, which has been omitted, allowed for the husband to claim damages from the adulterer. Section 35, which has also been omitted, allowed for the court to order the adulterer to pay costs. Section 36 allows for a petition for alimony pendente lite, and Section 37 allows for the court to order permanent alimony. Section 38 allows for the court to direct that alimony be paid to the wife or to a trustee on her behalf. Section 39, which has been omitted, allowed for the court to inquire into the existence of ante-nuptial or post-nuptial settlements and make orders accordingly. Section 40 allows for the court to make orders for the custody, maintenance, and education of minor children in suits for separation, dissolution, or nullity. Section 41 allows for interim orders to be made, and Section 42 allows for such orders to be made after a decree of judicial separation. Section 43 allows for the court to make orders for the custody, maintenance, and education of minor children in suits for dissolution or nullity. Section 44 allows for the court to make orders for the custody, maintenance, and education of minor children after a decree of dissolution or nullity. Section 45 states that all proceedings under this Act shall be regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure. Section 46 allows for the use of certain forms for the respective purposes mentioned in the Schedule. Section 47 requires that every petition under this Act shall state that there is not any collusion or connivance between the petitioner and the other party to the marriage. Section 48 allows for suits on behalf of lunatics, and Section 49 allows for suits by minors. Section 50 requires that every petition under this Act shall be served on the party to be affected, either within or without India, in such manner as the High Court directs. Section 51 outlines the mode of taking evidence, and Section 52 allows for the husband and wife to be competent and compellable to give evidence of or relating to cruelty or desertion. Section 53 allows for the court to close the doors of the court. Section 54 allows for the court to adjourn the hearing of any petition. Section 55 outlines the enforcement and appeals from orders and decrees, and Section 56 allows for an appeal to the Supreme Court. Section 57 allows for liberty to parties to marry again after a decree for dissolution or nullity has become final. Section 58 states that no clergyman in Holy Orders of the Church of England shall be compelled to solemnize the marriage of any person whose former marriage has been dissolved on the ground of his or her adultery. Section 59 allows for a Minister of the Church of England to permit any other Minister in Holy Orders of the Church to perform a marriage-service in his church or chapel. Section 60 states that every decree for judicial separation or order to protect property obtained by a wife under this Act shall, until reversed or discharged, be deemed valid. Section 61 bars a suit for criminal conversation after this Act comes into operation. Section 62 allows for the High Court to make rules under this Act. The Schedule contains forms for petitions and statements.
