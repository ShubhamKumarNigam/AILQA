THE ORPHANAGES AND OTHER CHARITABLE HOMES (SUPERVISION AND CONTROL) ACT, 1960

This Act, enacted on 9th April, 1960, aims to provide for the supervision and control of orphanages, homes for neglected women or children, and other similar institutions. The Act consists of five chapters.

Chapter I: Preliminary

* This chapter defines the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act.
* It also provides definitions for various terms used throughout the Act.

Chapter II: The Board of Control and Its Powers and Functions

* This chapter establishes a Board of Control for the supervision and control of homes in the State.
* It outlines the composition, term of office, and functions of the Board.
* The Board has the power to give directions to the manager of a recognized home and conduct inspections.

Chapter III: Recognition of Homes

* This chapter mandates that no person shall maintain or conduct any home except under, and in accordance with, the conditions of a certificate of recognition granted under this Act.
* It outlines the process for applying for and granting certificates, as well as the conditions that must be met by recognized homes.

Chapter IV: Management of Recognized Homes

* This chapter outlines the management of recognized homes, including the appointment of a managing committee and a manager.
* The manager is responsible for complying with all the requirements of this Act and the rules, regulations, directions, and orders thereunder in respect of every woman or child admitted into the recognized home.

Chapter V: Miscellaneous

* This chapter outlines various miscellaneous provisions, including penalties for non-compliance, sanction for prosecutions, protection of acts done in good faith, and the power of the State Government to exempt homes from the operation of the Act.
* The Act also provides for the making of rules and regulations by the State Government and the Board, respectively.

In summary, THE ORPHANAGES AND OTHER CHARITABLE HOMES (SUPERVISION AND CONTROL) ACT, 1960, provides for the supervision and control of orphanages, homes for neglected women or children, and other similar institutions. The Act establishes a Board of Control, outlines the process for recognizing homes, and provides for the management and regulation of recognized homes. The Act also includes provisions for penalties, sanctions, and exemptions.
