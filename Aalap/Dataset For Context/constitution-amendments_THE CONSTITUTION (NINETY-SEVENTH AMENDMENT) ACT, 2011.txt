Summary:

The Constitution (Ninety-Seventh Amendment) Act, 2011 aims to revitalize the cooperative sector in India, which has made significant contributions to the national economy but has shown weaknesses in protecting the interests of members and fulfilling the objectives for which these institutions were organized. The Act seeks to ensure the autonomous, democratic functioning and professional management of cooperative societies by keeping them free from unnecessary outside interference. The Act empowers the Parliament to make laws for multi-State cooperative societies and State Legislatures to make laws for other cooperative societies, covering vital aspects of their working such as democratic member-control, member-economic participation, autonomous functioning, and more. The Act also lays down provisions for incorporation, regulation, and winding up of cooperative societies, a fixed term of five years for elected members of the board and its office bearers, independent professional audit, right of information to the members of the cooperative societies, periodic reports of activities and accounts of cooperative societies, reservation of seats for the Scheduled Castes or the Scheduled Tribes and women on the board of every cooperative society, and offences relating to cooperative societies and penalties in respect of such offences. The Act is expected to ensure the autonomous and democratic functioning of cooperatives, accountability of management to the members and other stakeholders, and provide for deterrence for violation of the provisions of the law.
