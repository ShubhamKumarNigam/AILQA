Summary:
THE CARRIAGE BY ROAD ACT, 2007

The Carriage by Road Act, 2007, is an Act that regulates common carriers, limiting their liability for loss or damage to goods. The Act defines a common carrier as a person engaged in the business of collecting, storing, forwarding, or distributing goods to be carried by goods carriages under a goods receipt or transporting for hire of goods from place to place by motorized transport on road, undiscriminatingly for all persons. The Act applies to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and came into force on 29th September, 2007.

The Act lays down provisions for the registration of common carriers, specifying that no person shall engage in the business of a common carrier without a certificate of registration. The Act also specifies the conditions for granting or renewing a certificate of registration and the validity period of such certificates. The holder of a certificate of registration is required to maintain a register, display a certificate of registration, and submit annual returns to the registering authority.

The Act specifies the liability of common carriers for loss or damage to goods, which is limited to a prescribed amount based on the value, freight, and nature of goods, documents, or articles of the consignment, unless the consignor or any person duly authorized has expressly undertaken to pay a higher risk rate fixed by the common carrier. The Act also specifies the conditions under which a common carrier may be exonerated from liability.

The Act prohibits the carriage of goods of dangerous or hazardous nature to human life by a common carrier, except in accordance with prescribed procedure and safeguards. The Act also empowers the Central Government to specify, in public interest, the goods or class of goods that shall not be carried by a common carrier.

The Act specifies the rights of common carriers in case of consignee's default and the procedure for the disposal of unclaimed consignments. The Act also specifies the notice period for instituting a suit against a common carrier for any loss or damage to goods.

The Act specifies the general responsibility of common carriers and the circumstances under which they are not liable for loss, destruction, damage, or deterioration in transit or non-delivery of any consignment entrusted to them for carriage. The Act also specifies the punishment for contravention in relation to non-registration, carrying goods of dangerous or hazardous nature, or prohibited goods.

The Act empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act, and specifies the procedure for compounding of offences. The Act also empowers the Central Government to remove any difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of this Act.

The Act repeals the Carriers Act, 1865, and specifies that anything done or any action taken under the said Act shall, insofar as such thing or action is not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, be deemed to have been done or taken under the provisions of this Act and shall continue in force accordingly until superseded by anything done or any action taken under this Act.
