Summary:

The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (Act No. 1 of 2010) came into force on 13th January, 2010. The Act establishes and enforces standards of weights and measures, regulates trade and commerce in weights, measures and other goods which are sold or distributed by weight, measure or number and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

The Act defines various terms used in it, such as 'Controller', 'dealer', 'Director', 'export', 'import', 'label', 'Legal Metrology', 'legal metrology officer', 'manufacturer', 'notification', 'protection', 'pre-packaged commodity', 'person', 'premises', 'prescribed', 'repairer', 'State Government', 'sale', 'seal', 'stamp', 'transaction', 'verification', and 'weight or measure'.

Chapter I of the Act deals with preliminary provisions, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act, and the definitions of various terms used in the Act.

Chapter II of the Act deals with standard weights and measures. The Act specifies that every unit of weight or measure shall be in accordance with the metric system based on the international system of units. The base units of length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, luminous intensity, and amount of substance are specified in section 5. The specifications of the base units, derived units, and other units are prescribed by the Central Government. The base unit of numeration is the unit of the international form of Indian numerals, and every numeration shall be made in accordance with the decimal system. The decimal multiples and sub-multiples of the numerals shall be of such denominations and be written in such manner as may be prescribed.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the appointment and powers of the Director, Controller, and legal metrology officers. The Central Government may appoint a Director of Legal Metrology, Additional Director, Joint Director, Deputy Director, Assistant Director, and other employees for exercising the powers and discharging the duties conferred or imposed on them by or under this Act in relation to inter-State trade and commerce. The qualifications of the Director and legal metrology officers appointed under section 13 shall be such as may be prescribed. The Director and every legal metrology officer appointed under section 13 shall exercise such powers and discharge such functions in respect of such local limits as the Central Government may, by notification, specify. The Director, the Controller, and every legal metrology officer authorized to perform any duty by or under this Act shall be deemed to be a public servant within the meaning of section 21 of the Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860).

The State Government may appoint a Controller of Legal Metrology, Additional Controller, Joint Controller, Deputy Controller, Assistant Controller, Inspector, and other employees for the State for exercising the powers and discharging the duties conferred or imposed on them by or under this Act in relation to intra-State trade and commerce. The qualifications of the Controller and legal metrology officers appointed under section 14 shall be such as may be prescribed. The Controller and every legal metrology officer appointed under section 14 shall exercise such powers and discharge such functions in respect of such local limits as the State Government may, by notification, specify. Every legal metrology officer appointed under section 14 shall exercise and discharge the duties under the general superintendence, direction, and control of the Controller.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the verification and stamping of weights and measures. Every person having any weight or measure in his possession, custody, or control in circumstances indicating that such weight or measure is being, or is intended or is likely to be, used by him in any transaction or for protection, shall, before putting such weight or measure into such use, have such weight or measure verified at such place and during such hours as the Controller may, by general or special order, specify in this behalf, on payment of such fees as may be prescribed.

Chapter V of the Act deals with offences and penalties. The Act provides for penalties for the use of non-standard weights or measures, alteration of weights and measures, manufacture or sale of non-standard weights or measures, making any transaction, deal, or contract in contravention of the prescribed standards, quoting or publishing, etc., of non-standard units, selling, etc., of non-standard packages, and obstructing the Director, Controller, or legal metrology officer. The Act also provides for compounding of offences and penalties for counterfeiting of seals, etc., manufacture of weight and measure without licence, repair, sale, etc., of weight and measure without licence, tampering with licence, and offences by companies.

Chapter VI of the Act deals with miscellaneous provisions. The Act provides for appeals, the power of the Central Government to make rules, the power of the State Government to make rules, delegation of powers, act not to apply in certain cases, existing Director, Controller, and legal metrology officer not to be affected by the new qualification to be prescribed, repeal of the Standards of Weights and Measures Act, 1976 and Standards of Weights and Measures (Enforcement) Act, 1985, and savings and transitional provisions.

The Act is a comprehensive legislation dealing with the standards of weights and measures and their verification and stamping. The Act aims to ensure accuracy and uniformity in the measurement of weights and measures and to prevent fraud and deception in trade and commerce. The Act provides for stringent penalties for the violation of its provisions and also provides for the compounding of offences. The Act also provides for the appointment and powers of the Director, Controller, and legal metrology officers for enforcing the provisions of the Act. The Act is a significant piece of legislation for ensuring fair trade practices and protecting the interests of consumers.
