Summary:

THE AFRICAN DEVELOPMENT BANK ACT, 1983 (ACT NO.13 OF 1983) is a law passed in India to implement the international agreement for the establishment and operation of the African Development Bank. The Act, which extends to the whole of India, came into force on December 15, 1983.

The Act contains five sections, with the first section outlining the short title, extent, and commencement of the law. The second section defines key terms used in the Act, including "Agreement" and "Bank." The third section outlines the requirement for payments to the Bank, including subscriptions payable by the Central Government to the Bank under Articles 6, 7, 10, and 21 of the Agreement, as well as any sums payable by the Central Government to the Bank under Article 28 of the Agreement. The Central Government may also create and issue non-interest bearing and non-negotiable notes or other obligations to the Bank.

The fourth section of the Act states that the Reserve Bank of India shall be the depository of the Indian currency holdings of the Bank. The fifth section confers status and certain immunities, exemptions, and privileges on the Bank and its officers and employees, and provides for the conferment of certain immunities, exemptions, and privileges on its officers and employees. The Central Government may amend the Schedule in conformity with any amendments, duly made and adopted, of the provisions of the Agreement set out therein.

The Act also includes a Schedule, which sets out the provisions of the Agreement establishing the African Development Bank and the status, immunities, exemptions, and privileges accorded to the Bank in the territory of each member. These provisions include full judicial personality, immunity from legal process, immunity of assets and archives from restriction, freedom of assets from restriction, privilege for communications, personal immunities and privileges, exemption from taxation, notification of implementation, and application of immunities, exemptions, and privileges. The Board of Directors may waive the immunities and exemptions provided in the Agreement in cases where its action would further the interests of the Bank. The President has the right and the duty to waive the immunity of any official in cases where the immunity would impede the course of justice and can be waived without prejudice to the interests of the Bank.
