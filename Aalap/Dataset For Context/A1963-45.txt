THE ADMINISTRATORS-GENERAL ACT, 1963

The Administrators-General Act, 1963 is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to the office and duties of Administrators-General. The Act defines various legal terms, including "assets," "letters of administration," "next-of-kin," and "prescribed."

Chapter I of the Act deals with preliminary matters, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act.

Chapter II establishes the office of the Administrator-General, including the appointment, powers, and duties of the Administrator-General and Deputy Administrator-General. The Administrator-General is a corporation sole by the name of Administrator-General of the State for which he is appointed, and has perpetual succession and an official seal.

Chapter III outlines the rights, powers, and duties of the Administrator-General, including the grant of letters of administration and probate, revocation of grants, and the distribution of assets.

Chapter IV deals with the grant of a certificate by the Administrator-General in certain cases where the value of the assets left by the deceased does not exceed a certain amount.

Chapter V deals with the liability of the Government, including the circumstances under which the Government is liable to make good all sums required to discharge any liability of the Administrator-General.

Chapter VI sets out the fees charged in respect of the duties of the Administrator-General, including the disposal of fees.

Chapter VII establishes the audit of the Administrator-General’s accounts, including the powers of auditors to summon and examine witnesses, and to call for documents.

Chapter VIII deals with miscellaneous matters, including the general powers of administration, the power to summon and examine witnesses, the power of person beneficially interested to inspect the Administrator-General’s account, and the transfer of assets to the Government.

The Act also includes a schedule of rules made by the State Government for carrying out the purposes of the Act.
