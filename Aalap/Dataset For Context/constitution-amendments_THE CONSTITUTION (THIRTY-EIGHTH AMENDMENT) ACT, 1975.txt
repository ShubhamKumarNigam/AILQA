Insertion of new article 352A.-

After article 352 of the Constitution, the following article shall be inserted, namely:-

"352A. The President may by Proclamation-

(a) on a ground of internal disturbance beyond the normal proportions, declare that the security of India or any part of the territory thereof is threatened by such disturbance;

(b) on a ground of internal disturbance within the meaning of clause (c) of article 352, declare that the security of India or any part of the territory thereof is threatened by such disturbance:

Provided that nothing in this article shall empower the President to issue a Proclamation on the ground specified in clause (a) or clause (b) of this article unless the decision of the Cabinet in this regard, indicating the grounds on which such Proclamation is proposed to be issued, has been communicated to him in writing.

Explanation.-

For the purposes of this article, the expression "internal disturbance" does not include any disturbance owing to any industrial dispute or any industrial strike or lockout or any industrial disorder or any industrial unrest."




Amendment of article 359.-

In article 359 of the Constitution, in clause (1), for the words "he may suspend", the words "he may, by order, declare that the right to move any court for the enforcement of such of the rights conferred by Part III as may be mentioned in the order" shall be substituted.




Amendment of article 359A.-

In article 359A of the Constitution, in clause (1), for the words "he may suspend", the words "he may, by order, declare that the right to move any court for the enforcement of such of the rights conferred by Part III as may be mentioned in the order" shall be substituted.




Insertion of new article 359B.-

After article 359A of the Constitution, the following article shall be inserted, namely:-

"359B. (1) While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation, the President may by order declare that the right to move any court for the enforcement of such of the rights conferred by Part III as may be mentioned in the order shall remain suspended.

(2) Every such order shall specify the rights so suspended and the extent to which and the duration for which the enforcement of such rights shall remain suspended and may also provide for excluding the application of the provisions of any law for the time being in force relating to the enforcement of such rights.

(3) Notwithstanding anything in clause (2), no such order shall remain in force for more than six months unless it is approved by resolutions of both Houses of Parliament before the expiration of the six-month period and notwithstanding anything in article 123, no resolution approving the continuance in force of any such order shall be passed by either House of Parliament unless a motion making provision for the consideration of such resolution has been made by a Minister and moved and seconded in that House and the motion has been carried by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting.

(4) Any law made by Parliament or by any State Legislature in exercise of the powers under clause (1) of article 358 or clause (1) of article 359A, as the case may be, shall, notwithstanding anything in this Constitution, cease to have effect immediately on the expiration of a period of six months beginning with the date of the repeal of the Proclamation issuing under article 352 or article 356, as the case may be, or, if before the expiration of that period a resolution approving the continuance in force of the law has been passed by both Houses of Parliament, until the date on which the law ceases to operate in pursuance of that resolution.

(5) For the removal of doubts, it is hereby declared that nothing in this article shall affect the provisions of article 359A."
