Summary:
The Passports Act, 1967 is an Act to provide for the issue of passports and travel documents, to regulate the departure from India of citizens of India and other persons and for matters incidental or ancillary thereto. The Act defines terms such as "departure," "passport," "passport authority," "prescribed," and "travel document." It outlines the classes of passports and travel documents that may be issued under this Act and specifies the classes of persons to whom they may be issued. The Act also lays down the procedure for applications for passports, travel documents, and endorsements, and the powers of the passport authority to refuse or grant such applications. It specifies the grounds for refusal of passports or travel documents and the conditions and forms of passports and travel documents. The Act also empowers the passport authority to vary, impound, or revoke passports and travel documents under certain circumstances. The Act provides for appeals against orders of the passport authority and lays down the penalties for offences under this Act. The Act also empowers the Central Government to delegate its powers to officers or authorities and to exempt any person or class of persons from the operation of the Act. The Act is in addition to and not in derogation of certain other enactments relating to foreigners and foreign exchange. The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
