Summary:
The Industrial Development Bank (Transfer of Undertaking and Repeal) Act, 2003, transfers the undertaking of the Industrial Development Bank of India (Development Bank) to a newly formed company, the Industrial Development Bank of India Limited (Company). This transfer includes all business, assets, rights, powers, authorities, and privileges, as well as all properties, liabilities, and obligations of the Development Bank. On the appointed day, the Central Government, along with other shareholders of the Development Bank, becomes shareholders of the Company. Officers and employees of the Development Bank continue their employment in the Company under the same terms and conditions, with the exception of directors, chairman, managing director, and whole-time directors. The Company is deemed to be a banking company under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, but is not required to obtain a license or maintain a percentage of assets for five years. The Banking Regulation Act, 1949, applies to the Company with certain exceptions, modifications, and adaptations. The Act has overriding effect over any other law or instrument, and its provisions are in addition to and not in derogation of any other law. The Industrial Development Bank of India Act, 1964, stands repealed on the appointed day, except for the provisions related to arrangements entered into by the Development Bank with industrial concerns, which continue to be applicable to the Company. The Act also grants power to the Central Government to remove any difficulties in implementing the provisions of the Act for a period of three years from the date of commencement.
