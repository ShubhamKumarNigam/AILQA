Summary:

The Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 (Act No. 34 of 1971) is an Act that provides for the termination of certain pregnancies by registered medical practitioners and related matters. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government.

The Act defines certain terms such as "guardian," "mentally ill person," "minor," and "registered medical practitioner." A registered medical practitioner is a medical practitioner who possesses a recognized medical qualification, whose name is entered in a State Medical Register, and who has the required experience or training in gynaecology and obstetrics.

Under the Act, pregnancies may be terminated by a registered medical practitioner if the length of the pregnancy does not exceed twelve weeks, or if the length of the pregnancy exceeds twelve weeks but does not exceed twenty weeks, and if not less than two registered medical practitioners are of the opinion that the continuance of the pregnancy would involve a risk to the life of the pregnant woman or of grave injury to her physical or mental health, or there is a substantial risk that if the child were born, it would suffer from such physical or mental abnormalities as to be seriously handicapped.

The Act sets out the places where pregnancy may be terminated, which include hospitals established or maintained by the Government, or a place approved for the purpose of this Act by the Government or a District Level Committee constituted by that Government.

The Act provides for exceptions to sections 3 and 4 in cases where the termination of pregnancy is immediately necessary to save the life of the pregnant woman.

The Act also sets out penalties for terminating pregnancy in a place other than those mentioned in section 4, owning a place that is not approved under clause (b) of section 4, and contravening or failing to comply with the requirements of any regulation made under sub-section (1).

The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act, and the State Government may make regulations to implement the Act. No suit or other legal proceeding shall lie against any registered medical practitioner for any damage caused or likely to be caused by anything which is in good faith done or intended to be done under this Act.
