Summary:

THE BONDED LABOUR SYSTEM (ABOLITION) ACT, 1976, is an Act to provide for the abolition of the bonded labour system with a view to preventing the economic and physical exploitation of the weaker sections of the people and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act came into force on the 25th day of October, 1975.

Chapter I of the Act deals with the preliminary provisions, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act. The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "advance," "agreement," "ascendant" or "descendant," "bonded debt," "bonded labour," "bonded labourer," "bonded labour system," "family," "nominal wages," and "workman."

Chapter II of the Act deals with the abolition of the bonded labour system. The Act abolishes the bonded labour system and provides that every bonded labourer shall stand freed and discharged from any obligation to render any bonded labour. The Act also prohibits making any advance under the bonded labour system or compelling any person to render any bonded labour or other forms of forced labour.

Chapter III of the Act deals with the extinguishment of liability to repay bonded debt. The Act extinguishes the liability of a bonded labourer to repay any bonded debt or such part of any bonded debt as remains unsatisfied immediately before the commencement of the Act. The Act also prohibits the recovery of any bonded debt or any part thereof and provides for the restoration of any property or product of labour seized or forfeited by the creditor.

Chapter IV of the Act deals with the implementing authorities. The Act empowers the State Government to confer powers and impose duties on a District Magistrate to ensure that the provisions of the Act are properly carried out. The Act also imposes a duty on the District Magistrate and other officers to promote the welfare of the freed bonded labourer by securing and protecting their economic interests.

Chapter V of the Act deals with the Vigilance Committees. The Act provides for the constitution of Vigilance Committees in each district and Sub-Division to advise the District Magistrate or any officer authorized by him as to the efforts made and action taken to ensure that the provisions of the Act are properly implemented. The Vigilance Committees are also responsible for providing for the economic and social rehabilitation of the freed bonded labourers, coordinating the functions of rural banks and co-operative societies with a view to canalising adequate credit to the freed bonded labourer, keeping an eye on the number of offences of which cognizance has been taken under this Act, making a survey as to whether there is any offence of which cognizance ought to be taken under this Act, and defending any suit instituted against a freed bonded labourer or a member of his family or any other person dependant on him for the recovery of the whole or part of any bonded debt or any other debt which is claimed by such person to be bonded debt.

Chapter VI of the Act deals with offences and procedure for trial. The Act provides for punishment for the enforcement of bonded labour, advancement of bonded debt, extracting bonded labour under the bonded labour system, and the omission or failure to restore possession of property to bonded labourers. The Act also provides for the abetment of offences and the trial of offences by Executive Magistrates.

Chapter VII of the Act deals with miscellaneous provisions, including protection of action taken in good faith, jurisdiction of civil courts barred, power to make rules, and repeal and saving.

The Act also contains a schedule, an annexure, an appendix, and forms.
