Summary:
THE ANCIENT MONUMENTS AND ARCHAEOLOGICAL SITES AND REMAINS ACT, 1958

This Act, enacted on 28th August, 1958, aims to preserve ancient and historical monuments and archaeological sites and remains of national importance, regulate archaeological excavations, and protect sculptures, carvings, and other like objects. The Act applies to the whole of India.

Key definitions include:
- Ancient monument: any structure, erection, or monument, or any tumulus or place of interment, or any cave, rock-sculpture, inscription, or monolith, which is of historical, archaeological, or artistic interest and has been in existence for not less than one hundred years.
- Antiquity: any coin, sculpture, manuscript, epigraph, or other work of art or craftsmanship, any article, object, or thing detached from a building or cave, any article, object, or thing illustrative of science, art, crafts, literature, religion, customs, morals, or politics in bygone ages, or any article, object, or thing declared by the Central Government to be an antiquity for the purposes of this Act, which has been in existence for not less than one hundred years.
- Archaeological officer: an officer of the Department of Archaeology of the Government of India not lower in rank than Assistant Superintendent of Archaeology.
- Protected area: any archaeological site and remains which is declared to be of national importance by or under this Act.
- Protected monument: an ancient monument which is declared to be of national importance by or under this Act.

The Act categorizes and classifies ancient monuments or archaeological sites and remains declared as of national importance under Sections 3 and 4. The Central Government, upon the recommendation of the Authority, prescribes categories and classifies all ancient monuments or archaeological sites and remains declared as of national importance under Sections 3 and 4.

The Act provides for the acquisition of rights in a protected monument, allowing the Director-General to purchase, take a lease of, or accept a gift or bequest of a protected monument. The owner of any protected monument may also constitute the Director-General as the guardian of the monument. The owner retains the same estate, right, title, and interest in and to the monument as if the Director-General had not been constituted a guardian thereof.

The Act enables the preservation of protected monuments by agreement between the Central Government and the owner. An agreement may provide for the maintenance of the monument, custody of the monument and duties of any person who may be employed to watch it, restrictions on the owner's right to use the monument for any purpose, charge any fee for entry into, or inspection of, the monument, destroy, remove, alter, or deface the monument or build on or near the site of the monument, facilities of access to be permitted to the public or any section thereof or to archaeological officers or to persons deputed by the owner or any archaeological officer or the Collector to inspect or maintain the monument, notice to be given to the Central Government in case the land on which the monument is situated or any adjoining land is offered for sale by the owner, the right to be reserved to the Central Government to purchase such land or any specified portion of such land at its market value, payment of any expenses incurred by the owner or by the Central Government in connection with the maintenance of the monument, proprietary or other rights which are to vest in the Central Government in respect of the monument when any expenses are incurred by the Central Government in connection with the maintenance of the monument, appointment of an authority to decide any dispute arising out of the agreement, and any matter connected with the maintenance of the monument which is a proper subject of agreement between the owner and the Central Government.

The Act regulates the use and maintenance of protected monuments by the public, and any person who fails to comply with the provisions may be punished with imprisonment which may extend to three months, or with fine which may extend to five thousand rupees, or with both.

The Act also regulates archaeological excavations, protecting antiquities, and controlling the movement of antiquities. Any person who moves any antiquity in contravention of a notification issued under Section 25 shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years or with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees or with both.

The Act empowers the Central Government to delegate its powers to officers or authorities subordinate to it or to State Governments or officers or authorities subordinate to them. The Act also specifies penalties for offenses committed under the Act, with imprisonment which may extend to two years or with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees or with both.

The Act provides for the protection of action taken under the Act, stating that no suit for compensation and no criminal proceeding shall lie against any public servant in respect of any act done or in good faith intended to be done in the exercise of any power conferred by this Act. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act.
