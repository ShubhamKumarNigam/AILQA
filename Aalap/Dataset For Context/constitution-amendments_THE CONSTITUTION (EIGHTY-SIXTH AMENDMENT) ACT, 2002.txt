The Constitution (Eighty-sixth Amendment) Act, 2002, adds a new article 21A to the Constitution, which guarantees free and compulsory education to all children between the ages of six and fourteen. This amendment is significant as it makes education a fundamental right for children in India.

Furthermore, the Act substitutes a new article 45 for the existing one, which now mandates the State to provide early childhood care and education to all children until they complete the age of six years. This provision emphasizes the importance of early childhood education in preparing children for formal schooling.

Lastly, the Act amends article 51A of the Constitution by adding a new clause (k), which imposes a duty on parents or guardians to provide opportunities for education to their children or wards between the ages of six and fourteen. This provision seeks to ensure that parents and guardians play an active role in their children's education and are aware of their responsibility towards their education.

Overall, the Constitution (Eighty-sixth Amendment) Act, 2002, is a significant step towards strengthening the educational system in India, ensuring that all children have access to quality education and are provided with a safe and supportive learning environment.
