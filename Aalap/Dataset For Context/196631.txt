The Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966 (Act No. 31 of 1966) was enacted on the 18th of September, 1966, to reorganize the existing State of Punjab and for matters connected therewith. The Act is divided into three parts, with Part I being the preliminary section that defines various terms used in the Act.

Part II of the Act deals with the reorganization of the State of Punjab. Section 3 of the Act provides for the formation of a new State called Haryana, comprising certain territories of the existing State of Punjab. These territories include Hissar, Rohtak, Gurgaon, Karnal, and Mahendragarh districts, as well as certain tahsils and territories from Sangrur, Ambala, and Kharar tahsils. The new State of Haryana will also include a separate district known as Jind, formed from the territories referred to in clause (b) of sub-section (1) of Section 3.

Section 4 of the Act provides for the formation of a new Union territory called the Union territory of Chandigarh, comprising certain territories of Manimajra and Manauli kanungo circles of Kharar tahsil of Ambala district in the existing State of Punjab.

Section 5 of the Act provides for the transfer of territory from Punjab to Himachal Pradesh. The territories added to the Union territory of Himachal Pradesh include Simla, Kangra, Kulu, and Lahaul and Spiti districts, as well as certain tahsils and territories from Ambala, Una, and Pathankot districts of the existing State of Punjab.

Section 6 of the Act defines the State of Punjab and its territorial divisions. The Rupar district is formed from the territories that are not transferred by virtue of sections 3, 4, and 5. The territories that form part of the Rupar district include those that were part of Ambala district and Una tahsil of Hoshiarpur district in the existing State of Punjab.

Part III of the Act deals with representation in the legislatures. Section 8 of the Act provides for saving powers of the Government, allowing the Government of Punjab or Haryana or the Administrator of the Union territory of Himachal Pradesh to alter the name, area, or boundaries of any district or other territorial division in the State or Union territory, as the case may be.
