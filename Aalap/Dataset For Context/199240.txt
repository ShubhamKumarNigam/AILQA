Summary:

The Central Agricultural University Act, 1992, establishes a university for the North-Eastern region for the development of agriculture and allied sciences. The university is headquartered in Imphal, Manipur and may establish campuses at other locations within its jurisdiction. The university is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, and may sue and be sued by the name of the Central Agricultural University. The university has the power to make provisions for instructions, research, and dissemination of findings in agricultural and allied sciences, grant degrees and other academic distinctions, cooperate or collaborate with other universities or authorities, establish and maintain colleges and institutions, create teaching, research, and extension education posts, and institute fellowships, scholarships, and prizes. The university's jurisdiction extends to the states of Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, and Tripura. The university is open to all classes, castes, and creeds, and may make special provisions for the employment or admission of women, handicapped, or persons belonging to weaker sections of society. The Visitor of the university is the President of India, who may cause an inspection or inquiry to be made and address the Vice-Chancellor with recommendations. The university has a Board, Academic Council, Finance Committee, Research Programme Committee, Extension Education Advisory Committee, and Board of Studies as its authorities. The Board is the principal executive body of the university, and the Academic Council is the principal academic body. The other authorities are responsible for specific functions. The university has faculties and other authorities as prescribed by the Statutes. The Statutes, Ordinances, and Regulations of the university are subject to the provisions of the Act and may be made, amended, or repealed by the Board with the assent of the Visitor. The annual report and accounts of the university are to be submitted to the Board, Visitor, and the Central Government, and published in the Official Gazette. The university has the power to remove difficulties during the transition period, and the Statutes, Ordinances, and Regulations are to be published in the Official Gazette and laid before Parliament.
