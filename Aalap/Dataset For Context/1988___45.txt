Summary:

The Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988 is an Act to prohibit benami transactions and the right to recover property held benami and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act defines benami transactions as a transaction or an arrangement where a property is transferred to, or is held by, a person, and the consideration for such property has been provided, or paid by, another person, and the property is held for the immediate or future benefit, direct or indirect, of the person who has provided the consideration. The Act also defines benamidar as a person or a fictitious person, as the case may be, in whose name the benami property is transferred or held and includes a person who lends his name. The Act prohibits benami transactions and provides for the confiscation of benami property. The Act establishes an Adjudicating Authority and an Appellate Tribunal to enforce the provisions of the Act. The Act also provides for the powers and functions of the authorities, the procedure for adjudication and appeal, and the offences and penalties for contravention of the Act. The Act extends to the whole of India. The provisions of sections 3, 5 and 8 shall come into force at once, and the remaining provisions of this Act shall be deemed to have come into force on the 19th day of May, 1988.
