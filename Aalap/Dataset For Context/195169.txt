The Plantations Labour Act, 1951, is an Act to provide for the welfare of labor and to regulate the conditions of work in plantations. It applies to plantations used for growing tea, coffee, rubber, cinchona, or cardamom that are 5 hectares or more in size and where 15 or more persons are employed. The Act also applies to any other plantation that the State Government may direct, as long as it is 5 hectares or more in size and 15 or more persons are employed.

The Act provides for the registration of plantations and sets out the powers and functions of inspectors. It also includes provisions for medical facilities, housing, and other facilities for workers. The Act also sets out provisions for the safety of workers and the prohibition of employment of children.

The Act includes provisions for leave with wages, sickness and maternity benefits, and accidents. It also sets out penalties and procedures for enforcement. The Act includes provisions for the protection of action taken in good faith and exemption of employer from liability in certain cases. The Act also includes provisions for the cognizance of offenses and protection of action taken in good faith.

The Act includes provisions for power to give directions, power to exempt, and general power to make rules. The Act also includes a schedule of fines and penalties for various offenses.
