Summary:

The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 is a law in India that aims to prevent and control water pollution. The Act applies to the whole of the states of Assam, Bihar, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Tripura and West Bengal and the Union territories. The Act defines pollution as the contamination of water or alteration of its physical, chemical or biological properties that may harm public health or safety, or domestic, commercial, industrial, agricultural or other legitimate uses, or the life and health of animals or plants or of aquatic organisms.

The Act establishes the Central Pollution Control Board and State Pollution Control Boards to exercise powers and perform functions assigned to them under the Act. The Boards consist of members appointed by the Central or State Government, including officials, non-officials, and representatives of agriculture, fishery, industry, trade, and local authorities. The Act also specifies the terms and conditions of service of members, disqualifications, and vacation of seats.

The Act empowers the Central and State Boards to take samples of water, sewage, or trade effluents for analysis and lays down the procedure for the same. The results of such analysis are admissible in evidence in legal proceedings only if the provisions of the Act are complied with. The Act also authorizes the Boards to enter any place for the purpose of performing their functions and to take necessary measures to prevent or control water pollution.

The Act prohibits the discharge of poisonous, noxious, or polluting matter into any stream or well or on land without the previous consent of the State Board. The Act also regulates the establishment or taking of any steps to establish any industry, operation, or process, or any treatment and disposal system or any extension or addition thereto, which is likely to discharge sewage or trade effluent into a stream or well or sewer or on land. The Act also provides for appeals and revisions against orders of the State Board.

The Act imposes penalties for the contravention of its provisions, including fines and imprisonment. The Act also provides for the publication of names of offenders and the liability of companies and government departments for offences committed by them. The Act also specifies the powers and duties of local authorities to assist the Boards in discharging their functions.

The Act empowers the Central Government to supersede the Central Board and Joint Boards, and the State Government to supersede the State Board, in certain circumstances. The Act also empowers the Central and State Governments to make rules for the implementation and enforcement of the Act.
