Summary:
The Gold Bonds (Immunities and Exemptions) Act, 1993, was enacted on April 2, 1993, to provide immunities and exemptions to subscribers of Gold Bonds, 1998, issued by the Central Government. The Act aims to mobilize idle gold resources of residents in India.
The Act defines Gold Bonds as the Gold Bonds, 1998, issued by the Central Government, and subscribers as individuals, Hindu undivided families, trustees of a trust, a firm, or a company, who have initially subscribed to the Gold Bonds.
Section 3 of the Act authorizes the Central Government to frame a scheme for subscription to the Gold Bonds, 1998. The scheme must be laid before each House of Parliament.
Section 4 of the Act provides immunities to subscribers, including exemption from disclosing the nature and source of acquisition of the gold subscribed for the Gold Bonds, immunity from inquiry or investigation under any of the specified Acts, and inadmissibility of the fact that any subscriber owns the Gold Bonds as evidence in any proceedings under any of the specified Acts.
Section 5 of the Act exempts Gold Bonds from the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, and the Gift-tax Act, 1958, in certain cases.
Section 6 of the Act repeals the Gold Bonds (Immunities and Exemptions) Ordinance, 1993, and provides that anything done or any action taken under the said Ordinance shall be deemed to have been done or taken under the corresponding provisions of this Act.
