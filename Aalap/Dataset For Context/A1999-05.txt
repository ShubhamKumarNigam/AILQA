Summary:

The Leaders and Chief Whips of Recognised Parties and Groups in Parliament (Facilities) Act, 1998, was enacted on 7th January 1999, to provide facilities to Leaders and Chief Whips of recognised parties and groups in Parliament.

The Act defines 'recognised group' as a party with a strength of not less than fifteen members and not more than twenty-four members in the Council of States, or with a strength of not less than thirty members and not more than fifty-four members in the House of the People. A 'recognised party' is a party with a strength of not less than twenty-five members in the Council of States or with a strength of not less than fifty-five members in the House of the People.

The Act entitles each leader, deputy leader, and Chief Whip of a recognised group and a recognised party to telephone and secretarial facilities, except for those who hold an office of Minister, hold the office of the Leader of the Opposition, are entitled to similar facilities in any other capacity, or are entitled to similar facilities by virtue of holding any office of, or representation in, a Parliamentary Committee or other Committee, Council, Board, Commission, or other body set up by the Government.

The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act, which shall be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days. Any modification in the rule or agreement that the rule should not be made by both Houses shall result in the rule having effect only in the modified form or being of no effect.

The Act also amends section 3 of the Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959, by inserting a new clause (ac) that includes the office of the leader and deputy leader of a recognised party and a recognised group in either House of Parliament. The expressions 'recognised party' and 'recognised group' shall have the meanings assigned to them in the Leaders and Chief Whips of Recognised Parties and Groups in Parliament (Facilities) Act, 1998.
