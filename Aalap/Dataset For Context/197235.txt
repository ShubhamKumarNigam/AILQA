Summary:
The Delhi Co-operative Societies Act, 1972 is an Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to co-operative societies in the Union territory of Delhi. The Act defines a co-operative society as a society registered or deemed to be registered under this Act, and includes a co-operative society with limited liability and a co-operative society with unlimited liability. The Act also defines other terms such as committee, co-operative bank, officer, prescribed, Registrar, Reserve Bank, rules, and Tribunal.

The Act is divided into twelve chapters. Chapter I is the preliminary chapter, which includes the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act, as well as definitions of various terms used throughout the Act. Chapter II deals with the registration of co-operative societies, including the appointment of the Registrar, the powers of the Registrar, and the procedure for registration. Chapter III focuses on the members of co-operative societies and their rights and liabilities. Chapter IV deals with the management of co-operative societies, including the final authority in a co-operative society, annual general meetings, special general meetings, election and nomination of members of committees, and supersession of committees. Chapter V outlines the privileges of co-operative societies, including their status as bodies corporate, their first charge on certain assets, and their exemption from certain taxes, fees, and duties.

Chapter VI deals with the properties and funds of co-operative societies, including the investment of funds, restrictions on loans, and restrictions on other transactions with non-members. Chapter VII focuses on audit, inquiry, inspection, and surcharge, including the powers of the Registrar and other authorized persons to inspect the books and accounts of co-operative societies, and the procedure for holding inquiries and imposing surcharges. Chapter VIII deals with the settlement of disputes, including the reference of disputes to arbitration and the powers of the Registrar to decide disputes.

Chapter IX deals with the winding up of co-operative societies, including the grounds for winding up, the appointment of a liquidator, and the powers of the liquidator. Chapter X deals with the execution of awards, decrees, orders, and decisions, including the enforcement of charges and the execution of orders by the Registrar or other authorized persons. Chapter XI deals with appeals and revision, including the right to appeal and the powers of the Tribunal. Chapter XII contains miscellaneous provisions, including offences and penalties, notice necessary in suits, the Companies Act not to apply, saving of existing societies, bar of jurisdiction of courts, powers of civil court, indemnity, qualifications, remuneration, and other conditions of service of employees of co-operative societies, rules, and repeal and savings.

The Act includes a schedule that sets out the form of application for registration of a co-operative society, and an annexure that sets out the form of bye-laws of a co-operative society. The Act also includes several forms for use in various proceedings under the Act, such as forms for notice of an annual general meeting, application for registration of an amendment of bye-laws, and notice of withdrawal from a co-operative society.
