Summary:

The Labour Laws (Simplification of Procedure for Furnishing Returns and Maintaining Registers by Certain Establishments) Act, 1988, shortened as THE LABOUR LAWS (SIMPLIFICATION OF PROCEDURE) ACT, 1988, is an Act that aims to simplify the procedure for furnishing returns and maintaining registers under certain labour laws in India. The Act received the assent of the President on September 24, 1988, and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government.

The Act applies to the whole of India, except for the State of Jammu and Kashmir in relation to the Plantations Labour Act, 1951. The Act exempts employers of small and very small establishments from furnishing returns and maintaining registers required under certain labour laws, subject to certain conditions. The Act defines "employer," "establishment," "Form," "Scheduled Act," "small establishment," and "very small establishment."

The Act amends certain labour laws, including the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, the Weekly Holidays Act, 1942, the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, the Factories Act, 1948, the Plantations Labour Act, 1951, the Working Journalists and other Newspaper Employees (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1955, the Motor Transport Workers Act, 1961, the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965, the Beedi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966, the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, the Sales Promotion Employees (Conditions of Service) Act, 1976, the Equal Remuneration Act, 1976, the Inter-State Migrant Workmen (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1979, the Dock Workers (Safety, Health and Welfare) Act, 1986, the Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986, and the Building and Other Construction Workers (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1996.

The Act provides for the power to amend Form and remove difficulties. The Central Government may amend any Form by notification in the Official Gazette and remove any difficulty in giving effect to the provisions of this Act by order. The Act also provides for the savings of previous operation, rights, obligations, liabilities, penalties, forfeitures, punishments, investigations, legal proceedings, or remedies under any Scheduled Act. The Act defines "relevant period" as the period during which an establishment is or was a small establishment or a very small establishment under this Act.

The Act imposes a penalty on any employer who fails to comply with the provisions of this Act. The penalty for the first conviction is a fine extending up to rupees five thousand, and for the second or subsequent conviction, imprisonment for a period not less than one month but which may extend to six months or a fine not less than rupees ten thousand but which may extend to rupees twenty-five thousand, or both.

The Act provides for the power to amend Form. The Central Government may, if it is of opinion that it is expedient so to do, by notification in the Official Gazette, amend any Form, and thereupon such Form shall, subject to the provisions of sub-section (2), be deemed to have been amended accordingly.

The Act provides for the power to remove difficulties. If any difficulty arises in giving effect to the provisions of this Act, the Central Government may, by order, not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, remove the difficulty.

The First Schedule of the Act lists the Scheduled Acts, including the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, the Weekly Holidays Act, 1942, the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, the Factories Act, 1948, the Plantations Labour Act, 1951, the Working Journalists and other Newspaper Employees (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1955, the Motor Transport Workers Act, 1961, the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965, the Beedi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966, the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, the Sales Promotion Employees (Conditions of Service) Act, 1976, the Equal Remuneration Act, 1976, the Inter-State Migrant Workmen (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1979, the Dock Workers (Safety, Health and Welfare) Act, 1986, the Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986, and the Building and Other Construction Workers (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1996.

The Second Schedule of the Act specifies the Forms, including Form I, Annual Return, FORM II, REGISTER OF PERSONS EMPLOYED-CUM-EMPLOYMENT CARD, and FORM III, MUSTER ROLL-CUM-WAGE REGISTER. The Forms are to be furnished to the Inspector or the authority specified for this purpose under the respective Scheduled Acts before the 30th April of the following year, ending 31st March.

The Act applies to small and very small establishments, defined as establishments in which not less than ten and not more than 40 persons are employed or were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months, and establishments in which not more than nine persons are employed or were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months, respectively.
