Summary:
The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, is an Act to define and amend the law relating to Promissory Notes, Bills of Exchange, and Cheques. The Act is divided into three chapters: I. Preliminary, II. Of Notes, Bills, and Cheques, and III. Parties to Notes, Bills, and Cheques. The Act defines several legal terms, such as "Banker," "Bill of Exchange," "Cheque," "Drawee," "Drawer," "Holder," "Holder in Due Course," "Indorser," "Maker," and "Negotiable Instrument." The Act also specifies the requirements for a valid negotiable instrument, the rights and duties of parties to a negotiable instrument, and the consequences of dishonor or payment. The Act applies to the whole of India, but does not affect the Indian Paper Currency Act, 1871, or any local usage relating to instruments in an oriental language. The Act came into force on March 1, 1882.
