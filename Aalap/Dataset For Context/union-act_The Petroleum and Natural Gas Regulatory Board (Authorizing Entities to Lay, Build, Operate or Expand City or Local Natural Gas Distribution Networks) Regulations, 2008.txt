

Summary:

The Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board (Authorizing Entities to Lay, Build, Operate or Expand City or Local Natural Gas Distribution Networks) Regulations, 2008, outlines the process for authorizing entities to develop and operate city or local natural gas distribution networks (CGD networks) in India. These regulations apply to any entity that proposes to lay, build, operate, or expand a CGD network.

Entities interested in developing a CGD network can submit an expression of interest to the Board, which will then carry out a preliminary assessment based on certain criteria, such as natural gas availability and connectivity with existing or proposed natural gas pipelines. The Board may then issue an open advertisement inviting bids for the development of the CGD network.

To be eligible to submit a bid, an entity must meet certain minimum eligibility criteria, including paying the application fee and demonstrating technical capability in laying, building, operating, and maintaining a CGD network. The regulations outline specific qualifying criteria for technical capability, such as having previously laid and built a certain length of hydrocarbon steel pipeline or CGD network, or having a joint venture with another entity that has done so.

Once a bid is submitted, the Board will scrutinize it and determine whether the entity meets the minimum eligibility criteria. If the entity is deemed eligible, the Board will issue an authorization for the development of the CGD network. The authorization will specify the authorized area for the CGD network, which may be different from the area indicated in the expression of interest.

The regulations also outline the process for expanding an existing CGD network and provide for the levy of fees and other charges. Additionally, the regulations include provisions for addressing force majeure events and under-achievement of work programmes.

In summary, these regulations establish the process for authorizing entities to develop and operate CGD networks in India, with a focus on ensuring technical capability and natural gas availability. The regulations also provide for the levy of fees and other charges and include provisions for addressing force majeure events and under-achievement of work programmes.
