Summary:
THE SALARY AND ALLOWANCES OF LEADERS OF OPPOSITION IN PARLIAMENT ACT, 1977 (Act No. 33 of 1977) is an Act that provides for the salary and allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament. The Act defines a Leader of the Opposition as the member of the Council of States or the House of the People who is, for the time being, the Leader in that House of the party in opposition to the Government having the greatest numerical strength and recognized as such by the Chairman of the Council of States or the Speaker of the House of the People, as the case may be. The Act specifies the salary, daily, constituency, and sumptuary allowances for the Leaders of Opposition, which are the same as those for members of Parliament. It also provides for residence, traveling and daily allowances, medical treatment, and amenities for the Leaders of Opposition. The Act prohibits a Leader of the Opposition from receiving any sum out of funds provided by Parliament by way of salary or allowance in respect of his membership of either House of Parliament. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act, including the rates at which rent shall be payable by the family of a deceased Leader of the Opposition to the use of the furnished residence occupied by him, the traveling and daily allowances admissible to a Leader of the Opposition, the medical treatment admissible to a Leader of the Opposition and the members of his family, the telephone and secretarial facilities admissible to a Leader of the Opposition, and the advance payable to a Leader of the Opposition under section 8A. The Act also exempts the value of rent-free furnished residence (including maintenance thereof) provided to a Leader of the Opposition under sub-section (1) of section 4 from being included in the computation of his income chargeable under the heading “Salaries” under section 15 of the Income-tax Act, 1961.
