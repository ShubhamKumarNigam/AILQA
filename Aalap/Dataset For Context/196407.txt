Summary:
The Companies (Profits) Surtax Act, 1964 is an Act to impose a special tax on the profits of certain companies. The Act extends to the whole of India. The Act defines various terms used in the Act, including "advance surtax," "assessee," "assessment," "assessment year," "Board," "chargeable profits," "Income-tax Act," "prescribed," "regular assessment," and "statutory deduction." The Act also establishes tax authorities and defines their powers and functions. The Act imposes a surtax on the chargeable profits of a company that exceed the statutory deduction. The Act also provides for the return of chargeable profits, assessment, provisional assessment, advance payment of surtax, interest payable by the Government and assessee, penalties, opportunity of being heard, appeals to the Commissioner (Appeals), appeals to the Appellate Tribunal, rectification of mistakes, other amendments, surtax deductible in computing distributable income under the Income-tax Act, revision of orders prejudicial to revenue, revision of orders by the Chief Commissioner or Commissioner, application of provisions of the Income-tax Act, income-tax papers to be available for the purposes of this Act, failure to deliver returns, etc., false statements, abetment of false returns, etc., institution of proceedings and composition of offences, power to make exemption, etc., in relation to certain Union territories, and power to make rules. The Act also includes schedules for computing the chargeable profits and the capital of a company for the purposes of surtax.
