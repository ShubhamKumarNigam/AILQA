Summary:
The Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939 (Act No. 16 of 1939) is a law in India that provides for the registration of foreigners entering, being present in, and departing from the country. The Act defines a foreigner as a person who is not a citizen of India. The Central Government has the power to make rules with respect to foreigners for various purposes, such as requiring foreigners to report their presence to a prescribed authority, producing proof of identity, and reporting the name of any foreigner residing in certain premises. Any person who contravenes, or attempts to contravene, or fails to comply with any provision of any rule made under this Act shall be punished. The Central Government may exempt any individual foreigner or any class or description of foreigner from the application of the rules made under this Act. No suit, prosecution or other legal proceeding shall lie against any person for anything which is in good faith done or intended to be done under this Act. The provisions of this Act are in addition to, and not in derogation of, the provisions of the Foreigners Act, 1946 (31 of 1946) and any other law for the time being in force.
