Summary:
The Foreigners Act, 1946, is an Act that confers certain powers upon the Central Government in respect of foreigners. It extends to the whole of India. The Act defines a foreigner as a person who is not a citizen of India. The Central Government may by order prohibit, regulate, or restrict the entry of foreigners into India, their departure, their presence or continued presence, and may also impose conditions on their conduct while in India. The Act also provides for the arrest and detention of foreigners. The Central Government may exempt citizens of certain Commonwealth countries and other individuals or classes of foreigners from the application of the Act in certain cases. The Act also imposes obligations on the masters of vessels and pilots of aircraft to furnish information about foreign passengers, and on hotel keepers and others to furnish particulars about foreigners accommodated in their premises. The Act also empowers the Central Government to direct the closure or regulation of premises frequented by foreigners, and to determine the nationality of foreigners in certain cases. The Act imposes penalties for contravention of its provisions, and provides protection to persons acting under the Act. The Act also provides that the provisions of this Act shall be in addition to, and not in derogation of, the provisions of the Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939, the Indian Passport Act, 1920, and of any other enactment for the time being in force.
