Summary:
THE LEVY SUGAR PRICE EQUALISATION FUND ACT, 1976, was enacted on 16th February 1976, to ensure that the price of levy sugar is uniform throughout India. The Act establishes the Levy Sugar Price Equalisation Fund, which is credited with amounts representing all excess realisations made by producers and any loans or grants made by the Central Government. Producers are required to credit the amount representing excess realisations, together with interest, to the Fund within thirty days from the date of realisation. The Act also provides for the determination of questions as to whether any producer has realised an amount in excess of the controlled or fair price of levy sugar, and the discharge of the producer's liability to make repayment of such amounts to the persons entitled thereto upon crediting to the Fund. Buyers of levy sugar from whom any excess realisation was made by the producer or dealer are entitled to a refund from the Fund. The Fund vests in the Central Government after six months from the date of credit if the money remains unclaimed. The Central Government may specify the authority which shall maintain proper and separate accounts and other relevant records in relation to the Fund. The Act also empowers the Central Government to require producers to maintain accounts, produce books of account and other records, and furnish information relating to levy sugar. The Act provides for penalties for non-compliance and protection of action taken in good faith. The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act.
