Summary:

THE TOBACCO BOARD ACT, 1975 is an Act to provide for the development under the control of the Union of the tobacco industry. The Act establishes a Tobacco Board to promote, by such measures as it thinks fit, the development under the control of the Central Government of the tobacco industry. The Board has the power to regulate the production and curing of virginia tobacco, keep a constant watch on the virginia tobacco market, both in India and abroad, and ensure that the growers get a fair and remunerative price for the same, maintain and improve existing markets, and develop new markets outside India for Indian virginia tobacco and its products and devise marketing strategy in consonance with demand for the commodity outside India, including group marketing under limited brand names. The Board can also recommend to Central Government the minimum prices which may be fixed for purposes of export of virginia tobacco with a view to avoiding unhealthy competition amongst the exporters.

The Act also provides for the registration of growers of virginia tobacco, registration of growers of virginia tobacco seedlings for commercial purposes, registration of curers of virginia tobacco, registration of processors and manufacturers of virginia tobacco, licences to be obtained for grading work and construction of barns, registration of exporters, packers, auctioneers and dealers, and the duty of registered dealers and exporters to purchase at auction platforms, etc.

The Act also provides for the constitution of the Tobacco Fund, the power of the Board to borrow, the power of Central Government to authorise purchase of virginia tobacco, directions by Central Government, returns and reports, penalties, penalties for obstructing a member, officer or other employee of the Board in the discharge of his duties and for failure to produce books and records, other penalties, offences by companies, jurisdiction of court, previous sanction of Central Government, protection of action taken in good faith, suspension of operation of Act, application of other laws not barred, power of Central Government to make rules, power to make regulations, and schedules.

The Act applies to the whole of India and it came into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint. The Act has been extended to the States of Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka in respect of certain sections.
