Summary:

The Live-Stock Importation Act, 1898, also known as Act No. 9 of 1898, was enacted on the 12th of August, 1898. The Act aims to make better provisions for the regulation of the importation of livestock and live-stock products.

The Act defines "infectious or contagious disorders" as tick-pest, anthrax, glanders, farcy, scabies, and any other disease or disorder specified by the Central Government by notification in the Official Gazette. "Live-stock" is defined as horses, kine, camels, sheep, and any other animal specified by the Central Government by notification in the Official Gazette. "Import" means the bringing or taking, by sea, land, or air, into India. "Live-stock products" include meat and meat products of all kinds, including fresh, chilled, and frozen meat, tissue, organs of poultry, pig, sheep, goat, egg and egg powder, milk and milk products, bovine, ovine, and caprine embryos, ova, semen, pet food products of animal origin, and any other animal product specified by the Central Government by notification in the Official Gazette.

The Central Government may regulate, restrict or prohibit the import of any livestock that may be liable to be affected by infectious or contagious disorders and any fodder, dung, stable-litter, clothing, harness, or fittings appertaining to live-stock or that may have been in contact therewith, into India or any specified place therein. The Central Government may also regulate, restrict, or prohibit the import of any live-stock product that may be liable to affect human or animal health.

The State Government may make rules for the detention, inspection, disinfection, or destruction of imported live-stock and any fodder, dung, stable-litter, clothing, harness, or fittings appertaining to imported live-stock or that may have been in contact therewith. The State Government may also regulate the powers and duties of the officers appointed in this behalf.

No suit, prosecution, or other legal proceedings shall lie against any person for anything in good faith done or intended to be done under this Act.

The Act has been extended to Goa, Daman, and Diu, with modifications, by Regulation 12 of 1962, and to Pondicherry (w.e.f. 1-10-1963) by Regulation 7 of 1963. The Act has also been amended by various Acts, including Act 28 of 2001, Act 40 of 1953, Act 10 of 1914, and Act 62 of 1956.
