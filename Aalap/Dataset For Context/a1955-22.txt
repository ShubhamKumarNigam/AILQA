Summary:
The Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 (Act No. 22 of 1955) is a law in India that aims to prohibit and punish the practice of "Untouchability" and the enforcement of any disability arising therefrom. The Act was enacted on 8th May, 1955, and its provisions extend to the whole of India.

The Act defines "civil rights" as any right accruing to a person by reason of the abolition of "untouchability" under Article 17 of the Constitution. It also provides definitions for terms such as "hotel," "place," "place of public entertainment," "place of public worship," "Scheduled Castes," and "shop."

The Act punishes the enforcement of religious disabilities, such as preventing a person from entering a place of public worship or worshipping or offering prayers in a place of public worship, with imprisonment for a term of not less than one month and not more than six months and also with fine which shall be not less than one hundred rupees and not more than five hundred rupees.

The Act also punishes the enforcement of social disabilities, such as denying access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, or places of public entertainment, with the same punishment. It also punishes the refusal to admit a person to hospitals, dispensaries, educational institutions, or hostels on the ground of "untouchability," as well as the refusal to sell goods or render services on the same ground.

The Act also includes provisions for the cancellation or suspension of licenses in certain cases, the resumption or suspension of grants made by the government, the abetment of offences, and the power of the state government to impose collective fines.

The Act also includes provisions for enhanced penalty on subsequent conviction, presumption by courts in certain cases, limitation of jurisdiction of civil courts, offences by companies, protection of action taken in good faith, offences to be cognizable and triable summarily, duty of state government to ensure that the rights accruing from the abolition of "untouchability" may be availed of by the concerned persons, and the repeal of certain enactments.

The Schedule of the Act lists the enactments that are repealed to the extent to which they correspond or are repugnant to this Act or to any of the provisions contained therein.
