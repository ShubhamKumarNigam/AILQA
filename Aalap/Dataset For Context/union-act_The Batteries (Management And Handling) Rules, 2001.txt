
The Batteries (Management and Handling) Rules, 2001 are a set of regulations in India that govern the management and handling of batteries. These rules were enacted under the Environment (Protection) Act of 1986 and apply to a wide range of entities, including manufacturers, importers, reconditioners, assemblers, dealers, recyclers, auctioneers, consumers, and bulk consumers.

Under these rules, manufacturers, importers, assemblers, and reconditioners are responsible for ensuring that used batteries are collected back as per a schedule against new batteries sold, excluding those sold to original equipment manufacturers and bulk consumers. They must also file half-yearly returns of their sales and buy-back to the State Board and establish collection centers for the collection of used batteries from consumers or dealers. Additionally, they must ensure that used batteries collected are sent only to registered recyclers and make necessary arrangements for safe transportation from collection centers to the premises of registered recyclers.

Importers must register with the Ministry of Environment and Forests or an agency designated by it and provide details in Form II. Customs clearance of imports of new lead acid batteries is contingent upon valid registration with the Reserve Bank of India, one-time registration with the Ministry of Environment and Forests or an agency designated by it, an undertaking in Form III, and a copy of the latest half-yearly return in Form IV.

Dealers are responsible for ensuring that used batteries are collected back as per the schedule against new batteries sold, giving appropriate discounts for every used battery returned by the consumer, and filing half-yearly returns of the sale of new batteries and buy-back of old batteries to the manufacturer in Form V. They must also ensure safe transportation of collected batteries to the designated collection centers or to the registered recyclers and ensure that no damage is caused to the environment during storage and transportation of used batteries.

Recyclers must apply for registration to the Ministry of Environment and Forests or an agency designated by it if not already registered. They must ensure strict compliance with the terms and conditions of registration and submit annual returns as per Form VII to the State Board. They must also make available all records to the State Board for inspection, mark "Recycled" on lead recovered by reprocessing, and create public awareness with regard to the hazards of lead and the obligation of consumers to return used batteries only to the registered dealers or deliver at the designated collection centers.

The procedure for registration/renewal of registration of recyclers involves submitting an application in Form VI along with certain documents to the Joint Secretary, Ministry of Environment and Forests or any officer designated by the Ministry or an agency designated by it. The registration granted under this rule shall be in force for a period of two years from the date of issue or from the date of renewal unless suspended or cancelled earlier. An appeal shall lie against any order of suspension or cancellation or refusal of registration passed by the Joint Secretary to the Ministry of Environment and Forests or any officer designated by the Ministry or an agency designated by it. The State Boards are responsible for monitoring the compliance of conditions prescribed while according registration.
