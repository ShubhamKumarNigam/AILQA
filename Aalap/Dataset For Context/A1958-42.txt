Summary:

The International Finance Corporation (Status, Immunities and Privileges) Act, 1958, is an Act that implements the international agreement for the establishment and operation of the International Finance Corporation (IFC) in India, specifically addressing its status, immunities, and privileges.

The Act consists of four sections and a schedule.

Section 1 is the Short title and extent, stating that the Act may be called the International Finance Corporation (Status, Immunities and Privileges) Act, 1958, and it extends to the whole of India.

Section 2 provides the definitions of the terms used in the Act. The term "Agreement" refers to the Agreement for the establishment and operation of the IFC, and "Corporation" refers to the International Finance Corporation established under the Agreement.

Section 3, Conferment of status and certain immunities and privileges on the Corporation and conferment of certain immunities and privileges on its officers and employees, specifies that the provisions of the Agreement set out in the Schedule shall have the force of law in India. However, the section also clarifies that nothing in Section 9 of Article VI of the Agreement entitles the Corporation to import goods free of any duty of customs or confers on the Corporation any exemption from duties or taxes which form part of the price of goods sold or are in fact no more than charges for services rendered. The Central Government may, from time to time, by notification in the Official Gazette, amend the Schedule in conformity with any amendments, duly made and adopted, of the provisions of the Agreement set out therein.

Section 4, Power to make rules, enables the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions.

The Schedule lists the provisions of the Agreement which shall have force of law, including Article III, Section 1, which enables the Corporation to make investments of its funds in productive private enterprises in the territories of its members, and Article VI, Section 1, which sets forth the status, immunities, and privileges of the Corporation in the territories of each member. The section also specifies that the Corporation shall possess full juridical personality and, in particular, the capacity to contract, to acquire and dispose of immovable and movable property, and to institute legal proceedings. The section also states that actions may be brought against the Corporation only in a court of competent jurisdiction in the territories of a member in which the Corporation has an office, has appointed an agent for the purpose of accepting service or notice of process, or has issued or guaranteed securities. The section further provides that the property and assets of the Corporation shall be immune from all forms of seizure, attachment or execution before the delivery of final judgment against the Corporation. The section also provides that property and assets of the Corporation, wherever located and by whomsoever held, shall be immune from search, requisition, confiscation, expropriation or any other form of seizure by executive or legislative action. The section also states that the archives of the Corporation shall be inviolable, and all official communications of the Corporation shall be accorded the same treatment that it accords to the official communications of other members. Additionally, Section 8 of Article VI provides immunities and privileges of officers and employees of the Corporation, stating that all Governors, Directors, Alternates, officers, and employees of the Corporation shall be immune from legal process with respect to acts performed by them in their official capacity, and not being local nationals, shall be accorded the same immunities from immigration restrictions, alien registration requirements, and national service obligations and the same facilities as regards exchange restrictions as are accorded by members to the representatives, officials, and employees of comparable rank of other members. The section also states that no tax shall be levied on or in respect of salaries and emoluments paid by the Corporation to Directors, Alternates, officials, or employees of the Corporation who are not local citizens, local subjects, or other local nationals. Furthermore, no taxation of any kind shall be levied on any obligation or security issued by the Corporation or guaranteed by the Corporation, which discriminates against such obligation or security solely because it is issued by the Corporation or guaranteed by the Corporation. The Corporation, in its discretion, may waive any of the privileges and immunities conferred under this Article to such an extent and upon such conditions as it may determine.
