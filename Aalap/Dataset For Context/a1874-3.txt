Summary:

The Married Women’s Property Act, 1874, is a law in India that aims to clarify and amend the law related to certain married women, primarily focusing on their wages, earnings, and insurances. The Act is applicable to the whole of India, except for the state of Jammu and Kashmir, and it does not apply to any married woman who professed Hindu, Muhammadan, Buddhist, Sikh, or Jain religion at the time of her marriage, nor to her husband who professed any of those religions at the time of marriage. The State Government has the power to exempt any race, sect, or tribe or part of a race, sect, or tribe from the operation of the Act.

The Act consists of five chapters. Chapter I, Preliminary, includes the short title and extent of the application of the Act. Chapter II, Married Women’s Wages and Earnings, states that the wages and earnings of any married woman acquired or gained by her after the passing of this Act, in any employment, occupation, or trade carried on by her and not by her husband, and all savings from and investments of such wages, earnings and property, shall be deemed to be her separate property. Chapter III, Insurances by Wives and Husbands, explains that any married woman may effect a policy of insurance on her own behalf and independently of her husband, and the same and all benefits thereof, if expressed on the face of it to be so effected, shall enure as her separate property. Chapter IV, Legal Proceedings by and against Married Women, allows a married woman to maintain a suit in her own name for the recovery of property of any description which, by force of the said Indian Succession Act, 1865, or of this Act, is her separate property, and she shall have, in her own name, the same remedies, both civil and criminal, against all persons, for the protection and security of such property, as if she were unmarried. Chapter V, Husband’s Liability for Wife’s Debts, states that a husband married after the thirty-first day of December, 1865, shall not by reason only of such marriage be liable to the debts of his wife contracted before marriage, but the wife shall be liable to be sued for, and shall, to the extent of her separate property, be liable to satisfy such debts as if he had continued unmarried.

This Act has been extended to and brought into force in Dadra and Nagar Haveli by Regulation 6 of 1963, and it is made applicable to the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir and the Union territory of Ladakh. The Act has also been amended several times, including by Act 61 of 1959, Act 13 of 1923, Act 21 of 1929, Act 18 of 1927, and Act 12 of 1891.
