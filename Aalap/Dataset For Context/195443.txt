Summary:

The Special Marriage Act, 1954 (Act No. 43 of 1954) provides a special form of marriage for Indian citizens and foreigners, regardless of their religion or caste. The Act defines the conditions for the solemnization of special marriages, including the fulfillment of certain conditions such as neither party having a spouse living, both parties being of the legal age, and not being within the degrees of prohibited relationship. The Act also lays down the procedure for giving notice of intended marriage, publishing the notice, and dealing with objections.

The Act also provides for the registration of marriages celebrated in other forms, including those celebrated under Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh, or Jaina religions. The registration of such marriages may be declared to be of no effect if it was in contravention of any of the conditions specified in the Act.

The Act has provisions for restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation, and divorce. The grounds for divorce include adultery, cruelty, desertion, and mental disorder. The Act also provides for the legitimacy of children of void and voidable marriages and the rights and disabilities of parties married under the Act.

The Act also lays down the jurisdiction and procedure for presenting petitions for divorce or judicial separation, and the powers of courts in passing decrees. The Act provides for alimony pendente lite and permanent alimony and maintenance. The Act also provides for the custody of children and the enforcement of decrees and orders.

The Act repeals the Special Marriage Act, 1872, and any corresponding law in force in any Part B State immediately before the commencement of this Act. The Act also provides for the saving of certain marriages and suits and proceedings in causes and matters matrimonial.

The Act extends to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and applies also to citizens of India domiciled in the territories to which this Act extends who are in the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act came into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.
