The Chit Funds Act, 1982 is a law in India that governs the operation of chit funds, which are a form of rotating savings and credit scheme. The Act defines a chit as a transaction where a person enters into an agreement with a specified number of people to subscribe a certain sum of money or a certain quantity of grain by way of periodical installments over a definite period, and each subscriber is entitled to the prize amount in turn. The Act sets out the rules for the registration, conduct, and winding up of chit funds, and provides for the regulation and supervision of chit funds by the state governments.

Under the Act, no chit can be commenced or conducted without obtaining the previous sanction of the state government and registering the chit in accordance with the provisions of the Act. The Act also specifies the form and content of the chit agreement, and requires the foreman of the chit to maintain certain books and records. The Act also sets out the rights and duties of the foreman and the subscribers, and provides for the appointment of a Registrar to oversee the registration and regulation of chit funds.

The Act also provides for the winding up of chit funds in certain circumstances, such as if the chit has terminated, if the foreman commits any act that impairs the security for the chit, if the foreman fails to deposit any amount required under the Act, if the foreman is unable to pay the amounts due to the subscribers, or if the affairs of the chit are being conducted in a manner prejudicial to the interests of the subscribers.

Overall, the Chit Funds Act, 1982 provides a framework for the regulation and supervision of chit funds in India, with the aim of protecting the interests of subscribers and ensuring the integrity of the chit fund industry.
