Summary:
The National Council for Teacher Education Act, 1993, establishes a National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE) to regulate and maintain the standards of teacher education in India. The NCTE is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, and is headquartered in Delhi with regional offices in other parts of India. The NCTE consists of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, Member-Secretary, and other Members appointed by the Central Government. The term of office for the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, and Member-Secretary is four years or until they reach the age of sixty, whichever is earlier. The NCTE is responsible for ensuring planned and coordinated development of teacher education, determining and maintaining standards for teacher education, and making recommendations to the Central and State Governments, Universities, and recognized institutions in the field of teacher education. The NCTE may also co-opt up to three persons for specific purposes, and may appoint officers and other employees as necessary. The NCTE is authorized to inspect recognized institutions to ensure compliance with the provisions of the Act, and may withdraw recognition of a recognized institution if it contravenes any of the provisions of the Act or any rules, regulations, orders, or conditions subject to which recognition was granted. Any person aggrieved by an order of the NCTE may prefer an appeal to the Council within the prescribed period. The Council shall have its own fund, and shall prepare a budget and annual report for each financial year. The accounts of the Council shall be audited by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India. The Central Government may supersede the Council if it is unable to perform its duties, has persistently made default in performing its duties, has exceeded or abused its powers, or has wilfully or without sufficient cause failed to comply with any direction issued by the Central Government. The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of the Act, and the NCTE may make regulations not inconsistent with the provisions of the Act and the rules made thereunder. Every rule and every regulation made under the Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. The Central Government may also make orders to remove any difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of the Act.
