THE INDIAN RESERVE FORCES ACT, 1888 (ACT NO. 4 OF 1888)

The Indian Reserve Forces Act, 1888, was enacted on March 2, 1888, to regulate the Indian Reserve Forces. The Act divides the Reserve Forces into the Regular Reserve and the Supplementary Reserve, and establishes that a person belonging to the Indian Reserve Forces is liable to serve beyond the limits of India as well as within those limits. The Central Government has the power to make rules and orders for the government, discipline, and regulation of the Indian Reserve Forces. A person belonging to the Indian Reserve Forces is subject to military law in the same manner and to the same extent as a person belonging to the Regular Army. If a person belonging to the Indian Reserve Forces fails to attend at any place as required by or in pursuance of any rule or order under this Act, or fails to comply with any such rule or order, or fraudulently obtains any pay or other sum contrary to any such rule or order, he shall be liable to punishment. An employer is required to reinstate a person belonging to the Indian Reserve Forces in his employment on the termination of the period of his training, muster, or army service in an occupation and under conditions not less favorable to him than those which would have been applicable to him had his employment not been so interrupted. If an employer fails to obey the order of any such authority as is referred to in the proviso to sub-section (1), he shall be punishable with fine which may extend to one thousand rupees. The Act has been extended to and brought into force in Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Lakshadweep, and Pondicherry.
