Summary:
The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, is an act that amends and codifies the law relating to marriage among Hindus. It applies to any person who is a Hindu by religion in any of its forms or developments, including a Virashaiva, a Lingayat or a follower of the Brahmo, Prarthana or Arya Samaj, as well as to any person who is a Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh by religion, and to any other person domiciled in the territories to which this Act extends who is not a Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew by religion. The Act defines various terms related to Hindu marriages, including "custom," "district court," "full blood," "half blood," "uterine blood," "prescribed," "sapinda relationship," and "degrees of prohibited relationship." It also specifies the conditions for a Hindu marriage, including the requirement that neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage, that both parties are capable of giving a valid consent to the marriage, that the bridegroom has completed the age of 21 years and the bride the age of 18 years, and that the parties are not within the degrees of prohibited relationship or sapindas of each other, unless the custom or usage governing each of them permits of a marriage between the two. The Act also provides for the registration of Hindu marriages, the restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation, and divorce, and specifies the grounds for nullity and voidable marriages. It also regulates the jurisdiction and procedure for proceedings under the Act.
