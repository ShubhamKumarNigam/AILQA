Summary:

THE ORISSA (ALTERATION OF NAME) ACT, 2011 is an Act passed on the 23rd of September, 2011, to alter the name of the State of Orissa to the State of Odisha. The Act defines the "appointed day" as the date appointed under sub-section (2) of section 1 for the coming into force of this Act, and "appropriate Government" as the Central Government for laws relating to matters enumerated in List I of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution, and the State Government for any other law.

The Act states that as of the appointed day, the State of Orissa will be known as the State of Odisha. It also amends certain provisions in the Constitution, including article 164, article 273, the First Schedule, and the Fourth Schedule, to replace the word "Orissa" with "Odisha."

The Act also grants the appropriate Government the power to adapt and modify any law made before the appointed day, whether by way of repeal or amendment, as may be necessary or expedient, and for the courts, tribunals, or authorities to construe the law in such a manner as may be necessary or proper in regard to the matter before them.

Finally, the Act states that where immediately before the appointed day any legal proceedings are pending to which the State of Orissa is a party, the State of Odisha shall be deemed to have been substituted for the State of Orissa in those proceedings.
