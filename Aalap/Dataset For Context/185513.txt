Summary:

THE FATAL ACCIDENTS ACT, 1855, also known as Act No. 13 of 1855, aims to provide compensation to families for loss caused by the death of a person due to an actionable wrong.

The Act applies to the entire country of India, and it allows for a suit to be brought against a person who has caused the death of another through a wrongful act, neglect, or default. This suit can be brought even if the death was caused under circumstances that would amount to a felony or other crime.

The suit for compensation can be brought by and in the name of the executor, administrator, or representative of the deceased person, and it is for the benefit of the wife, husband, parent, and child of the deceased. The court may award damages that it deems proportionate to the loss resulting from the death to the parties involved, and the amount recovered will be divided among the aforementioned parties as the court directs.

The Act also specifies that only one action or suit may be brought for the same subject matter of complaint, and that the plaintiff in such an action or suit must provide a full particular of the person or persons for whom or on whose behalf the action or suit is brought, as well as the nature of the claim in respect of which damages are sought.

The Act includes an interpretation clause, which defines certain words and expressions used throughout the Act. For example, the word "person" includes bodies politic and corporate, and the word "parent" includes father, mother, grandfather, and grandmother. The word "child" includes son, daughter, grandson, granddaughter, step-son, and step-daughter.

The Act has been extended to Laccadive, Minicoy, and Amindivi Islands, as well as to the Union territory of Pondicherry. Certain words have been omitted from the Act, such as those relating to the limitation period for bringing a suit. The Act originally specified that such a suit must be brought within twelve calendar months after the death of the deceased person, but this requirement has been omitted.
