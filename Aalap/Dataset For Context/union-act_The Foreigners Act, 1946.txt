The Foreigners Act, 1946, is a law in India that regulates the entry, stay, and exit of foreigners in the country. The Act defines a foreigner as a person who is not a citizen of India. The Act gives the Central Government the power to make orders prohibiting, regulating, or restricting the entry of foreigners into India or their departure therefrom or their presence or continued presence therein. These orders may provide conditions for entry, departure, and presence in India, including restrictions on movements, association with certain persons, engagement in specific activities, and use or possession of certain articles. Foreigners may be required to enter into a bond with or without sureties for the due observance of these restrictions or conditions. The Act also empowers the Central Government to exempt citizens of Commonwealth countries and other persons from the application of the Act in certain cases. The Act imposes obligations on masters of vessels, pilots of aircraft, hotel keepers, and others to furnish particulars and information about foreigners. The Act also gives the prescribed authority the power to control places frequented by foreigners and determine the nationality of foreigners in certain cases. The Act places the burden of proof on any person to prove that he or she is not a foreigner or is not a foreigner of a particular class or description. The Act empowers any authority to take steps and use force for securing compliance with any direction or order made under the Act. The Act also provides for penalties for contravention of its provisions, including imprisonment and fines. The Act applies in addition to and not in derogation of the provisions of the Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939, the Indian Passport Act, 1920, and any other enactment for the time being in force.
