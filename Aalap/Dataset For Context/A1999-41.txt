Summary:

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 1999 establishes the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority (IRDA) to regulate, promote and ensure orderly growth of the insurance industry in India. The Act defines the IRDA as a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, having the power to acquire, hold and dispose of property, both movable and immovable, and to contract and sue or be sued. The IRDA shall consist of a Chairperson, not more than five whole-time members and not more than four part-time members appointed by the Central Government. The Chairperson and other whole-time members shall hold office for a term of five years, while part-time members shall hold office for a term not exceeding five years. The IRDA shall have the duty to regulate, promote and ensure orderly growth of the insurance business and re-insurance business. The powers and functions of the IRDA include, among others, issuing certificates of registration, protecting the interests of policyholders, specifying qualifications, code of conduct and practical training for intermediaries, specifying the code of conduct for surveyors and loss assessors, promoting efficiency in the conduct of insurance business, and regulating investment of funds by insurance companies. The IRDA shall also have the power to levy fees and other charges for carrying out the purposes of this Act, call for information from, undertake inspection of, conduct enquiries and investigations including audit of the insurers, intermediaries, insurance intermediaries and other organizations connected with the insurance business, and exercise such other powers as may be prescribed.
