Summary:

The Dakshina Bharat Hindi Prachar Sabha Act, 1964, declares the Dakshina Bharat Hindi Prachar Sabha as an institution of national importance. This Act, which came into force on the 1st of June, 1964, defines certain terms, such as "memorandum," "rules and regulations," and "Sabha."

The Act allows the Sabha to hold examinations and grant degrees, diplomas, and certificates for proficiency in Hindi or in teaching Hindi, as determined by the Sabha. The Sabha is required to maintain proper accounts and prepare an annual statement of accounts, which must be audited by a chartered accountant appointed annually by the Sabha.

The Sabha must obtain the prior approval of the Central Government for certain actions, such as altering, extending, or abridging its purposes, amalgamating with any other institution or society, altering or amending its memorandum or rules and regulations, or being dissolved.

The Central Government may constitute one or more committees to review the work done by the Sabha and evaluate its progress. The Sabha must afford all necessary facilities to any committee constituted for this purpose. The report of any committee constituted under this section must be submitted to the Central Government within the time and in the manner directed by that Government.

If the Central Government is not satisfied with the action taken by the Sabha in response to the result of a review or evaluation, it may issue directions to the Sabha, which must comply with such directions. The members of any committee constituted under this section are entitled to allowances fixed by the Central Government, and the expenses incurred by any such committee in the performance of its functions shall be paid out of the funds of the Sabha.
