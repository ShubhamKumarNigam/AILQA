Summary:
The Asiatic Society Act, 1984 declares the Asiatic Society, an institution established in Calcutta in 1784, to be of national importance. The Act outlines the powers and duties of the Society, including its functions related to research, literary, library, scientific, and museological activities, collection of manuscripts, coins, and art objects, and the publication of periodicals, books, and other literature. The Central Government may provide financial assistance to the Society for these purposes. The Comptroller and Auditor-General of India is responsible for auditing the Society's accounts, and the Society is required to submit an annual report to the Central Government. The Society must obtain the prior approval of the Central Government for certain actions, such as altering its purposes, amending its memorandum or regulations, selling or disposing of property acquired with money specifically provided by the Central Government, or being dissolved. The Central Government may establish a Planning Board to advise the Society on the planning and implementation of its developmental programs and other matters. The Act also allows for the creation of committees to review the Society's work, inspect its assets, and evaluate its progress. The Society is required to afford all necessary facilities to these committees to enable them to carry out their duties. The Central Government may issue directions to the Society and take over the management of the Society or its activities if necessary. The Act has overriding effect over any inconsistent provisions in the Society's memorandum, regulations, or any other law. The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of the Act.
