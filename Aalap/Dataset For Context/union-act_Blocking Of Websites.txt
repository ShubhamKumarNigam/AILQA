Summary:
The Indian government has designated the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) as the single authority for issuing instructions to block websites. While there is no explicit provision in the Information Technology Act, 2000 for blocking of websites, websites promoting hate content, slander, gambling, racism, violence, terrorism, pornography, including child pornography, and violent sex can be reasonably blocked. The document outlines the procedure for submitting a complaint to CERT-In for blocking of a website. Complaints can be submitted by various officers listed in the Gazette Notification, including secretaries of national security, home affairs, external affairs, and state human rights commissions, among others. The complaint should contain the name and contact details of the complainant, the address of the offending website, the name of the organization promoting/hosting the website, specific reasons for requesting blocking, and any other relevant information. The complaint should be submitted in writing on the letterhead of the complainant's organization and assigned a complaint number. CERT-In staff will verify the complainant's identity and assign a technical expert to view the website and print the offending content as a sample. A committee consisting of representatives from CERT-In, DIT, and the Law Ministry/Home Ministry will examine the complaint and sample content and make a decision on whether to block the website within a day. The decision will then be submitted to the Secretary, DIT, for approval. If approved, CERT-In will issue instructions to the Department of Telecommunications (DOT) to block the website. The entire exercise should be completed within seven working days of the receipt of the complaint.
