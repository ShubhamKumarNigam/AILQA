Summary:

THE INTERNATIONAL MONETARY FUND AND BANK ACT, 1945 (Act No. 47 of 1945) is an Act to implement the International Monetary Fund and Bank Agreements. The Act defines the International Monetary Fund Agreement and the International Bank Agreement, which were drawn up at the United Nations Monetary and Financial Conference held at Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, in July 1944. The Act has several sections that provide for the payment of subscriptions, charges, and compensation to the International Fund and Bank, the Reserve Bank of India as the depository for the Indian currency holdings of the International Fund and Bank, and the use, receipt, acquisition, holding, transfer, or operation of special drawing rights by the Reserve Bank on behalf of the Central Government.

The Act also includes provisions for the power to call for information, where the International Fund requires the Central Government to furnish any information, the Central Government or the Reserve Bank may require any person to furnish detailed information as determined to be essential for the purpose of complying with the request of the International Fund. The Act also provides for certain provisions of the Agreements to have force of law, notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any other law, the provisions of the Fund and Bank Agreements set out in the Schedule shall have the force of law in India.

The Schedule to the Act contains provisions of the Fund Agreement, including paragraph (b) of section 2 of Article VIII, which states that exchange contracts that involve the currency of any member and are contrary to the exchange control regulations of that member maintained or imposed consistently with this Agreement shall be unenforceable in the territories of any member. It also includes Article IX, which sets forth the status, immunities, and privileges of the Fund in the territories of each member, and Section 2 of Article IX, which states that the Fund shall possess full juridical personality and the capacity to contract, acquire and dispose of immovable and movable property, and institute legal proceedings.

The Act also includes provisions of the Bank Agreement, such as Article VII, which sets forth the status, immunities, and privileges of the Bank in the territories of each member, and Section 2 of Article VII, which states that the Bank shall possess full juridical personality and the capacity to contract, acquire and dispose of immovable and movable property, and institute legal proceedings. The Act also includes Section 3 of Article VII, which states that actions may be brought against the Bank only in a court of competent jurisdiction in the territories of a member in which the Bank has an office, has appointed an agent for the purpose of accepting service or notice of process, or has issued or guaranteed securities.

Overall, THE INTERNATIONAL MONETARY FUND AND BANK ACT, 1945 provides the legal framework for implementing the International Monetary Fund and Bank Agreements in India, including provisions for payments, the Reserve Bank as the depository, calling for information, and the status, immunities, and privileges of the Fund and Bank.
