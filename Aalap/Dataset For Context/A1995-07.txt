Summary:

The Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995 is a law in India that regulates the operation of cable television networks in the country. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on the 29th day of September, 1994.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "authorised officer," "cable service," "cable television network," "company," "person," and "programme." The Act also specifies the eligibility criteria and conditions for registration as a cable operator.

Under the Act, no person shall operate a cable television network unless they are registered as a cable operator. The registration of cable operators is done by the registering authority, and the cable operator must fulfill the prescribed eligibility criteria and conditions. The Act also specifies the terms and conditions of registration, including additional criteria or conditions to be fulfilled by the cable operator.

The Act also includes provisions for the transmission of programmes through digital addressable systems and specifies the measures and steps to be taken for its implementation. The Act also specifies the number of free-to-air channels to be included in the package of channels forming basic service tier and fixes the tariff for basic service tier.

The Act also includes provisions for the right of way for cable operators and permission by public authority. The Act grants cable operators the right to lay and establish cables and erect posts under, over, along, across, in or upon any immovable property vested in or under the control or management of a public authority, subject to certain conditions.

The Act also includes provisions for the maintenance of a register indicating the programmes transmitted or re-transmitted through the cable service during a month, and the compulsory transmission of certain channels, including Doordarshan channels and the channels operated by or on behalf of Parliament.

The Act also includes provisions for the use of standard equipment in cable television networks and prohibits the use of any equipment or digital addressable system in the cable television network unless it conforms to the Indian Standard.

The Act also includes provisions for the seizure and confiscation of certain equipment used for operating cable television networks and prohibits the transmission of certain programmes in the public interest.

The Act also includes provisions for offences and penalties for contravention of the provisions of the Act. The Act also includes provisions for the cognizance of offences and the power to prohibit the operation of any cable television network in the public interest.

The Act also includes provisions for the application of other laws, such as the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, the Pharmacy Act, 1948, the Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950, the Drugs (Control) Act, 1950, the Cinematograph Act, 1952, the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954, the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954, the Prize Competitions Act, 1955, the Copyright Act, 1957, the Trade and Merchandise Marks, Act, 1958, the Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act, 1986, the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, and the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997.

The Act also includes provisions for the power to make rules to carry out the provisions of the Act. Every rule made under the Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament while it is in session for a total period of thirty days. If both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be.

The Act also includes provisions for the repeal and savings of the Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Ordinance, 1995, and anything done or any action taken under the said Ordinance shall be deemed to have been done or taken under the corresponding provision of this Act.
