Summary:

The Constitution (Ninety-fifth Amendment) Act, 2009, is an amendment to the Constitution of India that aims to continue the reservation of seats and nomination of members for the Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Anglo-Indian community for a further period of ten years. This amendment is intended to support the continued progress of these communities, which has been ongoing for the past sixty years. The Act received assent on January 25, 2010, and came into force on the same day.

This amendment is significant because it highlights the Indian government's commitment to ensuring equal representation and opportunities for marginalized communities. By extending the reservation of seats and nomination of members for these groups, the government aims to promote their socio-economic development and political participation.

The Constitution (Ninety-fifth Amendment) Act, 2009, is an essential piece of legislation that underscores India's commitment to promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion. It demonstrates the government's recognition of the ongoing challenges faced by marginalized communities and its dedication to addressing these issues through affirmative action policies.
