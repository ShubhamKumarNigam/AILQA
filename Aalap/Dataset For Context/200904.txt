Summary:
The Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 establishes Gram Nyayalayas, or village courts, in India with the aim of providing access to justice to citizens at the grassroots level. The Act extends to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and includes the tribal areas of certain states. The Act defines Gram Nyayalayas, Gram Panchayats, High Court, notification, Nyayadhikari, Panchayat at intermediate level, prescribed, Schedule, and State Government.

Chapter I of the Act deals with preliminary matters, including the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act.

Chapter II establishes Gram Nyayalayas and sets out the qualifications for appointment of Nyayadhikari, as well as their duties and powers.

Chapter III outlines the jurisdiction, powers, and authority of Gram Nyayalayas, including their criminal and civil jurisdiction. The Act allows Gram Nyayalayas to take cognizance of certain offences and grant relief under various enactments.

Chapter IV deals with the procedure in criminal cases, including the overriding effect of the Act, the use of summary trial procedure, plea bargaining, and the conduct of cases in Gram Nyayalayas.

Chapter V covers the procedure in civil cases, including the institution of suits, claims, or disputes, the execution of decrees and orders, and the duty of Gram Nyayalayas to make efforts for conciliation and settlement of civil disputes.

Chapter VI sets out the procedure generally applicable to Gram Nyayalayas, including the language of proceedings, the application of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, the record of oral evidence, and the use of affidavits.

Chapter VII deals with appeals, including appeals in criminal and civil cases, and the powers of the Court of Session and the District Court.

Chapter VIII contains miscellaneous provisions, including the power to remove difficulties, the power of the High Court to make rules, the power of the State Government to make rules, and the inspection of Gram Nyayalayas.

The First Schedule to the Act lists the offences under the Indian Penal Code and other central acts that can be tried by Gram Nyayalayas, while the Second Schedule lists the suits or proceedings of a civil nature that fall within the jurisdiction of Gram Nyayalayas.

The Act also includes provisions for the appointment of Nyayadhikaris and the establishment of Gram Nyayalayas, as well as rules for their conduct and administration.
