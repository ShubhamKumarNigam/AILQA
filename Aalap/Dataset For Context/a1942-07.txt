Summary:

The Coffee Act, 1942, is an Act of the Parliament of India that provides for the development of the coffee industry under the control of the Union. The Act declares that it is expedient to provide for the development of the coffee industry under the control of the Union. The Act consists of 50 sections and one schedule. The Act defines various terms related to coffee and its production, such as coffee, curing, curing establishment, dealer, estate, free sale quota, owner, prescribed, registered estate, registered owner, surplus pool, and year.

The Act establishes the Coffee Board, which is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal. The Board is responsible for the development of the coffee industry and has the power to acquire and hold property, both movable and immovable, and to contract. The Board is also responsible for the vesting of property in the Board, consultation with the Board, committees, staff, and agents, and the appointment of the Chief Coffee Marketing Officer, Secretary, and other staff.

The Act also provides for the registration of owners of coffee estates and the powers and duties of the registering officer. The Act lays down the procedure for the fixation of prices for sale of coffee, the sale of coffee, and the export and re-import of coffee. The Act also provides for the control of sale, export, and re-import of coffee and the regulation of coffee curing establishments.

The Act imposes penalties for various offenses, such as failure to register, contravention of sections 16, 17, and 18, unlicensed curing establishment, and false returns. The Act also provides for the cognizance of offenses and the power of the Central Government to make rules.

In summary, the Coffee Act, 1942, is an important piece of legislation that provides for the development of the coffee industry in India under the control of the Union. The Act defines various terms related to coffee and its production, establishes the Coffee Board, and provides for the registration of owners of coffee estates, the fixation of prices for sale of coffee, and the control of sale, export, and re-import of coffee. The Act also imposes penalties for various offenses and provides for the cognizance of offenses and the power of the Central Government to make rules.
