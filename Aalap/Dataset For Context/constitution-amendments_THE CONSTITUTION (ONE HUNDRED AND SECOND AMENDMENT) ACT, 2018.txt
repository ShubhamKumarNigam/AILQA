The One Hundred and Second Amendment Act of the Indian Constitution, called the Constitution (One Hundred and Second Amendment) Act, 2018, is focused on updating and modifying the current legal framework for addressing socially and educationally backward classes (SEBCs) in India. This amendment includes several key changes:

1. Amendment of Article 338: The words, brackets, and figures "to such other backward classes as the President may, on receipt of the report of a Commission appointed under clause (1) of article 340, by order specify and also" have been omitted from clause (10) of Article 338.
2. Insertion of new Article 338B: A new article, 338B, has been added to the Constitution, which establishes the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC). This commission will consist of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, and three other Members. The commission will have the power to regulate its own procedure, and it will be responsible for investigating and monitoring matters related to SEBCs, evaluating the working of safeguards, inquiring into specific complaints, and participating in and advising on socio-economic development.
3. Insertion of new Article 342A: A new article, 342A, has been added to the Constitution, which empowers the President, with respect to any State or Union territory, to specify SEBCs for the purposes of the Constitution. Parliament may include or exclude any socially and educationally backward class from the Central List. The President's notification shall not be varied by any subsequent notification, and a separate explanation clause has been added to clarify that states and union territories have the right to prepare and maintain their lists of SEBCs.
4. Amendment of Article 366: A new clause, (26C), has been added to Article 366, which defines "socially and educationally backward classes" as those deemed under Article 342A for the purposes of the Constitution.

The primary objective of this amendment is to ensure that all SEBCs are given equal opportunities in education and employment, and that their rights and safeguards are adequately monitored and protected. By creating the National Commission for Backward Classes and establishing a clear process for identifying and addressing SEBCs, the Indian government aims to promote social and economic development for these communities, and to uphold the values enshrined in the preamble of the Indian Constitution.
