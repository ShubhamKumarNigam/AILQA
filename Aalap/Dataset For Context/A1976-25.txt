Summary:

THE EQUAL REMUNERATION ACT, 1976 (ACT NO. 25 OF 1976) came into force on the 11th of February, 1976, with the main objective of providing for the payment of equal remuneration to men and women workers and for the prevention of discrimination against women in matters of employment. The Act applies to the whole of India and has overriding effect over any other law or instrument that is inconsistent with its provisions.

Chapter I of the Act deals with preliminary matters and defines key terms used throughout the Act. The term "appropriate Government" refers to the Central Government in cases where the employment is carried out by or under the authority of the Central Government, a railway administration, a banking company, a mine, oilfield, or major port, or any corporation established by or under a Central Act. In all other cases, the "appropriate Government" refers to the State Government. The term "employer" is defined as any person who employs one or more workers in an establishment or employment in which this Act has come into force. "Man" and "woman" are defined as male and female human beings, respectively, of any age. "Remuneration" includes basic wage or salary and any additional emoluments payable in cash or kind to a person employed in respect of employment or work done in such employment. "Same work or work of a similar nature" means work in respect of which the skill, effort, and responsibility required are the same when performed under similar working conditions by a man or a woman.

Chapter II of the Act focuses on the payment of remuneration at equal rates to men and women workers and other matters. Section 4 states that no employer shall pay to any worker remuneration at rates less favorable than those at which remuneration is paid by him to workers of the opposite sex in the same establishment or employment for performing the same work or work of a similar nature. Section 5 prohibits discrimination in recruitment and other conditions of service, such as promotions, training, or transfer, based on sex. However, this provision does not affect any priority or reservation for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, ex-servicemen, retrenched employees, or any other class or category of persons in the matter of recruitment to the posts in an establishment or employment.

Chapter III of the Act contains miscellaneous provisions. Section 8 mandates employers to maintain registers and other documents in relation to the workers employed by them. Section 9 empowers the appropriate Government to appoint Inspectors for the purpose of making investigations as to whether the provisions of this Act or the rules made thereunder are being complied with by employers. Section 10 lays down penalties for non-compliance with the provisions of the Act, including failure to maintain registers or other documents, making recruitment in contravention of the provisions of the Act, making payment of remuneration at unequal rates, and making discrimination between men and women workers.

The Act also contains provisions relating to offences by companies, cognizance and trial of offences, power to make rules, power of Central Government to give directions, act not to apply in certain special cases, power to make declaration, power to remove difficulties, and repeal and saving.
