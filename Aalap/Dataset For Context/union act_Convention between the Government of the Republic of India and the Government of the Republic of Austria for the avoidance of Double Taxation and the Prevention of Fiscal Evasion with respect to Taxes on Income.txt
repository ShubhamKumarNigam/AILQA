Summary:

The Convention between the Government of the Republic of India and the Government of the Republic of Austria for the avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with respect to Taxes on Income was signed on November 8, 1999, and entered into force on September 5, 2001. The Convention applies to residents of either Contracting State and covers taxes on income imposed in Austria or India. The Convention includes definitions of terms such as "Austria," "India," "fiscal year," "tax," "person," "company," "enterprise of a Contracting State," "enterprise of the other Contracting State," "competent authority," and "national." The Convention also includes provisions for the elimination of double taxation, non-discrimination, mutual agreement procedure, and exchange of information. The Protocol signed along with the Convention clarifies certain provisions related to Articles 6, 7, 24, and 26. The Convention supersedes the previous Convention between the two countries for the avoidance of double taxation with respect to taxes on income signed on September 24, 1963. Either Contracting State may terminate the Convention by giving written notice through diplomatic channels on or before June 30 of any calendar year beginning after the expiration of a period of five years from the date of its entry into force.
