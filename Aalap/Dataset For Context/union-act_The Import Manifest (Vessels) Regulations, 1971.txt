Summary:

The Import Manifest (Vessels) Regulations, 1971 outline the requirements for the import manifest of vessels arriving in India. These regulations specify the information that must be included in the import manifest, such as the name of the vessel, the name of the master, the port of loading, the date of sailing, the number of packages, the gross weight, the net weight, the markings and numbers of the packages, the port of discharge, the name of the consignee, the name of the notify party, whether the goods are for home consumption or for warehousing, whether the goods are for transshipment or for importation, whether the goods are liable to duty and if so, the rate of duty and the amount of duty payable, and the date of arrival of the vessel at the port.

The regulations also specify that the import manifest must be delivered in duplicate, cover all the goods carried in a vessel, and consist of an application for entry inwards in Form 1, a general declaration in Form II, a cargo declaration in Form III, a vessel's stores list in Form IV, and a list in Form V of Private property in the possession of the Master, Officers, Crew.

Further, the regulations outline the manner of declaring cargo, specifying that the cargo declaration shall be delivered in separate sheets in respect of each of the following categories of cargo: cargo to be landed, unaccompanied baggage, goods to be transhipped, and same bottom or retention cargo. The regulations also specify that the cargo declaration in respect of certain categories of cargo, such as arms, ammunition, explosives, narcotics, dangerous drugs, gold, and silver, shall be delivered in separate sheets and shall be set out in the order of the ports of loading.

The regulations also require that the vessel's stores list and the list of private property in the possession of the master, officers, and crew may be delivered along with the cargo declaration, but shall not in any case be delivered later than twenty-four hours after the arrival of the vessel at the port.

These regulations aim to ensure that all necessary information about the goods being imported is provided to the customs authorities in a clear and organized manner, to facilitate the smooth and efficient clearance of goods through customs.
