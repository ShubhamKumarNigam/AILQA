Summary:
The Reciprocity Act, 1943, is a law in India that provides for reciprocal treatment of persons domiciled in British Possessions in regards to entry, travel, residence, acquisition, holding or disposal of property, educational facilities, holding of public office, carrying on of any occupation, trade, business or profession, and franchise in India. The Act is designed to ensure that Indian nationals receive the same treatment in British Possessions as British nationals receive in India.

The Act defines a British Possession as any part of His Majesty's dominions and includes protectorates or other territories administered by a British Possession as a mandatory on behalf of the League of Nations. The Act applies to the whole of India, except for Part B States.

Under the Act, the Central Government has the power to impose reciprocal disabilities on persons domiciled in British Possessions if persons of Indian origin are subject to disabilities in that British Possession that are not applicable to persons domiciled in that British Possession in India. The burden of proof is on the person claiming exemption from these disabilities.

The Act also provides for the establishment of machinery to ascertain the disabilities in respect of any of the matters specified in section 3 to which persons of Indian origin are subject in any British Possession. The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act, including the setting up of machinery to ascertain the disabilities, establishing a suitable agency to administer the rules, specifying the disabilities to be imposed on persons not being of Indian origin who are domiciled in any British Possession, and enforcing these rules by the prescription of a penalty by way of imprisonment or fine or both.

The Immigration into India Act, 1924, has been repealed by this Act. The Act has been extended to the Union territory of Pondicherry by Act 26 of 1968.
