Summary:

The Destructive Insects and Pests Act, 1914 is a law in India that aims to prevent the introduction and transport of any insect, fungus, or other pests that may be destructive to crops. The Act empowers the Central Government to prohibit or regulate the import of any article or class of articles likely to cause infection to any crop. The Act also enables the Central Government to exercise control over the importation and movement of live insects which are or may be destructive to crops. The Act extends to the whole of India and defines terms such as "crops," "import," and "infection." The Act also includes provisions for penalties and protection for persons acting under the law. The Act has been amended several times to expand its scope and to enable the Government to levy and collect fees for inspection, fumigation, etc., on imported consignments and for validation of the fees already levied or collected.
