Summary:
THE ENERGY CONSERVATION ACT, 2001 is a law that provides for efficient use of energy and its conservation in India. The Act establishes the Bureau of Energy Efficiency, which is responsible for coordinating with designated consumers, designated agencies, and other agencies to promote energy conservation. The Bureau is governed by a Governing Council, which consists of a Chairperson and other members appointed by the Central Government. The Act also empowers the Central Government to enforce efficient use of energy and its conservation by specifying energy consumption standards, designating certain consumers as designated consumers, and requiring energy audits of designated consumers. The Act also provides for the establishment of a State Energy Conservation Fund to promote energy conservation within the State. The Act also lays down penalties for non-compliance with its provisions. The Appellate Tribunal for Energy Conservation hears appeals against orders of the adjudicating officer or the Central Government or the State Government or any other authority under this Act. The Act applies to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government.
