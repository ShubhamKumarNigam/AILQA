Summary:
The Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931, is a law that allows for the immediate effect of provisions in Bills related to the imposition or increase of duties of customs or excise for a limited period. It is also known as Act No. 16 of 1931 and was enacted on the 28th of September, 1931.

The Act includes the following key provisions:

- The Act may be called the Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931.
- The term "declared provision" refers to a provision in a Bill that has been declared under section 3 to have immediate effect.
- The Central Government may declare that any provision of a Bill relating to the imposition or increase of duties of customs or excise shall have immediate effect.
- A declared provision shall have the force of law immediately on the expiry of the day on which the Bill containing it is introduced.
- A declared provision shall cease to have the force of law under this Act when it comes into operation as an enactment, with or without amendment, or when the Central Government directs that it shall cease to have the force of law, or if it has not already ceased to have the force of law under clause (a) or clause (b), then on the expiry of the 75th day after the day on which the Bill containing it was introduced.
- Where a declared provision comes into operation as an enactment in an amended form before the expiry of the 75th day after the day on which the Bill containing it was introduced, refunds shall be made of all duties collected which would not have been collected if the provision adopted in the enactment had been the declared provision.
- Where a declared provision ceases to have the force of law under clause (b) or clause (c) of sub-section (2) of section 4, refunds shall be made of all duties collected which would not have been collected if the declaration in respect of it had not been made.

This Act has been extended to Berar by the Berar Laws Act, 1941, to Goa, Daman and Diu (with modifications) by Regulation 12 of 1962, to the whole of the Union territory of Lakshadweep by Regulation 8 of 1965, and to Pondicherry by Notification No. G.S.R. 392, dated 27-2-1963. The words "either Chamber of" were omitted by the A.O. 1948 and the words "sixtieth day" were substituted by Act 45 of 1964, s. 2, for "sixtieth day" (w.e.f. 1-2-1965).
