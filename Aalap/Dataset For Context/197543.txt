Summary:

The Delhi Sales Tax Act, 1975, is a law that governs the levy of tax on the sale of goods in the Union territory of Delhi. The Act defines various terms used in the Act, such as "Administrator," "Appellate Tribunal," "business," "dealer," "goods," "manufacture," "registered," "sale," "sale price," "tax," "turnover," and "year."

The Act specifies the incidence and levy of tax on sales of goods. Every dealer whose turnover during the year immediately preceding the commencement of this Act exceeds the taxable quantum and every dealer who is registered or is liable to pay tax under the Central Sales Tax Act, 1956, at the commencement of this Act, shall be liable to pay tax under this Act on all sales effected by him on or after such commencement. The tax liability continues until the expiry of three consecutive years during each of which the dealer's turnover has failed to exceed the taxable quantum and such further period after the date of such expiry as may be prescribed.

The tax rate is specified in the Act. The tax payable by a dealer under this Act shall be levied at the rate of twelve paise in the rupee on taxable turnover in respect of the goods specified in the First Schedule. The tax rate may be amended by the Administrator by notification in the Official Gazette.

The Act also provides for the collection and recovery of tax, appeals and revision, offences and penalties, and other miscellaneous provisions. The Act is applicable to the whole of the Union territory of Delhi and came into force on such date as the Administrator may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.
