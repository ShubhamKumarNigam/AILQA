Summary:
The High Courts (Seals) Act, 1950, is an Act that provides for the use of seals of common form and design by the High Courts in the States. The Act has three sections.

Section 1 of the Act states that it may be called the High Courts (Seals) Act, 1950.

Section 2 of the Act specifies the design and inscription of the seals of the High Courts in the States and the Judicial Commissioner's Court for a Union territory. Every High Court in a State shall have and use, as occasion may arise, a seal bearing a device and impression of the Asoka capital within an exergue or label surrounding the same. The seal shall have the following inscriptions at convenient places, namely, “THE SEAL OF THE HIGH COURT AT (or OF) ..............”, followed by the name of the seal of the High Court or the name of the State, as the case may be, and “Satyameva Jayate” in Devanagari script. Similarly, every Court of the Judicial Commissioner for a Union territory shall have and use, as occasion may arise, a seal similar to the seal of a High Court in a State, but with the first inscription reading “THE SEAL OF THE JUDICIAL COMMISSIONER'S COURT FOR .......................” followed by the name of the Union territory. The provisions of this section shall supersede any provision contrary thereto or inconsistent therewith contained in any Letters Patent, Order, direction or other law relating to the use of seals by High Courts.

Section 3 of the Act, which was earlier the Repeal of Ordinance 13 of 1950, has been repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act, 1957.

In summary, the High Courts (Seals) Act, 1950, provides for the use of seals of common form and design by the High Courts in the States and the Judicial Commissioner's Court for a Union territory, specifying the design and inscription of the seals, and superseding any provision contrary to or inconsistent with it. Section 3 of the Act has been repealed.
