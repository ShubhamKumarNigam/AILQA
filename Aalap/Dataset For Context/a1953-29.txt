Summary:

The Tea Act, 1953, is an Act of the Parliament of India that provides for the control by the Union of the Tea industry, including the control, in pursuance of the International Agreement now in force, of the cultivation of tea in, and of the export of tea from, India. The Act establishes a Tea Board and imposes a duty of excise on tea produced in India. The Act is divided into seven chapters, with Chapter I containing preliminary provisions, Chapter II dealing with the Tea Board, Chapter III controlling the extension of tea cultivation, Chapter IIIA managing or controlling tea undertakings or tea units by the Central Government in certain circumstances, Chapter IV controlling the export of tea and tea seed, Chapter V dealing with finance, accounts and audit, Chapter VI providing for general control by the Central Government, and Chapter VII containing miscellaneous provisions. The Act defines various terms related to the tea industry, such as "Board," "broker," "cess," "Commissioner of Customs," "dealer," "export," "export allotment," "Fund," "manufacturer," "member," "owner," "prescribed," "tea," and "tea seed." The Act also sets out the powers and duties of the Tea Board, including the power to promote the development of the tea industry, improve the quality of tea, register and license manufacturers, brokers, tea waste dealers and persons engaged in the business of blending tea, train in tea testing and fixing grade standards of tea, increase the consumption of tea in India and elsewhere, and collect statistics from growers, manufacturers, dealers and such other persons as may be prescribed. The Act also provides for the dissolution of the Board, the control of the extension of tea cultivation, the management or control of tea undertakings or tea units by the Central Government in certain circumstances, and the control of the export of tea and tea seed. The Act also contains provisions for finance, accounts and audit, general control by the Central Government, and miscellaneous provisions. The Act applies to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and came into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.
