Summary:

THE RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT, 2005 is an Act to provide for setting out the practical regime of right to information for citizens to secure access to information under the control of public authorities, in order to promote transparency and accountability in the working of every public authority. The Act defines key terms such as 'appropriate Government', 'Central Information Commission', 'Central Public Information Officer', 'Chief Information Commissioner' and 'Information Commissioner', 'competent authority', 'information', 'prescribed', 'public authority', 'record', 'right to information', 'State Information Commission', 'State Chief Information Commissioner' and 'State Information Commissioner', 'State Public Information Officer'.

The Act is divided into five chapters. Chapter I, Preliminary, includes Sections 1-2, which provide the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act, as well as definitions of key terms. Chapter II, Right to Information and Obligations of Public Authorities, includes Sections 3-7, which establish the right to information for all citizens, outline the obligations of public authorities, and provide for the designation of Public Information Officers. Chapter III, The Central Information Commission, includes Sections 12-14, which establish the Central Information Commission, provide for the terms of office and conditions of service of the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners, and outline the removal of the Chief Information Commissioner or Information Commissioners. Chapter IV, The State Information Commission, includes Sections 15-17, which establish the State Information Commission, provide for the terms of office and conditions of service of the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners, and outline the removal of the State Chief Information Commissioner or State Information Commissioners. Chapter V, Powers and Functions of the Information Commissions, Appeal and Penalties, includes Sections 18-20, which outline the powers and functions of the Information Commissions, provide for appeal, and establish penalties for non-compliance with the Act.

The Act also includes a First Schedule, which provides a form of oath or affirmation to be made by the Chief Information Commissioner/Information Commissioner/State Chief Information Commissioner/State Information Commissioner, and a Second Schedule, which lists intelligence and security organizations established by the Central Government to which the Act does not apply. Finally, the Act includes provisions for the laying of rules, the removal of difficulties, and the repeal of the Freedom of Information Act, 2002.
