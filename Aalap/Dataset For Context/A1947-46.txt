Summary:

The United Nations (Privileges and Immunities) Act, 1947, is an Act that gives effect to the Convention on the Privileges and Immunities of the United Nations in India. The Act has four sections and a schedule. Section 1 is the short title of the Act. Section 2 confers privileges and immunities on the United Nations and its representatives and officers, and these provisions have the force of law in India. The Central Government may amend the schedule in conformity with any amendments made and adopted to the provisions of the Convention. Section 3 empowers the Central Government to confer similar privileges and immunities on other international organizations and their representatives and officers. Section 4 authorizes the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. The schedule contains the Convention on the Privileges and Immunities of the United Nations, which includes provisions related to the juridical personality, property, funds, and assets, facilities in respect of communications, representatives of members, officials, experts on missions for the United Nations, United Nations laissez-passer, and settlement of disputes. The Convention aims to ensure the independent exercise of functions by the United Nations and its representatives and officials, and to facilitate their work in India.
