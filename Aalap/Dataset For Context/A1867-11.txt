Summary:
The Oriental Gas Company Act, 1867, is an Act to empower the Oriental Gas Company Limited to extend their operations to certain places in India. The Act was enacted on 1st March, 1867.

The Act empowers the Central Government to extend the Oriental Gas Company Act, 1857, to any place in India, except Calcutta and its environs, with the previous sanction of the Central Government. Section 7 of the said Act shall be read as if for the words ‘Town of Calcutta’, the name of the place to which the said act shall be so extended were substituted. Section 22 of the said Act shall be read as if after the words ‘Joint Stock Companies’ Act, 1856’, the following words were inserted; (that is to say) ‘the Indian Companies’ Act, 1866, or any other Statute or Act for the time being in force relating to Joint Stock Companies’.

The Act also provides for the substitution of certain expressions in the said Act, such as the substitution of the name of the highest Civil Court of appeal in the place where the Act is extended for the expression ‘Supreme Court of Judicature at Fort William’, and the substitution of the expression ‘the States’ for the expression ‘the territories of the East India Company’.

The interpretation clause of the Act has been repealed by the A.O. 1937. The Act was originally enacted to confer certain powers on the Oriental Gas Company, Limited, exercisable only in Calcutta and its environs. The present Act empowers the Company to extend its operations to any other place in India, subject to the previous sanction of the Central Government.
