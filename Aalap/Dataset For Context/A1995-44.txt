Summary:
THE TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT BOARD ACT, 1995 is an Act to provide for the constitution of a Board for payment of equity capital or any other financial assistance to industrial concerns and other agencies attempting development and commercial application of indigenous technology or adapting imported technology to wider domestic applications. The Act establishes the Technology Development Board (TDB) as a body corporate with the power to contract, sue, and be sued. The TDB consists of members from various ministries and departments of the Central Government, as well as up to four members appointed by the Central Government from among experts in technology development and application, banking and finance, industry, agriculture, and rural development. The TDB may provide equity capital or financial assistance to industrial concerns and other agencies for the commercial application of indigenous technology or the adaptation of imported technology to wider domestic applications. The TDB may also provide financial assistance to research and development institutions engaged in developing indigenous technology or adapting imported technology for commercial application. The TDB is funded through grants and loans from the Central Government, as well as through sums received from other sources, recoveries of amounts granted from the Fund, and any income from investment of the amount of the Fund. The TDB is required to maintain proper accounts and prepare an annual statement of accounts, which is audited by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India. The TDB is also required to submit an annual report to the Central Government, which is laid before each House of Parliament. The Act also includes provisions for the delegation of powers, the protection of action taken in good faith, and the making of rules and regulations.
