Summary:
The International Development Association (Status, Immunities and Privileges) Act, 1960, is a law in India that implements the international agreement for the establishment and operation of the International Development Association (IDA) insofar as it relates to the status, immunities, and privileges of that Association. The Act has the force of law in India, and it extends to the whole country. It came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government through a notification in the Official Gazette.

The Act defines the "Agreement" as the Agreement for the establishment and operation of the IDA and "Association" as the International Development Association established under the Agreement. The Act confers certain immunities and privileges on the Association and its officers and employees, in accordance with Article VIII of the Agreement. However, these immunities do not entitle the Association to import goods free of customs duties without restrictions on their subsequent sale or confer any exemption from duties or taxes that form part of the price of goods sold or that are in fact no more than charges for services rendered.

The Central Government may amend the Schedule in conformity with any amendments made and adopted to the provisions of the Agreement set out therein. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out its purposes, and any notification issued under sub-section (2) of section 3 and every rule made under section 4 shall be laid before each House of Parliament while it is in session for a period of thirty days.

The Schedule to the Act sets out the provisions of the agreement that shall have the force of law in India, including Article VIII, which deals with the status, immunities, and privileges of the Association. The Article provides that the Association shall possess full juridical personality and capacity to contract, acquire and dispose of immovable and movable property, and institute legal proceedings. The Association's property and assets shall be immune from all forms of seizure, attachment, or execution before the delivery of final judgment against it. The archives of the Association shall be inviolable, and its official communications shall be accorded the same treatment as those of other members.

The Article also provides immunities and privileges to the officers and employees of the Association, who shall be immune from legal process with respect to acts performed in their official capacity, except when the Association waives this immunity. They shall also be accorded the same immunities from immigration restrictions, alien registration requirements, and national service obligations as are accorded to representatives, officials, and employees of comparable rank of other members. The Association, its assets, property, income, and transactions authorized by the Agreement shall be immune from all taxation and from all customs duties. No tax shall be levied on or in respect of salaries and emoluments paid by the Association to its Executive Directors, Alternates, officials, or employees who are not local citizens, local subjects, or other local nationals. No taxation of any kind shall be levied on any obligation or security issued or guaranteed by the Association, which discriminates against such obligation or security solely because it is issued or guaranteed by the Association or if the sole jurisdictional basis for such taxation is the location of any office or place of business maintained by the Association.
