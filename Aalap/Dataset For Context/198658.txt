Summary:

The Delhi Apartment Ownership Act, 1986 (Act No. 58 of 1986) is a law that provides for the ownership of an individual apartment in a multi-storeyed building and of an undivided interest in the common areas and facilities appurtenant to such apartment. The Act aims to make such apartment and interest heritable and transferable and to regulate matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act is divided into six chapters, with Chapter I containing preliminary provisions, Chapter II dealing with ownership, heritability, and transferability of apartments, Chapter III dealing with the Deed of Apartment and its registration, Chapter IV establishing the Association of Apartment Owners and its bye-laws, Chapter V dealing with common profits, common expenses, and other matters, and Chapter VI containing miscellaneous provisions. The Act applies to every apartment in a multi-storeyed building which was constructed mainly for residential or commercial or other specified purposes on freehold or leasehold land, with a lease period of thirty years or more. The Act provides for the exclusive ownership and possession of each apartment and the undivided interest in the common areas and facilities appurtenant to such apartment, with the percentage of such interest being specified in the Deed of Apartment. The Act also provides for the heritability and transferability of each apartment, subject to certain conditions, and for the rights and obligations of apartment owners, the Association of Apartment Owners, and the Manager. The Act also contains provisions for the registration of the Deed of Apartment and for the regulation of the affairs of the Association of Apartment Owners through bye-laws. The Act also provides for the insurance of the property, the disposition of property in the event of damage or destruction, and the liability of apartment owners for common expenses. The Act also contains miscellaneous provisions, including provisions for the reduction or remission of stamp duty, registration fees, and court fees, and for the power to make rules to carry out the provisions of the Act.
