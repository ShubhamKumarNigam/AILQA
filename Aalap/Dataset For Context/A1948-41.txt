Summary:
The Diplomatic and Consular Officers (Oaths and Fees) Act, 1948 (Act No. 41 of 1948) provides for the administration of oaths by diplomatic and consular officers and the levy of fees in respect of certain of their official duties. The Act defines "consular officer" and "diplomatic officer" and gives them the power to administer oaths, take affidavits, and perform notarial acts in any foreign country or place where they are exercising their functions. Any oath, affidavit, or notarial act administered, sworn, or done by or before any such person shall be as effectual as if duly administered, sworn, or done by or before any lawful authority in a State. The Act also provides for punishment for offences such as swearing falsely in any oath or affidavit taken or made in accordance with the provisions of this Act, forging or fraudulently altering the seal or signature of any person authorized by this Act to administer an oath, and tendering in evidence, or otherwise using, any affidavit having any seal or signature so forged, counterfeited, or fraudulently altered knowing the same to be forged, counterfeited, or fraudulently altered. The Act also provides for the trial of offences and the power to prescribe fees. The Central Government may prescribe the fees to be levied in respect of any matter or thing done by a diplomatic or consular officer in the execution of his office. The Act also provides for the publication and issue of tables of fees and the power to make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act.
