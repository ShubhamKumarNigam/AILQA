Summary:
The Special Economic Zones Act, 2005, is an Act of the Parliament of India that provides for the establishment, development, and management of the Special Economic Zones (SEZs) for the promotion of exports and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act defines SEZs as specific areas notified by the Central Government for the purpose of setting up Units for manufacturing, trading, or rendering services or as Free Trade and Warehousing Zones. The Act also provides for the constitution of a Board of Approval to approve proposals for setting up SEZs, and an Approval Committee to approve proposals for setting up Units within SEZs. The Act also lays down the procedures for the development, operation, and maintenance of SEZs, and provides for certain exemptions and concessions to Units and Developers in SEZs. The Act also provides for the establishment of an Authority to undertake measures for the development, operation, and management of the SEZ for which it is constituted. The Act also provides for the protection of action taken in good faith and the power to modify provisions of this Act or other enactments in relation to SEZs. The Act also provides for the power of the State Government to grant exemption and savings. The Act has overriding effect and certain provisions do not apply. The Act also provides for the amendment of certain enactments. The Act also provides for savings. The Act also has certain Schedules, including the First Schedule, which lists the enactments that are amended by this Act, and the Second Schedule, which lists the modifications to the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Third Schedule lists the amendments to certain enactments.
