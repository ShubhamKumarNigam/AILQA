Summary:
The Expenditure-Tax Act, 1987 (Act No. 35 of 1987) is a law in India that imposes a tax on expenditure incurred in certain hotels or restaurants. The Act extends to the whole of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The tax is levied at a rate of 10% of the chargeable expenditure incurred in a hotel and 15% of the chargeable expenditure incurred in a restaurant. The tax is collected by the person who carries on the business of the hotel or restaurant, or by any other person who provides services specified in the Act. The tax collected is to be paid to the credit of the Central Government by the 10th of the month immediately following the calendar month in which the tax was collected. Every person responsible for collecting the tax is required to furnish a return to the Assessing Officer in the prescribed form and manner, showing the aggregate of the payments received in respect of chargeable expenditure, the amount of tax collected, the amount of tax paid to the credit of the Central Government, and such other particulars as may be prescribed. The Act also provides for the assessment of chargeable expenditure and the payment of tax, as well as penalties for failure to collect or pay the tax, failure to furnish prescribed returns, concealment of chargeable expenditure, and failure to comply with notices. The Act also includes provisions for appeals and revisions, and applies certain provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, with necessary modifications.
