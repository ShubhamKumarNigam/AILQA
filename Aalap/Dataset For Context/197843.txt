Summary:

THE PRIZE CHITS AND MONEY CIRCULATION SCHEMES (BANNING) ACT, 1978, aims to ban the promotion or conduct of prize chits and money circulation schemes in India. The Act applies to the whole country and defines various terms related to prize chits and money circulation schemes.

Section 3 of the Act prohibits the promotion or conduct of any prize chit or money circulation scheme, enrollment as a member, or participation in it. Violation of this provision can result in imprisonment for up to three years, a fine of up to INR 5,000, or both.

Section 5 of the Act imposes penalties on individuals or entities involved in printing, publishing, selling, distributing, or possessing any ticket, coupon, or other document related to prize chits or money circulation schemes. The punishment includes imprisonment for up to two years, a fine of up to INR 3,000, or both.

Section 6 holds companies and their officers responsible for offenses committed under this Act. If a company commits an offense under this Act, every person in charge of, and responsible for, the company's business at the time of the offense can be deemed guilty and punished accordingly.

Section 7 empowers police officers and authorized officers to enter, search, and seize premises, documents, books, records, or any other literature related to prize chits or money circulation schemes.

Section 8 allows the State Government to declare any copy of a newspaper or other publication containing material related to prize chits or money circulation schemes to be forfeited to the State Government.

Section 9 states that no court inferior to that of a Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or Chief Judicial Magistrate shall try any offenses punishable under this Act.

Section 10 declares that all offenses punishable under this Act shall be cognizable.

Section 11 exempts certain prize chits or money circulation schemes from the purview of this Act, including those promoted by State Governments, companies wholly owned by State Governments, banking companies, banking institutions, the State Bank of India, subsidiary banks, corresponding new banks, Regional Rural Banks, co-operative banks, and charitable or educational institutions notified by the State Government in consultation with the Reserve Bank.

Section 12 lays down transitional provisions for persons conducting prize chits or money circulation schemes at the commencement of this Act. They may continue to conduct such chits or schemes for a specified period for winding up the business, subject to certain conditions.

Section 13 empowers the State Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act, in consultation with the Reserve Bank.

Section 14 repeals the Andhra Pradesh Money Circulation Scheme (Prohibition) Act, 1965, and the Madhya Pradesh Dhan Parichalan Skeem (Pratishedh) Adhiniyam, 1975, and saves any action taken under the repealed Acts that is not inconsistent with this Act.
