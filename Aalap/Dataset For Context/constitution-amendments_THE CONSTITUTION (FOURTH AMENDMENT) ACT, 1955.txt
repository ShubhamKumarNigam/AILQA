Amendment of article 19.-

In article 19 of the Constitution, in clause (6), for the words "Subject to the provisions of articles 301, 302, 303, the trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India", the words "Subject to the provisions of articles 301, 302, 303, the trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India except in so far as the President may, by order, direct" shall be substituted.

Amendment of article 31.-

In article 31 of the Constitution, in clause (2), for the words "nor shall any property be compulsorily acquired or requisitioned save for a public purpose and save by authority of law", the words "nor shall any property be compulsorily acquired or requisitioned save for a public purpose and save by authority of law which provides for compensation for the property taken" shall be substituted.

Amendment of article 305.-

In article 305 of the Constitution, after clause (1), the following clause shall be inserted, namely:-

"(1A) No law providing for the acquisition or requisitioning of any property, or for the imposition of any tax, cess or fee in respect of any property or transaction, shall be called in question in any court on the ground that such law is not within the authority of the Legislature that enacted it on the ground of repugnancy to any other law, whether passed before or after such law, or on the ground of unreasonableness or arbitrariness."

Amendment of article 314.-

In article 314 of the Constitution, in clause (2), for the words "Subject to the provisions of articles 301, 302, 303", the words "Subject to the provisions of articles 301, 302, 303, except as otherwise expressly provided by Parliament" shall be substituted.

The Constitution (Fourth Amendment) Act, 1955, was enacted to clarify the State's power of compulsory acquisition and requisitioning of private property and distinguish it from cases where the operation of regulatory or prohibitory laws of the State results in "deprivation of property". It also aimed to extend the scope of article 31A to cover certain categories of essential welfare legislation and include in the Ninth Schedule to the Constitution two more State Acts and four Central Acts which fall within the scope of sub-clauses (d) and (f) of clause (1) of the revised article 31A for their complete, retrospective validation. Additionally, the Act proposed an amendment to article 305 to make it clear that any question regarding State monopoly in a particular sphere of trade or commerce ought to be left to the final decision of the Legislature. The Act also made amendments to articles 19, 31, 305, and 314, and added entries to the Ninth and First Schedules of the Constitution.
