Summary:

The Northern India Canal and Drainage Act, 1873, is an Act to regulate irrigation, navigation, and drainage in Northern India. The Act extends to Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, and Delhi and applies to all lands, whether permanently settled, temporarily settled, or free from revenue. The Act defines various terms used throughout, such as "canal," which includes all canals, channels, reservoirs, works, embankments, structures, supply and escape channels, and any part of a river or stream to which the State Government has applied the provisions of Part II of this Act. The Act also outlines the powers and duties of various officers, such as Commissioners, Collectors, Canal-officers, Superintending Canal-officers, Divisional Canal-officers, and Sub-Divisional Canal-officers. Part II of the Act deals with the application of water for public purposes and sets out the process for making claims for compensation for any damage caused by the exercise of the powers conferred by this Act. Part III deals with the construction and maintenance of works, while Part IV deals with the supply of water. Part V deals with water-rates, and Part VI deals with canal navigation. Part VII deals with drainage, and Part VIII deals with obtaining labor for canals and drainage-works. Part IX deals with jurisdiction, while Part X deals with offences and penalties. Part XI deals with subsidiary rules, and the Act includes a schedule and forms. The Act has been amended and repealed in various parts of India and has been declared not to apply to certain canals in Punjab.
