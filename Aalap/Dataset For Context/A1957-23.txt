Summary:

THE RAILWAY PROTECTION FORCE ACT, 1957, is an Act of the Indian Parliament that establishes a Railway Protection Force for the better protection and security of railway property. The Act extends to the whole of India and came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government.

The Act defines various terms used in the Act, including "Force," "Director-General," "enrolled member of the Force," "Force custody," "member of the Force," "passenger," "passenger area," "prescribed," "railway property," "subordinate officer," "superior officer," and "under officer."

The Force is constituted and maintained by the Central Government and consists of superior officers, subordinate officers, under officers, and other enrolled members. The Force is under the command and supervision of the Director-General, who is appointed by the Central Government. The superintendence of the Force vests in the Central Government, and the administration of the Force is carried on by an Inspector-General, an Additional Inspector-General, or a Deputy Inspector-General within the limits of a railway.

The Act empowers any member of the Force to arrest without a warrant any person who voluntarily causes hurt to, or attempts to cause hurt to, or wrongfully restrains or attempts to wrongfully restrain, or assaults, threatens to assault, or uses or threatens to use criminal force to, any member of the Force in the execution of his duty, or with intent to prevent or deter him from discharging his duty. The Act also empowers any member of the Force to search without a warrant any person who has been concerned in, or against whom a reasonable suspicion exists of his having been concerned in, or who is found taking precautions to conceal his presence under circumstances which afford reason to believe that he is taking such precautions with a view to committing a cognizable offence which relates to railway property, passenger area, and passengers.

Any member of the Force making an arrest under this Act shall, without unnecessary delay, make over the person so arrested to a police officer or take such person to the nearest police station. Every member of the Force shall, for the purposes of this Act, be considered to be always on duty and shall, at any time, be liable to be employed at any place within India.

No member of the Force shall, without the previous sanction in writing of the Central Government or of the prescribed authority, be a member of, or be associated in any way with, any trade union, labour union, political association, or with any class of trade unions, labour unions, or political associations, or be a member of, or be associated in any way with, any other society, institution, association, or organization that is not recognized as part of the Force or is not of a purely social, recreational, or religious nature, or communicate with the press or publish or cause to be published any book, letter, or other document except where such communication or publication is in the bonafide discharge of his duties or is of a purely literary, artistic, or scientific character or is of a prescribed nature.

A member of the Force shall not, by reason of his suspension from office, cease to be a member of the Force and shall, during that period, be subject to the same responsibilities, discipline, and penalties to which he would have been subject if he were on duty. Every person who for any reason ceases to be a member of the Force shall forthwith surrender to any superior officer empowered to receive the same, his certificate of appointment, the arms, accoutrements, clothing, and other articles which have been furnished to him for the performance of his duties as a member of the Force.

The Act provides for penalties for neglect of duty, violation of duty, wilful breach or neglect of any rule or lawful order made by a superior officer, withdrawal from duties without permission, failure to report for duty on the expiration of leave, engaging in employment other than duty as an enrolled member of the Force, or cowardice. The Act also provides for the investigation and trial of offences committed by members of the Force and empowers the Central Government to invest Assistant Inspector-General, Senior Commandant, or Commandant with the powers of a Magistrate of any class for the purpose of inquiring into or trying any offence committed by an enrolled member of the Force.

The Act applies to members of the Force as it applies to members of a police force and provides for the protection of acts of members of the Force. The Act also provides that no legal proceeding, whether civil or criminal, which may lawfully be brought against any member of the Force for anything done or intended to be done under the powers conferred by, or in pursuance of, any provision of this Act or the rules thereunder shall be commenced after three months from the date on which the act complained of shall have been committed.

The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act, including regulating the classes and grades and the pay and remuneration of members of the Force, regulating the powers and duties of members of the Force authorized to exercise any functions by or under this Act, fixing the period of service for members of the Force, prescribing the description and quality of arms, accoutrements, clothing, and other necessary articles to be furnished to the members of the Force, prescribing the places of residence of the members of the Force, instituting, managing, and regulating any fund for any purpose connected with the administration of the Force, regulating matters with respect to Force custody under this Act, including the procedure to be followed for taking persons into such custody, regulating matters with respect to disposal of cases relating to offences under this Act and specifying the places in which person convicted under this Act may be confined, and any other matter which has to be, or may be, imposed or in respect of which rules are required to be made under this Act. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament while it is in session for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.
