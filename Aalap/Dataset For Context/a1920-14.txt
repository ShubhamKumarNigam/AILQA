Summary:
The Charitable and Religious Trusts Act, 1920, is an Act that aims to provide more effective control over the administration of charitable and religious trusts in India. The Act extends to the whole of India, except for the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act empowers any person having an interest in an express or constructive trust created or existing for public purposes of a charitable or religious nature to apply to the Court for an order embodying directions, such as directing the trustee to furnish particulars regarding the trust and directing that the accounts of the trust be examined and audited. The Act also allows trustees to apply for the opinion, advice, or direction of the Court on any question affecting the management or administration of the trust property. The costs, charges, and expenses of and incidental to any petition under this Act are in the discretion of the Court. The Act also provides for the savings of certain circumstances, such as if a suit is pending in respect of the trust, if the trust property is vested in a specific authority, or if a scheme for the administration of the trust property has been settled or approved by a Court or authority. The provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, apply to all proceedings under this Act. No appeal shall lie from any order passed or against any opinion, advice, or direction given under this Act. The Act has been extended to Berar, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Pondicherry, and Goa, Daman and Diu. The Act has been amended in its application to Rajasthan and has ceased to be in force in its application to the State of Madras. The Act is applicable to the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir and the Union territory of Ladakh. In the application of this Act to West Bengal, a new section 13 has been inserted, which provides that the provisions of this Act shall not, so far as they are inconsistent with those of the Bengal Wakf Act, 1934, apply to any Wakf property in West Bengal.
