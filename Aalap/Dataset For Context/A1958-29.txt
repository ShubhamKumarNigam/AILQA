Summary:

THE WORKING JOURNALISTS (FIXATION OF RATES OF WAGES) ACT, 1958, is an Act to provide for the fixation of rates of wages in respect of working journalists and for matters connected therewith. The Act defines key terms such as "Committee," "prescribed," "Wage Board," "Wage Board decision," "Wages," "Working Journalists Act," and "words and expressions."

The Act establishes a Committee to fix rates of wages in respect of working journalists in the light of the Judgment of the Supreme Court, dated the 19th day of March, 1958, relating to the Wage Board decision, and in the light of all other relevant circumstances. The Committee's functions include calling upon newspaper establishments and working journalists and other persons interested in the Wage Board decision to make representations, examining materials placed before the Wage Board and further materials obtained under this Act, and making recommendations to the Central Government for the fixation of rates of wages in respect of working journalists.

The Act grants the Committee the power to exercise all or any of the powers which an industrial tribunal, constituted under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, exercises for the adjudication of an industrial dispute referred to it and to regulate its own procedure. The Committee may also examine accounts or documents or obtain statements from any person, and the authorised officer may exercise all or any of the powers which an industrial tribunal may exercise under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.

The Central Government may enforce the recommendations of the Committee by making an order in terms of the recommendations or subject to such modifications as it thinks fit. The order shall come into operation on the date of publication or on such date, whether prospectively or retrospectively, as may be specified in the order.

Every working journalist shall be entitled to be paid by his employer wages at a rate which shall in no case be less than the rate of wages specified in the order. If any question arises as to the amount due under this Act to a working journalist from his employer, the State Government may refer the question to any Labour Court constituted by it under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, or under any corresponding law relating to investigation and settlement of industrial disputes in force in the State.

The Act includes provisions for the authentication of orders, letters, etc., of the Committee and for the recovery of money due to working journalists. The Act also specifies that the provisions of this Act shall have effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith in the terms of any award, agreement, contract of service, whether made before or after the commencement of this Act. The Act also includes provisions for penalties for non-compliance.

The Central Government may make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act, and every rule made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament. The Act repeals and saves certain provisions of the Repealing and Amending Act, 1960.
