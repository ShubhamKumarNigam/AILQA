Summary:
The Reserve and Auxiliary Air Forces Act, 1952 (Act No. 62 of 1952) provides for the constitution and regulation of certain Air Force Reserves and an Auxiliary Air Force in India. The Act comprises six chapters, with Chapter I containing preliminary provisions, Chapter II dealing with the Regular Air Force Reserve, Chapter III with the Air Defence Reserve, Chapter IV with the Auxiliary Air Force, Chapter V with liability and discipline of members of Reserve and Auxiliary Air Forces, and Chapter VI with miscellaneous provisions.

Chapter II outlines the provisions for the constitution of the Regular Air Force Reserve, which consists solely of persons transferred or appointed to it under section 5. The competent authority may transfer or appoint officers or airmen of the Air Force to the Regular Air Force Reserve, and any officer or airman so transferred or appointed shall be deemed to be a member of the said Reserve. The competent authority may also cancel any order made under sub-section (1) or (2) of section 5, and on the cancellation of such order, the person in respect of whom the order had been made shall cease to be a member of the Regular Air Force Reserve.

Chapter III deals with the Air Defence Reserve, which is raised and maintained for the purpose of providing a reserve of officers and airmen for the Air Force. The Central Government may raise and maintain an Air Defence Reserve, which shall consist of persons deemed under the provisions of section 16 to be enrolled therein. Members of the Air Defence Reserve are divided into three classes: general duties officers, ground duties officers, and airmen. Every citizen of India who holds or has held a public transport pilot’s license, has had not less than two hundred hours’ experience of solo flying, holds or has held a first class navigator's license, has had at least four years’ aviation experience, holds or has held a first class radio telegraph operator’s license, holds or has held a radio telephone operator’s license, holds or has held a license as ground engineer in any of the categories A, B, C, D or X issued under the Indian Aircraft Rules, 1937, or is or was at any time employed in connection with any aerodrome or in connection with the control and movement of aircraft, in such capacity as may be prescribed, shall within the prescribed period correctly fill up, or cause to be filled up, to the best of his knowledge and belief the prescribed form, and sign and lodge it with the competent authority nearest to his usual place of residence or business.

Chapter IV deals with the Auxiliary Air Force, which is a voluntary force raised and maintained for the purpose of providing a reserve of officers and airmen for the Air Force. The Central Government may raise and maintain an Auxiliary Air Force, which shall consist of general duties officers, ground duties officers, and airmen. The President may grant to such person as he thinks fit a commission as an officer in the Auxiliary Air Force with designation of rank corresponding to that of any commissioned officer in the Air Force. Any citizen of India may offer himself for enrolment in the Auxiliary Air Force and may, if he satisfies the prescribed conditions, be so enrolled on such terms as may be prescribed. Every officer and every enrolled person shall, subject to any rules that may be made in this behalf under this Act, be required to serve in the Auxiliary Air Force for a period of five years from the date of his appointment or enrolment but may, after the completion of his period of service, volunteer to serve therein for further periods each of not more than five years’ duration.

Chapter V deals with the liability and discipline of members of Reserve and Auxiliary Air Forces. Every member of an Air Force Reserve or the Auxiliary Air Force shall, during the period of his service, be liable to be called up for training for such period as may be prescribed and for medical examination, for service in aid of the civil power, and for Air Force service in India or abroad. Every member of an Air Force Reserve or the Auxiliary Air Force shall, when called up for training, medical examination or for service under this Act, be subject to the Air Force Act, 1950 (45 of 1950), and the rules made thereunder in the same manner as a person belonging to the Air Force and holding the same rank is subject to the said Act and rules and shall continue to be so subject until duly released from such training, medical examination or service, as the case may be.

Chapter VI contains miscellaneous provisions, including provisions relating to the reinstatement in civil employ of persons required to perform service under this Act, the preservation of certain rights of persons called up for service, pay and allowances, penalties, service of notice, competent authority to be public servant, power of Central Government to grant exemptions, and power to make rules. The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act. Any rule made under this section may provide that a contravention thereof shall be punishable with fine which may extend to fifty rupees. Every rule made under this section shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.
