Summary:
The Constitution (Eightieth Amendment) Act, 2000, proposes a new scheme for sharing Union taxes and duties between the Center and the States. Under this scheme, 26% of the gross proceeds of Union taxes and duties (excluding certain specified taxes) will be assigned to the States in lieu of their existing share in income-tax, basic excise duties, special excise duties, and grants in lieu of tax on railway passenger fares. Additionally, 3% share in the gross proceeds of all Central taxes and duties (excluding the same specified taxes) will be assigned to the States in lieu of their existing share in Additional Excise Duties in lieu of Sales Tax on tobacco, cotton, and sugar. The benefits of this scheme include sharing the aggregate buoyancy of Central taxes, greater flexibility in tax reform, and greater certainty of resource flows to the States. This scheme was recommended by the Tenth Finance Commission and was agreed to in principle by the previous Government, subject to certain modifications. The percentage share of the States will be reviewed by successive Finance Commissions instead of being frozen for fifteen years, and the sharing of gross proceeds will be changed to the sharing of net proceeds to maintain consistency with other articles of the Constitution. The scheme will be effective from April 1, 1996, and the recommendations of the 11th Finance Commission will cover the five-year period starting from April 1, 2000. The Bill seeks to amend articles 269, 270, and 272 of the Constitution to bring several Central taxes and duties at par with personal income-tax as far as their constitutionally mandated sharing with the States is concerned.
