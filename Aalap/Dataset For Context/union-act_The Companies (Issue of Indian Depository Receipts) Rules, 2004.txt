
The Companies (Issue of Indian Depository Receipts) Rules, 2004, outline the regulations for issuing Indian Depository Receipts (IDRs) to foreign companies. These rules apply to foreign companies, whether or not they have established a place of business in India. IDRs are a type of depository receipt issued by a domestic depository in India against the underlying equity shares of a foreign company. The issuing company must be incorporated outside India and authorized by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) to issue IDRs. The rules specify the eligibility criteria for issuing IDRs, including requirements related to the issuing company's financial performance, debt-equity ratio, and compliance with SEBI regulations. The rules also outline the procedure for making an issue of IDRs, including the requirement to obtain prior permission from SEBI and to file a prospectus or letter of offer. The rules further specify other conditions for the issue of IDRs, such as restrictions on redemption and repatriation of proceeds. The rules also provide for the registration of documents, conditions for the issue of prospectus and application, listing of IDRs, procedure for transfer and redemption, continuous disclosure requirements, distribution of corporate benefits, penalty, repeal and savings, and power of Central Government to decide certain questions.
