Summary:
The Auroville Foundation Act, 1988, establishes the Auroville Foundation to manage and develop Auroville, an international cultural township founded by the "Mother" on February 28, 1968. The Act vests the undertakings of Auroville in the Central Government for a limited period and then transfers them to the Foundation to ensure better management and further development in accordance with its original charter. The Act includes definitions of key terms and chapters on preliminary provisions, acquisition and transfer of undertakings, the Auroville Foundation, and miscellaneous provisions. The Foundation consists of the Governing Board, the Residents' Assembly, and the Auroville International Advisory Council. The Governing Board has the power to appoint committees and perform various functions, including promoting the ideals of Auroville, reviewing basic policies and programmes, monitoring and reviewing activities, preparing a master-plan, and authorizing and coordinating fund-raising. The Residents' Assembly is responsible for allowing the admission or termination of residents, organizing various activities, formulating the master plan, and recommending proposals for raising funds. The Auroville International Advisory Council may advise the Governing Board on any matter relating to the development and management of Auroville. The Act also includes provisions for the dissolution of the Foundation, grants by the Central Government, duty to furnish returns, borrowing powers, accounts and audit, and protection of action taken in good faith.
