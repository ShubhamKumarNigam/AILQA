The Delhi Rent Control Act of 1958 specifies the standard rent for land and buildings in New Delhi. The rateable value of a property is the maximum amount of rent that can be charged, and is calculated as ten percent of the annual rent, minus any allowances for repairs, insurance, and other expenses. The rateable value of land that is not built upon is fixed at five percent of its estimated capital value. Plant and machinery contained in or upon any land or building are considered part of the property for the purpose of determining the rateable value.

Water supplied to any land or building is subject to a charge, which is determined by the Council at a rate prescribed for different classes of lands and buildings. The Council may also prescribe conditions for the use of water and for charges to be paid when a meter is out of order or under repair. Any sum payable for water supplied that is not paid when it becomes due is recoverable as an arrear of tax under this Act.

Union properties are exempt from property tax, except for lands and buildings that were previously subject to tax and continue to be taxed by the Council. If possession of a Union property has been delivered to a displaced person, association of displaced persons, or any other person, property tax is leviable and recoverable from the date of possession.

The property tax is primarily leviable upon the lessor, superior lessor, or person in whom the right to let the land or building vests. In the case of a building that is severally owned, the liability for property tax is joint and several. If the land or building is let or sub-let, the tenant or sub-tenant is entitled to receive the difference between the amount of property tax levied and the amount that would be levied if the rateable value were equal to the difference between the amount of rent received and the amount of rent paid.

If property tax is not recovered from the person primarily liable, the Chairperson may recover the tax from every occupier of the land or building by attachment of the rent payable by the occupier. An occupier who has paid property tax may recover the amount from the person primarily liable.

Property tax due under this Act is a first charge on the interest in the land or building of the person liable for the tax, as well as on any goods or other movable properties found within or upon the land or building and belonging to the person liable for the tax. The term "property tax" includes the costs on recovery of property tax and any penalty payable as specified in the bye-laws.
