Summary:
The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 (Act No. 27 of 2006) was enacted on 16th June, 2006. The Act aims to facilitate the promotion and development of micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in India, and to enhance their competitiveness.

The Act establishes a National Board for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (Board) to advise the Central Government on matters related to the promotion and development of MSMEs. The Board consists of various members, including Ministers of State Governments, Members of Parliament, the Administrator of a Union territory, Secretaries to the Government of India, the Chairman of the Board of Directors of the National Bank, the Chairman and Managing Director of the Small Industries Bank, the Chairman of the Indian Banks Association, an officer of the Reserve Bank, representatives of associations of MSMEs, and persons of eminence from the fields of economics, industry, and science and technology.

The Act also provides for the classification of enterprises based on the investment in plant and machinery or equipment. Enterprises engaged in the manufacture or production of goods are classified as micro, small, or medium enterprises based on the investment in plant and machinery, while enterprises engaged in providing or rendering of services are classified based on the investment in equipment.

The Act specifies measures for the promotion and development of MSMEs, including the development of skills in employees, management, and entrepreneurs, provisioning for technological upgradation, marketing assistance, infrastructure facilities, and cluster development. The Act also provides for credit facilities to MSMEs and specifies policies and practices to ensure timely and smooth flow of credit to such enterprises.

The Act also addresses the issue of delayed payments to MSMEs. It specifies that buyers shall make payment for goods supplied or services rendered by MSMEs on or before the agreed-upon date, which shall not exceed 45 days from the date of acceptance or the date of deemed acceptance. The Act provides for the payment of compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank, from the appointed day or the date immediately following the agreed-upon date, in case of delayed payments.

The Act establishes Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Councils to resolve disputes between suppliers and buyers regarding delayed payments. The Councils may either conduct conciliation or refer the dispute to any institution or centre providing alternate dispute resolution services. The Act also provides for the recovery of the amount due, and the setting aside of any decree, award, or other order made by the Council or any institution or centre providing alternate dispute resolution services.

The Act also provides for the establishment of one or more funds by notification, and the crediting of grants made by the Central Government to the Fund or Funds. The Central Government shall administer the Fund or Funds and shall be responsible for the coordination and ensuring timely utilization and release of sums in accordance with the prescribed criteria.

The Act specifies penalties for contravention of certain provisions, and provides for the jurisdiction of courts. The Act also empowers the Central Government to make rules to carry out the provisions of the Act, and provides for the repeal of the Interest on Delayed Payments to Small Scale and Ancillary Industrial Undertakings Act, 1993.
