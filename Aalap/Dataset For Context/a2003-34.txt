Summary:

This document is titled "THE CIGARETTES AND OTHER TOBACCO PRODUCTS (PROHIBITION OF ADVERTISEMENT AND REGULATION OF TRADE AND COMMERCE, PRODUCTION, SUPPLY AND DISTRIBUTION) ACT, 2003." It was enacted on May 18, 2003. The Act aims to prohibit the advertisement of cigarettes and other tobacco products, regulate their trade and commerce, production, supply, and distribution, and protect public health.

The Act defines various terms, such as "advertisement," "cigarette," "distribution," "export," "foreign language," "import," "Indian language," "label," "package," "prescribed," "production," "public place," "sale," "smoking," "specified warning," and "tobacco products."

The Act prohibits smoking in public places, unless a separate provision for smoking is made in hotels having thirty rooms or more or restaurants having seating capacity of thirty persons or more, and in airports.

The Act also prohibits the advertisement of cigarettes and other tobacco products through any medium and prohibits the sale of cigarettes and other tobacco products to any person who is under eighteen years of age and within a radius of one hundred yards of any educational institution.

The Act requires every package of cigarettes or other tobacco products to bear a specified warning, including a pictorial warning, as prescribed, and to indicate the nicotine and tar contents on each cigarette or other tobacco product, along with the maximum permissible limits thereof.

The Act specifies the manner in which the specified warning shall be made and the language in which it shall be expressed. It also specifies the size of letters and figures for the specified warning and indication of nicotine and tar contents.

The Act empowers the Central Government to grant recognition to a testing laboratory for testing the nicotine and tar contents in cigarettes and other tobacco products.

The Act authorizes police officers, officers of State Food or Drug Administration, or any other officer, holding the equivalent rank being not below the rank of Sub-Inspector of Police, to enter and search any factory, building, business premises, or any other place where any trade or commerce in cigarettes or any other tobacco products is carried on or cigarettes or any other tobacco products are produced, supplied, or distributed, or where any advertisement of cigarettes or any other tobacco products has been or is being made.

The Act authorizes the seizure of any package of cigarettes or any other tobacco products or any advertisement material of cigarettes or any other tobacco products, in respect of which any provision of this Act has been or is being contravened.

The Act provides for the confiscation of any package of cigarettes or any other tobacco products or any advertisement material of cigarettes or any other tobacco products, in respect of which any provision of this Act has been or is being contravened.

The Act empowers the court to give an option to pay costs in lieu of confiscation.

The Act specifies the limits of confiscation and the powers of the court to adjudge confiscation or costs.

The Act provides for the return of seized packages to the owner if it is established that he is not responsible for the contravention of the provisions of this Act.

The Act provides for an appeal against the decision of the court adjudging confiscation or costs.

The Act specifies the punishment for failure to give specified warning and nicotine and tar contents.

The Act specifies the punishment for smoking in certain places, which is a fine not exceeding two hundred rupees.

The Act specifies the punishment for advertisement of cigarettes and tobacco products, which is imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years or fine which may extend to one thousand rupees or both for the first conviction, and imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and fine which may extend to five thousand rupees for the second or subsequent conviction.

The Act provides for the forfeiture of advertisement and advertisement material.

The Act specifies the punishment for sale of cigarettes or any other tobacco products in certain places or to persons below the age of eighteen years, which is a fine not exceeding two hundred rupees.

The Act provides for the prevention, detention, and place of trial of offences under sections 4 and 6.

The Act specifies the punishment for offences by companies.

The Act specifies that offences under this Act shall be bailable.

The Act provides for the composition of offences.

The Act provides for the protection of action taken in good faith.

The Act empowers the Central Government to make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

The Act does not apply to any cigarette or other tobacco products or package of cigarettes or other tobacco products which is exported.

The Act repeals the Cigarettes (Regulation of Production, Supply and Distribution) Act, 1975, and provides for the continuance of anything done or any action taken under the provisions of the aforesaid Act.

The Schedule to the Act lists the tobacco products to which this Act applies.
