Summary:
THE LEGAL REPRESENTATIVES’ SUITS ACT, 1855 (ACTNO. 12 OF 1855) was enacted on 27th March, 1855. The Act enables executors, administrators, or representatives to sue and be sued for certain wrongs committed in the lifetime of the deceased person, provided the wrong has caused pecuniary loss to the estate and an action would have been maintainable by the deceased person. The action must be commenced within one year before the person's death. Additionally, an action may be maintained against the executors, administrators, heirs, or representatives of a deceased person for any wrong committed by him, provided the wrong was committed within one year before the person's death.

The Act also states that the death of either party will not abate the suit, and the action may be continued by or against the executors, administrators, or representatives of the party deceased. However, in any case in which an action is continued against the executors, administrators, or representatives of a deceased party, they may set up a want of assets as a defense to the action.

The Act has been extended to the whole of the State of Karnataka, and it has been declared in force in the whole of India, except Part B States and the Scheduled Districts. The Act has also been extended to and brought into force in Dadra and Nagar Haveli, the Union Territory of Pondicherry, and the State of Sikkim.

Section 1 of the Act has been renumbered as section 1A, and before the section 1A as so renumbered, the following section has been inserted, namely: “1. Short title and extent.—This Act may be called the Legal Representatives’ Suits Act, 1855, and it extends to the whole of the State of Karnataka.”

The words “and provided such action shall be brought within one year after the death of such person” and “and so as such action shall be commenced within two years after the committing of the wrong” have been repealed by Act 9 of 1871, s.2 and the First Schedule, and the Indian Limitation Act, 1963 (36 of 1963), respectively.
