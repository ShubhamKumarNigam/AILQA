Summary:

The Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937, is an Act that extends to the whole of India. Its purpose is to make provision for the application of the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) to Muslims in various matters. These matters include intestate succession, special property of females, marriage, dissolution of marriage, maintenance, dower, guardianship, gifts, trusts and trust properties, and wakfs (other than charities and charitable institutions and charitable and religious endowments). This Act applies to all questions regarding these matters, except those related to agricultural land.

Under this Act, any person who satisfies the prescribed authority that they are a Muslim, are competent to contract within the meaning of section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, and are a resident of the territories to which this Act extends can make a declaration in the prescribed form and file it before the prescribed authority. Once this declaration is made, the provisions of section 2 of this Act will apply to the declarant and all their minor children and their descendants as if adoption, wills, and legacies were also specified.

The State Government has the power to make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act. These rules may provide for prescribing the authority before whom and the form in which declarations under this Act shall be made, prescribing the fees to be paid for the filing of declarations and for the attendance at private residences of any person in the discharge of his duties under this Act, and prescribing the times at which such fees shall be payable and the manner in which they shall be levied.

This Act repeals certain provisions of the Acts and Regulations mentioned in section 6 that are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act. These provisions include Section 26 of the Bombay Regulation IV of 1827, Section 16 of the Madras Civil Courts Act, 1873, Section 3 of the Oudh Laws Act, 1876, Section 5 of the Punjab Laws Act, 1872, Section 5 of the Central Provinces Laws Act, 1875, and Section 4 of the Ajmere Laws Regulation, 1877.

In summary, the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937, provides for the application of the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) to Muslims in various matters, including intestate succession, special property of females, marriage, dissolution of marriage, maintenance, dower, guardianship, gifts, trusts and trust properties, and wakfs (other than charities and charitable institutions and charitable and religious endowments). This Act applies to all questions regarding these matters, except those related to agricultural land. The Act also provides for the making of rules by the State Government to carry out its purposes and repeals certain provisions of the Acts and Regulations mentioned in section 6 that are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act.
