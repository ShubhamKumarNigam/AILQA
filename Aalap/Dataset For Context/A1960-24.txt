THE DELHI LAND HOLDINGS (CEILING) ACT, 1960

The Delhi Land Holdings (Ceiling) Act, 1960, is a law that aims to regulate land holdings in the Union territory of Delhi. The Act sets a ceiling limit on the amount of land that a person or family can hold, and any excess land beyond this limit is to be vested in the Government.

The Act defines key terms such as "Chief Commissioner," "competent authority," "family," "person," and "person under disability." The Chief Commissioner is the head of the Delhi administration, while the competent authority is a person or authority authorized by the Chief Commissioner to perform certain functions under the Act. A family includes the person, his or her spouse, and minor children, while a person under disability includes a widow, a minor, a woman who is unmarried or divorced or judicially separated from her husband, a member of the Armed Forces, and a person incapable of cultivating land due to physical or mental disability.

The Act sets a ceiling limit on land holdings, which varies depending on the type of land and its irrigation source. For instance, a person can hold up to 7.25 hectares of land that is assured of irrigation from a private source and is capable of yielding at least two crops in a year. The Act also provides for the conversion of land holdings from one category to another, and sets out the procedure for determining excess land.

The Act provides for the submission of returns by persons holding land in excess of the ceiling limit, and sets out the procedure for determining excess land. It also empowers the competent authority to collect information through other agencies if a return is not submitted. The Act lays down the procedure for the determination of excess land, and provides for the publication of a list of excess land in the Official Gazette and in other specified manner.

The Act provides for the selection of excess land in cases of certain transfers, and lays down the consequences of such transfers. It also provides for the abatement of certain suits for the specific performance of a contract for the transfer of land.

The Act provides for the vesting of excess land in the Government, and sets out the procedure for the payment of compensation to the landowner. It also provides for the payment of an annuity to religious or charitable institutions in lieu of compensation.

The Act lays down the manner of payment of compensation, and provides for the levy of interest on delayed payments. It also sets a limit on future acquisition of land, and provides for the reservation of excess land for certain purposes. The Act empowers the Chief Commissioner to allot excess land to specified persons, and provides for the finality of orders and the bar of jurisdiction.

The Act protects the action taken under the Act from legal proceedings, and provides for the making of rules by the Chief Commissioner. It also repeals certain provisions of the Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954.
