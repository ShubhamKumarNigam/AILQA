Summary:
The Mumbai Port Trust (Licensing and Control of Pilots) Regulations, 2007 outlines the rules for licensing and controlling pilots at the Port of Mumbai. These regulations cover various aspects, including definitions, qualifications and conditions for obtaining a pilot license, training and examination, classification of pilots, shore station monitoring and control, duties of pilots, and administrative procedures.

Key definitions include "Board," "Chairman," "Deputy Chairman," "Control Station," "Deputy Conservator," "Dock Master," "Harbour Master," "Limits of compulsory pilotage waters," "Pilot," "Pilot Licence," and "Port."

To obtain a Pilot Licence, candidates must meet specific qualifications, including being an Indian national, producing certificates of good character and sobriety, holding a Certificate of Competency as Master (Foreign Going) or a certificate as Dredge Master, obtaining a certificate of physical fitness, and serving a period of probationary training of not less than six months.

The regulations also cover the subjects of examination, examination committee, failure to pass examination, and issue of licence. Pilots are classified based on their experience and are permitted to pilot vessels of increasing tonnage as they gain more experience.

The Shore Station is responsible for monitoring and controlling the boarding and disembarking of pilots and other activities. The Harbour Master and Dock Masters have control over Master pilots and pilots in pilotage charge of vessels while entering or leaving the port, mooring or unmooring and berthing or un-berthing at any anchorage, berth, or dock within the port.

Pilots are required to obey the orders of the authority, exercise strict sobriety, and use almost care and diligence for the safety of the vessel and other property. They are also required to obtain a certificate of services performed by them and go on board vessels in good time.

The regulations also cover the duties of pilots when moving vessels, reporting on landing, not bringing vessels into dock when the signal is against them, carrying their licence when on duty, and reporting the loss of their licence. Pilots are also required to report any alterations in navigational marks and report casualties.

Finally, the regulations cover administrative procedures, including maintaining a log book at the Control Station, senior pilots assisting and instructing junior pilots and probationary pilots, pilots giving evidence, pilot's examination of charts, and uniform. The regulations also include provisions for interpreting the regulations and repealing and saving previous regulations.
