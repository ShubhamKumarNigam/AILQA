Summary:

The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (Act No. 1 of 2014) establishes the Lokpal, an anti-corruption ombudsman, for the Union and Lokayuktas for States to inquire into allegations of corruption against certain public functionaries. The Act defines the Lokpal as a body consisting of a Chairperson and Members. The Chairperson must be a retired Chief Justice of India, a retired Judge of the Supreme Court, or an eminent person who fulfills certain eligibility criteria. The Members must be persons of impeccable integrity and outstanding ability having special knowledge and expertise of not less than twenty-five years in the matters relating to anti-corruption policy, public administration, vigilance, finance including insurance and banking, law and management. The Act specifies the qualifications, disqualifications, and conditions of service of the Chairperson and Members.

The Act provides for the appointment of the Lokpal by the President on the recommendations of a Selection Committee consisting of the Prime Minister, the Speaker of the House of the People, the Leader of Opposition in the House of the People, the Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by him, and an eminent jurist recommended by the other members of the Selection Committee. The Act also provides for the appointment of the Secretary, other officers and staff of the Lokpal.

The Act establishes an Inquiry Wing and a Prosecution Wing of the Lokpal. The Inquiry Wing is responsible for conducting preliminary inquiries into any offence alleged to have been committed by a public servant punishable under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. The Prosecution Wing is responsible for prosecuting public servants in relation to any complaint by the Lokpal under this Act.

The Act provides for the expenses of the Lokpal to be charged on the Consolidated Fund of India. The Act also provides for the jurisdiction of the Lokpal to include the Prime Minister, Ministers, members of Parliament, Group A, B, C and D officers and officials of Central Government, and other specified persons and entities.

The Act provides for the procedure for making complaints, conducting preliminary inquiries, and investigating allegations of corruption. The Act also provides for the powers and functions of the Lokpal, the Inquiry Wing, and the Prosecution Wing.

The Act includes provisions for the removal and suspension of the Chairperson and Members of the Lokpal, and for the appointment of an acting Chairperson in certain circumstances. The Act also includes provisions for the protection of action taken in good faith by any public servant, and for the protection of action taken in good faith by others.

The Act includes provisions for the declaration of assets by public servants, and for the punishment of false complaints and the payment of compensation to public servants in certain circumstances. The Act also includes provisions for the offences and penalties for non-compliance with the provisions of the Act.

The Act includes provisions for the amendment of certain enactments, and for the making of rules and regulations by the Central Government and the Lokpal. The Act also includes provisions for the removal of difficulties in giving effect to the provisions of the Act.

The Act includes a Schedule that amends certain enactments, including the Commissions of Inquiry Act, 1952, the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946, the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, and the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003.

The Act came into force on 1st January, 2014.
