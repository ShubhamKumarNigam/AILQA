Summary:
The Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969, is a law that provides for the regulation of registration of births and deaths in India. The Act applies to the whole of India and came into force on different dates in different parts of a State. The Act defines various terms related to births and deaths, such as "birth," "death," "foetal death," "live-birth," "prescribed," "State Government," and "still-birth." The Act establishes various offices and authorities responsible for implementing the provisions of the Act, including the Registrar-General, India, Chief Registrar, District Registrar, and Registrar.

Under the Act, it is the duty of certain persons to give information regarding births and deaths to the Registrar. These persons include the head of the house or the oldest adult male person present in the house in the case of a birth or death in a house, the medical officer in charge or any person authorized by him in the case of a birth or death in a hospital, health centre, maternity or nursing home or other like institution, the jailor in charge in the case of a birth or death in a jail, the person in charge in the case of a birth or death in a choultry, chattram, hostel, dharmasala, boarding-house, lodging-house, tavern, barrack, toddy shop or place of public resort, and the headman or other corresponding officer of the village or the officer in charge of the local police station in the case of a new-born child or dead body found deserted in a public place.

The Act also specifies the duties of the Registrar, including entering in the register maintained for the purpose all information given to him under section 8 or section 9, taking steps to inform himself carefully of every birth and of every death which takes place in his jurisdiction, and ascertaining and registering the particulars required to be registered. The Registrar is required to have an office in the local area for which he is appointed and to attend his office for the purpose of registering births and deaths on such days and at such hours as the Chief Registrar may direct. The Registrar may, with the prior approval of the Chief Registrar, appoint Sub-Registrars and assign to them any or all of his powers and duties in relation to specified areas within his jurisdiction.

The Act provides for the registration of births and deaths in a plantation, with the superintendent of the plantation being responsible for giving or causing to be given to the Registrar the information referred to in section 8. The Act also specifies the duties of certain persons to notify births and deaths and to certify the cause of death, and provides for the correction or cancellation of entry in the register of births and deaths.

The Act requires the Registrar to keep registers of births and deaths in the prescribed form and to send periodical returns to the Chief Registrar for compilation. The Chief Registrar is required to cause the information in the returns furnished by the Registrars to be compiled and to publish for the information of the public a statistical report on the registered births and deaths during the year at such intervals and in such form as may be prescribed.

The Act provides for the registration of information as to births and deaths of citizens of India outside India received by the Registrar-General under the rules relating to the registration of such citizens at Indian Consulates made under the Citizenship Act, 1955. The Act also specifies the powers and duties of the Registrar, including the power to obtain information regarding birth or death and the power to give directions.

The Act provides for penalties for various offences, including failing to give information, giving false information, refusing to write one's name, description, and place of abode in the register, neglecting or refusing to register a birth or death, neglecting or refusing to submit returns, and contravening any provision of the Act for which no penalty is provided. The Act also provides for the compounding of offences and the sanction for prosecution.

The Act provides that Registrars and Sub-Registrars shall be deemed to be public servants within the meaning of section 21 of the Indian Penal Code, and that the State Government may delegate its powers under the Act to any officer or authority subordinate to it. The Act also provides for the protection of action taken in good faith and for the repeal and saving of certain laws. The Act empowers the State Government to make rules to carry out its provisions. Every rule made under the Act shall be laid before the State Legislature.

The Act provides that if any difficulty arises in giving effect to its provisions in a State, the State Government may, with the approval of the Central Government, by order make such provisions or give such directions as appears to be necessary or expedient for removing the difficulty. No order shall be made under this section in relation to any area in a State after the expiration of two years from the date on which the Act comes into force in that area.
