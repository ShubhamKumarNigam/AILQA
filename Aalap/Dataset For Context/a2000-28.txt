The Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2000 (Act No. 28 of 2000) is an Act to provide for the reorganisation of the existing State of Madhya Pradesh and for matters connected therewith. The Act includes the following parts:

Part I: Preliminary
This part consists of two sections. Section 1 is about the short title of the Act, which is the Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2000. Section 2 provides definitions for various terms used in the Act.

Part II: Reorganisation of the State of Madhya Pradesh
This part consists of three sections. Section 3 deals with the formation of the State of Chhattisgarh, which includes the territories of Bastar, Dantewada, Dhamtari, Durg, Janjgir-Champa, Jashpur, Kanker, Kawardha, Korba, Koria, Mahasamund, Raigarh, Raipur, Rajnandgaon, and Surguja districts. Section 4 deals with the State of Madhya Pradesh and its territorial divisions, which will comprise the territories of the existing State of Madhya Pradesh other than those specified in Section 3. Section 5 deals with the amendment of the First Schedule to the Constitution, which adds the State of Chhattisgarh and excludes the territories specified in Section 3 from the State of Madhya Pradesh. Section 6 provides saving powers to the State Government to alter the name, area, or boundaries of any district or other territorial division in the State.

Part III: Representation in the Legislatures
This part consists of three subheadings - The Council of States, The House of the People, and The Legislative Assembly.

Under The Council of States, Section 7 deals with the amendment of the Fourth Schedule to the Constitution, which renumbers entries 9 to 27 as entries 10 to 28 and inserts a new entry 9 for Chhattisgarh with 5 seats. Section 8 deals with the allocation of sitting members, which states that the sixteen sitting members of the Council of States representing the existing State of Madhya Pradesh shall be deemed to have been elected to fill the seats allotted to the States of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, as specified in the First Schedule to this Act.

Under The House of the People, Section 9 deals with the representation in the House of the People, which allocates 29 seats to the successor State of Madhya Pradesh and 11 to the successor State of Chhattisgarh. Section 10 deals with the delimitation of Parliamentary and Assembly constituencies, which stands amended as directed in the Second Schedule to this Act. Section 11 deals with the provision as to sitting members, which deems every sitting member of the House of the People representing a constituency which, on the appointed day by virtue of the provisions of section 10, stands allotted, with or without alteration of boundaries, to the successor States of Madhya Pradesh or Chhattisgarh, to have been elected to the House of the People by that constituency as so allotted.

Under The Legislative Assembly, Section 12 deals with the provisions as to Legislative Assemblies, which states that the number of seats as on the appointed day in the Legislative Assemblies of the States of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh shall be two hundred and thirty and ninety respectively. Section 13 deals with the allocation of sitting members, which states that every sitting member of the Legislative Assembly of the existing State of Madhya Pradesh elected to fill a seat in that Assembly from a constituency which on the appointed day by virtue of the provisions of section 10 stands allotted, with or without alteration of boundaries, to the State of Chhattisgarh shall, on and from that day, cease to be a member of the Legislative Assembly of Madhya Pradesh and shall be deemed to have been elected to fill a seat in the Legislative Assembly of Chhattisgarh from that constituency so allotted. Section 14 deals with the duration of Legislative Assemblies, which states that the period of five years referred to in clause (1) of article 172, shall, in the case of Legislative Assembly of the State of Madhya Pradesh and the State of Chhattisgarh, be deemed to have commenced on the date on which it actually commenced in the case of Legislative Assembly of the existing State of Madhya Pradesh. Section 15 deals with the Speakers and Deputy Speakers, which states that the persons who immediately before the appointed day are the Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Legislative Assembly of the existing State of Madhya Pradesh shall continue to be the Speaker and Deputy Speaker respectively of that Assembly on and from that day.

In summary, the Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2000 is an Act that provides for the reorganisation of the existing State of Madhya Pradesh and for matters connected therewith. The Act includes provisions related to the formation of the State of Chhattisgarh, amendment of the First Schedule to the Constitution, representation in the Legislatures, and other related matters. The Act aims to facilitate the smooth transfer of territories, administration, and representation in the Legislatures from the existing State of Madhya Pradesh to the newly formed State of Chhattisgarh.

This document outlines how certain financial obligations, debts, and liabilities are to be divided between the successor states of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh after the bifurcation of Madhya Pradesh.

1. Debts:
a. Debts owed to local bodies, body corporates, or other institutions within a local area will be considered the debt of the State where the local area is located.
b. Debts owed to institutions that become inter-State institutions, such as the Madhya Pradesh State Electricity Board, will be divided between the two successor states in the same proportion as their assets are divided.
2. Sinking funds or depreciation funds:
a. Securities held in respect of investments made from these funds will be divided between the successor states in the same proportion as the total public debt is divided.
3. Floating loans:
a. Liabilities for floating loans to provide short-term finance to local bodies, body corporates, or other institutions will be determined by mutual agreement between the successor states. If no agreement is reached, the Central Government will determine the liability.
4. Refund of taxes collected in excess:
a. The liability to refund taxes collected in excess on property, including land revenue, will be the liability of the successor state where the property is situated.
b. The liability to refund any other tax collected in excess will be the liability of the successor state where the place of assessment of that tax or duty is included.
5. Deposits, etc.:
a. Liabilities in respect of civil or local fund deposits will be the liability of the state where the deposit has been made.
b. Liabilities in respect of charitable or other endowments will be the liability of the state where the institution entitled to the benefit of the endowment is located or of the state to which the objects of the endowment, under the terms thereof, are confined.
6. Provident fund:
a. The liability of the existing state of Madhya Pradesh in respect of the provident fund account of a government servant in service on the appointed day shall, as from that day, be the liability of the state to which that government servant is permanently allotted.
7. Pensions:
a. The liability of the existing state of Madhya Pradesh in respect of pensions shall pass to, or be apportioned between, the successor states of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh in accordance with the provisions contained in the Sixth Schedule to this Act.
8. Contracts:
a. Contracts made by the existing state of Madhya Pradesh for any purposes of the state shall be deemed to have been made in the exercise of the executive power of the successor state if the purposes of the contract are, on and from the appointed day, exclusive purposes of that state.
b. All rights and liabilities which have accrued, or may accrue under any such contract, shall, to the extent to which they would have been rights or liabilities of the existing state of Madhya Pradesh, be rights or liabilities of the state of Chhattisgarh or the state of Madhya Pradesh, as the case may be.
9. Liability in respect of actionable wrong:
a. If the cause of action arose wholly within the territories of either of the successor states, the liability shall be that of the successor state.
b. In any other case, the liability shall be initially that of the state of Madhya Pradesh, but subject to financial adjustment as may be agreed upon between the states or directed by the Central Government.
10. Liability as guarantor:
a. If the area of operations of the society or person is limited to the territories of either of the successor states, the liability shall be that of the successor state.
b. In any other case, the liability shall be initially that of the state of Madhya Pradesh, subject to financial adjustment as may be agreed upon between the states or directed by the Central Government.
11. Items in suspense:
a. If any item in suspense is ultimately found to affect an asset or liability of the nature referred to in any of the foregoing provisions of this Part, it shall be dealt with in accordance with that provision.
12. Residuary provision:
a. The benefit or burden of any asset or liability of the existing state of Madhya Pradesh not dealt with in the foregoing provisions of this Part shall pass to the state of Madhya Pradesh in the first instance, subject to financial adjustment as may be agreed upon between the states or directed by the Central Government.
13. Apportionment of assets or liabilities by agreement:
a. If the successor states agree on a different apportionment of an asset or liability than that provided for in the foregoing provisions, it shall be apportioned in the manner agreed upon.
14. Power of Central Government to order allocation or adjustment in certain cases:
a. If the Central Government is of the opinion that it is just and equitable that property or benefits should be transferred to, or shared with, the other successor state, or that a contribution towards a liability should be made by the other successor state, it may order such allocation or adjustment.
15. Certain expenditure to be charged on Consolidated Fund:
a. All sums payable by either of the successor states or the Central Government to the other state shall be charged on the Consolidated Fund of the state or the Central Government, as the case may be.
