Summary:

The United Nations (Security Council) Act, 1947, is a law enacted in India to enable the implementation of certain provisions of the Charter of the United Nations. The Act is also known as Act No. 43 of 1947 and was enacted on December 20, 1947.

The Act has two sections. The first section is the short title, which states that the Act may be called the United Nations (Security Council) Act, 1947.

The second section deals with measures under Article 41 of the Charter of the United Nations. According to this section, if the Security Council of the United Nations calls upon the Central Government of India to apply any measures, not involving the use of armed force, to give effect to any decision of that Council, the Central Government may, by order published in the Official Gazette, make such provisions (including provisions having extra-territorial operation) as appear to it necessary or expedient for enabling those measures to be effectively applied. The section also provides for the punishment of persons offending against the order.

In summary, the United Nations (Security Council) Act, 1947, is a law in India that enables the implementation of certain provisions of the Charter of the United Nations. The Act allows the Central Government to make provisions for the effective application of measures called upon by the Security Council, including the punishment of persons offending against the order.
