Summary:
The Special Criminal Courts (Jurisdiction) Act, 1950 is a law that extends to the whole of India, except for the territories that were previously Part B States. This Act confers jurisdiction upon special criminal courts, constituted by or under certain state laws, to try offences against laws with respect to any of the matters enumerated in the Union List. The term "special Criminal Court" refers to any special court of criminal jurisdiction constituted by or under any law included in the Schedule. The Schedule includes various state laws providing for the trial of offences by special criminal courts, such as The Bombay Public Security Measures Act, 1947, The Central Provinces and Berar Public Safety Act, 1948, The West Bengal Special Courts Ordinance, 1949, The West Bengal Special Courts Act, 1950, The Assam Special Courts Act, 1950, The West Bengal Criminal Law Amendment (Special Courts) Act, 1949, and The Tribunals of Criminal Jurisdiction Ordinance, 1952 (West Bengal Ordinance). The Central Government may add to the Schedule any other state law providing for the trial of offences by special criminal courts through a notification in the Official Gazette. The Special Criminal Courts (Jurisdiction) Ordinance, 1950, is repealed by this Act, but anything done or any action taken under the Ordinance shall be deemed to have been done or taken under this Act.
