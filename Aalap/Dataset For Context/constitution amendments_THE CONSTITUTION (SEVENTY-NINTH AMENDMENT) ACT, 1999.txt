Summary:
The Constitution (Seventy-Ninth Amendment) Act, 1999, also known as the Constitution (Eighty-Fourth Amendment) Bill, 1999, extends the reservation of seats for the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes and the representation of the Anglo-Indian community by nomination in the Lok Sabha and the Legislative Assemblies of the States for an additional period of ten years. This extension applies to the reservation that was due to expire on January 25, 2000, and it will now continue until January 25, 2010. The amendment aims to address the fact that although there has been progress for these groups, the reasons for the original reservation have not completely disappeared. The amendment is achieved by substituting the words "fifty years" with "sixty years" in Article 334 of the Constitution. This amendment is the seventh extension of the reservation period, with previous extensions occurring in 1960, 1970, 1980, 1990, 2000, and 2010.
