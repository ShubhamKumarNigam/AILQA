The Authoritative Texts (Central Laws) Act, 1973, is an Act that provides for authoritative texts of Central laws in certain languages. The Act may be called the Authoritative Texts (Central Laws) Act, 1973, and it came into force on a date appointed by the Central Government.

Section 2 of the Act states that a translation in any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, published under the authority of the President in the Official Gazette, of any Central Act or of any Ordinance promulgated by the President, or of any order, rule, regulation, or bye-law issued under the Constitution or under any Central Act, shall be deemed to be the authoritative text thereof in such language.

The Central Government may make rules for carrying out the purposes of this Act, and every rule made under this section shall be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days. If both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall have effect only in the modified form or be of no effect.

The Act uses the term "authoritative texts" to refer to the translations of Central laws in certain languages, and it defines these texts as the official and legally valid versions of the laws in those languages. The Act ensures that the translations are accurate and reliable, and it provides a mechanism for updating and amending the translations as necessary. The Act applies to all Central laws, including Acts, Ordinances, orders, rules, regulations, and bye-laws, and it covers all languages specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution.
