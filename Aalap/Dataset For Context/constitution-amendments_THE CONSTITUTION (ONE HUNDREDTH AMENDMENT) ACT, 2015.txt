The Constitution (One Hundredth Amendment) Act, 2015 is an amendment to the Constitution of India, which was enacted on May 28, 2015. This amendment aims to provide constitutional recognition to the National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC), which is responsible for the appointment and transfer of judges to the higher judiciary in India.

Prior to this amendment, the appointment of judges to the higher judiciary was made through a collegium system, where a panel of senior judges would recommend appointments to the government. However, this system was criticized for being opaque and lacking accountability.

The NJAC was established as an alternative to the collegium system, with the intention of making the appointment process more transparent and accountable. It consists of six members, including the Chief Justice of India, two senior judges of the Supreme Court, the Union Law Minister, and two eminent persons nominated by a committee consisting of the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and the Chief Justice of India.

The Constitution (One Hundredth Amendment) Act, 2015 amends the Constitution to provide for the establishment of the NJAC and to specify its composition, functions, and powers. The Act also amends various provisions of the Constitution relating to the appointment and transfer of judges to the higher judiciary, including Articles 124, 217, and 222.

Under the new system, the NJAC will recommend appointments and transfers of judges to the higher judiciary, and the government will be required to act on these recommendations. The Act provides for certain safeguards to ensure the independence of the judiciary, including provisions that require the NJAC to consult with the Chief Justice of India and other senior judges before making recommendations.

The Constitution (One Hundredth Amendment) Act, 2015 has been the subject of much debate and controversy, with some arguing that it undermines the independence of the judiciary and compromises the separation of powers between the judiciary and the executive. However, supporters of the Act argue that it will make the appointment process more transparent and accountable, and will help to ensure that only the most qualified and deserving candidates are appointed to the higher judiciary.
