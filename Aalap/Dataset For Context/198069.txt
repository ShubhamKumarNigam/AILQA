Summary:
The Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, with the Act ID ACT NO. 69 OF 1980, came into force on 25th October 1980. The Act aims to provide for the conservation of forests and matters related to it. The Act consists of five sections and one explanation.

Section 1 of the Act provides the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act. The Act extends to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir.

Section 2 of the Act restricts the dereservation of forests or the use of forest land for non-forest purposes. No State Government or other authority can make any order directing that any reserved forest or any portion thereof shall cease to be reserved or that any forest land or any portion thereof may be used for any non-forest purpose without the prior approval of the Central Government. The explanation to this section defines non-forest purpose as the breaking up or clearing of any forest land or portion thereof for any purpose other than reafforestation.

Section 3 of the Act authorizes the Central Government to constitute a Committee to advise it regarding the grant of approval under section 2 and any other matter connected with the conservation of forests.

Section 4 of the Act empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days.

Section 5 of the Act repeals the Forest (Conservation) Ordinance, 1980, and saves anything done or any action taken under the provisions of the said Ordinance.

There are no annexures, appendices, or forms associated with this Act. However, a footnote mentions that the words "except the State of Jammu and Kashmir" were omitted by Act 34 of 2019, section 95, and the Fifth Schedule (w.e.f. 31-10-2019).
