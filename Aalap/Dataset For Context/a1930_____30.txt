Summary:
The Hindu Gains of Learning Act, 1930, is a law that aims to clarify the rights of a member of a Hindu undivided family in property acquired through learning. The Act, which applies to the whole of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir, defines gains of learning as all acquisitions of property made substantially by means of learning, whether such acquisitions were made before or after the commencement of the Act. Learning is defined as education and training of every kind that enables a person to pursue a trade, industry, profession, or avocation in life.

Under this Act, gains of learning cannot be held not to be the exclusive and separate property of the acquirer merely because the acquirer's learning was imparted to him by a family member, with the aid of the joint funds of the family, or if the acquirer or his family was maintained or supported by the joint funds of the family while he was acquiring his learning.

The Act also includes provisions to ensure that it does not affect any transfer of property made or effected before its commencement, the validity of anything already suffered or done before its commencement, any right or liability created under a partition or agreement for a partition of joint family property made before its commencement, or any remedy or proceeding in respect of such right or liability. Additionally, any remedy or proceeding pending in a Court at the commencement of the Act may be enforced, instituted, or continued as if the Act had not been passed.

The Act has been extended to the Union territories of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Pondicherry, and it is now also applicable to the Union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
