The Constitution (Eighty-third Amendment) Act, 2000, is an amendment to the Constitution of India that provides for the exemption of the State of Arunachal Pradesh from the reservation of seats for the Scheduled Castes in Panchayats. This is because Arunachal Pradesh is a State inhabited fully by indigenous tribal people, with no Scheduled Castes existing in the State.

The Constitution (Eighthy-third Amendment) Act, 2000, inserts a new clause (3A) in article 243M of the Constitution of India, which exempts the State of Arunachal Pradesh from the application of article 243D relating to the reservation of seats in Panchayats for the Scheduled Castes. This is to provide a legal and constitutional basis for Panchayat Raj Institutions in Arunachal Pradesh in accordance with the socio-political ethos of the State.

The Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation, 1873 and the Chin Hill Regulations, 1896 provide special protection and safeguard for the peaceful existence of the indigenous tribal people of Arunachal Pradesh. These laws prohibit the entry of outsiders in the tribal area without Inner Line Permit. Only the indigenous tribal people are allowed to participate in the democratic process.

The tribal society in Arunachal Pradesh is casteless, with social equality among men and women prevailing over centuries and ages.

The Constitution (Eighty-third Amendment) Act, 2000, seeks to achieve the aforesaid objective.
