Summary:
The Constitution (Second Amendment) Act, 1952, is a law passed in India that amends the Constitution. Specifically, it changes Article 81(1)(b), which previously required that there be not less than one member for every 750,000 of the population in each parliamentary constituency. This provision was removed by the amendment, meaning that there is no longer a limit on the maximum population for a parliamentary constituency. The purpose of this change was to allow for greater flexibility in assigning representatives to parliamentary constituencies based on population. The act received assent from then President Rajendra Prasad on 1 May 1953 and came into force on the same date, but was notified in the Gazette of India on 2 May 1953.
