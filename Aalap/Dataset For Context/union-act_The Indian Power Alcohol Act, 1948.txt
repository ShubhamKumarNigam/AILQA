Summary:

The Indian Power Alcohol Act, 1948, is a law that applies to the entire country of India, with the exception of the state of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act allows the Central Government to regulate the power alcohol industry, which involves the production and sale of alcohol used as fuel. The Act defines certain key terms, including "molasses," which refers to the residual syrup left over from the production of sugar, and "petrol," which is defined as dangerous petroleum as defined in the Petroleum Act of 1934. The Act also defines "power alcohol" as ethyl alcohol containing at least 99.5% by volume of ethanol, with a strength of 74.4 over proof. This Act is intended to ensure that the production and sale of power alcohol is regulated in the public interest.
