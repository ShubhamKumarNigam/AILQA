Summary:
The Company Secretaries Act, 1980 (Act No. 56 of 1980) aims to regulate and develop the profession of Company Secretaries in India. The Act establishes the Institute of Company Secretaries of India (ICSI) as a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal. The ICSI is responsible for maintaining a Register of members, which includes details such as the member's full name, date of birth, domicile, residential and professional addresses, qualifications, and whether they hold a certificate of practice.

The Act defines professional or other misconduct and establishes the Disciplinary Directorate, Board of Discipline, Disciplinary Committee, and Appellate Authority to investigate and penalize members for such misconduct. The Act also includes provisions for the removal of a member's name from the Register in certain circumstances, such as non-payment of prescribed fees or if the member is subject to any of the disabilities mentioned in section 8.

The Act also establishes Regional Councils to advise and assist the ICSI on matters concerning its functions. These Regional Councils are constituted in such manner and exercise such functions as may be prescribed.

The Act includes provisions for penalties for falsely claiming to be a member, using the name of the Council or awarding a degree of Company Secretary, and for companies not to engage in Company Secretaryship. The Act also prohibits unqualified persons from signing documents on behalf of a Company Secretary in practice or a firm of such Company Secretaries.

The Act includes provisions for the protection of action taken in good faith, the dissolution of the Institute of Company Secretaries of India registered under the Companies Act, and the transfer of its assets and liabilities to the ICSI. The Act also includes miscellaneous provisions such as the power of the Central Government to make rules and the power of the Council to make regulations.

The First Schedule to the Act defines professional misconduct in relation to company secretaries in practice, members of the Institute in service, members of the Institute generally, and other misconduct in relation to members of the Institute generally. The Second Schedule to the Act defines professional misconduct in relation to members of the Institute generally and other misconduct in relation to members of the Institute generally.
