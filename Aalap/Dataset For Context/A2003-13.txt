Summary:

The Control of National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002, abbreviated as THE CONTROL OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS (LAND AND TRAFFIC) ACT, 2002, Act ID: ACT NO. 13 OF 2003, was enacted on the 14th of January, 2003. This Act aims to regulate and control the land within the National Highways, the right of way, and traffic moving on the National Highways, as well as to remove any unauthorized occupation thereon.

The Act is divided into two chapters. Chapter I, titled PRELIMINARY, provides the short title, extent, and commencement of the Act in Section 1. It also defines various terms used throughout the Act in Section 2. For instance, "building" refers to any work of construction done in any manner by use of any material and includes a farm building for agricultural purposes, plinth, doorstep, wall, drain, advertisement board, and other things fixed with such building. "Construct" refers to construct, reconstruct, erect, re-erect, extend, or alter structurally a building. "Cost of land" means the market value of the land as determined by the competent authority of the State Government or the Government of the Union territory appointed for such determination, as the case may be.

Chapter II, titled HIGHWAYS ADMINISTRATION AND TRIBUNALS, ETC, outlines the establishment of Highway Administrations, their powers and functions, and the establishment of Tribunals. Section 3 establishes a body or authority known as Highway Administration to exercise powers and discharge functions conferred on it under this Act. Section 4 provides that a Highway Administration shall exercise powers and discharge functions throughout its jurisdiction specified under this Act subject to such conditions or limitations as may be imposed.

Section 5 establishes the Tribunal for the purposes of this Act and specifies the limits of the Highway within which, or the length of Highway on which, the Tribunal may exercise jurisdiction for entertaining and deciding the appeals filed before it. The Tribunal has the power to entertain appeals from the orders passed or actions taken under sections 26, 27, 28, 36, 37, and 38 by the Highway Administration or an officer authorized on its behalf.

Section 14 provides that a Tribunal shall exercise the jurisdiction, powers, and authority to entertain appeals from the orders passed or actions taken under sections 26, 27, 28, 36, 37, and 38 by the Highway Administration or an officer authorized on its behalf. Section 15 bars any court or other authority, except the Supreme Court and a High Court exercising jurisdiction under articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution, from having or being entitled to exercise any jurisdiction, powers, or authority in relation to the matters specified in section 14.

It establishes Highway Administrations and Tribunals to exercise powers and discharge functions under this Act.
