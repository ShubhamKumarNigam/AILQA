Summary:
The Census Act, 1948, is a law that provides for the taking of a census in India or any part thereof. The Act defines key terms such as "premises," "prescribed," and "vehicle." The Central Government is responsible for taking the census and may appoint a Census Commissioner and Directors of Census Operations to supervise the process. The State Government may also appoint census-officers to take, aid in, or supervise the taking of the census within a specified local area. These census authorities are deemed to be public servants within the meaning of the Indian Penal Code. The Act also includes provisions for the discharge of duties by certain persons in connection with the taking of the census, the power to call upon certain persons to give assistance, and the requisitioning of premises, vehicles, and animals for the purpose of taking the census. Compensation is to be paid to the persons interested in any premises that are requisitioned, and the amount of compensation is to be determined by taking into consideration the rent payable for similar premises in the locality and any reasonable expenses incidental to changing residence or place of business. The Act also includes provisions for the obtaining of information, the power of entry into and inspection of premises, the eviction from requisitioned premises, the release of premises from requisition, the delegation of functions of the Central Government with regard to requisitioning, and penalties for contravention of any order regarding requisitioning. The Act also specifies that every person of whom any question is asked under this Act shall be legally bound to answer such question to the best of his knowledge or belief, and that no person shall be bound to state the name of any female member of his household or of any other person whose name she is forbidden by custom to mention. The Act also includes provisions for the penalties for refusing to perform any duty imposed by this Act or any rule made thereunder, hindering or obstructing another person in performing any such duty, neglecting to use reasonable diligence in performing any duty imposed upon him or in obeying any order issued to him in accordance with this Act or any rule made thereunder, disclosing any information which he has received by means of, or for the purposes of, a census return, removing, secreting, damaging or destroying any census document or dealing with any census document in a manner likely to falsify or impair the tabulations of census results, failing to comply with an order made under section 4A, intentionally giving a false answer to, or refusing to answer to the best of his knowledge or belief, any question asked of him by a census-officer which he is legally bound by section 8 to answer, refusing to allow a census-officer reasonable access to any house, enclosure, vessel or other place, removing, obliterating, altering, or damaging any letters, marks or numbers which have been painted or affixed for the purposes of the census, or knowingly and without sufficient cause to comply with the provisions of section 10 or makes any false return there under, or trespasses into a census office. The Act also includes provisions for the sanction required for prosecutions, the operation of other laws not barred, certain offences to be cognizable and triable summarily, jurisdiction, records of census not open to inspection of admissible in evidence, protection of service interests of members of census staff, protection of action taken in good faith, temporary suspension of other laws as to mode of taking census in municipalities, grant of statistical abstracts, power to extend the provisions of Act to other operations, and power to make rules.
