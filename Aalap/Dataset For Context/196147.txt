Summary:
The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation Act, 1961, establishes a corporation for the purpose of insuring deposits and guaranteeing credit facilities. The Act defines relevant terms, including "banking," "banking company," "Board," "corporation," "credit institution," "defunct banking company," "defunct co-operative bank," "deposit," "eligible co-operative bank," "existing banking company," "existing co-operative bank," "financial institution," "insured bank," "insured deposit," "new banking company," "new co-operative bank," "premium," "prescribed," "Regional Rural Bank," "Reserve Bank," "State Bank," "subsidiary bank," and "co-operative society."

The Act is divided into four chapters. Chapter I, Preliminary, includes sections 1 and 2, which define short title, extent, and commencement, and definitions. Chapter II, Establishment and Management of the Deposit Insurance Corporation, includes sections 3-9, which deal with the establishment, capital, management, and committees of the corporation. Chapter III, Registration of Banking Companies and Co-operative Banks as Insured Banks and Liability of Corporation to Depositors, includes sections 10-13C, which deal with the registration, cancellation of registration, and liability of the corporation to depositors in the event of winding up or liquidation. Chapter IV, Funds, Accounts, and Audit, includes sections 22-32, which deal with funds, accounts, and audit of the corporation.

The Act also includes a First Schedule, which contains a declaration of fidelity and secrecy, and a Second Schedule, which has been repealed. The Appendix contains forms for use under the Act. The Act includes various provisions for penalties, offenses, and indemnity. It also includes provisions for the protection of action taken under the Act, the application of the Companies Act, 1956, and the Insurance Act, 1938, and the liquidation of the corporation. The Act also includes provisions for the giving of directions by the Central Government, the settlement of disputes regarding the amount of premium, and the regulation of the corporation.
