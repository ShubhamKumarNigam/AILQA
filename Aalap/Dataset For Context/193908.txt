Summary:
The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, is a law that applies to the entire country of India and outlines the grounds for which a woman married under Muslim law can obtain a decree for the dissolution of her marriage. The Act specifies ten grounds for divorce, including the husband's absence for a period of four years, neglect or failure to provide maintenance for two years, imprisonment for seven years or more, failure to perform marital obligations for three years, impotence at the time of marriage and continuation, insanity for two years or a virulent venereal disease, repudiation of the marriage before the age of eighteen if given in marriage by her father or other guardian before she attained the age of fifteen, and cruelty by the husband. The Act also provides for notice to be served on the heirs of the husband when his whereabouts are not known, and specifies that the renunciation of Islam by a married Muslim woman or her conversion to another faith shall not by itself operate to dissolve her marriage. The woman shall be entitled to obtain a decree for the dissolution of her marriage on any of the grounds mentioned in section 2. The Act also states that nothing contained in this Act shall affect any right which a married woman may have under Muslim law to her dower or any part thereof on the dissolution of her marriage.
