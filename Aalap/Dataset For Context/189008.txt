The Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, is an Act that consolidates and amends the law relating to guardians and wards. The Act extends to the whole of India, except the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The Act is divided into four chapters, with Chapter I being the preliminary chapter, Chapter II dealing with the appointment and declaration of guardians, Chapter III outlining the duties, rights, and liabilities of guardians, and Chapter IV containing supplemental provisions.

Under Chapter II, the Act provides for the appointment and declaration of guardians by the Court. The Court may make an order appointing or declaring a guardian of the person or property, or both, of a minor if it is satisfied that it is for the welfare of the minor. The Court may also remove any guardian who has not been appointed by will or other instrument or appointed or declared by the Court. The Act specifies the persons who are entitled to apply for an order, including the person desirous of being, or claiming to be, the guardian of the minor, any relative or friend of the minor, the Collector of the district or other local area where the minor ordinarily resides or in which he has property, or the Collector having authority with respect to the class to which the minor belongs. The Court having jurisdiction to entertain the application is determined by whether the application is with respect to the guardianship of the person or property of the minor.

Chapter III of the Act outlines the duties, rights, and liabilities of guardians. A guardian stands in a fiduciary relation to his ward and must not make any profit out of his office. The fiduciary relation of the guardian to his ward extends to and affects purchases by the guardian of the property of the ward and by the ward of the property of the guardian. A guardian of the property of a ward is bound to deal with the property as carefully as a man of ordinary prudence would deal with his own property. The Act also specifies the powers and limitations of guardians of property appointed or declared by the Court.

Chapter IV of the Act contains supplemental provisions, including provisions for the enforcement of orders made under the Act, the penalty for removal of a ward from the jurisdiction of the Court, the penalty for contumacy, reports by Collectors and subordinate Courts, the finality of orders, costs, and the power of the High Court to make rules.

In summary, the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, is an Act that consolidates and amends the law relating to guardians and wards in India. The Act provides for the appointment and declaration of guardians by the Court, outlines the duties, rights, and liabilities of guardians, and contains supplemental provisions for the enforcement of orders made under the Act.
