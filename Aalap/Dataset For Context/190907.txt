Summary:
The Anand Marriage Act, 1909, is a law in India that recognizes and validates the marriage ceremony called Anand, which is common among the Sikhs. This Act, which extends to the entire country, states that any marriage solemnized according to the Anand ceremony is valid and legal. However, this Act does not apply to marriages between non-Sikhs or marriages that have been declared null and void by a court. The Act also does not affect the validity of any marriage solemnized according to any other customary Sikh ceremony. Furthermore, the Act does not validate any marriage between persons who are related to each other in a way that would make their marriage illegal according to Sikh customary law.

The Anand Marriage Act, 1909, also includes provisions for the registration of marriages. The State Government is required to make rules for the registration of Anand marriages, and the parties to the marriage may have the particulars of their marriage entered in a Marriage Register. The Marriage Register is open for inspection and is admissible as evidence of the statements contained therein. However, the validity of an Anand marriage is not affected by the omission to make an entry in the Marriage Register. Every rule made by the State Government under this section must be laid before the State Legislature as soon as possible after they are made.

The Anand Marriage Act, 1909, has been extended to Dadra and Nagar Haveli by Regulation 6 of 1963, and the words "except the State of Jammu and Kashmir" were omitted by Act 34 of 2019. The Act was also amended by Act 29 of 2012 to include the term "Anand Karaj" in the short title and to provide that the parties to the marriage, whose marriage has been registered under this Act, shall not be required to get their marriage registered under any other law for the time being in force, including State Act.
