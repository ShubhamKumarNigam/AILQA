Summary:
The Citizenship Act, 1955, is an Act to provide for the acquisition and determination of Indian citizenship. The Act defines various terms used throughout the Act, including "a Government in India," "illegal migrant," "Indian consulate," "minor," "Overseas Citizen of India Cardholder," "person," "prescribed," and "undivided India." The Act also outlines the different ways in which a person can acquire Indian citizenship, including by birth, descent, registration, naturalization, and incorporation of territory. The Act also includes provisions for the renunciation and termination of citizenship, as well as the conferment of rights on Overseas Citizen of India Cardholders. The Act also includes provisions for the registration of births and deaths of persons of any class or description born or dying outside India, and the levy and collection of fees in respect of applications, registrations, declarations, and certificates under the Act. The Act also includes provisions for the making of rules to carry out the purposes of the Act.
