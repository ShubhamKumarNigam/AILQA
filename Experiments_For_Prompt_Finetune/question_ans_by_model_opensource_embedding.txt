(1) What is the maximum period that a juvenile can be sentenced for a crime? Are there are any exceptions to this?
Ans:In India, the maximum detention period that can be imposed on a juvenile is 3 years in a Special Home, regardless of the gravity of the offence committed. However, if the crime committed is a heinous one such as murder or rape, the plea of juvenility can be set aside and the juvenile can be tried in a criminal court.

(2) Can a child in India be charged as an adult? How is it different from the law in the United states of America?
Ans:In India, a child between 10 to 18 years can be criminally responsible for their actions and be tried by the Youth Court. However, if the crime committed is a heinous one such as murder or rape, the plea of juvenility can be set aside and the child can be tried in a criminal court. In the United States, the age of criminal responsibility varies from state to state, but is generally between 8 and 18 years.

(4) On whom is the burden of proof to prove that his assets were obtained legitimately with respect to the prevention of money laundering act of India?
Ans:The burden of proof to prove that his assets were obtained legitimately with respect to the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act (PMLA) of India lies on the person accused of an offence under Section 3 of PMLA, whose property is attached and proceeded against for Confiscation. The person must disclose the sources of his Income, Earnings or Assets, out of which or means by which he has acquired the property attached, to discharge the burden that the property does not constitute proceeds of crime.

(5) Give me a summary of the nirbhaya case and the ramifications of it on the Indian legal field.
Ans:The Nirbhaya case was a 2012 gang rape and murder of a 23-year-old woman in Delhi, India. The perpetrators of the crime included a juvenile, who was sentenced to three years in a reform facility as per Section 15 of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000. The incident caused public outrage and led to calls for reform in the Juvenile laws in India, as there had been a steep rise in serious crimes involving youth of 16-18 years of age. The punishment for juvenile offenders was made more deterrent in order to create a feeling of fear in the minds of criminals.

(6) What is the procedure that the police must follow when they have been informed of a cognizable offence? Please provide relevant sections to bolster the answer.
Ans:According to Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) of India, if the information given to the police discloses the commission of a cognizable offence, registration of an FIR is mandatory and no preliminary inquiry is permissible in such a situation. If the information does not disclose a cognizable offence but indicates the necessity for an inquiry, a preliminary inquiry may be conducted only to ascertain whether a cognizable offence is disclosed or not. If the inquiry discloses the commission of a cognizable offence, the FIR must be registered. Action must be taken against erring officers who do not register the FIR if information received by him discloses a cognizable offence.

(7)Under what circumstances can the court join the charges of an offender pertaining to Indian law?
Ans:According to Section 218 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) of India, a court can join the charges of an offender if the charges are of the same kind, or if they are based on the same act or series of acts, or if they are connected with the same matter of accusation. The court also has the discretion to order a separate trial even though the case is covered by one of the exceptions enabling a joint trial.

(8) What happens when there is a mistake during the framing of charges of a criminal defendant in Indian law? Please provide relevant sections of law.
Ans:According to Section 211 & Section 212 of the Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC), there is no necessity to adduce reasons for framing charges. However, the magistrate is required to write an order showing reasons if he decides to discharge the accused (Section 16). If the necessary information is conveyed to the accused and no prejudice is caused to him because of the charges, the accused cannot succeed by merely showing that the charges framed were defective. Nor could a conviction recorded on charged under wrong provisions be reversed if the accused was informed of the details of the offences committed and thus no prejudice was caused to him (Section 13). The mere omission to frame a charge or a mere defect in the charge is no ground for setting aside a conviction. Procedural laws are designed to subserve the ends of justice & not to frustrate them by mere technicalities.

(9) Is DNA evidence admissible in Indian courts?
Ans:Yes, DNA evidence is admissible in Indian courts. The Indian Evidence Act, 1872 includes section 112 which states that the fact that any person was born during the continuance of a valid marriage between his mother and any man, or within two hundred and eighty days after its dissolution, the mother remaining unmarried, shall be evidence. Additionally, the Hon'ble Supreme Court has accepted the Delhi High Court judgment of ordering Veteran Congress Leader N.D. Tiwari to undergo the DNA test, which is very important from the viewpoint of the admissibility of such evidence.
